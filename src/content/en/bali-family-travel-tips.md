### Visa / Electronic Customs Declaration / Tourist Tax:

Currently, you need a visa to enter Indonesia! Travelers from Taiwan can apply for an **e-Visa** or a **Visa on Arrival**, with a maximum stay of 30 days and a fee of IDR 500,000.

Everyone must also fill out/submit the electronic customs declaration and tourist tax. Please refer to the link below:

When traveling in Bali, staying connected with your phone and the internet is essential. You can ensure connectivity in several ways: first, purchase a local SIM card from local telecom operator stores or stalls—this is an affordable and convenient way to get online and use the internet anytime. ### Voltage and Plugs:

Indonesia and Bali use a standard voltage of **220 volts (220V)**—an important detail for travelers planning to bring electrical devices. Before packing any electronics, make sure they support the international 220V standard and the specific plug type, so you can use them without issues in Indonesia and Bali.

### Airport Pickup:

When visiting Bali for the first time, you have several options for arranging airport pickup to ensure a smooth arrival at your destination. Here are some common choices:

**Hotel Pickup Service:**

- Some hotels in Bali offer airport pickup services. You can inquire about this when booking your accommodation. It's a convenient and worry-free option—the hotel will send a dedicated car to pick you up, so you don't have to worry about transportation upon arrival.

**Airport Taxi/Ride-Hailing Services:**

- At Bali's airport, you can easily find a taxi stand with official taxi companies. After exiting the terminal, you can also locate the Gojek or Grab pickup lounge and use ride-hailing services. These taxis usually have fixed rates, making them a convenient and quick choice.

**Pre-Booked Private Transfer Service:**

- Before arriving in Bali, you can book a private transfer service online. These services are typically offered by local car rental drivers or online platforms, allowing you to choose your vehicle type and pickup time in advance to ensure someone greets you upon arrival. This option is often the cheapest, but not necessarily the best value.

Overall, Bali is safe, but always keep an eye on your belongings. Avoid walking down empty alleys, don't constantly hold your phone on the street, and ignore strangers who approach you, especially those from the Middle East.

### Respect Local Culture:

Bali is a Hindu region, so when visiting temples, dress modestly and avoid wearing overly revealing clothing.

## **Extra Tips for Traveling with Kids of All Ages**

- Bring your child's favorite toys and activities on the plane to keep them happy.
- In hot weather, make sure kids drink plenty of water and apply sunscreen.
- Bali's climate is hot during the day and cool in the evening; if you're concerned, you can pack a light jacket for the kids.
- Before visiting temples or other religious sites, remind children to respect the local culture.
- Learn some basic Indonesian phrases, like 'thank you.' Check out Jay's [video on how to pronounce them](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuG8s-u_I0Q&t=106s).
