The cultural atmosphere of this hill town is enchanting, making Ubud a prime spot for Instagram-worthy photos. Ancient temples, stunning rice terraces, and Balinese architecture at every turn create an irresistible visual appeal. Every corner of Ubud feels like a unique work of art, letting you feel the city’s deep soul and rich history.

### Ubud Water Palace (👉🏼 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

The Ubud Water Palace (Pura Taman Saraswati), located just a three-minute walk from the Ubud Royal Palace, is a hidden gem tucked behind a Starbucks that often goes unnoticed by tourists, yet boasts breathtaking views. Compared to the Ubud Royal Palace, this temple is more serene and mysterious, offering an excellent spot for photography to capture unique and exquisite moments.

The Ubud Water Palace is renowned for its distinctive water features, situated at the end of a lotus pond walkway. Its architecture is rich in Balinese artistic style, with mysterious stone carvings and structures reflecting the deep cultural roots of the area. Strolling here feels like traveling through time, immersed in ancient myths and religious influences.

### Monkey Forest (📍 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

Ubud's Monkey Forest is a treasure where nature and culture blend seamlessly. Step into this lush sea of greenery, and monkeys frolic around you as if you've become part of the natural world. You can interact with these clever little creatures, experiencing a wondrous connection with nature.

However, since the monkeys here are quite mischievous, visitors should stay alert when entering the forest. These curious little animals might climb all over you, or even snatch your glasses or other belongings. So, it's wise to interact cautiously, avoid bringing food or flashy items, and keep a low profile to prevent drawing their attention.

### World Heritage Rice Terrace Views (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Spots Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![世界遺產梯田景觀（地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_b32736a5ef98517bd7b4ebd87af9506f.jpg)

The rice terraces around Ubud are a highlight of Bali, offering a breathtaking landscape like a painting, and they've earned UNESCO World Heritage status. This terraced area not only showcases nature's wonders but also highlights the beautiful harmony between nature and humans, making it an unmissable destination in Bali.

Tegalalang Rice Terrace is one of the easiest and most popular rice terrace areas to reach from Ubud. The layout here is unique, with winding paths between the terraces weaving a green canvas that vividly displays nature's vitality. On this land, rice waves ripple, creating a moving agricultural poem that blends natural beauty with farming culture.

Another less commercialized rice terrace area is Jatiluwih Rice Terrace, but it's farther from Ubud—a 90-minute drive from Ubud Palace as a starting point—yet it offers a more pristine experience of the terraced fields' beauty!

The best time to capture the beauty of the rice terraces is at dawn, just as the sun rises. At this moment, golden sunlight spills over the terraces, casting rich layers of shadows and creating a mesmerizing play of light and shadow. This is the favorite time for photography enthusiasts and travelers, as the terraces seem immersed in a golden dream.

Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Sacred Tree Scenic Area is located in Buleleng Regency, northern Bali, and is famous for its magnificent thousand-year-old sacred tree. This giant white bark tree (Kayu Putih, meaning 'white wood' in Indonesian) is estimated to be over a thousand years old, with a trunk so thick it takes dozens of people to encircle it, and a towering canopy that covers a vast area. Local residents regard this ancient tree as sacred, believing it possesses spiritual power, and devotees often come here to pray and hold traditional ceremonies.

This sacred tree is not only a natural wonder but also a symbol of the harmonious coexistence of Balinese culture and nature. A small shrine beneath the tree is adorned with traditional black-and-white checkered cloth (poleng), highlighting its religious significance. Visitors can feel a sacred and tranquil atmosphere here, appreciating this ancient life that has witnessed centuries of change in Bali. The surrounding natural environment is serene, making it a unique spot to experience Bali's spiritual culture and natural beauty.

## [Around Pura Ulun Danu Bratan]

### Bali's Ulun Danu Beratan Temple (📍Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_0bcbb190a64dc4d8d05b92348d7fda9c.jpg)

Completed in 1633, Pura Ulun Danu Beratan Temple sits on the shores of Lake Bratan in central Bali, often referred to as the "Lake Bratan Water Temple." Since Lake Bratan is the primary irrigation source for central Bali, this temple once served as a venue for ceremonies honoring Dewi Danu, the goddess of water, lakes, and rivers. Additionally, the 11-tiered temple is dedicated to Shiva and Parvati, and a statue of Buddha is also enshrined here, making it a fusion of Hindu and Buddhist traditions.

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_bf3c1b5963a5c33d38b37b9e3375ccf4.jpg)

What's special is that this Bali water temple is featured on Indonesian currency—it's the second-largest denomination in circulation, with 50,000 Indonesian Rupiah worth about USD$2.

As a volcanic island, Bali is home to one of its most famous hot springs: Toya Devasya. Built on the shores of Lake Batur, this hot spring water park is one of the island's top attractions, renowned for its natural thermal pools, water park, and stunning lake views.

### Pura Besakih, the Mother Temple (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

Pura Besakih, the Mother Temple, is one of Bali's largest and most sacred Hindu temples, perched on the slopes of Mount Agung. Considered the heart of Balinese religion, it is revered as the 'Mother Temple.' The complex features multiple shrines and altars dedicated to various deities.

This grand temple complex sits amidst beautiful valleys and lush green rice terraces, offering breathtaking views. Visitors can soak in the ancient religious atmosphere, exploring intricate temple architecture and a sacred ambiance. Pura Besakih hosts many of Bali's religious and cultural events, such as festivals and ceremonies.

In 1963, Mount Agung erupted, killing 1,700 people, yet the lava flow passed just meters from the temple, sparing Pura Besakih. This is seen as a miracle by the Balinese, a sign that the gods displayed their power without destroying faith. The temple holds profound religious significance and cultural value, drawing visitors from around the world. Guests must adhere to dress codes and respect local religious customs to honor this sacred site.
