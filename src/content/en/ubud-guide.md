### Ubud Water Palace (👉🏼 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

The Ubud Water Palace (Pura Taman Saraswati) is located just a three-minute walk from the Ubud Royal Palace, tucked away in a corner behind Starbucks. Often overlooked by tourists, it boasts breathtaking beauty. Compared to the Ubud Royal Palace, this temple is more serene and mysterious, offering an excellent spot for photography where you can capture unique and exquisite moments.

The Ubud Water Palace is renowned for its distinctive water features, situated at the end of a lotus pond walkway. The architecture of the Ubud Water Palace is also rich in Bali's unique artistic style, with mysterious stone carvings and structures reflecting the deep cultural heritage of the region. Strolling here feels like traveling through time, immersed in ancient myths and religious influences.

### Monkey Forest (📍Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

Ubud's Monkey Forest (Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary) is a treasure trove where nature and culture blend. Step into this lush sea of trees, and monkeys play around you, as if you've become part of the wild. You can interact with these clever little creatures, experiencing a magical connection with nature.

However, since the monkeys here are quite mischievous, visitors should stay alert when entering the Monkey Forest. These curious little animals may climb all over you, or even snatch your glasses or other belongings. So, interact with them cautiously—avoid carrying food or obvious items to prevent drawing their attention.

### World Heritage Rice Terrace Views (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Spots Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![世界遺產梯田景觀（地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_b32736a5ef98517bd7b4ebd87af9506f.jpg)

The rice terraces around Ubud are a highlight of Bali, offering a breathtaking landscape like a painting. They've earned UNESCO World Heritage status, showcasing not only nature's wonders but also the harmonious coexistence of nature and humanity, making them an unmissable destination in Bali.

Tegalalang Rice Terrace is one of the most accessible and popular terraced areas near Ubud. Its unique layout features winding paths that weave through the terraces like a green canvas, vividly displaying nature's vitality. Here, rice paddies ripple in the breeze, creating a moving agricultural poem that blends natural beauty with farming culture.

Another less commercialized terrace area is Jatiluwih Rice Terrace, but it's farther from Ubud—about a 90-minute drive from Ubud Palace. However, it offers a more pristine experience of the terraces' original beauty!

The best time to capture the beauty of the rice terraces is at dawn, just as the sun rises. At this moment, golden sunlight spills over the terraces, casting layered shadows and creating a mesmerizing play of light and shadow. This is the favorite time for photography enthusiasts and travelers, as the terraces seem immersed in a golden dream.

### Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Sacred Tree Scenic Area (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

The Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Sacred Tree Scenic Area is located in Buleleng Regency, northern Bali, and is famous for its magnificent thousand-year-old sacred tree. This giant white bark tree (Kayu Putih, meaning 'white wood' in Indonesian) is estimated to be over a thousand years old, with a trunk so thick it takes dozens of people to encircle it, and a towering canopy covering a vast area. Locals regard this ancient tree as sacred, believing it possesses spiritual power, and devotees often come here to pray and hold traditional ceremonies.

This sacred tree is not only a natural wonder but also a symbol of the harmonious coexistence of Balinese culture and nature. A small shrine beneath the tree is adorned with traditional black-and-white checkered cloth (poleng), highlighting its religious significance. Visitors can feel a sacred and serene atmosphere here, appreciating this ancient life that has witnessed centuries of Bali's history and changes. The surrounding natural environment is tranquil, making it a unique spot to experience Bali's spiritual culture and natural beauty.

Even after Japan's occupation ended, these caves and tunnels were sometimes repurposed or renovated by local residents or government agencies. ## [Around Pura Ulun Danu Bratan]

### Pura Ulun Danu Bratan (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_0bcbb190a64dc4d8d05b92348d7fda9c.jpg)

Completed in 1633, Pura Ulun Danau (Ulun Danu Temple) sits on the shores of Lake Bratan in central Bali and is also known as the 'Lake Bratan Water Temple.' Since Lake Bratan is the primary irrigation source for central Bali, this temple once served as a venue for ceremonies honoring Dewi Danu, the goddess of water, lakes, and rivers. Additionally, the 11-tiered Ulun Danu Temple is dedicated to Shiva and Parvati, and a statue of Buddha is also enshrined here, making it a temple that blends Hinduism and Buddhism.

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_bf3c1b5963a5c33d38b37b9e3375ccf4.jpg)

Remarkably, this Balinese water temple is featured on Indonesia's currency—it appears on the second-largest denomination banknote in circulation, the 50,000 Indonesian Rupiah, which is worth about USD$3.

As a volcanic island, Bali is home to one of its most famous hot springs: Toya Devasya. This hot spring water park, built on the shores of Lake Batur, is one of the island's most popular attractions, known for its natural hot springs, water park, and stunning lake views.

### Pura Besakih, the Mother Temple (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

Pura Besakih is one of Bali's largest and most sacred Hindu temples, perched on the slopes of Mount Agung. Revered as the spiritual heart of Bali, it is also known as the 'Mother Temple.' The complex features multiple shrines and altars dedicated to various deities.

This magnificent temple complex is set amidst beautiful valleys and lush rice terraces, offering breathtaking views. Visitors can immerse themselves in the ancient religious atmosphere, exploring intricate temple architecture and a sacred ambiance. Pura Besakih hosts many of Bali's religious and cultural events, such as festivals and ceremonies.

In 1963, Mount Agung erupted, killing 1,700 people, yet lava flowed just meters away from the temple, sparing Pura Besakih. This is regarded by Balinese as a miracle—a sign that the gods displayed their power without destroying faith. The temple holds profound religious significance and cultural value, drawing visitors from around the world. Guests must adhere to dress codes and respect local religious customs to honor this sacred site.
