Its landscape is distinctly different from other parts of the island, renowned for its unique blend of religion, art, and natural beauty. The cultural atmosphere of this hill town is intoxicating, and Ubud is a prime spot for capturing Instagram-worthy shots. Ancient temples, stunning rice terraces, and ubiquitous Balinese architecture all showcase captivating visual charm. Every corner of Ubud feels like a unique work of art, letting you feel the city's profound soul and rich history.

### Ubud Water Palace (👉🏼 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

Ubud Water Palace (Pura Taman Saraswati) is located just a three-minute walk from Ubud Royal Palace, tucked away behind a Starbucks, often overlooked by tourists yet boasting breathtaking beauty. Compared to the Ubud Royal Palace, this temple is more serene and mysterious, offering an excellent spot for photography to capture unique and exquisite moments.

Ubud Water Palace is renowned for its distinctive water features, situated at the end of a lotus pond walkway. The architecture of Ubud Water Palace is also rich in Bali's unique artistic style, with mysterious stone carvings and structures reflecting the deep cultural heritage of the area. Strolling here feels like traveling through time, immersed in ancient myths and religious beliefs.

### Monkey Forest (👉🏼 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

Ubud Monkey Forest is a treasure trove where nature and culture blend seamlessly. Step into this lush sea of trees, and monkeys frolic around you as if you've become part of the natural world. You can interact with these clever little creatures and experience the wonder of coexisting with nature.

However, because the monkeys here are quite mischievous, visitors should stay alert when entering the Monkey Forest. These curious little animals may climb all over you or even snatch your glasses or other belongings. Therefore, it's wise to interact cautiously with the monkeys, avoid carrying food or obvious items, and steer clear of attracting their attention.

### Folk Pool & Gardens: A Serene Pool Club Hidden in Bustling Ubud

![Folk Pool & Gardens 隱藏在烏布鬧區中的幽靜Pool Club](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_2cc396ec6670466a36f49416c315f046.jpg)

In the heart of Ubud's lively center, amidst countless shops, unique artisanal finds, delicious and healthy international cuisine, and rejuvenating yoga and spa experiences, there lies a secluded paradise: Folk Pool & Garden.

This isn't your typical restaurant where people just eat and leave. It's different—spacious and unique. You can spend an entire day here, and it's a fantastic spot for photos!

It's the perfect final stop after a day of wandering and shopping through Ubud's center. Come here to relax, unwind, and snap some beautiful pictures. At night, they even screen open-air movies!

To swim at Folk Pool & Gardens, a single sun lounger costs IDR 150,000 (about USD$5), and they provide a towel so you can swim and take photos. To rent a bale (pavilion) that seats up to four people, it's IDR 350,000 (about USD$11), and it's best to [book in advance](https://www.folkubud.com/).

### World Heritage Rice Terrace Views (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![世界遺產梯田景觀（地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_b32736a5ef98517bd7b4ebd87af9506f.jpg)

The terraced landscapes around Ubud are a highlight of Bali, offering a breathtaking vista that feels like a painting and has earned UNESCO World Heritage status. This terraced area not only showcases nature's wonders but also highlights the harmonious coexistence of nature and humanity, making it an unmissable travel destination in Bali.

Tegalalang Rice Terrace is one of the most accessible and popular terraced areas near Ubud. Its unique layout features winding paths that weave through the terraces like a green canvas, vividly displaying the vitality of nature. Here, the rice paddies ripple in the wind, creating a moving agricultural poem that blends natural beauty with farming culture.

Another less commercialized terraced area is Jatiluwih Rice Terrace, though it's farther from Ubud—about a 90-minute drive from the Ubud Palace. However, it offers a more authentic experience of the pristine beauty of the terraces.

The best time to capture the beauty of the terraces is at dawn, just as the sun rises. At this moment, golden sunlight spills over the terraces, casting rich layers of shadow and creating a mesmerizing play of light. This is a favorite time for photographers and travelers, as the terraces seem immersed in a golden dream.

The Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Ancient Tree Scenic Area, located in Buleleng Regency in northern Bali, is famous for its spectacular thousand-year-old sacred tree. This massive Kayu Putih tree (meaning 'white wood' in Indonesian) is estimated to be over a thousand years old, with a trunk so thick it takes dozens of people to encircle it, and a towering canopy that covers a vast area. Local residents regard this ancient tree as sacred, believing it possesses spiritual power, and devotees often come here to pray and hold traditional ceremonies.

This ancient tree is not only a natural wonder but also a symbol of the harmonious coexistence of Balinese culture and nature. A small shrine sits beneath the tree, adorned with traditional black-and-white checkered cloth (poleng), highlighting its religious significance. Visitors can feel a sacred and serene atmosphere here, admiring this ancient life that has witnessed centuries of Bali's history. The surrounding natural environment is tranquil, making it a unique spot to experience Bali's spiritual culture and natural beauty.

During World War II, Japanese forces carried out construction projects on the island, including building bunkers, caves, and tunnels for defensive purposes. Even after the Japanese occupation ended, these caves and tunnels were sometimes repurposed or renovated by local residents or government agencies. Some were transformed into tourist attractions, while others were converted into irrigation channels. These caves bear witness to a bygone historical era, and today, they have become part of Bali's history and culture.

## [Around Pura Ulun Danu Bratan]

### Pura Ulun Danu Bratan (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_0bcbb190a64dc4d8d05b92348d7fda9c.jpg)

Completed in 1633, Pura Ulun Danau (Ulun Danu Temple) sits on the shores of Lake Bratan in central Bali and is also known as the 'Lake Bratan Water Temple.' Since Lake Bratan is the main irrigation source for central Bali, this temple once served as a venue for ceremonies honoring Dewi Danu, the goddess of water, lakes, and rivers. Additionally, the 11-tiered Pura Ulun Danau is dedicated to Shiva and Parvati, and a statue of Buddha is also enshrined here, making it a temple that blends Hinduism and Buddhism.

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_bf3c1b5963a5c33d38b37b9e3375ccf4.jpg)

What's special is that this Balinese water temple is featured on Indonesia's currency—it's the second-largest denomination in circulation, with 50,000 Indonesian Rupiah worth about USD$2.

### Toya Devasya Hot Spring Water Park (👉🏼 Map: [Top Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

As a volcanic island, Bali is home to one of its most famous hot springs: Toya Devasya. Built on the shores of Lake Batur, this hot spring water park is one of the island's most popular attractions, renowned for its natural hot springs, water park, and stunning lake views.

### Pura Besakih (Besakih Mother Temple) (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

Pura Besakih is one of Bali's largest and holiest Hindu temples, perched on the slopes of Mount Agung. Revered as the island's spiritual heart, it is also known as the 'Mother Temple.' The complex features multiple shrines and altars dedicated to various deities.

This magnificent temple complex sits amidst lush valleys and verdant rice terraces, offering breathtaking scenery. Visitors can soak in the ancient religious atmosphere, explore the intricate temple architecture, and feel the sacred ambiance. Pura Besakih hosts many of Bali's religious and cultural events, such as festivals and ceremonies.

In 1963, Mount Agung erupted, killing 1,700 people, yet lava flowed just meters away from the temple, sparing Pura Besakih. Balinese consider this a miracle, a sign that the gods displayed their power without destroying faith. The temple holds profound religious significance and cultural value, drawing visitors from around the world. Guests must adhere to dress codes and respect local religious customs to honor this sacred site.
