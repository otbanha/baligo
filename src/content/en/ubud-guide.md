Ancient temples, stunning rice terraces, and Balinese architecture at every turn create an irresistible visual charm. Every corner of Ubud feels like a unique work of art, letting you feel the city's profound soul and rich history.

### Ubud Water Palace (👉🏼 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

The Ubud Water Palace (Pura Taman Saraswati), located just a three-minute walk from the Ubud Royal Palace, is a hidden gem tucked behind a Starbucks, often overlooked by tourists yet boasting breathtaking beauty. Compared to the Ubud Royal Palace, this temple is more serene and mysterious, offering an excellent spot for photography where you can capture unique and exquisite moments.

The Ubud Water Palace is renowned for its distinctive water features, situated at the end of a lotus pond walkway. Its architecture is steeped in Bali's unique artistic style, with mysterious stone carvings and structures reflecting the region's deep cultural roots. Strolling here feels like traveling through time, immersed in the influence of ancient myths and religious beliefs.

### Monkey Forest (👉🏼 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

Ubud Monkey Forest is a treasure where nature and culture blend seamlessly. Step into this lush sea of trees, and monkeys frolic around you, making you feel like part of the natural world. You can interact with these clever little creatures, experiencing a wonderful connection with nature.

However, because the monkeys here are quite mischievous, visitors should stay alert when entering the Monkey Forest. These curious little animals may climb all over you or even snatch your glasses or other belongings. Therefore, it's wise to interact cautiously with the monkeys, avoid bringing food or obvious items, and steer clear of attracting their attention.

### World Heritage Rice Terrace Landscape (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![世界遺產梯田景觀（地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_b32736a5ef98517bd7b4ebd87af9506f.jpg)

The rice terraces around Ubud are a highlight of Bali, offering a stunning landscape like a painting, and they've earned UNESCO World Heritage status. This terraced area not only showcases nature's wonders but also highlights the beautiful harmony between nature and humans, making it one of Bali's must-visit destinations.

Tegalalang Rice Terrace is one of the easiest and most popular rice terrace areas to reach from Ubud. The layout here is unique, with winding paths through the terraces weaving a green canvas that vividly displays nature's vitality. The rice waves roll across the land, creating a moving agricultural poem that blends natural beauty with farming culture.

Another less commercialized rice terrace area is Jatiluwih Rice Terrace, but it's farther from Ubud—about a 90-minute drive from Ubud Palace as a starting point—yet it offers a more authentic experience of the terraces' raw beauty!

The best time to capture the beauty of the rice terraces is at dawn, just as the sun rises. At this moment, golden sunlight spills over the terraces, creating rich layers of shadow and delivering a mesmerizing play of light. It's a favorite time for photographers and visitors, as the terraces seem immersed in a golden dream.

Beyond the well-known Pura Tirta Empul, Ubud hides two other temples in quiet corners, all within a short distance of each other, waiting for explorers to discover. These two holy spring temples not only let you experience Bali's unique religious culture but also offer a sincere and peaceful bathing experience in a relatively serene setting.

**Dalem Pingit Holy Spring** is a relatively secluded temple. Not only does it feature unique architecture, but its sacred spring is also deeply revered by local devotees. **Pura Gunung Kawi Holy Spring** is a temple renowned for its rock carvings. Beyond the ancient statues, there is also a hidden holy spring where devotees and visitors can bathe to receive blessings. This peaceful temple area is steeped in mystery, making you feel as if you've traveled back in time to experience Bali's ancient sacredness.

These two different holy spring temples each have their unique charm and cultural depth, while also offering relatively quiet bathing environments, allowing you to find peace and purification in Bali's sacred atmosphere.

### Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Sacred Tree Scenic Area (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

The Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Sacred Tree Scenic Area is located in Buleleng Regency, northern Bali, and is renowned for its spectacular thousand-year-old sacred tree. This massive Kayu Putih tree (meaning 'white wood' in Indonesian) is estimated to be over a thousand years old, with a trunk so thick it takes dozens of people to encircle it, and a towering canopy that covers a vast area. Local residents regard this ancient tree as a sacred entity, believing it possesses spiritual power, and devotees often come here to pray and hold traditional ceremonies.

This sacred tree is not only a natural wonder but also a symbol of the harmonious coexistence of Balinese culture and nature. A small shrine at its base, adorned with traditional black-and-white checkered cloth (poleng), highlights its religious significance. Visitors can feel a sacred and tranquil atmosphere here, appreciating this ancient life that has witnessed centuries of Bali's history. Surrounded by serene natural scenery, it's a unique spot to experience Bali's spiritual culture and natural beauty.

By the way, many of these caves carved out in the Ubud area date back to Japan's brief occupation of Bali. During World War II, Japanese forces undertook construction projects on the island, building bunkers, caves, and tunnels for defensive purposes. These caves and tunnels were often used for military operations.

Even after Japanese rule ended, these caves and tunnels were sometimes repurposed or renovated by local residents or government agencies. Some were turned into tourist attractions, others into irrigation channels. These caves bear witness to a bygone historical era, and today, they form part of Bali's history and culture.

## Around Pura Ulun Danu

### Pura Ulun Danu Bratan (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_0bcbb190a64dc4d8d05b92348d7fda9c.jpg)

Pura Ulun Danu Bratan, completed in 1633, sits on the shores of Lake Bratan in central Bali, and is also known as the "Lake Bratan Water Temple." Since Lake Bratan is the main irrigation source for central Bali, Pura Ulun Danu Bratan was once used as a venue for ceremonies honoring Dewi Danu, the goddess of water, lakes, and rivers. Additionally, the 11-tiered Pura Ulun Danu Bratan is a temple dedicated to Shiva and Parvati, and a statue of Buddha is also enshrined here, making it a fusion of Hindu and Buddhist traditions.

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_bf3c1b5963a5c33d38b37b9e3375ccf4.jpg)

Interestingly, this water temple in Bali is even featured on Indonesian banknotes. It appears on the second-largest denomination currently in circulation—50,000 Indonesian rupiah, which is worth about USD$3.

### Toya Devasya Hot Spring Water Park (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![Toya Devasya溫泉水上樂園 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_d938aab64c88210c3c4550474c7836f5.jpg)

As a volcanic island, Bali is home to one of its most famous hot springs: Toya Devasya. This hot spring water park, built on the shores of Lake Batur, is one of the island's most popular attractions. It is renowned for its natural hot springs, water park, and stunning lake views.

Spanning 2.5 hectares, the hot spring water park features seven pools at different temperatures. ### Pura Besakih (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

Pura Besakih is one of Bali's largest and most sacred Hindu temples, perched on the slopes of Mount Agung. Revered as the island's spiritual heart, it is often called the 'Mother Temple.' The complex features numerous shrines and altars dedicated to various deities.

This magnificent temple complex is set amidst stunning valleys and lush green rice terraces, offering breathtaking views. Visitors can soak in the ancient religious atmosphere, exploring intricate temple architecture and a sacred ambiance. Pura Besakih hosts many of Bali's religious and cultural events, such as festivals and ceremonies.

In 1963, Mount Agung erupted, killing 1,700 people, yet the lava flow passed just meters from the temple, sparing Pura Besakih. Balinese consider this a miracle, seeing it as a divine sign of power without destroying faith. The temple holds profound religious significance and cultural value, drawing visitors from around the world. Guests must adhere to dress codes and respect local religious customs to honor this sacred site.
