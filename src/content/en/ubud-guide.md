Ancient temples, stunning rice terraces, and Balinese architecture at every turn showcase an irresistible visual charm. Every corner of Ubud feels like a unique work of art, letting you experience the city's profound soul and rich history.

### Ubud Water Palace (👉🏼 Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

Ubud Water Palace (Pura Taman Saraswati) is located just a three-minute walk from Ubud Palace, tucked away in a corner behind Starbucks, often overlooked by tourists yet boasting breathtaking beauty. Compared to Ubud Palace, this temple is more serene and mysterious, offering an excellent spot for photography where you can capture unique and exquisite moments.

Ubud Water Palace is renowned for its distinctive water features, situated at the end of a lotus pond walkway. The architecture of Ubud Water Palace is also rich in Bali's unique artistic style, with mysterious stone carvings and structures reflecting the deep cultural heritage of the region. Strolling here feels like traveling through time, immersed in ancient myths and religious influences.

### Monkey Forest (📍Map: [Ubud Center](/map/ubud/))

Ubud Monkey Forest is a treasure trove where nature and culture blend seamlessly. Step into this lush sea of trees, and monkeys frolic around you, as if you've become part of the natural world. You can interact with these clever little creatures and experience the wonder of coexisting with nature.

However, since the monkeys here are quite mischievous, visitors should stay alert when entering the Monkey Forest. These curious little animals may climb all over you, or even snatch your glasses, belongings, and other items. So, it's wise to interact cautiously, avoid carrying food or obvious items, and not attract their attention.

### World Heritage Rice Terrace Views (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Spots Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![世界遺產梯田景觀（地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_b32736a5ef98517bd7b4ebd87af9506f.jpg)

The rice terraces around Ubud are a highlight of Bali, offering a breathtaking landscape like a painting, and they've earned a UNESCO World Heritage designation. This terraced area not only showcases nature's wonders but also highlights the beautiful harmony between nature and humans, making it an unmissable destination in Bali.

Tegalalang Rice Terrace is one of the easiest and most popular terraced areas to reach from Ubud. The layout here is unique, with winding paths between the terraces weaving a green canvas that vividly displays nature's vitality. On this land, rice waves roll, creating a moving agricultural poem that blends natural beauty with farming culture.

Another less commercialized terrace area is Jatiluwih Rice Terrace, but it's farther from Ubud—about a 90-minute drive from the Ubud Palace as a starting point—yet it offers a more pristine experience of the terraced fields' beauty!

The best time to capture the beauty of the rice terraces is at dawn, just as the sun rises. At this moment, golden sunlight spills over the terraces, outlining rich layers of shadow and creating intoxicating light effects. This is the favorite time for photography enthusiasts and travelers, as the terraces seem immersed in a golden dream.

### Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Ancient Tree Scenic Area (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

Located in Buleleng Regency in northern Bali, the Wisata Kayu Putih Bayan Ancient Tree Scenic Area is famous for its magnificent thousand-year-old tree. This giant white bark tree (Kayu Putih, meaning 'white wood' in Indonesian) is estimated to be over a millennium old, with a trunk so thick it takes dozens of people to encircle it, and a towering canopy that covers a vast area. Local residents regard this ancient tree as sacred, believing it possesses spiritual power, and devotees often come here to pray and hold traditional ceremonies.

This ancient tree is not only a natural wonder but also a symbol of the harmonious coexistence of Balinese culture and nature. A small shrine beneath the tree is adorned with traditional black-and-white checkered cloth (poleng), highlighting its religious significance. Visitors can feel a sacred and serene atmosphere here, appreciating this ancient life that has witnessed centuries of change in Bali. The surrounding natural environment is tranquil, making it a unique spot to experience Bali's spiritual culture and natural beauty.

During World War II, Japanese forces carried out construction projects on the island, including building bunkers, caves, and tunnels for defensive purposes. Even after Japanese rule ended, these caves and tunnels were sometimes repurposed or renovated by local residents or government agencies. ### Pura Ulun Danau Bratan (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_0bcbb190a64dc4d8d05b92348d7fda9c.jpg)

Pura Ulun Danau Bratan, completed in 1633, sits on the shores of Lake Bratan in central Bali, and is also known as the "Lake Bratan Temple." Since Lake Bratan is the main irrigation source for central Bali, this temple was once used for ceremonies honoring Dewi Danu, the goddess of water, lakes, and rivers. Additionally, the 11-tiered Pura Ulun Danau Bratan is dedicated to Shiva and Parvati, and a statue of Buddha is also enshrined here, making it a temple that blends Hinduism and Buddhism.

![峇里島水神廟 （地圖：烏布周邊熱門景點）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_bf3c1b5963a5c33d38b37b9e3375ccf4.jpg)

Interestingly, this water temple in Bali is even featured on Indonesian banknotes. It appears on the second-largest denomination currently in circulation: 50,000 Indonesian rupiah, which is worth about USD$3.

### Pura Besakih, the Mother Temple (👉🏼 Map: [Popular Attractions Around Ubud](/map/ubud/))

Pura Besakih, the Mother Temple, is one of Bali's largest and most sacred Hindu temples, perched on the slopes of Mount Agung. Considered the spiritual heart of Bali, it is revered as the 'Mother Temple.' The complex features numerous shrines and altars dedicated to various deities.

This magnificent temple complex is set amidst stunning valleys and lush green rice terraces, offering breathtaking views. Visitors can soak in the ancient religious atmosphere, exploring the intricate temple architecture and sacred ambiance. Pura Besakih hosts many of Bali's religious and cultural events, such as festivals and ceremonies.

In 1963, Mount Agung erupted, killing 1,700 people, yet the lava flow miraculously passed just meters from the temple, sparing Pura Besakih. This was seen by Balinese as a divine sign—a display of power without destroying faith. The temple holds profound religious significance and cultural value, drawing visitors from around the world. Guests must adhere to dress codes and respect local religious customs to honor this sacred site.
