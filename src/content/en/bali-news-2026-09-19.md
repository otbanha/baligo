# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup | September 19, 2026

## 1. ✈️ Super Air Jet Resumes Bandung–Bali Direct Flights, 4 Weekly Flights Starting September 18

The top aviation news for travelers today is that **Super Air Jet has relaunched the route between Bandung's Husein Sastranegara Airport (BDO) and Bali's Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS)**.

Operating from **September 18, 2026**, this route runs every Monday, Wednesday, Friday, and Sunday. Flight IU-170 departs Bandung at **09:10, arriving in Bali at 11:55**; the return flight IU-171 leaves Bali at **17:20, touching down in Bandung at 18:10**. The aircraft is an Airbus A320-200 with 180 economy seats.

This route also holds significance for travelers from Taiwan, Hong Kong, and beyond, as Bandung is a popular Indonesian city in its own right. After arriving in Bali, passengers can continue onward to destinations like Lombok, Labuan Bajo, Makassar, and Kupang. In other words, **Bali isn't just a final stop—it can also serve as a transit hub for exploring eastern Indonesia**.

## 2. 🚨 13 Foreign Nationals Detained in Bali on Suspicion of Illegal Prostitution

Bali's Immigration Office and local police conducted a joint foreigner monitoring operation on the evening of the 17th, detaining 13 foreign nationals at three locations in **Umalas, Seminyak, and Canggu**.

Among them, 12 are women, and one man is being investigated for possibly providing related services. Those detained come from countries including Nigeria, Russia, Kazakhstan, and Ukraine, and are currently all held at the Ngurah Rai Immigration Detention Center pending further investigation.

Travelers should note that some of those involved hold **Kitas Remote Worker, Kitas Investor, or D12 Pre-Investment Visas**, while others hold general visitor residence permits or Visa on Arrival (VoA). The immigration office stated that these individuals may be implicated under Article 75 of Indonesia's Immigration Law and other criminal offenses.

For ordinary tourists, this news does not mean that Seminyak, Canggu, or Umalas are off-limits. Instead, it serves as a reminder: **when traveling or staying long-term in Bali, avoid any involvement in underground services or illegal activities that may violate Indonesian law.**

## 3. 🛂 Ngurah Rai Immigration Office Expands Jurisdiction to Cover All of Badung Regency

There's also a significant change in Bali's immigration management today.

The Ngurah Rai Immigration Office's scope of work has expanded to cover **the entire Badung Regency**, including **North Badung areas** that were previously outside its full jurisdiction. In other words, the Ngurah Rai Immigration Office now oversees all of Badung, as well as related areas like Denpasar.

Bali's immigration office stated that with this expanded scope, in addition to providing immigration services, they will also strengthen the monitoring of foreign nationals.

For short-term travelers, this adjustment does not mean a sudden change in visa regulations. However, for foreigners staying long-term in Bali with Kitas or other residence permits, **the expanded immigration management scope is worth noting.**

## 4. 💃 Truna Jaya Traditional Dance Competition at Tirtagangga Becomes a Cultural Highlight for Visitors

If you have plans for East Bali soon, keep an eye on cultural events at Tirtagangga Water Palace (Taman Tirtagangga).

On September 17, the Tirtagangga Festival 2026 will host the **Truna Jaya Traditional Dance Competition**, featuring 11 young dancers—6 male and 5 female.

What makes it special is that the stage is set on the Tirtagangga water pools. Visitors can sit by the pools to watch the performance while spotting koi fish swimming below, creating a uniquely Balinese cultural scene.

Organizers say the event is not just a dance competition but also aims to boost tourism appeal through traditional arts. The closing and award ceremony for the Festival Tirtagangga is scheduled for Saturday, September 19.

If you already plan to visit Tirtagangga, events like this are far more engaging than just snapping photos—you can also catch a glimpse of traditional Balinese dance.

## 5. 🏨 Bali's Hotel Rooms Reach Around 160,000—Is the Tourism Industry Rethinking 'More Is Better'?

This is a piece of Bali tourism industry news worth keeping an eye on over the long term.

NusaBali reported today that as **Bali approaches the 100th anniversary of its tourism development in 2027**, tourism operators and academics have begun discussing the future of Bali's tourism model.

Currently, Bali has approximately **160,000 hotel rooms**, including both operational and under-construction accommodations, with international visitor numbers hovering around **7 million**. This has prompted industry players to question: as accommodation supply continues to grow, can tourist numbers and hotel occupancy rates keep pace?

The discussion also highlighted that future assessments of Bali's tourism should go beyond just 'how many tourists arrive,' and instead consider factors such as **length of stay, spending, travel experiences, and whether visitors are dispersing to more areas**.

This actually reflects a significant shift in Bali's tourism industry in recent years: moving from a sole focus on tourist numbers to exploring how to balance tourism development with the environment, culture, and local communities.
