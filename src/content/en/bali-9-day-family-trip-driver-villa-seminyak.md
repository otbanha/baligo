9 people, 9 large suitcases, and a full 9 days—that was the reality we faced at the start of this Bali family trip. For the last 5 days, I even added some solo time. After the whole journey, I have only one thought: it was absolutely perfect. This article covers the real experiences with private drivers, villas, and Seminyak hotels, along with related guides from this site to help you plan your own itinerary.

## How We Structured the Trip: Family Villas First, Solo Time Later

The trip was split into two parts. The first half was a 9-person family trip with a private driver and two villas: 2 nights in Ubud and 2 nights in Uluwatu. The second half was my 5-day solo getaway, all spent in Seminyak, where I switched between two similarly priced hotels for comparison.

> This 'villas first, hotels later' arrangement aligns with the accommodation strategy from this site: [Bali Private Villa Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-private-villa-recommendations/) suggests staying in a villa first for private space and flexible itineraries, then switching to a hotel for convenient amenities and professional service—striking a balance. For large groups, villas shine even more. If you're wondering how to choose a three-, four-, or five-bedroom family villa, check out [Group Travel Villa Recommendations](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-group-villa-stay/).

> If you're still deciding how to split your trip across areas, this site's [Accommodation Area Comparison](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-accommodation-area-guide/) compares Kuta, Legian, Seminyak, Canggu, and Ubud in terms of transportation, shopping, nightlife, and natural scenery. For first-timers, the [Ultimate Beginner's Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-ultimate-guide-2026/) recommends a combination of the south (Seminyak or Kuta) and Ubud, so you can enjoy both beaches and culture. Our trip followed that foundation, with an extra stop in Uluwatu.

## Choosing a Private Driver: Compared 3 or 4 Options, Finally Went with A-Zhong

Before departure, we seriously compared 3 or 4 private driver options, with the only non-negotiable requirement being a Chinese-speaking driver. In the end, we chose A-Zhong, and looking back, it was absolutely the best decision of the entire trip.

The car was incredibly comfortable, the air conditioning was super cold, his driving was very steady, and his Chinese was fluent. The price was also very reasonable.

Why is hiring a private car so important for family trips? [Gobaligo's guide to private car hire](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-private-car-hire-guide/) clearly outlines the benefits: someone guides you through currency exchange and buying a SIM card, so you don't have to figure it out on your own; the driver knows the roads, making the trip safer and smoother; your luggage is looked after; there's extra help when traveling with kids; and in case of any unexpected situation, the driver is the first person who can assist. We personally experienced almost all of these points with A-Zhong, and I'll share the details with you in the next section.

> If you're still looking for a driver, check out our [list of top-rated private car drivers in Bali](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-private-car-drivers-guide/), compiled from the community. For language concerns, you can also browse the [guide to Chinese-speaking drivers, guides, and private car itineraries](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-chinese-tour-guide/). As for pricing, it's best to ask the driver directly for a quote.

## A-Zhong's Thoughtfulness: From a Bottle of Water to Currency Exchange, Supermarkets, and Family Photos

What touched us most was A-Zhong's thoughtfulness.

**He lent us money to buy water.** At first, he knew we hadn't exchanged any money yet, and it was hot—everyone might be thirsty. He proactively lent us money to buy water, and we paid him back after we got our currency exchanged.

**He took us to a safe currency exchange.** Later, he brought us to a super safe exchange with great rates. [Gobaligo's currency exchange guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-guide/) warns to avoid roadside exchange booths, and also lists [common exchange scams to watch out for](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-money-exchange-scams-2/). Having a driver take you directly to a reliable place really saves a lot of hassle.

**He recommended a better local supermarket.** The supermarket he suggested was even better than what we had researched on our own, and much more fun to browse. If you want to see some options first, check out [Gobaligo's guide to large supermarkets in Bali](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-supermarket-guide-map/). Stocking up the fridge after checking into your villa will make things a lot more convenient.

**He was always ready for last-minute requests.** A-Zhong responded to all our last-minute needs without hesitation. I'll save the story about helping with luggage for the accommodation section.

**He is amazing at taking photos.** His camera skills are practically professional. I truly realized that Balinese people are all born cinematographers. He captured so many beautiful family photos for us on this trip.

### Recommended: BOUTIQUE SEMINYAK FORMERL

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-11/1794201641405-15563.jpg)

Breakfast is a major highlight here—very generous and tasty. I even tried snake fruit for the first time. The staff are incredibly sweet and friendly; when they saw I couldn't open it, they kindly peeled it for me.

The best part is the free golf cart shuttle that takes you straight to the beach or town—super convenient. The ground-floor restaurant also has live music on Wednesdays and Fridays, creating a fantastic atmosphere, and dinner prices are very reasonable, around USD$10 per meal. I highly recommend staying one evening to eat and enjoy the music.

### Disappointment: D'primahotel Petitenget Seminyak 2

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-11/1794201773702-15564.jpg)

I moved here because reviews said the breakfast was good, but it turned out to be a huge letdown—the breakfast just didn't suit my taste. The room was average, no musty smell, but there was construction nearby, so it was a bit noisy.

The most frustrating part was that the bathroom didn't provide conditioner. Bali's water is quite hard, and without conditioner, my hair got unbearably dry and rough. This was the only accommodation I've stayed at that didn't offer conditioner, so a heads-up: remember to bring your own.

## South Seminyak or North Seminyak? I Choose the South, Closer to the Sea

After staying at both, I personally prefer South Seminyak where BOUTIQUE is located. This area is close to the beach, and it's a leisurely stroll to Seminyak Square and the sand.

North Seminyak (around D'primahotel) seems convenient for transport, but the traffic is just too heavy, lacking that relaxed vacation vibe. The difference in atmosphere between the two areas is like comparing 'Taipei Main Station' to 'Taipei 101 Xinyi District.'

## Lessons for the Next Traveler: What This Trip Taught Me

- **Compare driver prices; a Chinese-speaking driver is essential.** We compared 3 or 4 options and finally chose A-Zhong—the best decision of the whole trip.
- **Don't hesitate to ask at the villa.** A casual question about the mango tree led the butler to pick a whole bag of mangoes and even cut them for us.
- **Don't panic if the villa is down a narrow alley.** The big car couldn't fit, so A-Zhong sent a scooter team to carry all 9 large suitcases in.
- **I prefer the southern part of Seminyak.** It's close to the beach, and you can easily walk to Seminyak Square and the sand.
- **Bring your own conditioner.** The water in Bali is quite hard, and the only hotel that didn't provide conditioner during this trip was one I stayed at.

This 9-day Bali trip was absolutely perfect. If you're also planning to visit Bali, I highly recommend hiring driver A-Zhong.
