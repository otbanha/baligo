# Electronic payments are everywhere in Indonesia and Bali! How to pay? A complete guide to QRIS and GoPay e-payments

Still carrying a thick stack of Indonesian rupiah every day when traveling to Bali?

Now in Indonesia, especially Bali, more and more restaurants, cafes, spas, attractions, shops, and even street vendors are adopting electronic payments. Some places will even tell you directly:

**'Cashless Only'**

Meaning: **Only cashless electronic payments are accepted—no cash!**

If it's your first time in Bali, you can easily run into the awkward situation of 'having money but not being able to pay.'

And the most important keyword for electronic payments in Indonesia is:

## 🇮🇩 QRIS

With digital wallets like **GoPay**, traveling in Bali now significantly reduces the need to carry large amounts of cash.

Here's a comprehensive guide for you:
**What is QRIS? How can foreign tourists use it? How do you register for GoPay? And how can travelers from Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Malaysia make payments?**

## 🇮🇩 What is QRIS? Why Are QR Codes Everywhere in Bali?

QRIS stands for **Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard**, which can be simply understood as Indonesia's unified QR Code payment standard.

In Indonesia, you can find QRIS QR codes at restaurants, cafes, convenience stores, spas, small shops, tourist attractions, and even street stalls.

As long as your payment app supports QRIS, you can simply scan the merchant's QR code, enter the amount, and complete the payment.

Bank Indonesia states that QRIS is widely used across all provinces and cities in Indonesia, including micro-enterprises and tourist destinations.

So in Bali, you'll see:

**Scan QRIS / QRIS / QRIS Payment**

Basically, it's telling you:

📱 **"You can pay by scanning a QR Code!"**

***

## 💡 So, How Should Foreign Tourists Actually Use QRIS?

This is where many first-time visitors to Bali get confused.

Because:

**"QRIS is accepted" ≠ "Any country's credit card can directly scan QRIS."**

Indonesia has now introduced **QRIS Cross-Border**, allowing travelers from select partner countries to use their own payment apps to scan QRIS in Indonesia.

Currently, Bank Indonesia has partnered with:

🇹🇭 Thailand
🇲🇾 Malaysia
🇸🇬 Singapore
🇯🇵 Japan
🇰🇷 South Korea
🇨🇳 China

Wait for the market to establish QR cross-border payment cooperation.

In other words, travelers of different nationalities don't use it in exactly the same way.

***

## 🇸🇬 Travelers from Singapore: Can Use QRIS Cross-Border Directly

If you're traveling to Bali from Singapore, using QRIS is relatively convenient.

Currently, Bank Indonesia lists the following participating payment service providers from Singapore:

- OCBC
- UOB
- DBS

Travelers from Singapore can use supported payment apps to scan Indonesian merchants' QRIS codes directly.

When making a payment, enter the amount in Indonesian Rupiah. The app will convert it to your local currency based on the system rate. After confirming, enter your PIN to complete the transaction.

***

## 🇲🇾 Malaysian Travelers: DuitNow QR and QRIS Are Now Interoperable

Travelers from Malaysia will find it very convenient too.

Indonesia's QRIS and Malaysia's **DuitNow QR** have established a cross-border payment connection.

PayNet also explains that when Malaysian travelers are in Indonesia, they can directly use supported mobile banking apps to scan QRIS for payment.

Currently, Bank Indonesia lists related Malaysian payment service providers including CIMB, Hong Leong Bank, Maybank, Public Bank, Touch 'n Go eWallet, BigPay, and others.

So if you're already used to using mobile payments in Malaysia, you don't necessarily need to prepare a lot of cash when traveling to Bali.

***

## 🇹🇼 What about travelers from Taiwan and 🇭🇰 Hong Kong?

This is something you need to pay close attention to.

Currently, Taiwan and Hong Kong are not included in the QRIS Cross-Border cooperation market list announced by Bank Indonesia.

Therefore, **general banking apps or local payment apps from Taiwan and Hong Kong cannot simply scan and pay via QRIS just because they see the QR code.**

However, this does not mean that travelers from Taiwan and Hong Kong cannot use QRIS at all.

The most practical approach at the moment is:

## 📱 GoPay + Visa/Mastercard

GoPay now supports linking Visa or Mastercard credit/debit cards within the GoPay app, allowing you to use that card as the payment source for QRIS transactions.

In other words:

**Travelers from Taiwan and Hong Kong → GoPay → Link Visa/Mastercard → Scan QRIS**

This is a viable option worth exploring.

However, there is one very important limitation:

## ⚠️ GoPay Credit/Debit Card Payments via QRIS Are Not Unlimited

According to GoPay's official guidelines, when using a credit or debit card to pay via QRIS:

- Supports Visa and Mastercard
- Must be done within the **GoPay App**
- The traveler scans the merchant's QRIS code themselves
- Currently, card payments via QRIS must be **under Rp250,000**
- Merchants scanning the QR Code on your phone is not supported for this card payment method
- Some transactions may require entering a CVV or OTP.

So for higher-value expenses like hotels, spas, or restaurants, **do not rely solely on 'GoPay linked to a credit card' as your only payment method.**

It's best to prepare:

💳 Physical credit card
💰 A small amount of cash
📱 GoPay / other digital payments

A combination of all three.

**"Being able to link a card" and "being able to successfully pay" are two different things.**

Before traveling, it's recommended to confirm:

- Whether overseas transactions are enabled
- Whether online transactions are supported
- Whether OTP can be received
- Whether it is a Visa/Mastercard

## ⚠️ Traveling in Bali: Don't Rely Solely on QRIS for All Payments

While QRIS is becoming more widespread, I still wouldn't recommend traveling to Bali without carrying any cash.

The reason is simple:

Some places might have:

❌ No QRIS
❌ QRIS temporarily out of order
❌ Poor internet connection
❌ Cash only
❌ Physical credit card only
❌ QRIS amount exceeding your current payment tool's limit

Cash is still very useful, especially at small vendors, in remote areas, at traditional markets, or during special events.

So the ideal approach is:

### 📱 Digital Payments

Use at restaurants, cafés, spas, shops, and some attractions.

### 💳 Credit Cards

For hotels, department stores, and larger restaurants.

### 💵 Cash

For tips, small vendors, traditional markets, transportation, and when digital payments aren't accepted.
