The service is friendly, the facilities are modern, and the overall experience was great (awesome)! The twin double-bed room also suited our family of four—two adults and two kids.

🏞️ **Why Choose Omma Bali?**

Most waterfall spots require a hike to reach, but Omma Bali stands out for its convenience:

1. **Private Elevator to the Day Club**: Skip the tiring downhill trek—perfect for lazy days and family trips.
2. **Stunning Views**: Enjoy panoramic waterfall vistas right from your seat, soaking in nature's beauty with ease.
3. **Multiple Activities**: Choose to dip in the pool (with minimum spend), admire the falls, or walk down to the base for photos.

https://youtu.be/MmqfkXw211k?si=lRF-nUKK6GXJ-wKm

🍹 **Omma Bali Pricing Info**

- Non-alcoholic drinks and meals are reasonably priced, but alcoholic beverages are on the pricey side 🍸.
- **Minimum spend rule**: The closer your seat is to the pool or waterfall, the higher the minimum spend. If you're just stopping by briefly, there are also regular seats with no minimum spend.

👣 **A Handy Tip for Seeing the Waterfall**

It's just a 2–3 minute walk from Omma Bali to the waterfall, making it super easy. If you'd rather not move, the restaurant's seating offers a full view of the waterfall—its cascading flow is truly refreshing 💦.

This garden restaurant is just about 100 meters from our stay at **Bali Bohemia Huts**, an easy walk away. If you happen to visit the Monkey Forest, you can conveniently reach the restaurant from Gate 3.

🏡 **Restaurant Highlights & Atmosphere**

- **Garden Design:** The restaurant features indoor seating in a traditional stilt house and an open-air section in the back, where you can enjoy views of lush green rice terraces in a serene, relaxing setting.
- **Tradition & Entertainment:** The restaurant occasionally offers traditional Balinese dance performances and cooking classes, but it's best to check with the staff in advance as schedules are not fixed.
- **Avoid the Crowds:** If you're not a fan of the bustling crowds at Ubud's famous Bebek Bengil, this is a much quieter and more peaceful spot to enjoy crispy duck.

🍴 **Must-Try Dish: Signature Crispy Duck**

- **Highlights:** Half a duck marinated with spices and deep-fried to a golden, crispy perfection. The skin crackles with every bite, with just the right amount of fat—not greasy at all. Even the bones are easy to crunch, making it hard to stop eating.
**Duck Texture:** While the meat itself is a bit on the drier side, pairing it with the restaurant's three house-made sauces adds layers of flavor. Each sauce has a different level of spiciness, all fresh and delicious, leaving you licking your fingers for more.
- 🌟 **Other Dining Options**
Although the duck meat itself is a bit on the dry side, pairing it with the restaurant's three house-made sauces adds a richer overall texture. Each sauce has a different level of spiciness, with fresh and delightful flavors that make you want to lick your fingers for more.

🌟 **Other Culinary Options**

While the restaurant is best known for its crispy duck, the other dishes are also impressive:

1. **Lemon Chicken:**
   Tender meat with a tangy lemon sauce, served alongside soft potato balls—flavorful and filling.
2. **Spaghetti Carbonara:**
   Rich, creamy sauce with a strong milky flavor, perfectly cooked pasta—simple yet satisfying.
3. **Nasi Campur:**
   A classic Indonesian dish with a mix of traditional sides; the version here is said to be very authentic and worth trying.

🍹 **Drink Recommendations**

- **Mango Smoothie:** Rich fruit flavor with a smooth texture, perfect for cooling down.
- **Coconut Juice:** Fresh, pure coconut water—refreshing and thirst-quenching.
- **Ice Tea:** Light and palate-cleansing, pairs well with any meal.

💰 **Price Guide**

- **Lemon Chicken**: 60,000 IDR (approx. USD$2)
- **Spaghetti Carbonara**: 60,000 IDR (approx. USD$2)
- **Mango Smooth**: 35,000 IDR (about USD$2)
- **Coconut Juice**: 27,000 IDR (about USD$2)

Laka Leke Restaurant is a place that perfectly blends Bali's traditional garden charm with classic cuisine. Whether it's the spacious and comfortable dining environment, the distinctive design of traditional stilt houses, or the crispy texture of their signature crispy duck, it's well worth a visit.

If you want to avoid the crowds at Ubud's popular restaurants without missing out on classic Balinese flavors, be sure to add this understated yet stunning restaurant to your itinerary!

📍**Address & Transportation Tips:**

Located near Bali Bohemia, just a 100-meter walk from Monkey Forest Gate 3. It's best to visit the Monkey Forest in the morning and then walk over for lunch.

🐒 **Tips for Interacting with Monkeys**

Although the monkeys in the Monkey Forest are relatively gentle, you still need to follow these rules:

1. **Do not stare directly into the monkeys' eyes**
2. **Do not bring plastic bags or plastic bottles** (monkeys will snatch them)
3. **Do not touch or disturb the monkeys**
4. **Feeding the monkeys is prohibited**

Look up to see monkeys leaping among the treetops, while your feet tread on forest paths blanketed with banyan roots, surrounded by sacred Hindu temples. The harmonious coexistence of monkeys and temples brings a deeply healing tranquility.

🏛️ **17th-Century Ancient Temple**

Inside the park, there is a Hindu temple built in the 17th century. Although you cannot enter, the moss-covered walls of the temple are already breathtaking enough.

📸 **The Secret Weapon for Monkey Selfies**

The park staff offer professional monkey feeding and photo-taking services, which require purchasing a photo ticket for 50,000 IDR (about USD$2). They'll help capture fun moments like monkeys jumping on your shoulders or feeding, making it safe and efficient—definitely worth trying.

**⚠️Note: Do not feed the monkeys yourself to avoid risky behavior!**

**Introduction to the Holy Spring Temple: Sacred Waters and Ancient Beliefs**

The temple is named after the sacred springs that surround it, featuring over a dozen spouts, each symbolizing a different prayer intention, such as "cleansing the soul," "warding off misfortune," "healing ailments," and "banishing nightmares." After completing the purification ritual, local devotees enter the temple to pray, demonstrating their devotion to the divine.

**Visitor Information & Fees**

Visitors must purchase tickets to enter Tirta Empul Temple, and the fees are quite affordable:

- **Adult ticket: 50,000 IDR** (approximately USD$2)
- **Child ticket: 25,000 IDR** (approximately USD$1)

The ticket includes borrowing a traditional colored sarong, which is required for entering the temple. However, note that colored sarongs cannot be used for entering the water. If you want to experience the **holy spring purification ritual**, you'll need to pay additional fees:

- Green sarong rental: 10,000 IDR (approximately USD$0.30)
- Locker rental: 15,000 IDR (approximately USD$0.50)

These small fees are not only budget-friendly but also allow you to deeply engage with this unique cultural tradition.

**How to Participate in the Holy Spring Purification Ritual?**

The holy spring purification ritual may seem simple, but it follows a specific sequence and set of rules:

1. **Prepare a green sarong and a locker**, then change clothes before heading to the holy spring pool.
2. Facing the holy spring pool, start from the **left pool** and purify yourself at the first spout in sequence.
3. **Skip the second-to-last and third-to-last spouts** (these are reserved for prayers for the deceased).
4. After finishing the left pool, move to the **right pool**.
5. At the right pool, you only need to pray at the first spout.

At each spout, follow these steps to complete your prayer:

- Press your palms together in front of the spout and make a wish.
- Let the holy water wet your hair, and splash the spring water over your entire body.

The cool spring water instantly washes away the fatigue of travel, leaving you feeling light in body and soul. It's a whole new cultural experience—definitely worth a try!

**Touristy, yet still retaining a genuine warmth**

Although Tirta Empul Temple has become a popular tourist attraction, with Western visitors in green sarongs performing purification rituals in the pool—a rather amusing sight—the temple still preserves its local, down-to-earth hospitality. The staff are warm and friendly, with no expensive add-on fees, offering nothing but kindness and smiles.

⚠️ **Friendly reminder: Respect the local culture**

When experiencing the purification ritual, be sure to maintain a solemn and respectful attitude, as many local devotees are still praying and bathing devoutly. Avoid excessive photography that might disturb others.

**Recommended nearby attraction: Pura Gunung Kawi**

**The Story Behind Civet Coffee**

Civet coffee, known as **Kopi Luwak** in Indonesian and **Civet Coffee** in English, is unique because the civet cat first eats the coffee cherries, which then undergo a digestive enzyme process before the beans are excreted. This is why it's playfully called 'cat poop coffee.'

As one of the main producers of civet coffee, Bali has many tourist farms for visitors to tour and taste. Most farms don't charge an entry fee and offer free samples of coffee and tea, a tour often called a **Coffee/Tea Plantation Tour**. But free comes at a cost—during the visit, staff enthusiastically promote their products, which can feel a bit pressuring.

**Exploring Satria Farm: A Blend of Knowledge and Fun**

Upon arriving at **Satria Agrowisata**, the entrance opens into a lush botanical garden filled with fruit trees, coffee plants, and spices like mangosteen, Arabica coffee, and ginger. This green space gives us a first glimpse into the raw ingredients behind coffee.

Next, the farm staff introduce the process of making civet coffee, explaining how the beans are selected and cleaned from the civet's droppings. Surprisingly, even with just one or two visitors, the staff patiently explain everything with great warmth and enthusiasm.

**A Surprise Tasting of 13 Free Drinks**

After a brief introduction to coffee-making, the staff bring out **13 different drinks** for us to sample for free, including various coffees, teas, and specialty beverages—each with its own unique flavor and benefits. Sipping these under the dense canopy of trees is not only a treat for the palate but also a feast for the eyes, offering a truly relaxing experience.

**Highlight: Bali CatPoo Chino**

On a hillside, a striking sign reads 'Bali CatPoo Chino'—a playful twist on 'cappuccino' (cat=cat, poo=feces). It's quite amusing! We ordered a cup of civet coffee, priced at **50,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$3), to taste the legendary brew firsthand. For those of us who aren't coffee connoisseurs, it tasted much like regular black coffee—still, it was a unique experience.

**The Shop Area and Shopping Tips**

As the tour winds down, you enter the souvenir shop. Shelves are packed with products like coffee powder, spiced teas, and dried fruit, making it hard to choose. Despite reading online reviews that the take-home products don't taste as rich as the fresh samples, the staff's enthusiastic sales pitch won us over. We grabbed a pack of avocado coffee powder for **130,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$8)—consider it a small entry fee for the 'free' tour!

**Special Suggestion: Other Options**

If you feel uncomfortable with a pushy shopping environment, consider visiting **Bali Pulina**. According to online sources, there is an entrance fee of **100,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$6), which includes coffee and tea tasting along with snacks, offering a more relaxed and pleasant atmosphere. It's also said that the scenery there is quite beautiful, providing a different kind of experience.

**A Unique Memory of a Cup of Luwak Coffee**

Taking a break during the trip to savor a cup of luwak coffee—though for us 'coffee novices,' the flavors might be hard to distinguish—witnessing the production process and learning the story behind the beans is still a worthwhile experience. This cup of 'the world's most expensive coffee' offers not just a taste sensation but also a part of Bali's unique culture.

From chasing laughter through the rice fields of Ubud, to getting up close with nature at the zoo, to strolling hand in hand on the beach at sunset—every moment was filled with warmth and happiness. Bali is not just a relaxing vacation paradise; it's a place that brings families closer together. Here, time seems to slow down, allowing us to truly connect with each other and cherish this unique parent-child time. No matter where we go in the future, these images and memories of Bali will forever shine in our hearts. ✨ (**All prices mentioned above are reference prices from August 2022, for your travel planning.**)
