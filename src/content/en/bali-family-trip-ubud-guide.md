The twin double-bed room also suited our family of four, two adults and two kids.

🏞️ **Why Choose Omma Bali?**

Most waterfall spots require a hike to reach, but Omma Bali stands out for its convenience:

1. **Private Elevator to the Day Club**: Skip the tiring downhill trek—perfect for lazy travelers and families with kids.
2. **Stunning Views**: Sit in the restaurant and overlook the waterfall's grandeur, soaking in nature with ease.
3. **Multiple Ways to Enjoy**: Dive into the pool (with minimum spend), admire the falls, or walk down to the base for photos.

https://youtu.be/MmqfkXw211k?si=lRF-nUKK6GXJ-wKm

🍹 **Omma Bali Pricing Info**

- Non-alcoholic drinks and meals are reasonably priced, but alcoholic beverages are on the higher side 🍸.
- **Minimum spend rule**: The closer your seat is to the pool or waterfall, the higher the minimum spend. If you're just stopping by briefly, there are also regular seats with no minimum spend.

👣 **A Quick Tip for Seeing the Waterfall**

It's only a 2-3 minute walk from Omma Bali to the waterfall, making it super easy. If you'd rather not move, the restaurant's seating offers a full view of the waterfall—its cascading flow is truly refreshing 💦.

This garden restaurant is located near our accommodation, **Bali Bohemia Huts**, just about 100 meters away on foot. If you happen to visit the Monkey Forest, you can easily reach the restaurant from Gate 3.

🏡 **Restaurant Features & Atmosphere**

- **Garden design**: The restaurant has indoor seating in a traditional stilt house and an open-air area at the back. The rear seats offer views of lush green rice terraces, creating a tranquil and relaxing vibe.
- **Tradition & entertainment**: The restaurant occasionally hosts traditional Balinese dance performances and cooking classes, but it's best to check with them in advance as schedules are not fixed.
- **Avoiding crowds**: For those who dislike the bustling crowds at Ubud's popular Bebek Bengil, this is a much quieter and more comfortable spot for crispy duck.

🍴 **Recommended Dish: Signature Crispy Duck**

- **Highlights:**
  Half a duck marinated in spices, deep-fried to a golden, crispy skin that crackles with every bite. The fat is perfectly balanced—not greasy at all. Even the bones are so tender you can crunch them easily, making it impossible to stop at just one piece.

- **Duck Meat Texture:**
  While the duck meat itself is a bit on the drier side, pairing it with the restaurant's three house-made sauces adds layers of flavor. Each sauce has a different level of spiciness, with a fresh and delightful taste that will have you licking your fingers for more.

🌟 **Other Food Options**

Though the restaurant is best known for its crispy duck, the other dishes are also impressive:

1. **Lemon Chicken:**
   Tender chicken paired with a tangy lemon sauce and soft potato balls—flavorful and filling.
2. **Spaghetti Carbonara:**
   Rich, creamy sauce with a strong milky flavor, perfectly cooked pasta—simple yet satisfying.
3. **Nasi Campur:**
   A classic Indonesian dish with a mix of traditional sides. Word has it the version here is incredibly authentic and worth a try.

🍹 **Drink Recommendations**

- **Mango Smoothie:** Rich fruit flavor with a smooth texture—perfect for cooling down.
- **Coconut Juice:** Fresh, natural coconut water—refreshing and thirst-quenching.
- **Ice Tea:** Light and palate-cleansing, pairs well with any meal.

💰 **Price Guide**

- **Crispy Duck:** 140,000 IDR (approx. USD$5)
- **Lemon Chicken:** 60,000 IDR (approx. USD$2)
- **Spaghetti Carbonara:** 60,000 IDR (approx. USD$2)
- **Mango Smoothie:** 35,000 IDR (approx. USD$1)
- **Coconut Juice:** 27,000 IDR (approx. USD$1)
- **Ice Tea:** 18,000 IDR (approx. USD$1)

Laka Leke Restaurant is a perfect blend of Bali's traditional garden charm and classic cuisine. Whether it's the spacious and comfortable dining setting, the distinctive traditional stilt-house design, or the signature crispy duck's crunchy texture, this place is worth a visit.

📍**Location & Transportation Tips:**

Located near Bali Bohemia, just 100 meters from Monkey Forest Gate 3. It's recommended to visit the Monkey Forest in the morning, then walk directly to the restaurant for a meal.

❗**Important Notes**: Upon entry, you can pick up a multilingual paper guide map (including Chinese). A full loop of the park takes about 30–60 minutes. Mornings are less crowded with more monkeys out—ideal for an early visit.

🐒 **Tips for Interacting with Monkeys**

Although the monkeys in the forest are relatively gentle, please follow these rules:

1. **Don't stare directly into the monkeys' eyes**
2. **Don't bring plastic bags or plastic bottles** (monkeys may snatch them)
3. **Don't touch or disturb the monkeys**
4. **No feeding the monkeys**

Looking up, you can see monkeys leaping among the treetops; beneath your feet, a forest path blanketed with banyan trees, while sacred Hindu temples surround you. The harmonious coexistence of monkeys and temples brings a serene healing to the soul.

🏛️ **17th-Century Ancient Temple**

Within the park stands a 17th-century Hindu temple. Though you can't enter, the moss-covered walls alone are enough to inspire awe.

📸 **The Secret to Monkey Selfies**

Staff at the park offer professional monkey-feeding photo services, requiring an additional photo ticket for 50,000 IDR (about USD$2). They'll capture fun moments like monkeys jumping onto your shoulders or feeding—safe and efficient, well worth a try.

**⚠️ Note: Do not feed the monkeys on your own to avoid risky behavior!**

**About Pura Tirta Empul: Sacred Springs and Ancient Beliefs**

The temple takes its name from the holy springs surrounding it, featuring over a dozen spouts, each symbolizing a different prayer intention, such as 'cleansing the soul,' 'warding off misfortune,' 'healing ailments,' and 'banishing nightmares.' After completing the purification bathing ritual, local devotees enter the temple to pray, demonstrating their devotion to the divine.

**Visitor Information and Fees**

Visitors need to purchase tickets to enter Tirta Empul Temple, and the fees are quite affordable:

- **Adult Ticket: 50,000 IDR** (approximately USD$3)
- **Child Ticket: 25,000 IDR** (approximately USD$2)

The ticket includes borrowing a traditional colored sarong, which is essential for entering the temple. However, note that the colored sarong cannot be used for entering the water. If you want to experience the **holy spring purification ritual**, you'll need to pay additional fees:

- Green sarong rental fee: 10,000 IDR (approximately USD$1)
- Locker rental fee: 15,000 IDR (approximately USD$1)

These small fees are not only budget-friendly but also allow you to deeply engage with this unique traditional culture.

**How to Participate in the Holy Spring Purification Ritual?**

The holy spring purification ritual may seem simple, but it follows a specific order and rules:

1. **Prepare the green sarong and locker**, change clothes, and head to the holy spring pool.
2. Facing the spring pool, start from the **left pool**, purifying at the first spout in sequence.
3. **Skip the second-to-last and third-to-last spouts** (these are reserved for offerings to the deceased).
4. After completing the left side, move to the **right pool**.
5. At the right pool, you only need to pray at the first spout.

At each spout, follow these steps for prayer:

- Press your palms together and make a wish facing the spout.
- Wet your hair with the holy water and sprinkle the water over your entire body.

The cool spring water instantly washes away the fatigue of the journey, leaving you feeling light in body and soul. It's a brand-new cultural experience absolutely worth trying!

**Touristy yet still retaining a genuine local warmth**

Although Tirta Empul has become a popular tourist attraction, with Western visitors in green sarongs performing purification rituals in the pool—a quite amusing scene—the temple still preserves its authentic local hospitality. The staff are warm and friendly, with no hefty extra charges, making you feel surrounded by genuine kindness and smiles.

⚠️ **Friendly reminder: Respect the local culture**

When taking part in the purification ritual, be sure to maintain a solemn and respectful attitude, as many local devotees are still engaged in sincere prayer and bathing. Avoid excessive photography that might disturb others.

**Recommended nearby attraction: Pura Gunung Kawi**

If time allows, consider visiting **Pura Gunung Kawi**, not far from Tirta Empul. This site features stunning rock carvings and enchanting valley views—a cultural gem worth exploring near Ubud.

Additionally, Tirta Empul is very close to my favorite accommodation on this trip, **Sebatu Sanctuary Eco-Resort**. Staying here makes it easy to plan your itinerary—highly recommended!

**Experience Bali's spiritual cleansing**

Tirta Empul is more than just a photo-worthy spot in Bali; it's a spiritual place where you can dive into local culture. Taking part in the holy spring purification ritual, bathing in the crystal-clear water, not only relaxes your body and mind but also adds a layer of sacredness and meaning to your Bali journey.

**The Story Behind Kopi Luwak**

Kopi Luwak, known as civet coffee in English, gets its name from its distinctive process: the civet cat first eats the coffee cherries, which then undergo digestive enzymes before being excreted as specially processed beans. This is why it's playfully called 'cat poop coffee.'

As one of the main origins of Kopi Luwak, Bali boasts numerous tourist farms where visitors can observe and taste it. Most farms don't charge an entry fee and offer free samples of coffee and tea, a tour commonly referred to as a Coffee/Tea Plantation Tour. But free often comes with a catch—during the visit, staff enthusiastically promote their products, which can feel a bit pushy.

**Exploring Satria Agrowisata: A Blend of Knowledge and Fun**

Upon arriving at **Satria Agrowisata**, the entrance welcomes you with a lush botanical garden filled with fruit trees, coffee plants, and spices like mangosteen, Arabica coffee, and ginger. This area gave us a fascinating introduction to the raw materials behind coffee.

Next, the farm staff guide visitors through the process of making Kopi Luwak, explaining how the coffee beans are selected and cleaned from the civet's droppings. Surprisingly, even with just one or two visitors, the staff patiently explain everything with great enthusiasm and friendliness.

**A Surprise Tasting of 13 Free Beverages**

After a brief introduction to coffee-making, the staff brought out **13 different beverages** for us to sample for free, including various coffees, teas, and specialty drinks, each with a unique flavor and benefit. Sitting under the dense canopy of trees, sipping our drinks was not only a treat for the palate but also a feast for the eyes, allowing us to fully relax.

**Highlight: Bali CatPoo Chino**

On a hillside, a striking sign read the amusing words 'Bali CatPoo Chino'—a key attraction featuring civet coffee! It's a playful pun on 'cappuccino': cat=cat, poo=poop. Quite clever. We ordered a cup of civet coffee, priced at **50,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$2), to taste the legendary brew firsthand. For coffee novices like us, though, it didn't taste much different from regular black coffee—it was more of a unique experience.

**Shopping Area & Tips**

As the tour wound down, we entered the merchandise area. Shelves were packed with products like coffee powder, spiced teas, and dried fruits, making it hard to take it all in. Despite reading online reviews that the products you take home might not be as rich as what you taste on-site, the staff's enthusiastic sales pitch won us over, and we grabbed a pack of avocado coffee powder for **130,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$4). Consider it an 'admission fee' for the free tour!

**Special Recommendation: Other Options**

If you find the pushy shopping atmosphere uncomfortable, consider heading to **Bali Pulina**. According to online sources, it charges an entry fee of **100,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$3), which includes coffee and tea tastings along with snacks, offering a more relaxed and pleasant vibe. Plus, the scenery there is said to be stunning, providing a different kind of experience.

**A Unique Memory of a Cup of Civet Coffee**

Taking a break during the journey to savor a cup of civet coffee—though for us 'coffee outsiders,' the flavors might be hard to distinguish—seeing the process firsthand and learning the story behind the beans made it a worthwhile experience. This 'world's most expensive coffee' offered not just a taste sensation but also a glimpse into Bali's unique culture.
