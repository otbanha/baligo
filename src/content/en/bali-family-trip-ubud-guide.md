The twin-bed room with two double beds perfectly suited our family of four.

🏞️ **Why Choose Omma Bali?**

Most waterfall spots require a hike to reach, but Omma Bali stands out for its convenience:

1. **Private Elevator to the Day Club**: Skip the tiring walk down—perfect for lazy days and family trips.
2. **Stunning Views**: Enjoy the waterfall's grandeur right from your seat, soaking in nature's beauty effortlessly.
3. **Multiple Options**: Dive into the pool (with a minimum spend), admire the falls, or walk down to the base for photos.

https://youtu.be/MmqfkXw211k?si=lRF-nUKK6GXJ-wKm

🍹 **Omma Bali: Pricing Info**

- Non-alcoholic drinks and meals are reasonably priced, but alcoholic beverages are on the pricey side 🍸.
- **Minimum Spend Rule**: The closer your seat is to the pool or waterfall, the higher the minimum spend. If you're just stopping by briefly, there are also regular seats with no minimum spend.

👣 **A Quick Tip for Seeing the Waterfall**

It's just a 2-3 minute walk from Omma Bali to the waterfall—super easy. If you'd rather not move, the restaurant's seating offers a full view of the waterfall, with its cascading waters creating a refreshing and uplifting vibe 💦.

This garden restaurant is near our stay at **Bali Bohemia Huts**, just about 100 meters away on foot. If you happen to visit the Monkey Forest, you can easily reach the restaurant from Gate 3.

🏡 **Restaurant Features & Atmosphere**

- **Garden Design:** The restaurant is divided into an indoor seating area in a traditional stilt house and an open-air seating area at the back. The rear spot offers a better view of the lush green rice terraces, with a serene atmosphere that lets you fully relax.
- **Tradition & Entertainment:** The restaurant occasionally hosts traditional Balinese dance performances and cooking classes, but you'll need to check with them in advance for specific times, as these are currently irregular.
- **Avoid the Crowds:** If you're not a fan of the bustling crowds at Ubud's ultra-popular Bebek Bengil, coming here for crispy duck is a much quieter and more comfortable choice.

🍴 **Must-Try Dish: Signature Crispy Duck**

- **Highlights:**
  Half a duck is pre-marinated with spices, then deep-fried until the skin turns golden and crispy. Each bite gives a satisfying crunch, with just the right amount of fat—not greasy at all. Even the bones can be easily crunched, making it hard to stop at just one piece.
- **Duck Texture:**
  While the duck meat itself is a bit on the dry side, pairing it with the restaurant's three house-made sauces adds layers of flavor. Each sauce has a different level of spiciness, with a fresh and tasty profile that'll have you licking your fingers for more.

🌟 **Other Food Options**

Although the restaurant is best known for its crispy duck, the other dishes are also impressive:

1. **檸檬雞肉（Lemon Chicken）：**
   肉質鮮嫩，佐以酸香檸檬醬，搭配鬆軟馬鈴薯球，既有味道又有飽足感。
2. **奶醬義大利麵（Spaghetti Carbonara）：**
   醬汁濃郁，奶香十足，麵條熟度恰到好處，簡單卻讓人滿意。
3. **印尼雜菜飯（Nasi Campur）：**
   混合多種傳統配料的經典印尼菜色，據說這裡的版本味道非常地道，值得一試。

Laka Leke Restaurant is a place that perfectly blends Bali's traditional garden charm with classic cuisine. Whether it's the spacious and comfortable dining environment, the distinctive design of traditional stilt houses, or the crispy texture of their signature crispy duck, it's well worth a visit.

If you want to avoid the crowds at Ubud's popular restaurants without missing out on classic Balinese flavors, be sure to add this understated yet stunning restaurant to your itinerary!

❗**Notes**: Upon entry, you can pick up a multilingual paper guide map (including Chinese). A full loop of the sanctuary takes about 30-60 minutes. Mornings are less crowded with more monkeys, making it ideal for an early visit.

🐒 **Tips for Getting Along with the Monkeys**

Although the monkeys in the Monkey Forest are relatively gentle, you still need to follow these rules:

1. **Don't stare directly into the monkeys' eyes**
2. **Don't bring plastic bags or plastic bottles** (monkeys will snatch them)
3. **Don't touch or disturb the monkeys**
4. **Feeding the monkeys is prohibited**

Look up and you'll see monkeys leaping through the treetops; beneath your feet lies a forest path carpeted with banyan roots, surrounded by sacred Hindu temples. The harmonious coexistence of monkeys and temples creates a deeply calming atmosphere.

🏛️ **A 17th-Century Ancient Temple**

Within the park stands a 17th-century Hindu temple. While you can't enter, the moss-covered walls alone are enough to inspire awe.

📸 **The Secret to Monkey Selfies**

The park staff offer professional monkey-feeding photo services, which require purchasing a separate photo ticket for 50,000 IDR (about USD$2). Staff will help capture fun moments like monkeys jumping onto your shoulders or feeding them—safe, efficient, and well worth it.

**⚠️ Note: Do not feed the monkeys on your own to avoid risky behavior!**

**About Pura Tirta Empul: Sacred Springs and Ancient Beliefs**

Named after the holy springs that surround it, the temple features over a dozen spouts, each symbolizing a different prayer intention, such as 'cleansing the soul,' 'warding off misfortune,' 'healing ailments,' and 'banishing nightmares.' After completing the purification ritual, local devotees enter the temple to pray, demonstrating their devotion to the gods.

**Visitor Information and Fees**

Visitors need to purchase tickets to enter Pura Tirta Empul, with very affordable prices:

- **Adult ticket: 50,000 IDR** (approximately USD$2)
- **Child ticket: 25,000 IDR** (approximately USD$1)

The ticket includes the use of a traditional colored sarong, which is essential for entering the temple. However, note that the colored sarong cannot be used for entering the water. If you wish to experience the **Holy Spring Purification Ritual**, you will need to pay the following additional fees:

- Green sarong rental: 10,000 IDR (about USD$1)
- Locker rental: 15,000 IDR (about USD$1)

These small fees are not only affordable but also allow you to deeply engage with this unique traditional culture.

**How to Participate in the Holy Spring Purification Ritual?**

The Holy Spring Purification Ritual may seem simple, but it follows a specific order and set of rules:

1. **Prepare your green sarong and locker**, change your clothes, and head to the holy spring pool.
2. Facing the holy spring pool, start from the **left pool**, purifying yourself at the first spout in sequence.
3. **Skip the second-to-last and third-to-last spouts** (these are reserved for offerings to the deceased).
4. After completing the purification at the left pool, move to the **right pool**.
5. At the right pool, you only need to make an offering at the first spout.

At each spout, follow these steps for prayer:

- Press your palms together and make a wish facing the spout.
- Wet your hair with the holy water and sprinkle the water over your entire body.

The cool spring water instantly washes away the fatigue of the journey, leaving you feeling light in both body and mind. It's a brand-new cultural experience that's absolutely worth trying!

**Touristy, yet still retaining a genuine warmth**

Although Tirta Empul has become a popular tourist attraction, with the pool filled with Western visitors wearing green sarongs for the purification ritual—a rather amusing scene—the temple still preserves a local, down-to-earth warmth. The staff are friendly and welcoming, with no exorbitant extra charges, leaving you feeling nothing but goodwill and smiles.

⚠️ **Friendly reminder: Respect the local culture**

When experiencing the purification ritual, be sure to maintain a solemn and respectful attitude, as many local devotees are still engaged in devout prayer and bathing. Avoid excessive photography that might disturb others.

**Recommended nearby attraction: Pura Gunung Kawi**

If time permits, consider visiting **Pura Gunung Kawi**, not far from Tirta Empul. This site features stunning rock carvings and captivating valley views—a cultural gem worth exploring near Ubud.

Additionally, Tirta Empul is very close to my favorite accommodation on this trip, the **Sebatu Sanctuary Eco-Resort**. Staying here makes it easy to plan your itinerary, and I highly recommend it!

**Experience a spiritual cleansing in Bali**

Tirta Empul is more than just a photo-worthy spot in Bali; it's a spiritual place where you can dive deep into local culture. Taking part in the holy spring purification ritual, bathing in the clear waters, not only relaxes your body and mind but also adds a layer of sacredness and meaning to your Bali journey.

**The Story Behind Kopi Luwak**

Kopi Luwak, known as Civet Coffee in English, gets its name from the civet cat that eats coffee cherries. The beans pass through the animal's digestive system, where enzymes work their magic, before being excreted and processed. This quirky origin has earned it the nickname 'cat poop coffee.'

As one of the main producers of Kopi Luwak, Bali boasts numerous tourist farms where visitors can learn about and taste this coffee. Most farms don't charge an entry fee and offer free samples of coffee and tea, a setup often called a **Coffee/Tea Plantation Tour**. But free comes with a catch—during the visit, staff enthusiastically promote their products, which can feel a bit pushy.

**Exploring Satria Agrowisata: A Blend of Knowledge and Fun**

Upon arriving at **Satria Agrowisata**, we were greeted by a lush botanical garden filled with fruit trees, coffee plants, and spices like mangosteen, Arabica coffee, and ginger. This garden gave us a fascinating introduction to the raw ingredients behind coffee.

Next, a farm guide walked us through the process of making Kopi Luwak, explaining how the coffee beans are carefully selected and cleaned from the civet's droppings. Surprisingly, even with just a couple of visitors, the staff took their time to explain everything with warmth and patience.

**A Surprise Tasting of 13 Free Beverages**

After a brief introduction to coffee-making, the staff brought out **13 different beverages** for us to sample for free, including various coffees, teas, and specialty drinks—each with its own unique flavor and benefits. Sitting under the dense canopy of trees, sipping these drinks was not only a treat for the taste buds but also a visual delight, making it a truly relaxing experience.

**One of the Highlights: Bali CatPoo Chino**

On a hillside, a striking sign read the amusing 'Bali CatPoo Chino'—a playful twist on 'cappuccino' (cat=cat, poo=excrement), and a key feature of the civet coffee experience. Intrigued, we ordered a cup of civet coffee, priced at **50,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$2), to taste the legendary brew firsthand. For coffee novices like us, though, it tasted much like regular black coffee—still, it was a unique experience.

**Shopping Area & Tips**

Toward the end of the tour, we entered the merchandise area. Shelves were packed with products like coffee powder, spiced teas, and dried fruits, making it hard to take it all in. Despite reading online reviews that the products tasted less intense at home than on-site, the staff's enthusiastic sales pitch won us over, and we grabbed a pack of avocado coffee powder for **130,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$4). Consider it an 'admission fee' for the free tour!

**Special Recommendation: Other Options**

If you find the pushy shopping environment uncomfortable, consider heading to **Bali Pulina**. According to online sources, it charges an entry fee of **100,000 Indonesian Rupiah** (about USD$3), which includes coffee and tea tastings along with snacks, offering a more relaxed and pleasant atmosphere. Plus, the scenery there is reportedly stunning—a different kind of experience altogether.

**A Unique Memory with a Cup of Civet Coffee**

Taking a moment during the journey to savor a cup of civet coffee—though for us coffee outsiders, the nuances might be hard to discern—was still a worthwhile experience. Witnessing the production process and learning the story behind the beans added depth to the visit.
