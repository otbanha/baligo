After arriving in Bali, the biggest fear isn't getting sunburned—it's the sudden onset of **Bali Belly**. Whether it's from dietary changes, bacterial infection, or a sensitive stomach, the symptoms can really derail your trip. This article compiles a 'self-care checklist you can buy at local pharmacies in Bali,' covering rehydration, anti-diarrheals, fever reducers, and when to see a doctor—so you're prepared and won't panic!

## What Is Bali Belly? (Explaining 'Dirty Water Disease' Clearly)

- **What It Is**: Bali Belly is 'traveler's diarrhea,' often called 'dirty water disease' in Chinese. It's usually caused by consuming food or water contaminated with bacteria, not an official name for a specific illness.
- **When It Strikes**: Typically 1–3 days after arrival, but it can happen any day during your trip.
- **Typical Symptoms**: Watery diarrhea, abdominal cramps, nausea, and sometimes low-grade fever and fatigue.
- **How Long It Lasts**: Most people recover within 1–3 days on their own. As long as you stay hydrated, antibiotics are usually unnecessary.
- **Most Important Step**: Prioritize rehydration (ORS), not stopping diarrhea immediately.
- **When to See a Doctor**: Bloody stool, persistent high fever, severe dehydration (significantly reduced urine output, dizziness when standing), or symptoms lasting more than 3 days without improvement.

## 💊 Medicine List Available at Local Pharmacies in Bali (For Bali Belly)

### 1. Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) – The Core Treatment

![1. 口服補液鹽 (ORS) – 治療核心](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_a81dcdb7ad41f0217c48c227f3d2376a.jpg)

· Function: Prevents and corrects dehydration caused by diarrhea—this is more important than simply stopping the diarrhea.

· Common local brands: Oralit, Hydralyte.

· Key tip: Use as a first choice for diarrhea; always follow the mixing instructions. Note: Sports drinks are not a complete substitute.

### 2. Antidiarrheal (Adsorbent) – For Mild Diarrhea

![2. 止瀉藥 (吸附劑) – 用於輕度腹瀉](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_29f49f8dcdb2f01829087cc9573e464d.jpg)

· Action: Adsorbs bacteria and toxins in the intestines to relieve symptoms, but not a primary treatment.

· Common local brands: Norit, Entrostop.

· Key tip: May affect absorption of other medications; take at least 2 hours apart from other drugs (e.g., antibiotics).

### 3. Antidiarrheal (Antiperistaltic) – For Emergency Use

![3. 止瀉藥 (抗蠕動劑) – 應急使用](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_f236c2f98d598a09ea38642c11f57cad.jpg)

· Action: Strongly controls diarrhea frequency; for short-term emergencies only (e.g., long-distance travel).

· Common local brands: Loperamide (generic name, same ingredient as Imodium), Diapet, Lodia.

· Key tip: Do not use if you have a fever or blood in stool, as it may worsen the infection. Consult a doctor before use in children.

### 4. Pain Relievers / Fever Reducers

![4. 止痛/退燒藥](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_a30fbcb9351a7bd327b8c2e225f94572.jpg)

· Purpose: Relieves abdominal pain and fever.

· Common local brands: Paracetamol (generic name), Panadol, Sanmol, Ibuprofen.

· Key tip: Dengue fever is a risk in the area; for fever, Paracetamol is preferred. Ibuprofen may increase bleeding risk.

### 5. Antispasmodics for Stomach Cramps

![5. 腸胃解痙](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_f1999375de63a55c0537c176c31bd193.jpg)

· Scopma Plu (hyoscine) → Directly relaxes smooth muscles in the stomach and intestines, easing cramps.

### 6. Probiotics (Supportive)

· Purpose: Helps regulate gut flora.

· Common local brands: Interlac, Lacto-B.

· Key tip: This is a supportive measure and cannot replace rehydration or essential medications.

## ⚠️ Important Safety & Medical Alert

**1. Do not take antibiotics on your own**: Bali Belly can be caused by viruses or bacteria; misusing antibiotics is ineffective and harmful. Antibiotics should only be taken after a doctor's diagnosis.

**2. Prioritize hydration**: Whether or not you take medication, you must consistently and frequently replenish with oral rehydration solution or clean bottled water.

**3. Watch for red flags and seek medical help immediately**: If any of the following occur, stop self-treatment and seek medical assistance right away:

· Fever above 38.5°C (101.3°F).

· Blood in diarrhea or vomit.

· Severe abdominal pain.

· Inability to eat or drink, persistent vomiting.

· Signs of dehydration (dry mouth, dark or scant urine, dizziness, extreme weakness).

· Symptoms persist without improvement for more than 48–72 hours.
