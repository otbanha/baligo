## 🌊 High Wave Warning Issued for Bali Waters! Waves Up to 4 Meters—Check Ferry Schedules to Nusa Penida

Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BMKG) issued a marine warning on October 24, forecasting **waves of 2.5 to 4 meters in parts of Bali's surrounding waters from October 24 to 27**.

The warning covers the Badung Strait (Selat Badung), southern Bali waters, the southern Bali Strait, and the southern Lombok Strait. Notably, the Badung Strait is a key route between Sanur and Nusa Penida, where changing sea conditions may affect speedboat and other maritime traffic.

Meteorological authorities warn that strong winds and high waves could increase navigation risks, advising boat operators to check wind speed, wave height, and the latest marine updates before departure.

### What Should Travelers Do?

If you've already booked tickets to Nusa Penida, Lembongan, or other offshore islands, it's wise to reconfirm ferry schedules before heading out.

- **Don't rely on sunny skies alone:** Bright weather doesn't guarantee calm seas.
- **Check boat company announcements:** Ask about delays, cancellations, or schedule changes before departure.
- **Keep your itinerary flexible:** If you need to catch a flight that day, allow plenty of time between your island excursion and your return flight.
- **Follow crew instructions:** If the ferry company cancels trips due to sea conditions, don't push to go out just to stick to your schedule.

This alert doesn't mean all ferries have stopped, but island transportation in the coming days is worth paying extra attention to.

## 🏝️ Kelingking Beach Launches Automated Ticketing! Nusa Penida Tourism Services Get an Upgrade

Kelingking Beach on Nusa Penida unveiled new permanent tourism fee collection facilities on October 23.

Developed in partnership between the Klungkung Regency government and PT Ctrip Internasional Indonesia, this facility replaces the previous tent-based setup with a more permanent service space, adding three multilingual automated ticket machines.

Beyond ticketing and tourist information services, the collaboration also includes pavilion facilities at Kelingking Beach and other attractions, along with water-based sightseeing rescue equipment.

Kelingking Beach is one of Nusa Penida's most iconic spots, famous for its cliffs, turquoise waters, and unique coastal terrain, drawing crowds of visitors each year for photos.

### Have Ticket Prices Gone Up?

The facility launch announcement did not introduce any new ticket prices, so the activation of automated ticket machines should not be interpreted as a price hike.

Travelers should still check the applicable fee regulations at the site and whether additional tourism fees for Nusa Penida are required.

Additionally, while automated ticket machines help improve ticketing and information services, they do not mean that the attraction has fully resolved issues with queues, traffic, or crowd management. Special attention should be paid to safety on the trails and cliff areas of Kelingking Beach.

Admission tickets to Kelingking Beach are cashless; payments can be made by credit card or QRIS.

## 🏨 Hotels and Restaurants in Bali Risk Warnings for Not Using Local Products—Serious Cases May Face License Review

On October 23, Bali Governor Wayan Koster stated that the provincial government will intensify inspections of hotels, restaurants, and related businesses regarding their use of local Balinese products. Violators may receive first, second, and third warnings in sequence, after which their business licenses could be reviewed.

This policy is based on Bali Governor Regulation No. 99 (2018), which covers the promotion and use of local agricultural, fishery, and industrial products.

Local products mentioned by the provincial government include rice, eggs, fruits, vegetables, meat, beverages, and salt. The aim is to leverage the purchasing power of the tourism industry to create more opportunities for local farmers, small and medium enterprises, and food suppliers.

Bali's economy is heavily dependent on tourism, so the government hopes that hotels and restaurants will not only attract tourist spending but also ensure that more tourism revenue stays within the local community.

### Will This Affect Travelers' Accommodation and Dining?

In the short term, this policy primarily targets procurement and operational management for businesses, without announcing new regulations for tourist spending, nor does it require hotels to fully use local products.

For travelers, it may become easier to spot local agricultural products, food, and beverages at some accommodations and restaurants in the future, but the actual situation will still depend on each business's procurement arrangements.

This policy also reflects a new direction for Bali's tourism development: beyond increasing visitor numbers, the government is equally focused on how to ensure local residents and small-scale producers can share in the tourism revenue.

## 🇨🇭 Switzerland and Bali Discuss Deepening Tourism Cooperation! Waste and Wastewater Management Also on the Agenda

Bali Governor Wayan Koster met with Swiss Ambassador to Indonesia Konstantin Obolensky on October 23 to discuss cooperation opportunities in tourism, sustainable development, and investment.

The meeting coincided with the 75th anniversary of diplomatic relations between Indonesia and Switzerland. In addition to tourism exchanges, the discussions covered collaboration in innovative industries, waste management, and wastewater treatment.

The Bali Governor expressed hope that international cooperation could boost local economic development while simultaneously improving tourism and environmental management.

Switzerland, for its part, expressed interest in Bali's sustainable development and related investment opportunities.

### Why This News Matters

Bali has long faced challenges with waste management, wastewater treatment, and tourism development pressures. These issues are not just administrative matters for local government—they are closely tied to beach conditions, ocean quality, and the overall travel experience.

The two sides are still in the discussion phase regarding potential cooperation, with no specific new construction plans, investment amounts, or completion timelines announced yet. Therefore, this meeting should not be interpreted as a sign of comprehensive improvements to related facilities.

However, if wastewater treatment and waste management cooperation can be realized in the future, it would have practical significance for maintaining Bali's long-term tourism quality.

## 🌙 Indonesia Rises to Second in the 2026 Global Muslim Travel Index, Spotlight on Halal-Friendly Tourism

Indonesia ranked second globally in the 2026 Global Muslim Travel Index (GMTI), climbing three spots from fifth place in 2025—its best performance since joining the index. Indonesia's Minister of Tourism, Widiyanti Putri Wardhana, announced the news on October 22, stating that the improved international recognition helps boost the country's appeal across diverse tourism markets.

The global Muslim travel market encompasses travelers who prioritize halal dining, prayer facilities, and services aligned with religious needs. For a destination, beyond the attractions themselves, factors like dining options, accommodation services, and the accessibility of travel information can also influence travelers' decisions.

### What Does This Mean for Bali?

This ranking applies to Indonesia as a whole, not just Bali achieving second place individually. However, as a key international tourism destination in Indonesia, Bali stands to benefit from the overall enhancement of the country's tourism brand and service quality.

For travelers from markets with large Muslim populations, such as Malaysia and Singapore, halal dining and related service information remain important considerations when planning trips.
