When you think of durian, what’s the first thing that comes to mind?

Some adore its rich, creamy sweetness, while others are put off by its distinctive smell. But did you know? Durian isn’t a fruit that only gained popularity in modern times—it was already mentioned in ancient Southeast Asian texts over a thousand years ago, and was even offered as a royal tribute to kings and nobles.

Today, let’s journey into the history of durian and uncover the legend of this so-called 'King of Fruits.'

***

## The Origin of the Name Durian

The name 'durian' comes from the Austronesian word **duri**, meaning 'thorn.'

It’s a fitting name, as the most obvious feature of durian is its hard, spiky shell.

In 1774, Swedish botanist Carl Linnaeus adopted this long-standing local name when classifying the fruit, officially naming it **Durio zibethinus**—the most widely recognized scientific name for durian today.

***

## Ancient Texts from a Thousand Years Ago Already Recorded Durian

Many people assume that durian is a modern cultivated fruit, but that's not the case at all.

As early as the 9th century, the ancient Javanese literary work *Kakawin Ramayana* already mentioned durian, using the term '**dūriyan**' to describe this fruit.

This means durian has a written history spanning over a thousand years, making it a significant part of Southeast Asian culture.

## Durian Likely Originated in Borneo

The academic consensus is that durian's original homeland is likely **Borneo (known as Kalimantan in Indonesia)**.

Borneo is home to some of the world's oldest tropical rainforests and still preserves a vast number of wild durian varieties, making it a key evolutionary center for the fruit.

Later, through human migration and maritime trade, durian gradually spread to what is now Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Brunei, and the Philippines, evolving into hundreds of different varieties.

***

## Once a Precious Fruit Offered to Kings

On the famous Borobudur temple reliefs in Indonesia, carvings of trees bearing what appear to be durian fruits can be seen.

Many historians believe that in ancient Southeast Asia, durian—due to its limited yield, short ripening season, intense aroma, and unique texture—was often regarded as a precious fruit, presented as a gift to kings, royalty, and honored guests.

This has gradually established durian as a symbol of nobility and prestige.

***

## Why is durian called the 'King of Fruits'?

Durian is hailed as the 'King of Fruits,' rich in dietary fiber, vitamin C, B vitamins, potassium, and various antioxidants. It helps promote digestion, maintain immunity, support energy metabolism, and ensure proper nerve and muscle function. However, durian has higher calories, sugar, and potassium content than most fruits—about 145 to 170 calories per 100 grams—so it's best enjoyed in moderation.

People with diabetes, kidney disease, those managing their weight, or those who need to limit potassium intake should avoid eating too much durian at once. Additionally, those with poor digestion may experience bloating or discomfort if they consume large amounts. For a healthy adult, a serving of about 2 to 3 segments (roughly 100 to 150 grams of flesh) is sufficient, allowing you to savor the flavor while staying healthy.

For many, durian is more than just a fruit—it's a culture, a memory, and one of the most iconic culinary symbols of Southeast Asia.

***

## Can you eat durian in Bali?

The answer is yes.

From approximately **November to March of the following year**, durian is in peak season across most of Indonesia. In Bali, you can find fresh durian at traditional markets, roadside fruit stalls, and some supermarkets.

If you want to explore more varieties, consider visiting durian-producing regions like East Java, Sumatra, or Borneo/Kalimantan to experience Indonesia's rich and diverse durian culture.

## Conclusion

From ancient texts and the rainforests of Borneo to royal tributes and today's globally renowned 'King of Fruits,' the durian carries not just a rich aroma but a millennium-spanning history of Southeast Asia.

Next time you visit Indonesia or Bali, set aside any preconceived notions about its smell and taste this story-filled fruit for yourself. You might just discover that its reputation as the 'King of Fruits' in the hearts of many is well-deserved.

## FAQ

### Where is the durian originally from?

It is widely believed that durians originated in Borneo, where many wild durian varieties still thrive today.

### Why is durian called 'Durian'?

The name 'Durian' comes from the Austronesian word **duri**, meaning 'thorn,' describing the spiky shell of the fruit.

### Why is durian called the King of Fruits?

Although there is no clear record of the origin of this title, the durian is widely called the 'King of Fruits' due to its large size, unique flavor, rich nutrition, and significant role in Southeast Asian culture.
