# Key Topics and News Roundup for Bali Travelers

## 🚨 NTT 7.7 Earthquake Death Toll Rises to 47, Search and Rescue Ongoing

According to the latest data from Indonesia's National Disaster Management Authority (BNPB), the death toll from the **M7.7** earthquake that struck East Nusa Tenggara (NTT) on August 15 has risen to **47**. Authorities continue to deploy search and rescue teams to the affected areas, addressing damaged buildings and assisting victims.

It's important to note that **BNPB's casualty figures are still being updated**; an earlier update from detikBali reported 38 deaths, so travelers may see different numbers—this reflects real-time statistical changes during the rescue process, not separate incidents.

BMKG also issued a tsunami warning after the earthquake, affecting areas including the northern coast of Flores.

**Traveler Advisory:** If you have upcoming plans for a 'Bali + Labuan Bajo/Komodo' itinerary, it's advisable to check the latest status of flights, ferries, and local attractions. Bali itself is not the epicenter of this earthquake, but transportation to NTT and some tourism activities may be impacted.

## ✈️ Flights from Bali to NTT Currently Operating Normally, Travelers Heading to Labuan Bajo Need Not Panic

According to the latest report from ANTARA, **Bali–NTT flight routes are still operating normally**. This is crucial information for travelers currently in Bali who are preparing to transit to Labuan Bajo, Flores, or Komodo.

However, as the earthquake situation is still unfolding, normal air traffic doesn't mean all local tourism activities are back to normal. In areas affected by tremors, such as Labuan Bajo, Maumere, and Sikka, travelers should reconfirm accommodations, ferry schedules, and attraction announcements before departure.

**In short: There's no need to cancel your entire Bali trip because of the earthquake. But if your next stop is Flores, be sure to check the latest updates.**

## 🌴 Munduk Rises on Travel Trend Lists, North Bali Emerges as a New Hotspot

Nestled in the mountains of North Bali, **Munduk** has been popping up frequently on travel trends and recommendation lists.

With forests, waterfalls, valleys, coffee plantations, and a relatively serene natural setting, Munduk offers a much more laid-back vibe compared to popular southern areas like Kuta, Seminyak, and Canggu.

This reflects a clear trend in Bali travel in recent years: **visitors are heading to North Bali in search of experiences that feel less touristy.**

If you've already been to Ubud, Canggu, and Uluwatu, consider adding Munduk, Lovina, and Pemuteran to your itinerary next time.

## 🏨 North Bali Accommodation Gains More Attention, Lovina and Munduk Become Alternative Destinations

Hotels and lodgings in North Bali are increasingly catching travelers' eyes, with the area offering diverse attractions such as forests, coastlines, waterfalls, and historic towns.

For travelers to Bali, this means the north is no longer just 'a place you drive through from the south.'

Especially if you want to escape the traffic and crowds of South Bali, **Lovina + Munduk** is actually a great option for a 2–3 day mini getaway.

## 🇮🇩 Tomorrow is Indonesia's Independence Day! Bali's 81st Independence Day Celebrations on August 17

Tomorrow, August 17, is Indonesia's National Day (the **81st Independence Day (HUT RI ke-81)**), and communities across Bali have been holding flag-raising ceremonies, parades, and celebrations in recent days.

Bali Governor Wayan Koster has already officiated the inauguration of 72 provincial Paskibraka (flag-raising team) members, who will carry out the flag-raising ceremony on **August 17 at Niti Mandala Renon in Denpasar**.

For travelers, the key thing to note tomorrow is: **some roads may experience traffic adjustments or congestion due to flag-raising ceremonies, parades, and local celebrations.**

If you're heading to the Denpasar or Renon area tomorrow, it's advisable to allow extra travel time than usual.
