I had always wanted to take my daughter on a trip, just the two of us. This summer, right before she turned 8, we finally made that little wish come true.

We spent 6 days and 5 nights in Bali (June 27–July 2), without cramming in too many 'must-see' spots. Instead, we aimed for a relaxed, unhurried pace, savoring the time exploring the world together as mother and daughter. We played at Waterbom Water Park, went dolphin watching in Lovina, strolled through the Ubud rice terraces, and enjoyed the culturally rich Legong dance—each day brought its own surprises and memories.

Before we left, I also referred to many experiences shared by seasoned travelers in various forums. Now that our trip is over, I want to compile my practical insights into a travelogue, hoping to offer some useful references for those planning a Bali independent trip, especially for families with kids.

***

## Day 1–2｜Stay in Kuta First, No Rush to Hit the Road

After arriving in Bali, we checked into Kuta for two consecutive nights. On the second day, we spent the whole day at Waterbom Water Park.

> [【Bali Must-Do for Families】Complete Guide to Waterbom Bali Water Park: Asia's Best, Slide Ratings & Money-Saving Ticket Tips](/en/blog/waterbom-bali-water-park-guide/)

Considering the fatigue from flying and traveling with a child, I deliberately kept the first two days light. Choosing accommodation near the airport made transportation easy and allowed us to gradually adjust to the travel rhythm without having to endure a long drive right after landing. This made the trip feel relaxed from the very start.

Looking back now, this arrangement was truly perfect for a family trip. The kids had plenty of rest time, and we adults didn't have to scramble from the start—instead, we could enjoy each day even more.

## Day 3｜From Kuta to Lovina by Private Car—The Best Decision We Made

On June 29, we officially began our northward journey, heading from Kuta to Lovina.

Since the drive was quite long, we decided to hire a private car. Through a community recommendation, we found Ani and booked the car for two days, traveling from Lovina in the north all the way down to Ubud in the central region. Looking back now, this was truly one of the best decisions of the entire trip.

Not only did it save us the hassle of transferring between transport, but we could also set our own pace without worrying about time. Traveling with kids, being able to stop and rest whenever needed made things so much easier.

Along the way, Ani shared a lot about Bali's history, religious beliefs, culture, and local stories, turning the journey into more than just visiting sights—it became a way to slowly get to know the island during every drive.

***

## Day 4｜Dolphin Watching in Lovina and Alas Harum—Scenery More Stunning Than Imagined

On June 30, we set out before dawn to watch dolphins at sea.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-07/1783767974106-lovina2.jpeg)

(Photo by Jean Chen)

Luckily, we not only saw pods of dolphins leaping across the surface but also enjoyed a beautiful sunrise over the ocean. As the first rays of light rose from the horizon, painting the sky in golden hues, that moment made every early wake-up call feel completely worth it.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-07/1783768010278-mixcollage-11-jul-2026-06-01-pm-6034.jpg)

(Photo by Jean Chen)

But that day, a little hiccup happened.

While out at sea, I accidentally broke my power bank. With several more days of travel ahead and my phone needed for navigation, photos, and communication, I was honestly a bit panicked.

Unexpectedly, as soon as Ani found out, he immediately helped me look for shops along the way and even took the initiative to communicate with the owners. In the end, I managed to buy a reasonably priced power bank that also met airline regulations. This seemingly small gesture made me truly appreciate his attentiveness and kindness.

After leaving Lovina, we headed south all the way to Ubud.

Along the way, my daughter specifically wanted to visit the Alas Harum rice terraces. To be honest, I didn't have high expectations at first—I just thought, since the kids wanted to go, we might as well check it out.

Unexpectedly, the weather was absolutely perfect that day. Sunlight spilled over the layered terraces, turning the entire valley a lush green that looked like a painting—every snapshot looked like a postcard.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-07/1783768368173-mixcollage-11-jul-2026-05-59-pm-751.jpg)

(Photo by Jean Chen)

I also have to praise Ani's photography skills. He didn't just press the shutter; he really knew how to find the right angles and capture the light, and even guided my daughter on how to pose. Thanks to him, we ended up with many family photos that we'd normally struggle to take on our own.

Also, the Alas Harum estate is bigger than I imagined—it's not just the rice terraces; the entire environment is beautifully maintained. After walking through the whole place, only one thought remained in my mind:

**"I'm so glad I included this place."**

One more tip: the complimentary tea tasting at Alas Harum used to be free, but it's now a paid experience. Ani proactively informed us about the updated pricing before we arrived and offered alternatives without any hard sell—all decisions were ours to make, which felt very relaxed.

## Day 5｜Checking into Ubud, Watching the Most Classic Legong Dance

For our last two nights, we stayed in Ubud, a town brimming with art, culture, and spirituality.

If you're in Ubud, I highly recommend spending one evening at the Ubud Palace to watch the Legong Dance.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-07/1783768573781-mixcollage-11-jul-2026-06-00-pm-2889.jpg)

## The trip is over, but the memories are just beginning

The six-day, five-night journey ended quickly, but what I truly brought home isn't just the countless photos on my phone—it's every moment we shared together.

From chasing dolphins at sea for the first time, playing at Waterbom until we didn't want to go back to the hotel, strolling through lush green rice terraces, watching the Legong dance, to taking care of each other and solving little hiccups along the way—these seemingly ordinary moments are the most precious memories to look back on.

The meaning of travel, perhaps, has never been about how many sights you visit, but about seeing a bigger world together while accompanying each other and leaving behind your own stories.

Finally, I also want to thank every friend in the group who was willing to share their experiences. Thanks to the selfless information you all provided, we avoided many detours and made this mother-daughter trip much smoother.

I hope this travelogue can also offer some practical references for the next traveler heading to Bali. If you're planning a family trip, I wish you the best in creating your own beautiful memories in Bali.
