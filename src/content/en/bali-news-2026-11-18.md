## 🌦️ Bali Weather Outlook for November 18: Mostly Cloudy, with Localized Thunderstorms and Waves

The Indonesian Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BMKG) has released the Bali weather forecast for November 18, predicting mostly cloudy conditions, though some areas may still experience rain and thunderstorms.

According to BMKG data:

- Island-wide temperature range: 20–34°C
- Relative humidity: 65–95%
- Wind direction: East to south
- Wind speed: Approximately 4–30 km/h
- Wave height in northern Bali waters: About 0.5–1 meter
- Southern Bali sea wave height: approximately 0.5–2 meters

**Reminders for Travelers:**

If you're planning to visit mountain areas like Ubud, Kintamani, or Bedugul tomorrow, it's wise to allow flexibility for weather changes. For those heading to Nusa Penida or Nusa Lembongan, check with ferry operators about sea conditions and schedules before departure.

The rainy season doesn't mean it rains all day, but afternoon showers, slippery roads, and changing sea conditions are worth noting.

## 🚨 Two German Tourists Killed in North Bali Motorcycle Accident! Suspected Brake Failure on Downhill Section

On November 15 at around 2:30 PM, two German tourists died in a motorcycle accident in Selat Village, Sukasada District, Buleleng Regency, North Bali.

According to police data, the two deceased are:

- Walter Alois-Martin, 70, from Hamburg, Germany
- Kirchberg Kolberg, 55, from Heiligenstadt, Germany

At the time of the accident, the two were riding a Honda PCX scooter together on the road from Gobleg to Selat.

Police initially stated that the accident occurred on a steep downhill section, where the scooter's braking system appeared to malfunction, causing the driver to lose control and crash into a tree on the left side of the road.

The police are still investigating the cause of the accident, and brake failure remains a preliminary assessment.

This incident once again reminds travelers heading to North Bali, Munduk, Gobleg, and the surrounding mountainous areas that some local roads have steep gradients and numerous curves, a stark contrast to the flat roads in the southern tourist zones.

**Reminder for travelers:**

- If you are unfamiliar with Bali's mountain roads, avoid renting a scooter on your own.
- Never ride at high speeds or rely on brakes for extended periods on downhill sections.
- Check the scooter's brakes, tires, and overall condition before setting off.
- Don't blindly rely on Google Maps for mountain shortcuts; prioritize road safety instead.

Especially in the mountainous areas of North Bali, pay close attention to road conditions and steep gradients.

## 🛂 Bali Immigration Deports 18 Foreigners in a Week! Involves Illegal Activities and Overstays

Bali's Ngurah Rai Immigration Office announced that between November 9 and 13, 2026, a total of 18 foreigners were deported for violations including working outside their residence permits, engaging in illegal activities, and overstaying.

According to data released on November 17 by Ngurah Rai Immigration Office Head Muhammad Novyandri, the cases include:

- 5 Nigerian nationals: Involved in illegal sex trade activities in Bali.
- 1 Australian national: Held an Investor Stay Permit (ITAS Investor) but worked as a visa consultant, which is outside the permit's scope.
- 10 foreigners: Overstayed by more than 60 days, including 6 Russians, 2 Ukrainians, 1 German, and 1 Spanish national.

The immigration office also mentioned two individuals with dual Indonesian and Australian citizenship, whose Indonesian travel documents have been revoked.

The immigration office stated that foreigners residing in Indonesia must not only adhere to their stay limits but also ensure that their actual activities align with the visa and residence permit they hold.

This operation shows that Bali's immigration authorities are intensifying their monitoring of foreigners' residency and work activities.

For regular travelers, the most important thing is to confirm your visa validity, avoid overstaying, and refrain from using a visitor visa for unauthorized work.

## ⚠️ Armenian National Deported for Working at a Beauty Clinic in Canggu on a Visitor Visa

In addition to the deportation of the 18 individuals mentioned above, Bali's immigration office deported a 29-year-old Armenian man in another case on November 16.

The man held a B1 visitor residence permit (Izin Tinggal Kunjungan), originally valid until November 26, 2026, but an investigation revealed he was involved in operations and service work at a beauty clinic in Canggu, North Kuta District, Badung Regency.

Since his actual activities exceeded the scope allowed by his visitor visa, the immigration office imposed administrative immigration measures under Article 75, Paragraph 1 of Indonesia's Immigration Law, deporting him and recommending his inclusion on the blacklist.

Ramdhani, head of Bali's immigration office, emphasized that foreigners must comply with residence permit regulations and cannot use a visitor visa for work or business activities that violate the rules.

This news is particularly noteworthy for foreigners staying long-term in Bali, working remotely, or planning to run a business.

**Reminder:** A B1 visitor visa is not a work permit. Even assisting with store operations, providing services, or engaging in business activities cannot be assumed to be allowed under a visitor visa. Always verify the applicable visa and work permit regulations first.

## 🔐 Bali Police Warn Tourists About Snatch Thefts! Necklaces and Handbags Are Primary Targets

Bali police have recently reminded foreign tourists to stay vigilant, especially in tourist areas like Kuta, Seminyak, Legian, and Sanur, and to be cautious of motorbike snatch thefts and the safety of their belongings.

According to a case released by Denpasar police on November 7, authorities have arrested six individuals suspected of snatching belongings from foreign tourists in Kuta, two of whom are minors.

These incidents occurred between August and October 2026, with victims all being foreign tourists in Bali, including:

- 3 Indian tourists
- 1 Azerbaijani tourist
- 1 Pakistani tourist
- 1 Russian tourist

Police statistics show that the total property loss from these cases amounts to approximately 291.3 million Indonesian Rupiah.

The suspects primarily rode motorcycles, snatching necklaces or handbags from tourists while they were walking, then quickly fleeing the scene. Most incidents happened at night, but police also noted that similar events could occur during the day.

Police are also investigating a motorbike snatching case targeting foreign tourists in Sanur, advising travelers not to accept unsolicited transportation services from strangers.

**Police Safety Tips for Tourists:**

- Avoid wearing conspicuous gold necklaces and expensive jewelry while walking.
- Carry your handbag on the side away from the road.
- Do not keep your phone in a place where it can be easily snatched by passing motorcycles.
- Avoid walking alone on secluded roads at night.
- Do not accept unsolicited rides from strangers; use official transportation services.
- In case of robbery or other crimes, call the Indonesian police emergency number 110 or report to the nearest police station.

Bali remains a popular tourist destination overall, but tourist areas are not without security risks. Be especially vigilant when walking at night or carrying valuables.
