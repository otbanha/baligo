## Monkey Forest

Nestled in the heart of Ubud, the Monkey Forest is a popular attraction that blends wild macaques with Hindu temple culture. After winding through a maze of narrow lanes, you're greeted by a sprawling canopy of lush greenery, where towering trees form a natural shaded corridor lined with tropical vegetation, creating a verdant oasis. As you descend the entrance steps, you'll immediately spot several wild monkeys swinging and playing in the trees.

These monkeys are medium-sized, with sleek black or brown fur, incredibly agile and unpredictable, making them a constant source of fascination. The most delightful sight is watching mothers with their babies—tiny monkeys clinging to their backs or darting out from under their bellies, their playful antics utterly endearing. Occasionally, you'll see a tussle break out among the branches as they scramble for food.

During their walk, they also witnessed the monkeys' cunning firsthand. A black monkey suddenly leaped from a tree, landing precisely on a tourist, snatching a pair of valuable earrings from her ear before vanishing into the foliage. Clearly, these monkeys have a keen 'interest' in shiny jewelry—a reminder for visitors to stay vigilant.

Beyond the charming monkeys, the forest also houses the Pura Dalem Agung Padangtegal temple, featuring intricate hand-carved stone statues and sculptures. Following the path deeper in, you'll find a small pond with a fountain, shaded by an ancient banyan tree, exuding a serene and natural ambiance. Though compact, the Monkey Forest encapsulates Ubud's unique blend of nature and religious culture, making it a stop worth savoring.

## Ubud Traditional & New Market

Leaving the tranquil monkey forest behind, they soon walked to the Ubud Traditional Market. This long-standing market primarily sells fresh local produce and daily essentials. At the entrance, a long corridor is lined with stalls offering souvenirs like clothing and handicrafts. Stepping inside, you'll find rows of food vendors and produce sellers offering fresh ingredients such as fruits, vegetables, seafood, and meats. However, the market's open, aging structure leaves it exposed to the scorching sun and wind, making it feel stuffy at times. To address this, a new market was built nearby, offering a more comfortable shopping experience. This new market is fully enclosed, bright, and well-ventilated, with stalls organized by theme—such as clothing, home decor, and handicrafts. Browsing here feels pleasant and relaxed, adding a layer of comfort.

At the new market, they discovered several practical and charming handicrafts, like woven fruit baskets crafted into intricate patterns, quite unique. They also spotted beautiful surf-themed wood carvings, coconut shell crafts, and batik wall hangings in endless styles, exuding an exotic flair. While prices are slightly higher than the old market, they felt the quality of craftsmanship justified the splurge.

## Serayu Pottery Workshop

After escaping the bustling market, they headed to another famous Ubud attraction, Serayu Pottery, which specializes in pottery-making, ceramic painting, and various DIY hands-on activities in a serene and pleasant setting.

![Serayu Pottery 陶藝教室](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_b41e6268aabe4e4c156ff8807840bee3.jpg)

Upon entering the complex, they were greeted by lush, gnarled trees and vibrant pottery pieces, creating a natural, idyllic atmosphere. Next to the workshop, there's a gallery showcasing exquisite ceramic works painted by students, serving as inspiration. Before the class, staff gave a detailed introduction to the properties of clay and key tips for crafting, emphasizing care from kneading to shaping. Then it was time to get hands-on: they chose a plain clay pot as their canvas, while the instructor demonstrated various decorative techniques like glaze dripping, color blending, and pattern sketching—quite fascinating. During the painting session, the host also shared the rich history and artistic essence of pottery, enriching their experience. After the class, the finished pieces needed to be fired at high temperatures to become permanent works.

The staff will mail the finished pieces back in a few weeks, allowing them to relive this pottery experience time and again.

## Alas Harum Attraction

The highlight of this trip was visiting the themed attraction Alas Harum. This unique amusement park combines various traditional Balinese architectural styles, tropical recreational facilities, and coffee-making experiences. At the entrance, a grand carved wooden gate adorned with intricately detailed shrines and imposing statues greets visitors, resembling the gateway to a royal palace. Passing through the gate, a courtyard of Balinese-style buildings unfolds, exuding a relaxed and leisurely vibe. The park is divided into several themed areas:

1. Experience Zone: This area features various family-friendly interactive facilities, with the standout being the world's largest giant outdoor swing, nearly 9 meters high. Additionally, there are thrilling rides like zip lines and rope courses, offering plenty of excitement.

2. Pool Zone: Comprising a large semi-open swimming area with loungers by the poolside, perfect for cooling off and having fun. Next to the pool, there's a large water bar serving a variety of drinks and tropical fruits.

3. Exhibition Halls & Workshops: One area showcases local coffee craftsmanship and its origins, where visitors can sample authentic civet coffee. Staff explain the entire process from picking and roasting to hand-brewing, making it lively and engaging. Another section features hands-on workshops with artisans in bamboo weaving, wood carving, and painting.

4. Performance Areas: The park features themed performance zones, such as traditional dance and percussion shows, with different schedules daily for guests to enjoy.

Alas Harum seamlessly blends traditional culture with modern entertainment, offering diverse experiences that leave visitors enchanted and reluctant to leave.
