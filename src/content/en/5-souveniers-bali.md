5 Things You'll Regret Not Buying in Bali! These Souvenirs Might Be Much Cheaper Than Back Home

# 5 Things You'll Regret Not Buying in Bali! These Souvenirs Might Be Much Cheaper Than Back Home

When traveling to Bali, beyond soaking up the sun, beaches, resorts, and massages, don't forget to leave some suitcase space for shopping!

Many people's first thoughts for Bali souvenirs might be coffee, handicrafts, or local snacks. But in reality, some items aren't just locally distinctive—they might also be more affordable than buying them in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, or other overseas markets.

From Bali's Kintamani coffee and Asian skincare products to handmade leather goods, silver jewelry, and traditional massage oils, here are five categories worth adding to your shopping list.

But a quick heads-up: **Cheaper doesn't mean every shop is a bargain.** Brand, material, craftsmanship, and location all affect pricing. The suggestions below point to worthwhile comparisons, not guarantees that every item will be cheaper than back home.

## 1. Bali Coffee: Especially Kintamani Arabica

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791593665796-44.png)

If you're a regular coffee drinker, this is an absolute must-add to your shopping list.

Bali is more than just a vacation destination—it's also a coffee-growing region. The most noteworthy variety is **Kintamani Arabica Coffee**, which comes from the highlands surrounding Mount Batur.

Kintamani coffee grows at high altitudes and carries the distinct character of its terroir. Its flavor profile typically features bright acidity, and some cups even reveal citrus-like fruit notes, making it a great choice for those who enjoy a clean, fruity coffee.

Bali is also famous for its civet coffee (Kopi Luwak). However, this type of coffee is usually quite expensive, so it's best to verify its origin, authenticity, and production methods before purchasing—and to ensure the civets haven't been kept in poor conditions.

When buying, choose between whole beans or ground coffee based on your brewing habits. If you have pour-over equipment at home, go for whole beans to preserve aroma by grinding fresh. If you need convenience for the office or travel, pre-ground coffee is a practical option.

**Shopping tip:** If you have a chance to visit Kintamani, consider adding a coffee plantation or roasting experience to your itinerary. If that's not possible, you can find local coffee at cafes, supermarkets, or souvenir shops in town. Prices and quality vary widely between stores, so don't rush to buy just because a shop claims it's 'direct from the source.'

## 2. Asian Skincare & Cosmetics: Sunscreen, Face Masks, and Serums Worth Comparing

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791593935093-47.png)

If you're into skincare, set aside some shopping time during your Bali trip.

Bali has stores selling Korean and Japanese beauty brands, as well as local Indonesian cosmetics and body care products. Common finds include sunscreen, sheet masks, hydrating serums, facial cleansers, and body lotions.

Why is it worth browsing? Because traveling gives you a chance to compare prices, promotions, and product sizes across different channels—especially for items you use regularly. If you come across a reasonably priced bundle, it might actually be cheaper than buying back home.

That said, Korean or Japanese brands aren't necessarily cheaper in Bali. Some imported goods may still carry higher prices due to shipping, distributor fees, and retail costs. So don't assume a 'discount' is a steal—check the prices in your home country first.

If you want to shop with more of a Balinese flair, consider local Indonesian skincare brands, such as those focusing on botanical scents, coconut oil, and body care products. These items are not only great for personal use but also make excellent gifts for friends who love skincare or fragrances.

**Shopping tips:**

- Prioritize products you already use and know suit your skin type.
- Check the packaging, expiration date, seal, and ingredient labels.
- Choose trustworthy stores and verify the BPOM registration information for Indonesian cosmetics.
- Don't just look at the price for sunscreen; check the SPF, UVA protection labels, and whether the product suits your needs.

If you're planning to restock anyway, jot down the prices of your usual items before you go, then compare them in Bali. Buying products you truly need at a reasonable price is more practical than blindly stocking up.

## 3. Handcrafted Leather Goods: Sandals, Wallets, and Bags

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791594382440-48.webp)

If you don't want to bring back a pile of consumable souvenirs, handcrafted leather goods are another option worth considering.

Bali is home to numerous leather craft workshops offering wallets, belts, sandals, handbags, and other leather accessories. Some shops also accept custom orders, allowing customers to choose the leather, color, size, or specific design details.

Compared to international luxury brands, locally made leather goods offer a chance to buy handcrafted designs at more reasonable prices. However, this doesn't mean every item is cheap, nor that all products labeled 'Genuine Leather' are of the same quality.

For first-time buyers, I'd recommend starting with practical small items:

- **Leather sandals:** You can try them on directly to check the fit and comfort.
- **Wallets and card holders:** Compact and easy to store, they also make great souvenirs.
- **Handcrafted leather bags:** If you're looking for a more memorable piece, carefully compare the leather, stitching, hardware, and lining.
- **Custom-made items:** Ideal for those seeking unique designs, but remember to allow time for production.

If you plan to order custom shoes or sandals, it's best to inquire early in your trip rather than waiting until the last day. Custom orders may require measurements, design confirmation, and production time, so always confirm the actual delivery timeline with the shop.

**Shopping tips:** Carefully check for visible cracks in the leather, neat stitching, sturdy hardware, and ask about the materials used. A higher price doesn't always mean better quality—taking time to compare is usually more worthwhile.

## 4. Bali Local Accessories: Celuk Silver Jewelry, Beaded Bracelets, and Handmade Earrings

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791594659310-51.jpg)

If you love accessories that carry travel memories, this category is well worth browsing slowly.

Bali is renowned for its silver craftsmanship, and one of the most notable places to explore is the **Celuk Silver Village** in Gianyar. Known for its gold and silver artistry, you can find rings, earrings, necklaces, bracelets, and other pieces, with some artisans offering custom designs. Certain workshops even allow visitors to observe the silver-making process up close, or try their hand at crafting their own piece to take home.

Compared to mass-produced jewelry, handmade silver pieces shine in their details. Whether it's Balinese-inspired carvings, intricate silver filigree, or minimalist everyday styles, you can choose or create a unique piece that reflects your personal taste.

## 5. Body Massage Oils and Fragrance Products: Bring the Bali SPA Feeling Home

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791596085671-54.jpg)

If you've had a massage in Bali, you're probably familiar with the scents used in local spas.

From frangipani and ylang-ylang to coconut oil, lemongrass, and other botanical notes, there are plenty of products focused on body care, massage oils, fragrances, and home spa experiences.

The advantage of these items is their practicality—they let you extend the feeling of your trip long after you're home.

For example, you can pick a favorite massage oil to use after your shower, or choose soaps, body scrubs, and room fragrances to add a vacation vibe to daily life. If you're looking for gifts, opt for well-packaged, compact products.

If you're looking for local brands, keep an eye out for Bali's body care brands like Sensatia, Utama Spice, or Bali Alus. Their product lines vary in ingredients, scents, and positioning, so it's best to test the fragrance or check the label before choosing what suits you.

It's worth noting that **"natural," "organic," and "made in Bali" are not the same thing.** Some products use coconut oil or plant extracts, while others may rely on mineral oil or synthetic fragrances. Always read the full ingredient list rather than just looking at the botanical illustrations on the packaging.

**Shopping tip:** If you're buying essential oils or massage oils, confirm their intended use and application method. Essential oils aren't always suitable for direct skin contact, and those with sensitive skin should check ingredients and do a patch test first. Also, if you're flying, massage oils, lotions, and other liquid products must be packed according to your airline's carry-on and checked baggage rules.

## 4 More Things to Note Before Shopping in Bali

### 1. Don't blindly trust discounts in tourist areas

Pricing and promotions can vary widely between stores. When you see a discount, check the size, material, brand, and specifications before comparing the actual price.

### 2. Verify the materials of handmade items

Especially for silver jewelry and leather goods, ask about the materials, craftsmanship, care instructions, and return/exchange policies. Handmade doesn't always mean good value.

### 3. Mind your luggage and customs regulations

International carry-on liquid restrictions typically limit each container to no more than 100 ml, but always check the latest rules from your departure airport and airline. If you're bringing items back to Taiwan, also be mindful of duty-free allowances and specific product regulations.

### 4. Leave Room for What You Truly Love

Don't stuff your suitcase on day one. Browse and compare first, then make purchases later in your trip—this also helps curb impulse buying.
