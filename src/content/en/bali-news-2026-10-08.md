## 🚌 11 Electric Buses Expected to Launch in November, First Route Connecting GWK and Sanur Area

Bali's public transportation is making new strides.

11 electric buses have arrived in Bali and are awaiting official service. The Bali Transportation Department plans a **launch ceremony on October 22, with operations starting November 1**. ([Bali Post]())

These buses are part of Bali's **Bali E-Mobility** project and will initially serve:

**Sarbagita Corridor → GOR Ngurah Rai → Udayana University → GWK**

The 11 electric buses are now ready, with charging facilities already installed.

However, one detail travelers might care about:

**Ticket prices have not yet been confirmed.**

The transportation department is still studying whether to adopt a free or paid model, so for now, these electric buses cannot yet be considered bookable or rideable tourist transportation. ([Bali Post]())

If operations go smoothly in the future, it would offer travelers who don't want to use GoJek, Grab, or hire a private driver every day a more eco-friendly public transit option.

## 🌱 Sanur could become Bali's first 'low-emission zone,' potentially reshaping future transportation

A noteworthy new direction emerged in Sanur today that's worth tracking long-term.

World Resources Institute (WRI) Indonesia believes that **Sanur is highly suitable as a pilot area for Bali's Low Emission Zone**, aiming to reduce air pollution from traffic.

Sanur has already begun its low-emission transition, so WRI sees it as an ideal testbed before evaluating whether to expand the initiative to other areas. ([ANTARA Bali]())

If Sanur's pilot succeeds, future studies may explore:

- Denpasar's Gajah Mada historic district
- Kuta
- Other high-density tourist zones

This is currently **not a 'Sanur car ban'**, nor has it been announced that tourists cannot drive into the area.

A more accurate way to understand it is:

**Bali has begun seriously studying how to reduce the reliance on private cars and motorcycles in popular tourist areas.**

For long-term tourism development, I actually see this as a promising direction. Sanur has always been more suited for walking, cycling, and using public transport than places like Seminyak or Canggu. If traffic conditions improve further in the future, the travel experience could become even more enjoyable.

## 🚩 Multiple beaches in Badung step up red-flag zone patrols; foreign tourists ignoring warnings become a major issue

This news is different from yesterday's sea conditions report—the focus here is on **tourists disregarding beach safety signs**.

Badung police and Balawista now patrol the coastal areas daily, from:

**Munggu, Seseh, Pererenan, Batu Mejan, Batu Bolong, Berawa, Batu Belig, Petitenget**

and other beaches, paying special attention to red-flag zones. ([Bali Post]())

Police say one of the most common problems they encounter is foreign tourists still entering the water despite seeing red flags.

The meaning of the red flag is very simple:

**Swimming, bathing, or engaging in water activities is prohibited.**

On-site staff will proactively explain the dangers to visitors, and most people cooperate after understanding the risks.

This is worth reiterating:

**Bali's beaches are not safe to just jump into just because the water looks beautiful.**

Especially in areas like Canggu, Pererenan, Berawa, and Batu Belig, the currents and wave conditions at some beaches are not suitable for general tourists to swim in.

If you see a red flag, do not enter the water.

## 🛄 Ngurah Rai Airport Passenger Luggage Theft: Police Remind Travelers Not to Leave Valuables in Unlocked Bags

A luggage theft incident involving a passenger was reported today at Ngurah Rai Airport.

An airport porter is suspected of stealing **IDR 18,000,000 in cash** from a passenger's backpack in the baggage handling area of the domestic terminal.

Police discovered suspicious behavior by a porter near the conveyor belt through CCTV footage, and arrested him on October 6. They found **IDR 15.8 million** in cash at his residence. ([detikBali]())

The police are still investigating the case.

This incident does not represent a widespread security issue at Ngurah Rai Airport, but it serves as a very practical reminder for travelers:

### **Do not place cash, passports, phones, or other valuables in checked luggage.**

Especially avoid sending unlocked backpacks or bags onto the conveyor belt and then completely ignoring them.

**It's best to keep valuables with you at all times.**

This is a basic yet easily overlooked safety habit when traveling abroad.

## ⚡ October 7: Power maintenance in parts of Bali, affecting Uluwatu, Jungut Batu, and other areas

PLN Bali is conducting power system maintenance today, resulting in **scheduled temporary power outages** in some areas.

Affected areas include:

### South Bali

**Uluwatu, Jalan Labuan Sait and surrounding areas**

11:00 AM – 2:00 PM

### North Bali

**Parts of Negara**

9:00 AM – 11:30 AM
1:00 PM – 3:30 PM

### Nusa Lembongan

**Jungut Batu and surrounding areas**

1:00 PM – 3:00 PM

### Karangasem

**Tista, Br. Taman Seraya**

10:00 AM – 1:00 PM

This is a **scheduled grid maintenance**, not a major outage. PLN also reminds that the actual timing may still be adjusted based on on-site work conditions. ([detikBali]())

If you're staying at a villa, guesthouse, or need to work for long hours, it's a good idea to ask your accommodation:

**'Is there any PLN maintenance today?'**

Travelers in Jungut Batu, Nusa Lembongan, in particular, are more likely to be affected.
