# [2026 Bali Travel Outlook] From the Peak of the Dry Season to the Arrival of the Rainy Season: A Complete Guide to Festivals and Weather from July to December

**Bottom Line First**: The best travel windows in the second half of 2026 are **July–August** (peak dry season, humidity as low as 10–20%, only 1–3 days of rain, prime time for water sports and hiking); **September–October** is still a great travel period but with increasing rainfall; **November–December** marks the prelude to the rainy season, with noticeably more rain but fewer tourists and lush green scenery, ideal for travelers looking to avoid crowds. Remember to book accommodation early for the New Year's peak season to avoid pitfalls.

Planning a trip to **Bali** in the second half of 2026? This period marks the island's transition from ultimate dryness to a lush, colorful phase. You might enjoy the clearest waters and bluest skies of the year in July, or welcome the vibrant greenery brought by the rains in November.

To help you travel like a seasoned pro, we've combined climate data with local festivals to put together this 'July–December Travel Outlook.'

***

# 🌦️ Climate and Seasons: The Shift from Peak Dry Season to the Prelude of the Rainy Season

![氣候與季節：乾季巔峰到雨季預告的轉換](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1780917212578-mixcollage-08-jun-2026-05-52-pm-8861.jpg)

These six months witness Bali's complete journey from the peak of the dry season to the preparation for the rainy season:

- **July–August (Peak Dry Season):** These are the driest months of the year, with the lowest humidity (10–20%) and very little rain (1–3 days). Temperatures are comfortable (24–31°C), offering the best feel, making it the golden season for water activities and hiking.
- **September–October (End of Dry Season):** Sunshine is still abundant, but rainfall begins to increase (2–6 days). Temperatures rise slightly (24–31°C), and humidity increases a bit (15–30%), but it remains a good travel period.
- **November to December (Pre-monsoon to Early Rainy Season):** Rainfall increases significantly (10–15 rainy days), humidity rises (50–60%), while temperatures remain steady (24–31°C). This is the low season but offers a unique charm—fewer tourists, lush green landscapes, making it the best time to avoid crowds.

# 📅 Month-by-Month Deep Dive: Festivals & Tips to Avoid Pitfalls

## **July: A Month of Kite Flying and Artistic Flourish**

- **Weather Overview:** July is the absolute peak of the dry season. Rainfall is minimal, with humidity as low as 10–20% and average temperatures ranging from 24–31°C. Days are brilliantly sunny, and nights are cool and comfortable, making it the driest and most pleasant month of the year. Rare morning mountain views and excellent ocean visibility make this a dream time for outdoor enthusiasts.
- **Packing Tips:** Light, breathable cotton clothing is essential, along with high-SPF sunscreen (SPF 50+) and sunglasses. For early morning hikes, a lightweight long-sleeve sun-protection jacket is recommended.

![7 月：島嶼風箏與藝術飛揚的月份](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1780922788814-7.jpg)

### **Major Festivals:**

- **July 10–12: Penglipuran Village Festival:** Held in the ancient traditional village of Penglipuran, this festival showcases Bali Aga culture, handicrafts, local cuisine, and cultural performances. Visitors can deeply experience the unique charm of this traditional village.
- **July 11–25: Festival Bali Jani (Bali Contemporary Art Festival):** A creative festival focusing on modern and contemporary Balinese art, showcasing new forms of artistic collaboration and cross-disciplinary creativity.
- **July 18–19: Jatiluwih Festival:** Held at the UNESCO World Heritage Jatiluwih Rice Terraces, this festival combines cultural performances, natural beauty, local agricultural products, and hiking activities. The early morning light on the terraces in July is most enchanting, perfect for a peaceful stroll.
- **July 24–26: Lovina Festival:** A cultural feast in the coastal town of Lovina in North Bali, featuring stage performances, traditional markets, and fireworks displays.
- **July 26–30 – Bali International Choir Festival:** An international choir competition and concert series that brings together voices from around the world.
- **July–October – Makepung Buffalo Race Season:** A traditional farming culture performance in Jembrana, West Bali, where buffaloes pull wooden chariots at high speed in thrilling races.
- **July–August – Bali Kite Festival:** Held on Sanur Beach, participants from around the globe compete with giant traditional kites. July offers the most stable weather for optimal kite flying.

## **August: A Lively Season of Music Festivals and Independence Celebrations**

- **Climate Overview:** August continues the dry season with excellent weather. Expect only 1–3 rainy days, humidity at 10–20%, and average temperatures ranging from 24–31°C. Like July, it's one of the driest months of the year. With abundant sunshine and crystal-clear waters, it's perfect for all water sports and outdoor activities. The only difference is the influx of peak-season crowds, so early booking for accommodations and experiences is recommended.
- **Packing Tips:** Same as July: lightweight, breathable clothing, high-SPF sunscreen, and sunglasses are essential. Carry sunscreen for frequent reapplication.

## **September: The Transition from Dry to Rainy Season**

- **Climate Overview:** September marks the beginning of subtle changes. Rainfall increases to 2–4 days, humidity rises to 15–30%, and average temperatures remain between 24–31°C. While still part of the dry season, clouds start to gather, bringing occasional showers that usually come and go quickly. Mornings and evenings stay refreshing, while midday remains hot.
- **Packing Tips:** Keep your sun protection from July and August, but start carrying a lightweight foldable umbrella or a rain jacket to handle sudden showers.
- **Avoiding Crowds:** Late September edges closer to October, which marks the peak of the 'transition season' with more travelers arriving. To dodge the crowds, plan your trip for early September.

- **September 5: Tumpek Kandang (Livestock Thanksgiving):** The second time in the year (the first being in February) that Bali honors its livestock. You can witness heartwarming scenes in villages where animals are bathed, decorated, and blessed.
- **September 24–27: Bali International Airshow:** Featuring the latest aircraft and aviation technology from around the world, including flight displays, exhibitions, and industry exchanges—perfect for tech enthusiasts.

## **October: The 'Transition Season' – A Preview of Peak Travel**

- **Climate Overview:** October marks the official transition from the dry to the rainy season. Rainfall increases to 5–9 days, humidity rises to 30–50%, and average temperatures range from 25–30°C. Days may still be sunny, but brief afternoon showers become more frequent, and cloud cover thickens. Despite this, October is part of Bali's 'transition season'—one of the five peak periods—so visitor numbers start to climb noticeably.
- **Packing Tips:** In addition to sun-protective clothing and a hat, be sure to carry a foldable umbrella or lightweight raincoat. Cotton quick-dry clothes are more practical than standard summer wear.
- **Avoiding Crowds:** After mid-October, tourist numbers surge, and prices for accommodations and car rentals rise accordingly. It's best to travel in early October or opt for late September instead.

- **Oct 10–11: Klungkung Heritage Festival:** Centered on the history and culture of the Klungkung region, featuring exhibitions and stage performances.

## **November: Early Rainy Season's Verdant Calm**

- **Climate Overview:** November marks the start of the rainy season. Rainfall increases significantly to 10–15 days, humidity rises to 50–70%, and average temperatures range from 24–31°C. Rain typically falls in the afternoon and evening, while mornings remain relatively stable. The vegetation turns lush and vibrant with ample moisture, and rice paddies showcase their most beautiful green, creating stunning landscapes. Correspondingly, tourist numbers drop sharply, and hotel prices fall, making it the best time to avoid crowds.
- **Packing Tips:** Make rain protection a must. Bring a waterproof jacket, rain cover for your backpack, and water shoes. Moisture-wicking synthetic clothing is more practical than pure cotton. Evenings can bring a cool breeze, so a light long-sleeved jacket will come in handy.
- **Travel Tips:** The rainy season doesn't mean you can't travel. Mornings are usually clear, with rain arriving in the afternoon and evening. It's best to start your day early and return to your hotel for rest or a spa treatment in the afternoon. Travel experiences at this time often feel more immersive, with fewer tourists and more personalized service.

## **December: Mid-Rainy Season Prelude to Christmas and New Year**

- **Climate Overview:** December is the middle of the rainy season. Rainfall remains at 10–15 days (similar to November), with humidity at 60–80% and average temperatures of 24–31°C. Rain is more frequent but typically comes as afternoon showers, while mornings and evenings stay relatively dry. Temperatures rise slightly, making it feel more humid. Despite the rainy season, late December sees a surge of international tourists and local Indonesian holidaymakers for Christmas and New Year, creating the 'Nataru' (Christmas-New Year) peak season.
- **Packing Tips:** Rain gear is a must, but given the humid heat, opt for loose, breathable clothing. A waterproof backpack or dry bags will protect your electronics. Quick-dry clothes are more practical than long garments.
- **Avoid the Crowds:** From December 20 to January 5, it's peak season for the "Nataru" holiday period. Hotel and car rental prices may double, traffic gets congested, and attractions are packed. If you enjoy the buzz, join the festivities; if you seek tranquility, it's best to avoid dates after December 20.

![12 月：雨季中期的耶誕與新年序曲](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1780924308707-12.jpg)

Although Bali is predominantly Hindu, Christmas is still a public holiday. Churches and Christian communities hold prayers and celebrations. This also marks the start of the "Nataru" holiday period.
- **December 25 – Pandawa Beach Festival:** A beach festival held at Pandawa Beach, combining stage performances and festive activities with Christmas celebrations, drawing large crowds. The scenic beach paired with traditional performances makes it a worthwhile experience.
- **December – Bali Rockin' Blues Festival:** A music festival blending rock and blues, held on the beach with food and drink markets.
- **December – Denpasar Festival:** A year-end city festival in Denpasar featuring concerts, markets, and cultural programs, celebrating the end of the year and anticipation of the new one.
- **December – Besakih Festival:** Held near Besakih Temple, Bali's mother temple, this festival aims to promote local tourism and culture. The temple is the most sacred on the island, and during the festival, many pilgrims come to pray and seek blessings.

## Editor's Smart Travel Tips 💡

![小編的聰明旅遊筆記](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1780924530878-15.jpg)

### **Season Selection Strategy:**

- **For Dry Weather & High Visibility:** July to August is the best choice all year, but it's also the most crowded with the highest room rates.
- **For Savings & Experience:** Early September to early October is the 'golden transition period'—weather is still good, crowds are manageable, and room rates are reasonable.
- **Seeking solitude and depth:** November is a hidden gem of a month. Early rainy season, lush green landscapes, few tourists, and affordable accommodation—the perfect time for an immersive travel experience.
- **Seeking vibrancy and celebration:** December's Christmas and New Year period is the festive peak, but accommodation prices double and attractions are overcrowded.

### **Debunking the Rainy Season Myth:**

- Rainy season ≠ all-day rain. It's usually just afternoon showers; mornings and nights remain dry.
- Rainy season = lush green Bali. The scenery is at its most beautiful, offering the richest photographic opportunities.
- Rainy season = off-peak deals. Accommodation and spa prices are negotiable, often leading to a more personalized travel experience.

### **Booking Recommendations:**

- For July–August and mid-to-late December, book rooms at least three months in advance, especially in Ubud and Sanur.
- For specific festivals (Jazz Festival, Writers Festival, Spirit Festival), book one month in advance.
- In November, you can book on the fly with more choices and more affordable prices.

![預約建議：](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1780924627221-16.jpg)

### **Transportation Tips:**

- Car rentals and private drivers see a 20-30% price hike from July to August and mid-to-late December, with limited availability.
- January offers smooth traffic and reasonable prices, making it the best time for self-driving or hiring a driver.
- During the rainy season, if your itinerary includes mountainous areas or outlying islands, allow extra time for weather-related delays.

### **Festival Etiquette:**

- Dress modestly when attending temple ceremonies (sarong and sash are ideal).
- Always ask locals or your guide before taking photos, out of respect for sacred rituals.
- Some festivals prohibit outsiders from entering the inner sanctum—please respect this.
