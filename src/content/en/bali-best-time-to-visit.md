**Bottom line**: The **dry season (April–October)** offers sunny skies and is ideal for travel; the **rainy season (November–April)** isn't something to fear—tropical downpours usually pass quickly within the day (often under an hour), and it brings fewer crowds, lower hotel rates, and cooler weather, making it a great choice for budget-conscious travelers. Rainfall varies significantly across Bali, so **it's rare for the whole island to rain at once**—just because one area is wet doesn't mean others are.

When planning a trip to Bali, one of the most common questions is: 'When is the best time to go?' Although Bali's climate is divided into dry and rainy seasons, the reality can be more unpredictable than you might think.

Before you go, check the [Bali weather forecast](/en/weather/) for real-time updates, which also includes a 12-month rainfall probability and travel suitability chart.

## **Bali's Seasons**

The dry season typically runs from April to October, offering sunny skies and pleasant weather—perfect for travel. The rainy season lasts from November to April, but this isn't a hard-and-fast rule. In this tropical region, weather forecasts often miss the mark; sometimes you're caught in a downpour while your phone still shows a sunny icon.

## **Surprises of the Rainy Season**

Even during the rainy season, there's no need to worry too much. Tropical thunderstorms usually pass quickly within the day and don't linger. While the dry season is hailed as the best time, the rainy season has its perks: fewer crowds since it's not peak season, generally lower hotel rates, and cooler weather. When it rains, just find a spot to take shelter, enjoy a cup of coffee, and the skies often clear up in no time!

## **Rainfall Patterns in Bali**

Don't forget, rainfall in Bali varies by region. While November to March is the wetter season, we've visited during this time and only encountered light, brief showers that usually stopped within an hour. This might be because most rainfall comes from convective thunderstorms, and conditions differ from one area to another.

So, you don't need to keep asking, '**Is it raining in Bali? / Will it rain in Bali?** ' The chance of rain across the entire island is actually quite low; just because one area is sunny doesn't mean the rest will be the same!

## **Be Prepared, No Matter When You Visit**

Whenever you choose to visit Bali, always carry rain gear and stay alert to weather changes. Your trip might bring some unexpected weather surprises, but that's part of Bali's charm. We hope you enjoy the unique beauty and wonderful moments this stunning island offers, rain or shine!

## Rainy Season Tips

Bali's rainy season requires extra attention from travelers. Here are eight tips for navigating it:

1. **Bring waterproof gear**: Pack raincoats, umbrellas, and waterproof bags to handle sudden downpours.
2. **Choose suitable transportation**: Wet, slippery roads make safer options like private cars or taxis better than scooters.
3. **Allow extra travel time**: Rain can cause traffic jams or delays, so padding your schedule helps avoid disruptions.
4. **Avoid beach areas**: Heavy rain can stir up rough seas and murky water, increasing risks—staying away from beaches is a safe bet.
5. **Heed local warnings**: Local authorities and tourism offices often issue alerts for typhoons or floods; travelers should monitor these closely and follow advice.
6. **Skip hiking or trekking**: Mountain trails can become slippery and muddy in the rain, raising danger levels, so it's best to avoid these activities.
7. **Avoid visiting outlying islands**: Taking a ferry from Bali to nearby islands during the rainy season may lead to major delays or cancellations due to poor sea conditions, disrupting your plans.
8. **Skip white-water rafting**: Booked rafting trips might become hazardous with fast currents, or operators may cancel them for safety reasons.
