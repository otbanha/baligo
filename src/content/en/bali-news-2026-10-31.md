## 🌧️ Heavy Rain Alerts Issued for 8 Regencies in Bali! Southern Sea Waves Could Reach 2.5 Meters

The Bali Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BMKG) issued the latest weather warning on October 30, alerting that several areas in Bali may experience moderate to heavy rainfall.

The warning period is from **8:00 AM on October 30 to 8:00 AM on October 31**, covering the following 8 regencies:

- Badung
- Bangli
- Buleleng
- Gianyar
- Klungkung
- Karangasem
- Jembrana
- Tabanan

BMKG predicts that on October 30, Bali will see mostly light rain, though some areas may experience heavier downpours, with a high of around 32°C.

Beyond land weather, the waters off southern Bali also deserve attention. Wave heights in the southern sea could reach 2.5 meters, and if wind speeds and wave heights hit warning levels, ferry and small boat operations may be affected.

### What should travelers watch out for?

If you have outdoor plans in Ubud, Canggu, Jimbaran, or Nusa Penida, it's wise to keep some flexibility in your schedule.

- When heading to mountain attractions, watch out for slippery roads and localized flooding.
- Before boarding a boat, check with the company for the latest sea conditions and schedules.
- Avoid cliffs, waterfalls, and areas prone to rockfalls during heavy rain.
- Allow extra travel time when arranging airport transfers.

This warning is valid until 8:00 AM on October 31, but that doesn't guarantee it won't rain afterward. Keep an eye on new forecasts from BMKG.

## 💰 Bali Proposes Raising Tourist Fee for Foreign Visitors to IDR 1,000,000! May Also Include Travel Insurance

Bali's tourist fee system could be facing a major overhaul.

On October 29, Deputy Speaker of the Bali Regional House of Representatives, IGK Kresna Budi, proposed a new tourist fee concept to Governor Wayan Koster, aiming to consolidate the current fees paid by foreign visitors.

The proposal suggests raising the fee for foreign tourists to approximately **IDR 1,000,000 (about USD$32)** per person, integrating related services into a single system.

The proposal includes:

- Personal safety protection during the trip.
- Accident and medical insurance.
- Use appropriate medical facilities when necessary.
- Reduce the need for travelers to make duplicate payments at different attractions.

Additionally, the proposal suggests setting up a centralized service center with toll-like gates outside Ngurah Rai International Airport in the Patung Kuda area, allowing travelers to complete all relevant payments and service procedures in one go after leaving the airport.

Proponents believe this should not just be a place to collect fees, but could also incorporate Balinese traditional dance, welcome ceremonies, and local products, so travelers can feel the local culture upon arrival.

### Do You Really Have to Pay an Extra 1 Million Indonesian Rupiah Now?

**Not yet! This is just an idea proposed by local council members, not a policy that has been approved.**

Currently, the foreign tourist levy in Bali remains at 150,000 Indonesian Rupiah per person. The proposed new system has not yet completed policy evaluation, and there is no official implementation date.

Therefore, travelers planning a trip to Bali in the near future do not need to pay an extra 1 million Indonesian Rupiah because of this proposal.

However, this news is worth following. If it is actually implemented in the future, it will be a significant adjustment to Bali's tourism levy system.

## ✈️ Air New Zealand Adds Direct Christchurch–Bali Flights! Three Weekly Services from Winter 2027

Air New Zealand has announced an expansion of its international route network from Christchurch, with a key highlight being the launch of **direct flights from Christchurch to Denpasar, Bali**.

The new route is expected to commence in the winter of 2027, offering three weekly direct services through the end of August 2027.

This means travelers from New Zealand's South Island can now fly directly to Bali from Christchurch without needing to transfer at other airports.

The airline stated that this route expansion is part of efforts to strengthen Christchurch's international air connectivity. Meanwhile, the Christchurch–Singapore route will also be upgraded to year-round operations.

### What Impact Will This Have on Bali's Tourism Market?

Australia has long been a key source of international visitors to Bali, with New Zealand travelers also contributing a significant share.

The new direct flights from Christchurch are expected to make it more convenient for South Island travelers to visit Bali, while adding new air capacity to the winter 2027 tourism market.

For travelers from Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Singapore, this route does not directly add flight options to and from Asia, but it reflects the airline's continued confidence in Bali's international tourism demand.

Currently announced operational details indicate three weekly flights. Actual launch dates, flight schedules, and booking arrangements should be confirmed based on the airline's official announcements.

## 🇨🇳 Indonesia Actively Courts Chinese Travelers! Travel Agents Visit Bali and Komodo on Familiarization Trip

Indonesia's Ministry of Tourism is actively expanding into the Chinese travel market, recently inviting seven representatives from Chinese travel agencies and tourism businesses to join a familiarization trip from October 19 to 25.

The trip focused on two destinations: Labuan Bajo in East Nusa Tenggara, and Bali.

In Labuan Bajo, the delegation experienced traditional Pinisi schooner cruises, Padar Island, Pink Beach, and Komodo Island, and also toured upscale accommodations.

The group then moved on to Bali, where they explored Ubud's cultural, wellness, and culinary tourism offerings.

During the event, organizers also arranged business networking sessions with 14 local tourism stakeholders, including airlines, hotels, and travel agencies, aiming to develop more travel products tailored to the Chinese market.

### How Important Is the Chinese Traveler Market?

According to Indonesia's Central Statistics Agency (BPS):

- In 2025, around 1.34 million Chinese travelers visited Indonesia.
- From January to August 2026, Chinese arrivals reached 1.05 million.
- Compared to the 905,002 visitors during the same period in 2025, this represents an increase of approximately 16.46%.

Indonesia's Ministry of Tourism hopes to further attract Chinese travelers through familiarization trips for travel agents and customized tourism products.

For Bali, this means that beyond the Australian and Southeast Asian markets, the Chinese market remains a key source worth actively developing.

In particular, Ubud's cultural experiences, spas, wellness retreats, and cuisine are likely to become the main products promoted to Chinese travelers in the future.
