# Top Topics & News for Bali Travelers | September 24, 2026

## 🌊 Coastal Flooding Possible in Late September: Southern Shores and Ports on Alert

This is the most practical news for travelers today.

Bali’s Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BBMKG) has issued a warning: due to the **full moon on September 26**, some coastal areas of Bali may experience the highest sea levels and coastal flooding (rob) from **September 26 to 30**.

Affected areas may include:

- South Jembrana
- South Tabanan
- Badung coast
- Coastal Denpasar
- Coastal Gianyar
- South Klungkung

BBMKG warns that rising sea levels could affect **port loading and unloading, coastal housing, fishing, and other activities near the coast**.

More importantly for travelers, wave heights in southern Bali waters are forecast to reach up to **3 meters** on September 23-24; Nusa Dua around 2 meters, Sanur around 2 meters, Tanah Lot around 1.5 meters, and Kuta around 1.25 meters.

So if you've planned **speedboat rides, snorkeling, diving, or island-hopping trips**, don't just rely on general weather forecasts in late September—keep an eye on sea conditions and the latest updates from boat operators.

## 🇮🇳 Indian Travelers Become Bali's Third-Largest Source Market, Nearly 320,000 Arrivals from January to July

The Indian market is becoming increasingly important to Bali's tourism industry.

According to data released by the Bali Tourism Board for 2026, **Indian travelers totaled 318,948 arrivals from January to July this year**, making India the third-largest foreign tourist market for Bali, after Australia and China (including Hong Kong and Taiwan).

During the same period, Australian visitors reached 982,403, Chinese visitors 356,384, and India ranked third with 318,948 visitors.

Moreover, Indian travelers are not just the traditional beach holiday crowd. The local tourism industry is particularly optimistic about the Indian market for:

**Destination weddings, honeymoons, culture, spirituality, wellness, nature, and lifestyle travel.**

The Bali Tourism Board also aims to strengthen the Luxury, Wellness, and MICE markets, collaborating with Indian airlines, travel agencies, and wedding planners.

For the average Chinese traveler, this shift may not immediately affect their trips, but from a tourism market perspective, **India is no longer a niche source market that Bali can afford to ignore.**

## 🏝️ Bali's Tourism Industry Shifts from 'More People' to 'Spending More, Staying Longer'

Bali's hotel and restaurant operators once again highlighted a clear tourism transformation in recent years: **shifting from Quantity Tourism to Quality Tourism.**

The Bali Hotel and Restaurant Association (PHRI Bali) stated that Bali should no longer just chase visitor numbers, but instead focus more on **length of stay, spending power, and quality of travel experience.**

This direction aligns with the Bali Tourism Board's recent strategy for the Indian market—beyond attracting more visitors, the goal is to grow high-value segments like Luxury, Wellness, MICE, and Destination Weddings.

In other words, Bali is no longer just talking about:

"How many tourists came this year?"

Instead, it's starting to ask:

**"How long do these tourists stay in Bali? How much do they spend? What kind of travel value do they leave behind?"**

For travelers, this could also mean that Bali will be more actively developing high-quality, in-depth, and niche tourism products in the future.

## 🚑 Nusa Penida Launches Sea Ambulance, Adding a Lifeline for Cross-Island Emergency Care

This is the news you specifically requested, and I think it fits perfectly into the "Travel Safety" category.

The **Nusa Medica Sea Ambulance** has officially launched, with its base at Bias Munjul Harbor on Nusa Ceningan. It primarily provides **emergency medical evacuation services** between Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, and Nusa Ceningan.

This service is designed specifically for the unique geographical conditions of the Klungkung archipelago. If local residents or tourists on the outlying islands face an emergency requiring further medical treatment, they can be transferred via the sea ambulance to facilities with more comprehensive medical capabilities.

The concept of the new service is simple:

**Connecting Islands, Saving Lives.**

For the average traveler, this doesn't mean Nusa Penida is now "medically problem-free," but rather that the island now has a specialized resource for emergency medical transfers.

Especially as Nusa Penida has become extremely popular in recent years for water sports, diving, snorkeling, and cliffside attractions, **having a formal inter-island medical evacuation capability is a crucial safety infrastructure for both locals and tourists.**

## 🏖️ Sanur Seen by Industry as a Key Bali Destination, with Talent and Service as Critical Factors

Another piece of tourism industry news worth noting today is Sanur's position in Bali's travel market.

The Bali Hotel and Restaurant Association (PHRI Bali) stated that **Sanur has now become one of Bali's important tourist destinations**, partly due to the growing maturity of the local tourism workforce and service quality.

Sanur's recent tourism positioning differs from areas like Canggu and Seminyak.

Beyond traditional beach vacations, it is gradually developing **family travel, senior travel, wellness tourism, and more laid-back resort-style products**.

This is a trend worth noting for travelers: if you don't enjoy the livelier tourist vibe of Canggu or Seminyak, Sanur has increasingly become an ideal alternative for a 'slow-paced Bali' experience in recent years.
