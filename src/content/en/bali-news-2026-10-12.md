## 22 Protected Green Turtles Found in Serangan Waters, Police Investigate Suspected Smuggling Vessel

On October 10, an unattended boat was discovered off the coast of Serangan Island in South Denpasar, Bali, carrying 22 live green turtles. Authorities suspect this may involve illegal wildlife transport or smuggling.

Police responded to a public report and found a blue boat anchored near the shore, which did not resemble typical local fishing vessels. No one was on board when officers approached for inspection.

The 22 green turtles have been handed over to relevant authorities for care and health checks; if deemed fit, they will be released back into the wild. Police are currently investigating the boat's owner and any involved parties.

Green turtles are protected wildlife in Indonesia. For travelers, this news serves as a reminder to choose reputable operators that prioritize animal welfare and transparent sourcing when participating in turtle conservation or marine ecology activities, and to avoid purchasing or carrying protected marine species or their products.

## 3.6 kg of Liquid Cocaine Seized at Ngurah Rai Airport, Hidden in Shampoo and Personal Care Bottles

Bali's Ngurah Rai International Airport recently uncovered a suspected drug smuggling case. A 36-year-old Brazilian woman was detained on October 6 upon arrival at the international terminal, with police discovering liquid cocaine hidden in shampoo and other personal care bottles in her luggage, totaling approximately 3.66 kg. Authorities disclosed the details on October 11.

Police stated that the operation was a joint effort between airport customs and Bali police's drug investigation unit. Investigations are ongoing into the drug's origin, intended destination, and whether other individuals are involved.

As of the time of reporting, the case is still under investigation and the suspect has not been convicted by the court.

For general travelers, this news serves as a reminder to strictly adhere to local customs and drug-related laws when entering Indonesia. Do not carry luggage, packages, or unknown items for strangers, and do not overlook the potential risk of prohibited items even if they appear to be everyday goods.

## Kuta Coast Erosion Control Continues: Government Inspects Beach and Walkway Projects

On October 11, former Indonesian President Megawati Soekarnoputri, along with the Governor of Bali and representatives from Badung Regency, visited Kuta Beach to inspect coastal erosion control and coastal infrastructure improvement efforts.

The local government is currently advancing walkway and coastal restoration projects along Kuta, Legian, and Seminyak, aiming to improve the coastal environment and enhance safety and comfort for residents and tourists using the beach area.

According to data released during the inspection, Bali's total coastline is approximately 633 kilometers, of which about 215 kilometers are affected by varying degrees of coastal erosion. Between 2007 and 2025, the government has invested around 1.2 trillion Indonesian rupiah to address some of the eroded coastlines, with further areas still requiring ongoing management.

For tourists, Kuta Beach remains a popular sunset spot. However, if visiting the coastal walkway or areas near construction, it's advisable to be mindful of on-site barriers, construction signs, and temporary pathways. This inspection does not mean the entire Kuta-Legian-Seminyak coastline is complete; actual access conditions should be confirmed by on-site announcements.

## Klungkung Traditional Cultural Performance Attracts Tourists: Reog Ponorogo Takes the Stage at Kertha Gosa

On October 11, the traditional Indonesian dance Reog Ponorogo was performed at Taman Kertha Gosa in Klungkung, Bali, drawing local residents and tourists to watch.

Originating from East Java, Reog Ponorogo is renowned for its large ornamental headdresses, traditional costumes, music, and powerful dance movements, making it one of Indonesia's iconic traditional performing arts.

This performance also highlights that tourism in Bali is not just about beaches, temples, and resorts—local cultural spaces can equally become part of the travel experience.

If you're planning a day trip to Klungkung, keep an eye on Taman Kertha Gosa and its surrounding cultural events. However, this performance was announced for October 11, and there isn't enough information to confirm whether similar shows will be held afterward, so it's not advisable to treat it as a daily regular performance.
