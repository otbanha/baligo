# Hot Topics & News Roundup for Bali Travelers｜September 1, 2026

## 💰 Nusa Penida Attraction Tax Goes Live Tomorrow! Each Site Charges Separately

This is one of the most important updates for Bali travelers today.

The Klungkung Regency government has announced that **starting September 1, 2026, foreign tourists visiting individual attractions on Nusa Penida will be charged a local attraction tax (retribusi) per site**.

In other words, this isn't a simple one-time fee for entering Nusa Penida—it's a charge applied to each DTW (Daya Tarik Wisata, or tourist attraction) on the island.

This new system was officially announced today and **goes into effect tomorrow**.

For travelers used to hitting multiple spots like Broken Beach, Kelingking Beach, and Angel's Billabong in a single day, the key difference is:

**You can no longer budget just one lump sum for your Nusa Penida trip—you'll need to pay attention to each attraction's fee structure.**

If you're planning to visit Nusa Penida tomorrow or later, it's wise to factor this new policy into your trip budget.

This also serves as a reminder: many popular beachfront restaurants in Bali use large amounts of wood, thatch, or outdoor decorations, and in the event of a fire, the flames can spread very quickly.

## 🌊 Nusa Penida's Waves Could Generate 50MW! A Renewable Energy Plan Under Study

The Bali government is researching a rather unique energy project.

Bali Governor Wayan Koster stated that **the wave energy potential around Nusa Penida could reach at least 50MW (50,000 kilowatts)**.

The research is currently being conducted by Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB) and several universities in Bali, primarily to assess whether wave energy is feasible for actual development and commercial use.

Interestingly, Nusa Penida's current electricity demand is about **24MW**.

If the 50MW potential is confirmed to be effectively developable through research, it could theoretically not only supply Nusa Penida but also have the opportunity to transmit surplus power to the main island of Bali.

This project is still in the **research phase and is not a power plant that has already begun construction**.

However, for Nusa Penida, if it can truly harness renewable energy sources like wave and solar power on a large scale in the future, it could reduce the island's reliance on traditional fossil fuels.

## ✈️ Bali Hopes to Expand Visa-Free Countries! But No Official List Yet

The Bali provincial government stated today that it is positive about the central Indonesian government's ongoing study into expanding the list of **visa-free entry countries**.

The Bali Tourism Office said that adding visa-free countries can lower the entry barrier for foreign travelers visiting Bali, helping sustain the growth of international tourist arrivals.

However, a key note:

**No new visa-free country list has been officially announced yet.**

The relevant proposal is still under discussion among Indonesia's Ministry of Tourism, Immigration and Corrections, and other agencies, with the central government still gathering input from various parties.

The Bali provincial government also emphasized that granting visa-free treatment cannot be based solely on 'convenience'—it also requires consideration of:

- National security
- Passenger safety
- Economic benefits
- Tourism industry demands

So when you see news about 'Indonesia preparing to add visa-free countries,' **don't interpret it as Taiwan passports already being visa-free.**

Currently, the visa regulations for Taiwanese travelers to Indonesia have not changed immediately due to this discussion.

## 🍽️ Bali Interfood 2026 Kicks Off This Week! Food and Beverage Industry Gathers at Nusa Dua from September 2

If you happen to be in Nusa Dua tomorrow or the day after, keep an eye out for a major event starting soon.

**Bali Interfood 2026** will be held from **September 2 to 4** at the Bali Nusa Dua Convention Center (BNDCC).

Now in its 7th edition, in addition to the food and beverage exhibition, there will also be:

**Gelato World Cup Indonesia Selection**

along with activities such as culinary workshops, certifications, competitions, and business matching.

Among these, the Indonesia selection for the Gelato World Cup draws particular attention, as participants compete to represent Indonesia in subsequent international events.

This event primarily targets the food and beverage, hotel, café, and HORECA industries, so **general tourists don’t necessarily need to make a special trip** for it.

However, if you’re interested in coffee, desserts, gelato, or Bali’s culinary scene, keep an eye out for related activities around Nusa Dua during these days.
