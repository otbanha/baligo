# Bali Tourist Hot Topics & News Roundup | October 2, 2026

## 🚧 Key Seminyak–Denpasar Road Closed at Night for One Month, 10 PM–6 AM

If you're staying in **Seminyak** recently, take note of this traffic update.

**Jalan Kunti II**, which connects Denpasar with Seminyak and Kuta, will undergo drainage works requiring excavation. From **October 1 to November 1**, nighttime road closures and traffic diversions will be in effect.

The planned restrictions are:

**Daily from 10 PM to 6 AM**

Daytime access remains open.

Jalan Kunti II is a vital route linking Denpasar and Seminyak, so if you're traveling from:

**Denpasar → Seminyak**

or

**Seminyak → Denpasar**

travel may require detours via Gunung Athena, Gunung Soputan, Sunset Road, or other alternative routes.

For travelers, the biggest impact isn't that the road is closed, but that **evening travel times could be longer**.

If you need to head to the airport, a restaurant, or your hotel after 10:00 PM, don't rely too heavily on the travel times shown on Google Maps.

##  🛵 Around 400–500 motorcycle rental businesses in Bali may lack proper permits—renters, be extra cautious

This news is crucial for independent travelers.

Bali's government stated today that fewer than 5 locally operated foreign-invested enterprises (PMA) have obtained official motorcycle rental permits, yet the actual market is estimated to have **400–500** such rental businesses.

These figures are estimates from the Bali Investment and One-Stop Services Agency (DPMPTSP), and the government is currently stepping up oversight of these operators.

The government's concern isn't just about the rentals themselves, but also whether some foreign-owned businesses are operating under local names, and whether the business types registered with the authorities match their actual operations.

### What matters most for travelers?

Don't let this number make you think, 'I can't rent a scooter in Bali.'

Instead, if you do rent, it's best to choose a provider with **a fixed storefront, clear contracts, complete vehicle documents, and transparent insurance and deposit terms**.

Also, if you don't have the proper driving qualifications under Indonesian regulations, **don't ride just because the rental shop is willing to let you**.

## ✈️ Bali welcomed 4.58 million foreign tourists in the first 8 months of this year, a 1.62% decrease year-on-year

BPS Bali released the latest tourism data today.

From January to August 2026, Bali welcomed a total of:

**4,586,959 foreign visitors**

Compared to the 4,662,720 visitors in the same period of 2025, this represents a decrease of **1.62%**.

However, performance varies across different markets.

First 8 months:

- 🇦🇺 Australia: 1,138,634 visitors, up 7.06% year-on-year
- 🇨🇳 China: 401,762 visitors, up 6.04% year-on-year
- 🇮🇳 India: 352,510 visitors
- 🇬🇧 United Kingdom: 202,759 visitors
- 🇰🇷 South Korea: 191,386 visitors

Notably, **domestic tourism actually increased**.

During the same period, domestic tourist arrivals to Bali reached **18,155,881**, up 2.15% year-on-year; however, domestic visitors traveling to Bali from other provinces in Indonesia dropped by 8.05%.

So looking solely at 'a 1.62% year-on-year decline in foreign tourists' doesn't fully capture the current tourism flow structure in Bali.

##  🇫🇷 French Tourists Surge Suddenly, Surpassing India in August

Another BPS data point released the same day is also intriguing.

**In August 2026, 41,937 French tourists visited Bali, a 20.42% monthly increase**, ranking even above India to become the third-largest source of foreign tourists to Bali that month.

The top three in August were:

1. Australia: 161,258
2. China: 47,613
3. France: 41,937

BPS attributes the rise in French tourists to the European summer holidays and high temperatures in their home region.

By region, **European tourist arrivals rose 4.92% in August to 220,047**, making it one of the higher-growth markets among key source regions.

For Bali's tourism industry, this is a shift worth watching: beyond traditional markets like Australia, China, and India, **long-haul European markets still show considerable demand**.

##  🤖 Bali Begins Using AI to Strengthen Disaster Warnings, Aiming to Respond Faster to Emergencies

Bali's BPBD announced today that it is stepping up the use of **artificial intelligence (AI) for disaster early warning and information processing**.

Staff are currently being trained, with the hope that AI can assist in organizing disaster risk information, issuing early warnings, and speeding up the assessment of affected areas after a disaster strikes.

BPBD stated that when Bali experiences extreme weather, multiple regencies may be affected simultaneously, making the **speed of information transmission** crucial.

At present, this system is primarily an internal disaster management and early warning tool for the government, not an app that tourists can directly use.

However, for travelers, this development is still worth noting—especially as Bali enters its transitional weather period, when **heavy rain, floods, strong winds, and other sudden weather events** could impact travel plans.
