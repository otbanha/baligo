## How Is That Reflection Photo Actually Taken?

Almost every photo of the Gate of Heaven online features a perfect reflection—the sky, the gate, and Mount Agung mirrored on a calm surface. Many assume there's a pool in front of the temple or a specific angle that captures a natural reflection.

**The truth: The reflection comes from a phone screen, not water.**

Here's how it works on-site: You stand in front of the gate, and the temple photographer (or you) places a phone or tablet flat on the ground or at a low angle, screen facing up. Another phone shoots from the front, capturing you, the gate, and the reflected image from the screen below.

Key details:

- The larger and brighter the screen, the better the reflection effect (iPhone 15 Pro Max and Samsung S24 Ultra offer the most stable results).
- In strong light, the screen reflection can be washed out by sunlight; the best results occur between 7:00–8:00 AM when the light angle is low.
- The temple’s photographer knows the angles well, so you can finish shooting within 30 minutes.

**Two reader recommendations:**

**If you want reflection photos** → Arrive before 7:00 AM, pay the photographer’s service fee (see next section for costs), and wrap up in 30 minutes.

**If you just want to see the temple without queuing** → Enter after 3:00 PM when the crowds have thinned; Mount Agung may still be visible on dry-season afternoons. The cost is the same, but you won’t need to wait for the photographer.

## Location—Where is it in Bali? How far from where you're staying?

The Lempuyang temple complex is located in **Karangasem Regency** in eastern Bali, administratively part of the Abang district, near Tirta Gangga Water Palace and Amed Bay.

**Exact coordinates:** `-8.3920°S, 115.6297°E` (Pura Penataran Agung Lempuyang, home to the Gates of Heaven).

**Altitude Levels:**

- Scenic parking lot: approx. 350m
- Gate of Heaven (Pura Penataran Agung Lempuyang): approx. 600m
- Summit Temple (Pura Lempuyang Luhur): approx. 1,175m

At 600m, it's 4–6°C cooler than Kuta or Ubud town, with morning temperatures possibly dropping to 18–22°C—something to keep in mind when packing (see the clothing section).

## Lempuyang Isn't Just One Temple—The Full Six-Temple Structure (99% of Visitors Only See the Bottom)

The "Gate of Heaven" is merely the **lowest entrance** to the entire Lempuyang temple complex, and where nearly all tourists stop. The true pilgrimage route begins here, ascending the ridge through six temples, culminating at the summit temple Pura Lempuyang Luhur at 1,175m—one of Bali's nine directional temples (Sad Kahyangan Jagat).

**Six Temples from Lowest to Highest:**

| # | Temple Name | Elevation | Walking Time from Previous Stop | Worth It? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pura Penataran Agung Lempuyang | ~600m | ~20 min walk from parking lot | ✅ Must-visit, the Gateway to Heaven is here |
| 2 | Pura Pasar Agung | ~800m | ~45 min further up | ✅ Recommended, fewer crowds, ancient shrines, open views |
| 3 | Pura Puncak Bisbis | ~950m | Another 30 min | ⚪ Only if you have stamina; steep stone steps |
| 4 | Pura Lempuyang Madya | ~1,050m | Another 20 min | ⚪ Mid-mountain rest stop |
| 5 | Pura Lempuyang Luhur | ~1,175m | Another 25 min | ✅ Final sacred temple, one of Bali's most important |
| Subsidiary | Pura Telaga Mas | ~350m | Near parking lot | ⚪ Most visitors can skip |

**What You Need for a Full Summit:**

- One-way uphill takes about 2.5–3 hours (moderate fitness)
- Bring: 1.5L water, sunscreen, lightweight rain jacket (summit clouds roll in suddenly)
- Footwear: Non-slip soles; stone steps, no hiking gear required
- Must wear a sarong throughout (required at every temple along the way)

99% of visitors leave after snapping the reflection shot, but the higher you go, the quieter it gets. By the second temple, Pura Pasar Agung, there are almost no tourists—the atmosphere is completely different from the bottom. If you have an extra 1.5 hours and decent energy, it's well worth continuing upward.

**General Opening Hours:** Daily 07:00–17:00 (some sources say entry as early as 06:30)

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:30–08:00 | Reflection photos, sunrise chasers | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (best in dry season) | Light → increasing |
| 08:00–10:00 | Peak arrival for general tourists | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Heavy |
| 10:00–14:00 | Peak season rush | ⭐⭐⭐ | Most crowded |
| 14:00–17:00 | Just want to see the temple | ⭐⭐ (cloudy) | Decreasing |

**Peak Season (July–August, around Chinese New Year) Queue Situation:**

- Waiting time for reflection photos: 1–2 hours or more
- Actual photo time: Under 5 minutes
- Arrive at 06:30—not 07:00, but 06:30

**Low Season (Feb–Apr, Oct–Nov):**

- Wait times reduced to under 30 minutes
- Rainy season (Nov–Mar) yields poor reflection shots due to overcast skies, but the temple remains open, often saving 1–2 hours of queuing

**Rainy vs Dry Season:**

- Dry season (May–Oct) offers the highest chance of clear views of Mount Agung; from June to September, it's visible almost every morning
- Rainy season mornings are typically cloudy, with occasional clearing from 14:00–15:00, though unreliable

**Insider Tip:** During peak season, Monday to Thursday **09:30–11:00** sees the shortest wait times—early birds have left, and the next wave hasn't arrived yet. However, Mount Agung is often clouded by then, making it unsuitable for reflection shots; purely visiting the temple is fine.

## Dress Code, Taboos & On-Site Tips

### Sarong Rules

When entering any temple at Lempuyang, **both men and women are required to wear a sarong (kain/sarong).** The temple provides them for free at the entrance—just borrow one and return it when you leave. You can bring your own, but borrowing is perfectly fine.

Upper body: Shoulders must be covered. Vests or sleeveless tops are not allowed. While the temple provides wrap-around belts, that doesn't mean a tank top is acceptable—temple staff may stop you and ask you to add a shawl. **Wearing a shirt with sleeves is the simplest solution.**

### Temperature at 600m Elevation

Pura Penataran Agung (the Gate of Heaven itself) sits at about 600m, making it 4–6°C cooler than Kuta:

- Early morning 06:30–08:00: around 18–22°C, cooler when mountain breezes blow
- 09:00–12:00 AM: Around 22–26°C, similar to lowland temperatures
- If you continue climbing to the summit temple at 1,175m: temperatures may drop to 16–18°C, even cooler in fog

**What to wear:** A light jacket or thin long sleeves (even if it feels warm for photos, it gets cool while hiking). Don't just wear a tank top and shorts.

### No Entry During Menstruation

According to Balinese Hindu tradition, **women are not allowed to enter temples during their menstrual period**. Ticket booths usually verbally confirm this, and some temples post notices, but there is no mandatory verification. This is a basic courtesy to respect the sacred space—please use your own judgment.

### Restroom Situation

Public restrooms near the parking lot cost 2,000–5,000 IDR. Restrooms inside the temple are farther away, so **go before entering**.

### Is It Suitable for Kids?

- **Ages 5+ who can wear a sarong** → Yes, but be prepared for children to get restless during peak season waits
- **Toddlers (ages 1–3)** → Not recommended; it's a 20-minute walk from the parking lot to the temple entrance, plus stone steps
- **Continue climbing up the temple** → Suitable for children aged 8 and above with sufficient stamina; be sure to bring water

### Physical Requirements

Going only to the Gate of Heaven (first temple): Low physical demand, 20–30 minute walk, mostly flat terrain. Continuing up the temple: Basic hiking stamina recommended, 2.5–3 hours one way with continuous stone stair sections. Not recommended for those with severe knee issues or poor cardiovascular fitness to attempt the full route.

## On-Site Reflection Photo Guide

### Paid Photographer vs DIY

**Hire the temple photographer (recommended for beginners):**

- Cost: 150,000–350,000 IDR per 30 minutes per group
- Process: Queue up → Wait for your number → Stand in position → Photographer handles phone angle and composition guidance
- Pros: Precise angles, saves time figuring things out, consistent photo quality
- Cons: Long waits during peak season, unclear pricing

**Self-Shoot (Suitable for travelers who already have a concept):**

1. Bring a spare phone or tablet with a large screen (to use as a reflector)
2. Set up the composition on your main phone, and have a companion hold the spare phone horizontally at a low angle in front of you on the ground
3. Turn the screen brightness to maximum, and avoid operating under direct sunlight (morning side lighting works best)
4. Adjust the angle of the 'reflector' phone until the reflection appears in the main camera frame

### Composition Tips

- **Vertical (9:16)**: Center the subject, with the door frame symmetrical on both sides, and the reflection occupying the bottom third of the frame
- **Horizontal (3:2 / 4:3)**: Include more background, with Mount Agung fully in the frame
- Position the subject about 1 meter back from the center of the door frame, so the frame 'frames' Mount Agung behind
- On overcast days when Agung is hidden by clouds, it's not worth forcing the shot—the reflection photo's core is the volcano; without it, the shot loses its meaning

### Post-Processing

On-site reflection photos often look flat and lack contrast. Simple post-processing steps:

- Brighten the shadows (the reflection area)
- Slightly increase blue saturation (to make the sky bluer)
- Crop so the reflection takes up 1/4 to 1/3 of the frame
- Adjustments can be made in Lightroom Mobile or your phone's built-in editor

## A List of Disappointments to Know Before You Go

This isn't meant to scare you off, but to help you set the right expectations—so you don't end up thinking, 'That's it?'

**1. Wait 1–2 hours, shoot for 3 minutes**

During peak season at the Gates of Heaven, 90% of your time is spent queuing for the photographer, not appreciating the temple. If you're short on time or have low patience for lines, seriously consider visiting in the off-season or going early on a weekday.

**2. The reflection is fake (now you know)**

For some, this isn't a problem—a beautiful photo has its own value. But if you care about "authentic experiences," that 30-second "so what?" feeling after snapping the reflection shot might hit harder than expected.

**3. The Gap Between Religious Significance and Tourist Commercialization**

Pura Penataran Agung Lempuyang is an important religious site in Bali, but the area around the Gates of Heaven—queue barriers, a photographer call-out system, souvenir stalls—has become heavily commercialized. If you're seeking a genuine temple pilgrimage atmosphere, head up to the second temple or beyond.

**4. Mount Agung Might Not Cooperate**

During the rainy season or on cloudy days, there's a high chance Mount Agung will be completely obscured. You might queue for an hour only to face a blank white backdrop. It's a matter of luck, and no guide can guarantee otherwise.

**Who Should Skip This:**

- Those just following trends without caring about the result → [Tirta Gangga Water Palace](/en/blog/bali-tirta-gangga-empul/) is a better choice
- Those who get annoyed queuing for over 30 minutes → Visit in the off-season or skip this spot entirely
- Those with zero interest in religious culture, just wanting to snap and go → [Klook day tours](https://affiliate.klook.com/redirect?aid=116349&aff_adid=1277537&k_site=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.klook.com%2Fzh-TW%2Fsearch%2Fresult%2F%3Fquery%3DLempuyang%2520Temple) offer a schedule that suits you better

**Who Will Find It Worthwhile:**

- Want a photo that will make your Taiwan friends ask "Where is this?" → The Gates of Heaven is still Bali's most photogenic spot
- Willing to hike up to the second and third temples → That's where the authentic Balinese temple culture lies
- Staying in Amed or the eastern part of Bali → It's close, takes less time, and can be done in half a day

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: Do I need a ticket for the Gates of Heaven?**

A "temple donation" is required—it's mandatory, around 50,000–75,000 IDR per person. It's officially called a "voluntary donation," but you can't enter without paying. Additionally, the photographer's fee for the reflection shot is extra and not included in the entrance fee.

> Want to budget before you go? **[Bali Ticket Price Overview](/en/tickets/)** lists real-time reference prices for popular attractions across the site, making it easy to compare deals on Klook/Trip.com.

**Q2: Is the reflection photo a real water reflection?**

No. The reflection is created by placing a phone or tablet flat on the ground, screen facing up, and using the screen's reflection to capture the shot. There is no pond or natural water surface in front of the temple that can produce a reflection. Knowing this, you can still choose to take the photo—the image itself still looks great.

**Q3: Do I have to hire the temple photographer?**

To maintain order and efficiency, each group or individual receives a **number tag** upon entering the temple. Staff will call numbers in sequence. When your number is called, you must hand your phone to the temple's official photographer. The photographer works very quickly (usually just a few seconds per person, capturing 3–4 poses), then moves on to the next group.

**Q4: Can I take the reflection photo myself with my phone?**

You can't capture that effect on your own—it has to be done by the temple photographer. There are two practical reasons for this:

1. The photo spot is "restricted"

To keep things orderly, the **prime shooting spot** right in front of the Gates of Heaven (where the temple and Mount Agung behind it align perfectly) is controlled by temple staff.

- Regular visitors **cannot** simply walk over to that specific angle to take photos of their friends.
- Everyone queues up in order. When your number is called, you must hand your phone to the photographer stationed at the spot, who will take the photo for you.

**Q5: How long does it take to get there from Ubud?**

The drive takes about 1.5–2 hours, depending on traffic and departure time. During peak season, it's best to leave Ubud around 05:30–06:00, aiming to arrive at the temple entrance by 07:00–07:30. A full trip including queuing, photos, and rest breaks takes about 5–6 hours from departure to returning to Ubud.

**Q6: How long does it take to get there from Kuta?**

The drive takes about 2.5–3 hours, with a round trip of at least 5–6 hours. From Kuta, it's recommended to book a [private car day tour](/en/blog/bali-private-car-hire-guide/) and visit Tirta Gangga, Amed, or Taman Ujung on the same day to make the long drive worthwhile.

**Q7: Is it suitable for elderly travelers?**

It depends on the elderly person's condition. The walk from the parking lot to the Gate of Heaven (the first temple) takes about 20 minutes, with some uphill stone-paved sections. Those with mobility issues can take a shuttle to a closer drop-off point. The temple entrance itself has few steps and is accessible. However, climbing further up the temple is not recommended for those over 70.

**Q8: Is it suitable for children under 5?**

Children under 5 need to be carried over the stone-paved sections, and the long wait times during peak season can make it uncomfortable for them. If you must bring them, it's best to go on a weekday morning during peak season or visit during the low season to shorten the wait. Kids aged 5 and above who can walk on their own are usually fine.

**Q9: Can I visit during the rainy season?**

Yes. The main issue during the rainy season (November–March) is that Mount Agung is often obscured by clouds, so the reflection photo background may be white mist instead of the volcano. The temple itself is open year-round, and the cultural experience remains unaffected. The rainy season sees fewer crowds and shorter wait times, making it more comfortable than peak season if you simply want to visit the temple.

**Q10: What other attractions can I visit on a day trip?**

The Gate of Heaven is in eastern Bali. On the same day, you can visit (choose 2–3, not all): [Tirta Gangga Water Palace](/en/blog/bali-tirta-gangga-empul/), [Taman Ujung Water Palace](https://youtu.be/zePMt3KSwTo), [Amed Snorkeling](/en/blog/amed-tulamben-diving-guide/), and Besakih Mother Temple (requires an extra 1-hour drive).
