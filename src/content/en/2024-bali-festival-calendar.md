## January

### Day 1: New Year's Day (Indonesian National Holiday)

### **Day 9: Siwa Ratri (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

- January 1st, New Year's Day, is a national holiday recognized worldwide, including in Indonesia and Bali. Celebrations typically peak during the New Year's Eve countdown on December 31st. During this period, Indonesians enjoy a spontaneous "Nataru" holiday—check the festival introduction at the bottom of December for more details.

![跨年倒數是每年元旦的高潮！在峇里島也有很多地方舉辦倒數派對喔！](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_90f97b1bd0d4353fa00d0518a9fab011.png)

The New Year's countdown is the highlight of the year! In Bali, many venues host countdown parties too!

- January 9th marks [Siwa Ratri], one of the significant festivals in the Balinese calendar. Siwa Ratri emphasizes introspection, meditation, and seeking forgiveness. Rituals include staying awake for 36 hours, fasting for 24 hours, and maintaining silence for 12 hours. After this period, purification ceremonies are often held on beaches, while rituals take place at some of the island's major temples.

## February

### **Day 8: Ascension of the Prophet Muhammad (Indonesian National Holiday)**

### **Day 9–15: Chinese New Year Holiday**

### **Feb 28–Mar 9: Galungan Kuningan (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

- February 8, 2024, marks the Ascension of the Prophet Muhammad, an Indonesian national holiday. This is an important Islamic festival commemorating the Prophet Muhammad's ascension to heaven. On this day, Muslims gather at mosques for prayers and worship.
- February 9th to 15th marks our Lunar New Year! Indonesia has designated the first day of the lunar calendar (February 10, 2024) as a national holiday. This year, it coincides with another national holiday on February 8th (Isra' Mi'raj, the Prophet Muhammad's Ascension), so February 9th in between is a flexible holiday. That means friends in Indonesia can enjoy a four-day break during the Lunar New Year (including Sunday, February 11th)! While China's week-long Lunar New Year holiday doesn't directly affect Bali, it's a prime time for many travelers from China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan to take extended vacations, making it a peak season for Bali tourism!

![農曆新年假期大人放長假、小孩放寒假、還可以帶著家中長輩來峇里島避寒喔～](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_641795a1061cfdbbdd2df651788f4be8.png)

During the Lunar New Year holiday, adults get a long break, kids are on winter vacation, and you can even bring elderly family members to Bali to escape the cold!

- February 28th to March 9th: Galungan & Kuningan. This is one of Bali's most important festivals, typically lasting around 10 days, celebrating the creation of the universe and the triumph of good over evil. During this festival, people make offerings to their ancestors at home, seeking their blessings, and hold ceremonies at temples to honor the gods. Galungan & Kuningan also marks the start of the Balinese New Year, according to the Pawukon calendar, and is considered the day of cosmic creation. Schools in Bali close for two weeks during this period, highlighting its significance!

## October

### **3 - 12: Navaratri (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

### **12: Dussehra (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

- Navaratri is a Hindu festival celebrating the victory of Goddess Durga. It typically takes place in September or October each year, lasting nine days, during which people engage in fasting, prayer, flower offerings, and dance. The final day of Navaratri is Dussehra, commemorating Prince Rama's defeat of the demon king Ravana.

## November

### **1: Diwali (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

- Diwali is a Hindu festival celebrating the triumph of light over darkness. It usually occurs in October or November each year, with dates determined by the Hindu lunar calendar. In 2024, Diwali falls on November 1.

## December

### **Day 25: Christmas Day (Indonesian National Holiday)**

### **Day 26: Christmas Holiday (Flexible Leave)**

- December 25 is Christmas Day, a national holiday in Indonesia. For Christians, Christmas is an important celebration, and nearly 10% of Indonesia's population is Christian. Since Christmas and New Year's are close together, many Indonesians go to great lengths to take leave and connect the two holidays into an extended break. This gives them the chance to spend a longer vacation with family and friends, and to travel to other cities or countries. Indonesians have even coined the term "Nataru," meaning "Christmas-New Year holiday," combining "Natal" (Christmas) and "tahun baru" (New Year).
