## January

### Day 1: New Year's Day (Indonesian National Holiday)

### **Day 9: Siwa Ratri (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

- January 1st, New Year's Day, is a public holiday recognized worldwide, including in Indonesia and Bali. Celebrations typically peak with New Year's Eve countdowns on December 31st. During this period, Indonesians enjoy a spontaneous "Nataru" holiday—see the festival introduction at the bottom of December for details.

![跨年倒數是每年元旦的高潮！在峇里島也有很多地方舉辦倒數派對喔！](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_90f97b1bd0d4353fa00d0518a9fab011.png)

The New Year's Eve countdown is the highlight of the year! In Bali, many places host countdown parties too!

- January 9th marks [Siwa Ratri], a significant festival in the Balinese calendar that emphasizes introspection, meditation, and seeking forgiveness. Rituals include staying awake for 36 hours, fasting for 24 hours, and maintaining silence for 12 hours. After this period, purification ceremonies are often held on beaches, while rituals take place at some of the island's major temples.

## February

### **Day 8: Isra' Mi'raj (Prophet Muhammad's Ascension) (Indonesian National Holiday)**

### **Day 9–15: Chinese New Year Holiday**

### **February 28–March 9: Galungan Kuningan (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

- February 8, 2024, marks Isra' Mi'raj, the ascension of the Prophet Muhammad, a national holiday in Indonesia. This important Islamic festival commemorates the Prophet's journey to heaven. On this day, Muslims gather at mosques for prayers and worship.
- From February 9th to 15th, it's our Lunar New Year! Indonesia has designated the first day of the lunar year (February 10, 2024) as a national holiday. This year, February 8th is also a national holiday (Isra' Mi'raj, the Ascension of Prophet Muhammad), so February 9th, falling in between, is a flexible holiday. That means friends in Indonesia can enjoy a four-day break during the Lunar New Year (including Sunday, February 11th)! While the week-long Chinese New Year holiday in China doesn't directly affect Bali, it's a prime time for travelers from China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan to take long vacations, making it a peak season for Bali tourism!

![農曆新年假期大人放長假、小孩放寒假、還可以帶著家中長輩來峇里島避寒喔～](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_641795a1061cfdbbdd2df651788f4be8.png)

During the Lunar New Year holiday, adults get a long break, kids have winter vacation, and you can even bring elderly family members to Bali to escape the cold!

- From February 28th to March 9th, Galungan & Kuningan: This is one of Bali's most important festivals, typically lasting around 10 days, celebrating the creation of the universe and the triumph of good over evil. During this festival, people make offerings to their ancestors at home, seeking their blessings, and hold ceremonies at temples to honor the gods. Galungan & Kuningan also marks the start of the Balinese New Year, based on the Pawukon calendar, and is considered the day of cosmic creation. Schools in Bali close for two weeks during this period, highlighting its significance!

## October

### **3rd - 12th: Navaratri (Bali Hindu Festival)**

### **12th: Dussehra (Bali Hindu Festival)**

- Navaratri is a Hindu festival celebrating the victory of Goddess Durga. It typically falls in September or October and lasts for nine days, during which people engage in fasting, prayers, flower offerings, and dances. The final day of Navaratri is Dussehra, which commemorates Prince Rama's defeat of the demon king Ravana.

## November

### **1st: Diwali (Bali Hindu Festival)**

- Diwali is a Hindu festival celebrating the triumph of light over darkness. It usually takes place in October or November, with the date determined by the Hindu lunar calendar. In 2024, Diwali will be celebrated on November 1st.

## December

### **25th: Christmas (Indonesian National Holiday)**

### **Day 26: Christmas Flexible Leave**

- December 25 is Christmas, a national holiday in Indonesia. For Christians, Christmas is an important festival, and nearly 10% of Indonesia's population is Christian. Since Christmas and New Year's Day are close together, many Indonesians go to great lengths to take leave, connecting the two holidays into an extended break. This gives them the opportunity to spend a longer vacation with family and friends, and to travel to other cities or countries. Indonesians have even coined the term "Nataru," meaning "Christmas-New Year holiday," combining "Natal" (Christmas) and "tahun baru" (New Year).
