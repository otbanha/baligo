# Bali News｜September 3, 2026

## 🌡️ El Niño Still Affecting Bali! This Year's Rainy Season May Be Delayed

Bali's dry season this year could stretch longer than expected.

According to the latest climate analysis, **El Niño continues to influence Indonesia's weather**, potentially delaying Bali's rainy season—some areas may even stay dry through the end of the year.

For travelers, this is something of a double-edged sword.

If you're visiting Bali for a vacation, a longer dry season means more sunny days, which is great for beach outings, hiking, diving, and other outdoor activities.

But on the flip side, **prolonged dry conditions also raise the risk of water shortages and land or forest fires**.

Bali has already seen several land and forest fires recently, so if you're planning to visit mountainous areas, waterfalls, or natural attractions, be sure to keep an eye on the latest weather and fire updates.

***

## 💳 Bali's August Inflation Hits 3.45%! Travel Costs May Gradually Rise

Bali's latest economic data is out.

BPS Bali reports that **Bali's inflation rate reached 3.45% in August 2026**.

While this figure isn't directly tied to 'tourism prices,' it's still worth noting for frequent visitors to Bali.

Because inflation gradually impacts dining, transportation, accommodation, and daily expenses.

Especially since Bali remains one of Indonesia's top international tourist destinations, prices at restaurants, hotels, and services in tourist areas are already sensitive to demand shifts.

So if you find yourself thinking:

**'How come this same restaurant feels pricier than last year?'**

It's not just your imagination.

Of course, the overall inflation rate of 3.45% doesn't mean all travel expenses have risen uniformly by that amount—price changes for different goods and services still vary.

## 🏥 Nusa Ceningan to Get 24-Hour Sea Ambulance Service Starting September 22

This is a highly practical new measure for travelers heading to the Nusa Islands.

The Klungkung government has announced that **starting September 22, 2026, Nusa Ceningan will offer 24-hour sea ambulance services** to handle emergency medical transfers for both residents and tourists on the island.

Nusa Ceningan, Nusa Lembongan, and Nusa Penida have all become extremely popular tourist destinations in recent years, with water sports, diving, snorkeling, and island trips being very common.

But the biggest issue with these islands is:

**Medical resources are not as concentrated as on the main island of Bali.**

In the event of a serious accident, patients may need to be transferred across the sea.

Therefore, the 24-hour sea ambulance service is not just for local residents—it's also a crucial safety net for foreign tourists.

However, please note that this service **only starts on September 22** and is not yet fully operational.

## 💧 Bali's Dry Season Extended, Water Resources Under Scrutiny Again

Another impact of the El Niño phenomenon is on water resources.

Some areas of Bali have experienced a longer dry season this year, and local authorities have begun to focus on **drought and clean water supply issues**.

Current climate forecasts suggest that Bali's rainy season may be delayed, further increasing the importance of water resource management.

This may not be immediately noticeable for short-term tourists, but it is highly significant for local residents, agriculture, and the hotel industry in Bali.

In particular, Bali's tourism sector heavily relies on water resources:

Hotel swimming pools, villas, spas, restaurants, and laundry services all require substantial water usage.

Therefore, if the dry season continues to extend, we may see more places emphasizing **water conservation and resource management** in the coming months.

## 👗 Endek Takes the Stage at Bali Fashion Day! Bali's Traditional Woven Fabric Targets the Global Market

Bali's traditional woven fabric, **Endek**, has recently been drawing renewed attention.

A delegation from the Karangasem Regency Dekranasda participated in **DEKRANASDA Bali Fashion Day 2026**, held at Taman Budaya in Denpasar, with Endek fabric as a key theme.

Officials hope that Endek will not remain confined to the label of 'traditional culture,' but instead evolve into **a creative industry product with commercial value**, introducing Bali's textile culture to a wider international market.

This actually reflects a clear development direction for Bali in recent years:

**Culture → Design → Fashion → Commerce.**

For travelers, if you're looking for souvenirs with a distinctly Balinese character, Endek products offer far more cultural value than typical mass-produced tourist trinkets.
