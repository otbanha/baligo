> The following content is adapted from a travelogue shared by group member [Lin Dinosaur](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1DMYqVwzhq/):

**Bottom Line**: A 7-day, 6-night family trip to Bali during the rainy season (January) is totally doable. The key is to **book a private villa in Ubud as a rainy-day base**, arrange a private driver to plan routes that avoid traffic jams, and pack mosquito repellent and stomach medicine (to prevent Bali belly). You can still enjoy outdoor activities like visiting Tirta Empul Temple and riding ATVs.

From January 11 to 17, 2026, our family of all ages embarked on a joyful Bali family trip. Before departure, many friends warned us about the rainy season and traffic congestion. Now that the trip is over, it's time to share my real experiences and reflections.

First, about the **traffic jams** everyone worries about: **It was much better than expected!** Since we were a large group, we rented a 14-seater minibus. Throughout the trip, we only encountered slow-moving traffic for about two hours when entering a busy tourist area; the rest of the time, we moved smoothly. This taught me that in Bali, **choosing a private car and planning the route wisely** can effectively avoid most traffic nightmares.

![暴雨來襲，趕快開車離開海邊！！經過一些低窪地區，路面都變成河流](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_f4f9f96d6c813ec68ba40960047d0ffa.jpg)

A sudden downpour hit—we quickly drove away from the beach!! Some low-lying areas turned into rivers.

Now, let's talk about the **challenges and surprises of the rainy season**. Yes, we experienced intermittent rain on five out of seven days. But the rainy season isn't all bad—it actually brought some perks: cooler, more comfortable temperatures, lush and fresh greenery, and fewer tourists, so we avoided the crowds. Of course, sudden heavy rain required quick adjustments (like the flood scene in the photo where we 'fled' from the beach—now it's just a funny memory 🤣🤣). Interestingly, the weather forecast seemed unreliable. For instance, in Kuta, we received a **heavy rain warning**, but the two days we were there were mostly **sunny**, with only occasional evening showers. This taught us that in Bali, it's better to stay flexible than to worry about the weather. Our strategy was to always have a **rainy-day backup plan**, like heading to indoor attractions, museums, galleries, or markets, keeping the itinerary adaptable, with the rain becoming a soothing background noise.

> **Quick Reference:** [Bali Rainy Season: What to Do When It Rains in Bali? 60 Rainy-Day Backup Plans](/en/blog/2026-bali-indoor-activities/)

Of course, there were a few things to get used to on this trip: the traffic in the city center isn't very pedestrian-friendly, and crossing the road felt like playing a real-life version of 'Frogger'; the countless zeros on the Indonesian rupiah bills nearly caused my brain's CPU to overheat and smoke trying to do the conversions ♨️

## First, here's our itinerary:

### **Day 1: Arrival in Ubud, a Leisurely Prelude at the Villa**

After a long flight, we landed smoothly in Bali. The customs process was quicker than expected, and after collecting our luggage, we met our pre-booked private driver. As the car left the airport's hustle and bustle, the scenery gradually shifted from modern buildings to lush rice fields and intricate shrines, reminding us that we had truly stepped onto the 'Island of the Gods.'

![第一天：抵達烏布，入住 Villa 的悠閒序幕](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_872fd38d85050291bdc0602b42d36dd9.jpg)

When we arrived at our booked **Villa** in Ubud, the fatigue of the journey was instantly washed away by the view. The villa was nestled in greenery, featuring a private pool and an open-sided bale (gazebo). The kids cheered as soon as they saw the pool, while the adults quickly unloaded the luggage, eager to brew some tea and sit in the gazebo, soaking in Ubud's unique tranquil air. That night, lulled by a natural symphony of insects and frogs, we ended our relaxed first day, recharging for the adventures ahead.

### **Day 4: Exploring the Rustic Villages and Coastal Scenery of the East Coast**

Today, we set our sights on eastern Bali, experiencing a more pristine and tranquil side.

- **Tenganan Pegringsingan Village:** This well-preserved ancient village (Bali Aga) boasts a unique social structure and traditions. The streets are neatly aligned, exuding a quiet and solemn atmosphere. We watched villagers weaving the famous double-sided fabric "Geringsing," a craft of intricate patterns and rustic colors. There's no bustling commercial vibe here, only a profound sense of history—strolling through feels like a journey through time.
- **Tirta Gangga Water Palace:** Next, we visit the former royal water palace. This is a **paradise for photography enthusiasts**! Exquisite ponds, fountains, stone carvings, and water lilies complement each other, and you can spot plump koi fish in the pools. Hopping across the stone stepping stones in the water is a fun experience for both adults and children. The entire garden exudes a Zen-like serenity and coolness, making it utterly captivating.

- **Bukit Asah Desa Bugbug Seaside:** Finally, we head to this hidden seaside gem on the east coast. It's not a sandy beach but a grassy coastal hill (Bukit) overlooking the sea. The view is incredibly expansive, offering a panorama of azure waters and rocky reefs. Many locals and tourists leisurely picnic and fly kites here, creating a relaxed and carefree atmosphere. We quietly wait for the sunset here, experiencing a coastal vibe entirely different from Kuta Beach.

### **Day 5: The Perfect Balance of Speed, Thrills, and Leisurely Shopping**

## **Trip Notes & Practical Tips**

![旅程備忘錄與實用建議](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_2a859b507a199d6500bc5d3d47023c43.png)

Based on our firsthand experience, here are a few tips for future travelers planning a trip:

1. **Currency Exchange:** Locally, **exchanging US dollars for Indonesian Rupiah usually offers a better rate than using New Taiwan Dollars or other currencies.** It's best to bring USD cash and exchange it at a reputable money changer in town. **Quick Reference:** [Indonesia/Bali Travel Currency Exchange Guide: What currency is used in Indonesia/Bali? How to get the best exchange rate? Where to exchange money?](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-guide/)
2. **Internet:** The **DJB physical SIM card** we used had a very stable signal, providing smooth connectivity throughout the trip for navigation, communication, and checking information—very reliable. **Quick Reference:** [【Essential for Bali Travel】How to Get Internet/SIM Card/eSIM in Bali?](/en/blog/bali-sim-card-esim-roaming-guide/)
3. **Shopping & Price Comparison:** **Supermarket prices aren't always cheaper than street vendors,** especially for souvenirs. Vendors often allow haggling, while supermarkets have fixed prices. Compare based on the item type before buying.
4. **Transportation:** For short trips around town or last-minute rides, **ride-hailing apps like Gojek or Grab are very convenient and affordable,** making them a great alternative to hiring a driver. **Quick Reference:** [Grab or Gojek? A Complete Guide to Registering, Using, and Topping Up Gojek and GoPay in Indonesia/Bali](/en/blog/indonesia-gojek-gopay-guide/)
5. **Mosquito & Insect Repellent:** Bali has **plenty of flies and mosquitoes,** especially near fields or in the late afternoon. **Insect repellent is an absolute daily essential**—carry it with you and reapply regularly. **Quick Reference:** [What Essential Medicines/Supplies to Bring for Indonesia/Bali Travel? How to Buy Insect Repellent/Sunscreen?](/en/blog/bali-travel-medicine-checklist/)
6. **Digestive Health (Proven Effective):** To avoid stomach issues, we strictly followed these steps, and no one in our group experienced any digestive discomfort:
7. - **Take one probiotic every morning on an empty stomach.**

- **Use only bottled water for drinking and brushing teeth.**
- Be extra careful when showering to **avoid accidentally swallowing tap water.**
- When eating out, **wipe utensils with alcohol wipes** (especially for children).
- Choose restaurants that **prioritize cleanliness and have good reputations.**
- Always wash hands with **soap or hand sanitizer** before meals.
