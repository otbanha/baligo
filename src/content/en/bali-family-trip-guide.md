> The following content is adapted from a travelogue shared by group member [Lin Dinosaur](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1DMYqVwzhq/):

**Bottom Line First**: A 7-day, 6-night family trip to Bali during the rainy season (January) is totally doable. The key is to **book a private villa in Ubud as a rainy-day base**, arrange a private driver to plan routes that avoid traffic, and pack mosquito repellent and stomach medicine (to prevent Bali belly). You can still enjoy outdoor activities like the Tirta Empul Temple experience and ATV rides.

From January 11 to 17, 2026, our family of all ages embarked on a joyful Bali family trip. Before we left, many friends warned us about the rainy season and traffic jams. Now that the trip is over, it's time to share my real experiences and reflections.

First, about the **traffic issue** everyone's worried about: my conclusion is **it's much better than expected!** Since we were a large group, we rented a 14-seater minibus. Throughout the trip, we only hit slow-moving traffic for about two hours when entering tourist-heavy areas; the rest of the time, we moved smoothly. This taught me that in Bali, **choosing a private driver and planning a smart route** can effectively avoid most traffic nightmares.

![暴雨來襲，趕快開車離開海邊！！經過一些低窪地區，路面都變成河流](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_f4f9f96d6c813ec68ba40960047d0ffa.jpg)

Heavy rain hit, so we quickly drove away from the beach!! Some low-lying areas turned into rivers.

Now, let's talk about the **challenges and surprises of the rainy season**. Yes, we experienced intermittent rain on five out of seven days. But the rainy season isn't all bad—it actually brought some perks: cooler, more comfortable temperatures, lush and fresh greenery, and fewer tourists, so no crowds. Of course, sudden downpours require quick adjustments (like the photo of us 'fleeing' from the beach during a flood—now it's just a funny memory 🤣🤣). Interestingly, weather forecasts seemed unreliable. For instance, in Kuta, we got a **heavy rain warning**, but the two days we were there were mostly **sunny**, with only occasional evening showers. This taught us that in Bali, it's better to stay flexible than to worry about the weather. Our strategy was to always have a **rainy-day backup plan**, like heading to indoor attractions, museums, galleries, or markets, keeping the itinerary adaptable. The sound of rain became a soothing backdrop to our journey.

Of course, there were a few things to get used to on this trip: the traffic in the city center isn't very pedestrian-friendly, crossing the road feels like playing a real-life version of 'Frogger'; and the countless zeros on Indonesian rupiah bills nearly caused my brain's CPU to overheat from all the mental conversions ♨️

## Here's a look at our itinerary:

### **Day 1: Arrival in Ubud, a Leisurely Start at the Villa**

After a long flight, we landed smoothly in Bali. The customs process was quicker than expected, and after picking up our luggage, we met our pre-booked private driver. As the car left the airport's hustle and bustle, the scenery gradually shifted from modern buildings to lush rice fields and intricate shrines, reminding us that we had truly stepped onto the 'Island of the Gods.'

![第一天：抵達烏布，入住 Villa 的悠閒序幕](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_872fd38d85050291bdc0602b42d36dd9.jpg)

When we arrived at our booked **Villa** in Ubud, the fatigue of the journey instantly melted away at the sight before us. The villa was tucked away in a sea of greenery, featuring a private pool and an open-sided bale (gazebo). The kids cheered the moment they saw the pool, while the adults busied themselves unpacking, eager to brew some tea and sit in the pavilion, soaking in Ubud's unique tranquil air. That night, lulled by a natural symphony of crickets and frogs, we ended our first day in relaxed comfort, recharging for the adventures ahead.

Watching local devotees and international tourists line up in the purification pools to bathe and pray, their expressions focused and devout, brought a sense of peace to our hearts. The kids were curious about this 'lining up in the water' ritual, and we explained its religious significance—it was a wonderful cultural experience.

  This restaurant had a charming atmosphere, with dishes blending local flavors and modern cooking. We ordered a few signature items, and everyone enjoyed them thoroughly—a great pit stop to refuel.
- **Rotu Isyona Beauty Spa:** After a long day, the evening spa session was pure bliss. The masseuses were skilled, with just the right pressure, completely relaxing our tense muscles. Surrounded by the scent of essential oils, we nearly drifted off to sleep. Bali's **high-value massages are truly an indispensable daily ritual of happiness.**
- **Delta Supermarket:** Feeling refreshed after the massage, we headed to Delta Supermarket for some shopping. The store had a wide selection, from fresh produce and snacks to daily essentials. We bought some fruit, drinks, and a few packs of famous Indonesian instant noodles to prepare for our 'self-catering' evening.
- **Dinner: Instant Noodles at Our Villa**
  It might sound simple, but cooking instant noodles together in the villa's kitchen, adding eggs and vegetables, and sitting around the table chatting about the day's adventures—**this unplanned, everyday moment became one of the warmest and most unforgettable parts of the trip.** Relaxed, carefree, and stress-free, it was the perfect end to a fulfilling day.

### **Day 3: Between Nature's Spirit and the Bustling Streets**

Today's itinerary was full of contrasts and surprises, from forests to rice terraces, ancient trees to palaces, revealing Bali's many faces.

- **Sangeh Monkey Forest:** We entered this monkey forest with a mix of excitement and apprehension, having heard that the monkeys at **Ubud Monkey Forest** are completely unafraid of people, darting, chasing, and even trying to snatch food or plastic bags that are 'too obvious.' We firmly remembered the rule: 'no eating, no staring, no touching,' observing their behavior from a distance. The kids were both nervous and thrilled. Luckily, the monkeys here were much tamer! Still, it was a vivid lesson in respecting wildlife.

### **Day 4: Exploring the Eastern Coast's Rustic Villages and Coastal Scenery**

Today, we set our sights on eastern Bali, experiencing a more raw and tranquil side.

- **Tenganan Pegringsingan Ancient Village:** This well-preserved ancient village (Bali Aga) boasts a unique social structure and traditions. The streets are orderly, quiet, and solemn. We watched villagers weaving the famous double-sided fabric 'Geringsing,' a complex craft with rustic colors. There's no commercial hustle here, only a profound sense of history—strolling through feels like a journey through time.
- **Tirta Gangga Water Palace:** Next, we visited the former royal water palace. This is a **photographer's paradise**! Exquisite ponds, fountains, stone carvings, and water lilies complement each other, and the ponds are home to plump koi fish. Hopping across the stone stepping stones in the water is a fun experience for both adults and kids. The entire garden exudes zen and a refreshing coolness, making it utterly charming.

- **Bukit Asah Desa Bugbug Coastline:** Finally, we headed to this hidden coastal gem on the east coast. It's not a sandy beach but a grassy seaside hill (Bukit) with expansive views overlooking the blue sea and rocky reefs. Many locals and tourists picnic and fly kites here, creating a relaxed atmosphere. We quietly waited for the sunset here, experiencing a coastal vibe completely different from Kuta Beach.

### **Day 5: The Perfect Balance of Speed, Thrills, and Leisurely Shopping**

On the fifth day of the trip, we arranged the activity the younger members were most looking forward to.

- **ATV Off-Road Experience:** This was the day's highlight! We headed to the ATV track, and after a brief briefing from the instructor, we rode into the mountains, through fields, and along muddy trails. Crossing streams, climbing slopes, and diving down hills, the natural scenery along the way was incredibly pristine and beautiful. **For young people who love excitement and adventure, this is absolutely a 'highly recommended' activity!** Though we ended up covered in mud, the feeling of speeding freely and laughing out loud became one of the most vivid memories of the trip.

Based on our firsthand experience, we've compiled the following tips for those planning a trip in the future:

1. **Currency Exchange:** Locally, **exchanging US dollars for Indonesian Rupiah usually offers a better rate than using New Taiwan Dollars or other currencies.** It's recommended to bring USD cash and exchange it at a reputable money changer in the city. **Quick Reference:** [Indonesia/Bali Travel Currency Exchange Guide: What currency is used in Indonesia/Bali? How to get the best rate? Where to exchange?](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-guide/)
2. **Internet:** The **DJB physical SIM card** we used had a very stable signal, with smooth connectivity throughout the trip—perfect for navigation, communication, and checking information. **Quick Reference:** [【Essential for Bali Travel】How to Get Internet/SIM Card/eSIM in Bali?](/en/blog/bali-sim-card-esim-roaming-guide/)
3. **Shopping Price Comparison:** **Supermarket prices aren't always cheaper than street vendors**, especially for souvenirs. Vendors often allow bargaining, while supermarkets have fixed prices. It's best to compare based on the item type before buying.
4. **Transportation:** For short trips in the city or last-minute rides, **ride-hailing apps like Gojek or Grab are very convenient and affordable**, making them a great alternative to hiring a driver. **Quick Reference:** [Grab or Gojek? Comprehensive Guide to Registering, Using, and Topping Up Gojek and GoPay in Indonesia/Bali](/en/blog/indonesia-gojek-gopay-guide/)
5. **Mosquito and Insect Repellent:** Bali has **quite a few flies and mosquitoes**, especially near fields or in the evening. **Insect repellent is an absolute daily essential**—make sure to carry it with you and reapply regularly. **Quick Reference:** [Essential Medications and Supplies for Bali Travel: How to Buy Insect Repellent and Sunscreen?](/en/blog/bali-travel-medicine-checklist/)
6. **Digestive Health (Tested and Effective):** To avoid stomach issues, we strictly followed these steps, and no one in our group experienced digestive discomfort:

- Whether in villages or hotels, you'll always see people quietly sitting in **bale (gazebos)**, embodying the unique Balinese sense of leisure and calm.
- The **genuine and warm smiles** of the locals brightened every interaction, from asking for directions to casual chats.
- Despite years of tourism development, Bali's core spirit—that **sense of devotion and tranquility**—seems frozen in time, just as I remember it from two or three decades ago.
- The unforgettable local cuisine, from crispy **bebek betutu (Balinese fried duck)** to the wok-fried **Indonesian fried noodles**, tantalized our taste buds every day.
- **Enjoying daily massages at incredibly affordable prices** melted away all travel fatigue with essential oils and skilled techniques.
