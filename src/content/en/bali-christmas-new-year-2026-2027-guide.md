### Nataru: Indonesians Also Celebrate Christmas and New Year

This period is called Nataru in Indonesia, a blend of Natal (Christmas) and Tahun Baru (New Year). It's not an official public holiday but refers to the entire year-end break, and it's the busiest time for domestic travel across Indonesia.

The official dates for 2026–2027 are as follows:

- 12/24 (Thu): Joint Christmas Leave (cuti bersama)
- 12/25 (Fri): Christmas Day, national holiday
- 12/26–27: Weekend, so the Christmas break totals 4 days
- 1/1 (Fri): New Year's Day, national holiday, with the weekend giving a 3-day break
- 12/28–31: Not public holidays, so annual leave is needed
- 1/5 (Tue): Isra Mikraj, national holiday

The joint day off on 12/24 applies to civil servants; private companies decide for themselves. If Indonesian office workers take four days of annual leave from 12/28–31, they can enjoy an 11-day break from 12/24 to 1/3, so many people flock to Bali.

## 4. Free Fireworks Viewing

Jimbaran offers the widest view; Seminyak, Legian, Kuta, Berawa, Batu Bolong, and Melasti are also good spots. Public beaches fill up quickly, so arrive before 11 PM. Sanur is quieter and family-friendly; Kuta is the liveliest and most crowded.

## 5. How to Plan for Christmas

Most festive menus are concentrated on December 24 dinner and December 25 brunch. Standalone Christmas dinner options are rarer, so book December 24 evening or December 25 lunch for a big feast.

## 6. Three Must-Know Pitfalls

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-11/1794583620288-15636.jpg)

**1. Mandatory New Year's Eve Dinner/Entertainment Surcharge**
Some high-end resorts add mandatory charges for December 31 stays. For example, The Mulia listed a mandatory New Year's Eve entertainment surcharge for the 2025 holiday period. Always read the terms before booking.

**2. Minimum Night Stays**
According to travel platforms, during the Christmas-to-New Year peak season, luxury resorts often require a minimum stay of 5–7 nights. Policies vary by hotel, so early booking gives you more options.

**3. Moving Between Areas Is Nearly Impossible**
The worst traffic is in Canggu, Seminyak, Kuta, Legian, and Uluwatu, especially around midnight. Estimated travel times on maps will be inaccurate, and you may have to walk the last stretch. Don't plan to have dinner in Seminyak, watch fireworks in Canggu, and then head to Uluwatu for after-parties.
