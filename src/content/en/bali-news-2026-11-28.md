## **🌧️ BMKG Warns of Extreme Weather in Bali from November 27 to 30! Southern Waves Up to 2.5 Meters**

Bali's rainy season has entered a phase of more pronounced rainfall. The BMKG's Third Regional Meteorological Center issued a weather warning on November 26, alerting that from November 27 to 30, Bali may experience moderate to extremely heavy rain accompanied by strong winds.

Meteorological authorities note that recent changes in atmospheric circulation, including air convergence, the Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO), and Equatorial Rossby Waves, could increase rainfall intensity.

## **🏨 Bali Discusses New Quality Tourism Regulations! Service Standards and Price Management for Villas and Guesthouses Under Review**

On November 27, the Bali Provincial Parliament (DPRD Bali) and the provincial government further discussed the Bali Quality Tourism Business Management Regulation (Raperda Tata Kelola Usaha Pariwisata Bali Berkualitas).

This regulation aims to shift Bali from a focus on tourist numbers toward a model that prioritizes service quality, legal operations, and sustainable tourism development.

However, the regulation is still in the discussion and coordination phase, and has not yet been officially passed.

**Key Points Under Discussion:**

**① Standards for Tourist Accommodation and Service Quality**

The provincial council aims to establish minimum service quality standards, providing clearer service requirements for hotels, villas, guesthouses, and other tourism operators.

**② Preventing Unfair Low-Price Competition**

Discussions include restricting operators from selling services below cost to prevent a price war in the tourism market.

The specific system for minimum pricing is still under discussion with the Indonesian Competition Commission (KPPU), and no minimum room rates for all hotels or villas have been decided yet.

**③ Establishing a Comprehensive Database of Accommodation and Tourism Operators Across the Island**

The Bali Tourism Board hopes to create an integrated database covering tourism operators across different regencies, districts, and villages, updated every 3 to 6 months.

The discussions also propose completing an inventory of the number of villas and rooms across the island within three months to grasp the actual accommodation supply.

**④ Future Research on Tourist Numbers and Carrying Capacity Management**

Council members also suggested that future management of tourist numbers should be studied based on the carrying capacity of roads, public facilities, and the environment.

These are still under regulatory review and should not be considered as an already-implemented tourist quota system.

When is it expected to be completed?

The Provincial Council and Provincial Government hope to complete further coordination and regulatory procedures by December 25, 2026, but this still requires technical consultations with ministries and integration of provisions.

What impact will this have on travelers?

Currently, there are no immediate price hikes on accommodations, restrictions on villa bookings, or changes to entry regulations for tourists due to this draft.

However, if service standards, business registration, and price controls are formally implemented in the future, Bali's accommodation market may gradually see changes.

For travelers planning long-term stays or booking private villas, in addition to comparing prices, it will be worth paying more attention to whether the accommodation is legally registered and whether the services provided meet the standards.

## 🏛️ Denpasar's Gajah Mada Heritage District Gets a Parking Overhaul! Pasar Badung Square Improvements Underway

If you've ever visited Denpasar's Pasar Badung traditional market, you may have noticed ongoing efforts to tidy up the surrounding roads and heritage district in recent years.

On November 27, ANTARA reported that the Denpasar City Government, through the Pasar Sewakadarma public market management company, is improving the environment around Pasar Badung and the Gajah Mada Heritage district.

The goal of this overhaul is to gradually transform the Gajah Mada district into a more walkable and tourist-friendly historical and cultural area, while relocating parking spaces to designated lots nearby.

**Currently planned parking locations include:**

- Parking area around Plaza Pasar Badung
- Pasar Badung Basement Levels 1 & 2 Parking
- Pasar Kumbasari Parking
- Graha Parkir Pasar Lokitasari Parking

Two additional parking facilities are planned for the future:

- Adding a mezzanine parking space for motorcycles at Graha Yowana Suci.
- Constructing a multi-story parking lot at Batan Moning, with plans for EV charging stations and a coffee shop.

**Pasar Badung Square will also be improved simultaneously:**

- Revamping the square's plants and gardens.
- Improve nighttime lighting.
- Repair the basement electrical equipment damaged by previous flooding.
- Adjust the overall landscape of the square and surrounding traditional buildings.

This plan does not announce a full ban on cars along Gajah Mada Road, but aims to reduce street parking in the historic district, making the main tourist areas more organized.

**What impact will this have on travelers?**

If you plan a half-day trip to Denpasar, include Pasar Badung and Pasar Kumbasari in your itinerary to experience Bali's traditional markets and local life.

However, parking arrangements in the area are being adjusted. If you drive or hire a car, it's best to confirm available parking lots in advance—don't expect to park right by the market entrance.

I think this direction is promising. Beyond the markets, Denpasar has many historic buildings and cultural attractions. If the walking environment improves, it could develop into a more complete urban cultural tourism route in the future.
