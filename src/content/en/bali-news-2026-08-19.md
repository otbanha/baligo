# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup | August 19, 2026

## 🏔️ Chinese Tourist Falls While Taking Photos on Padar Island Summit, Unconscious and Hospitalized

On August 18, a Chinese tourist slipped and fell while climbing to the summit of **Padar Island in Komodo National Park** to take photos, losing consciousness.

A joint SAR team arrived at the scene by midday, then used a RIB speedboat to transport the injured tourist to Labuan Bajo Marina, where an ambulance took them to Siloam Labuan Bajo Hospital for treatment.

This incident serves as a reminder for travelers heading to Padar Island: **The summit photo area has steep terrain—never step beyond the safety zone for a photo.**

Especially since Flores just experienced a 7.7-magnitude earthquake, visitors to Labuan Bajo and Komodo should stay updated on the latest safety advisories.

## 🌋 After the Flores Earthquake, Bali Remains Safe for Tourism

According to the latest report from The Bali Sun, while Indonesia mourns the victims of the 7.7-magnitude Flores earthquake, **Bali Island itself is still considered safe for normal travel**.

The main part that needs reassessment is the itinerary to East Nusa Tenggara (NTT), including Flores, Labuan Bajo, and other areas.

This is especially important for travelers from Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Malaysia:

**Bali ≠ Flores.**

If your itinerary only covers Bali island spots like Kuta, Seminyak, Ubud, Sanur, Uluwatu, and Nusa Dua, there's currently no need to cancel your Bali trip because of the Flores earthquake.

However, if you've planned a 'Bali + Labuan Bajo + Komodo' trip, you should reconfirm flights, accommodations, and sea activities before departure.

The Bali provincial government has also provided aid to the NTT disaster area, with an initial allocation of **IDR 150 million** for purchasing and transporting relief supplies.

## 🇪🇺 European Travelers Remain a Key Source for Bali, Indonesia Becomes Asia's Second Most Popular Destination

Bali continues to be a major gateway and popular destination for European travelers visiting Indonesia.

According to a recent report from Tribun Bali, Bali remains an important destination attracting European tourists, while Indonesia as a whole maintains strong momentum in the Asian travel market.

This is a significant signal for Bali's tourism market: **Even with issues like traffic, environment, and accommodation management, Bali's appeal to international travelers remains strong.**

For travelers, this means popular areas like Seminyak, Canggu, Ubud, and Uluwatu may still see high crowds and accommodation demand during peak season.

## ✈️ Heads Up! International Flights to Bali May Soon Remind You to Pay the Tourist Levy Before Takeoff

This is definitely worth including in your morning briefing.

The Bali provincial government has reached an agreement with airlines, **starting from the end of August, international flights to Bali may broadcast announcements before takeoff, reminding foreign travelers to pay the Bali Tourist Levy.**

Currently, the Bali Tourist Levy remains at **150,000 Indonesian Rupiah per person**, and it's paid once per trip. This fee has been in effect since February 14, 2024.

Travelers can pay in advance via the **Love Bali** website or app, or upon arrival at designated locations like Ngurah Rai Airport or Benoa Port.

More notably, Bali government statistics show that as of August 2026, the tourist levy payment rate has risen to **43%**, with approximately **235 billion Indonesian Rupiah** collected in the first eight months of this year.

So if you hear an announcement saying 'Please pay the Bali Tourist Levy' before your flight, it's not a new fee at the airport—it's a reminder of the existing Bali Tourist Levy.

## 🏝️ Bali Tourist Levy Collection Rate Still Below Half, Government Turns to Airlines for 'Direct Reminders'

This is actually a more noteworthy figure behind the previous news story.

In 2025, the actual payment rate for the Bali tourism tax was only **35.4%**, and although it rose to **43%** by August 2026, it still means over half of foreign visitors haven't completed payment.

As a result, the Bali provincial government is extending its outreach to travelers **before departure**.

In the future, travelers might even hear reminders **on the plane flying to Bali**, rather than only discovering they need to pay upon arrival at Ngurah Rai Airport.

My advice is simple:

**Pay it before you leave.**

IDR 150,000 isn't a huge fee, but handling it in advance saves you the hassle of finding a counter, queuing, or forgetting to pay when you land at the airport.
