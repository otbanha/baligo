## 🗞️ Today's Highlights: Strong Recovery of International Events and Low-Carbon Tourism

- **'Oceanman Bali 2026' World Open Water Swim Officially Returns**:
After a two-year hiatus, the highly anticipated **Oceanman Bali 2026** international open water swim event, beloved by water sports enthusiasts worldwide, is officially set to take place from **June 19 to 21** at Kedonganan Beach in Jimbaran. With the theme 'Waves of Change,' the event has attracted over 900 top athletes and enthusiasts from 27 countries and regions, including Indonesia, Malaysia, Australia, Europe, and the Americas—a 30% surge in participants compared to previous years. The competition spans six categories, from a 500-meter children's race to the challenging 10-kilometer main event, promising to inject significant sports tourism benefits into the Jimbaran coastline.
- **From 'Consumer Tourism' to 'Contributive Tourism': A Green Transformation**:
Amid growing reflection on overtourism and environmental carrying capacity across the island, Bali's tourism industry is undergoing a fundamental shift toward an 'Eco-Tourism Future.' Leading hospitality players and local environmental groups are pivoting from a focus on 'maximizing visitor numbers and pure consumption' to 'driving long-term environmental contributions.' An increasing number of upscale villas and resorts are adopting rainwater harvesting, zero-plastic commitments, and community co-prosperity initiatives, attracting eco-conscious high-net-worth travelers to participate in local tree planting and coral restoration, turning tourism into a tangible force for protecting local ecosystems.

## 📄 Topics of Interest to Travelers: Curated Slow Travel, Art Exhibitions, and Community Initiatives

- **Nusa Dua Promotes Premium 'Wellcation' Long-Stay Retreats**:
To cater to international high-end professionals and slow travelers, luxury five-star resorts in the Nusa Dua special zone, such as Grand Hyatt Bali, have recently launched customized 'Long Stay Wellcation' experiences designed for modern needs. This model combines high-privacy ocean-view long-stay accommodations, local wellness and meditation therapies, healthy meal management, and cultural craft experiences, offering a refined sanctuary for long-term visitors seeking 'balance, recharge, and deep relaxation' away from the hustle and bustle.
- **Deep Cultural Immersion: Slow Travel Through Ubud's 'Timeless Elegance' Artisan Villages**
Beyond sun and surf, Bali's vibrant **traditional wood carving, painting, and weaving crafts** are becoming a core exploration focus for in-depth travelers this May. Along the Wos River in Ubud and its surrounding traditional villages, the Ubud Open Studios have recently launched immersive workshops led by local artisans. These allow foreign visitors to step into the craftsmen's homes and personally experience the centuries-old island soul and philosophy behind 'every chisel stroke and every brush of color,' making it a popular intellectual slow-travel route.
- **Tourism Revenue Giving Back: Annual CSR Initiatives Launch in Sanur and South Bali**
This week, prominent international hotel groups on the island—such as the Prime Plaza Hotel Sanur and the Marriott Business Council—have begun rolling out their major **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and charity fundraising programs** for 2026. Through events like charity golf tournaments and community benefit dinners, tourism revenue is being channeled into supporting local orphanages, underprivileged families, and grassroots social welfare foundations. This initiative not only warms the local community but also allows international travelers to witness the positive impact of the tourism industry on Balinese society while enjoying the island's beauty.
