# Bali Tourist Hot Topics + News Roundup｜August 29, 2026

## 🌅 Tanah Lot Art & Food Festival Officially Opens! Event Runs Through August 30

If you're in Bali tomorrow, you can still catch this event.

**Tanah Lot Art and Food Festival VII** officially opened on August 28 at Tanah Lot Temple and will run through **August 30**—a three-day affair.

This year's event features two main themes: **"Sunset in Paradise"** and **"Ulam Carik"**, offering not only traditional Balinese art performances but also local cuisine, handicrafts, and UMKM stalls.

The highlight is the **Gebogan and Baleganjur cultural parade** during sunset, where visitors can enjoy traditional music and offering processions while watching the sun go down.

Organizers aim to attract around **10,000 visitors** over the three days.

If you already have a **Tanah Lot + Canggu** itinerary planned for tomorrow, I'd say it's worth fitting this in—it adds an extra experience beyond just visiting the temple and leaving.

## 🛂 Bali Requires Hotels and Villas to Proactively Report Foreign Guest Data

Indonesia's Directorate General of Immigration has mandated that **hotels, guesthouses, villas, and other accommodations** in Bali actively use the APOA (Aplikasi Pelaporan Orang Asing) system to register foreign guest information.

This system directly integrates accommodation-submitted foreigner data into the immigration database, allowing authorities to more effectively monitor the stays and activities of foreign nationals in Bali.

Bali's immigration units have recently stepped up inspections of foreign nationals, emphasizing zero tolerance for violations of Indonesian laws and social norms.

For regular travelers, **this is not a new visa regulation** nor something that requires personal action.

When checking into a legitimate hotel, guesthouse, or villa, providing passport details as required by law is a standard procedure for accommodation and immigration management.

## 📈 Bali Welcomed 3.92 Million Foreign Tourists in the First Seven Months of 2026, Targeting 7 Million for the Year

The latest Bali tourism data is out.

As of the end of July 2026, Bali has received a cumulative total of **3,923,824 foreign tourists** this year. The Bali Provincial Tourism Office maintains its annual target of **7 million foreign tourists**.

In addition to international tourists, Bali also welcomed approximately **5.85 million domestic visitors** from January to July this year, bringing the total to nearly **9.8 million trips**.

According to the tourism board, the number of foreign visitors this year has remained largely stable compared to the same period in 2025, with a gradual increase starting in May.

Bali is now also looking to attract travelers through **sports tourism** and more athletic events, such as marathons and other large-scale activities.

In other words, if you feel like Bali is still crowded this year, it's not your imagination—**visitor numbers are indeed still very high**.

## 🏥 Indonesia's Ministry of Health Extends Emergency Medical Response in Flores Until September 12

Although this earthquake did not occur in Bali, it's worth noting for travelers planning a **Bali + Labuan Bajo / Flores** itinerary.

Indonesia's Ministry of Health has announced a two-week extension of the emergency medical response period following the Flores earthquake, now lasting until **September 12, 2026**, instead of the original August 28 deadline.

The ministry stated that medical support and health-related services are still being provided.

This also indicates that post-disaster recovery efforts in the Flores region are still ongoing.

If you have plans to travel from Bali to **Labuan Bajo, Komodo, or other parts of Flores** in the near future, it's advisable to double-check local transportation, attraction openings, and the latest updates from travel operators before departure.

## 🚨 Bali Strengthens Disaster Response Training, Launches Professional Certification

Bali Province's BPBD has recently begun enhancing the professional skills of disaster response personnel by holding a **Disaster Response Personnel Competency Certification Exam**.

Officials stated that with climate change and increasing disaster risks, response personnel must not only possess theoretical knowledge but also be able to quickly assess, coordinate, and take action during actual disasters.

While this measure is not a new regulation directly targeting tourists, it is highly significant for Bali.

After all, besides natural disasters like earthquakes, volcanoes, and floods, Bali also faces issues such as forest fires during the dry season.

Officials hope that through professional certification, Bali's overall disaster response capabilities will be improved.
