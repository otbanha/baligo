# Key Topics for Bali Travelers + News Roundup (August 11, 2026)

## 🔥 Mount Bromo Wildfire Expands, Tourist Zone Fully Closed

https://youtube.com/shorts/p_N64qSNLNI

The wildfire in East Java's Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park continues to spread, with a single day's burn area expanding to about 250 hectares. Reports indicate the fire is still spreading, leading to a full closure of the scenic area and the use of water cannons to assist in firefighting. While Mount Bromo is not in Bali, this news is crucial if your itinerary includes a 'Bali + Surabaya/Bromo' combo.

Traveler Advice: If you plan to visit Mount Bromo soon, be sure to check the latest opening status and accessible routes of the national park. Do not follow your old itinerary blindly.

## 🩺 Suspected Rabies Death of a Boy in North Bali; Pet Owners and Those Bitten Should Be Vigilant

The Bali Times reports that a boy in northern Bali passed away after showing symptoms consistent with rabies. The hospital initially identified it as a suspected rabies case, which has since been referred for further handling. Such incidents remind travelers not to take dog bites or scratches lightly while in Bali.

Traveler Advice: Untreated rabies has a near 100% fatality rate! If bitten or scratched by an animal, immediately clean the wound and seek medical attention as soon as possible. Do not wait for symptoms to appear before taking action.

## 💧 Bali's Ban on Plastic Bottles Under 1 Liter Confirmed, Travelers Must Adjust Water Buying Habits

Bali Live reports that Bali's ban on plastic water bottles under 1 liter remains in effect after a court challenge, meaning the restriction on small bottled water on the island won't be overturned by litigation. This regulation continues Bali's recent plastic reduction policies and will impact how hotels, restaurants, and shops supply water.

Traveler tip: When visiting Bali, it's best to bring a reusable water bottle or refill with larger containers available at hotels and restaurants.

## 🌊 Six Coastal Areas in Bali May Experience King Tides from August 11–17

ANTARA reports that BMKG has warned six coastal areas in Bali to watch for rising tides and potential coastal flooding from August 11 to 17, including southern Gianyar, Tabanan, Badung, Denpasar, and South Klungkung. Officials explain this is linked to the moon's perigee and tidal changes around the new moon.

Traveler tip: If you're heading to the beach, port, or taking a speedboat out, be sure to check BMKG and boat company announcements before departing to avoid unexpected delays or changing sea conditions.

## 📱 Indonesia Tests QRIS Payment Interoperability with Saudi Arabia and India, Bali's International Audience Continues to Grow

ANTARA reports that Indonesia is testing cross-border payment links between QRIS and markets like Saudi Arabia and India. Meanwhile, Bali still welcomed 3.2 million international visitors in the first half of this year, with Indian tourist numbers also on the rise. This shows that Bali's tourism remains strong, and its digital payments are becoming increasingly internationalized.

Travel tip: If you frequently use digital payments, keep an eye out for QRIS usage scenarios when you're in Bali; for merchants, this payment method is becoming increasingly common.
