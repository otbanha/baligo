When planning a trip to Bali, exchanging currency is an important step that requires careful consideration. As part of Indonesia, Bali exclusively uses the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR) as its transaction currency. In this article, we'll explore how to get the best exchange rates and recommend some trustworthy exchange shops.

## How to Exchange Money

**Exchange to US Dollars Before You Go**: First and foremost, convert your local currency to US dollars before departure, then bring those dollars to Indonesia/Bali to exchange for Indonesian Rupiah. This method offers the best exchange rates. If you hold Australian dollars, simply bring them to Bali to exchange for Rupiah. Since Australia has long been a major source of tourists to Bali, the rate is comparable to the US dollar.

Why is converting to US dollars first and then to Rupiah better? See the explanation [here](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-tips/)

Most visitors handle currency exchange upon arriving in Bali. You can ask a trusted driver to take you to a reputable Money Changer they know—this is the best way to avoid scams!

If you're choosing your own exchange method, here are a few options:

- **Bank Exchange**: Bringing US dollars to a local bank is a safe and reliable option. Banks offer relatively stable rates with low fees. Major cities and tourist areas in Bali have bank branches for convenient exchange.
- **Money Changer**: Bali's tourist areas have many dedicated money changers offering foreign currency exchange. When choosing one, make sure to pick a reputable shop with favorable rates, and watch out for any extra fees or commissions.
- **_ATM Withdrawals_: Bali has an extensive ATM network where you can withdraw cash using international credit or debit cards. This is convenient and usually uses the real-time exchange rate (close to the interbank rate). However, the _drawbacks are:_**

1. Taiwanese banks typically charge **overseas withdrawal fees**: about USD$2–5 per transaction
2. Plus a **1.5%–2% foreign transaction fee** (varies by card issuer)
3. Local ATMs in Bali sometimes add an **ATM usage fee** (around IDR 50,000–75,000 per transaction)
4. Some ATMs have withdrawal limits, so multiple withdrawals stack up fees
   It's best to withdraw a larger amount at once to reduce fees, check your bank's overseas withdrawal charges before you go, and choose ATMs from major banks like BCA or Mandiri for reliability.

### **Indonesia/Bali Currency Exchange FAQ**

## **1. What currency is used in Bali?**

As part of Indonesia, Bali uses the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Only Rupiah is accepted for transactions, so it's best to have cash ready in advance.

## **2. Should I bring US dollars to Bali?**

Yes, it's recommended to exchange your local currency into US dollars before departure, as the exchange rate for USD in Bali is often favorable. If you have Australian dollars, you can also bring them directly to Bali for exchange, as the AUD rate is quite good too. Reference: [Indonesia/Bali Currency Exchange: Direct Exchange vs. Exchanging to USD First, Which Is Better?](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-tips/)

## **3. Where’s the Best Place to Exchange Money?**

- **Money Changer:** Choose a reputable one—they usually offer better rates with little to no fees.
- **Bank:** Banks offer stable rates and are secure, but you might have to wait in line.
- **ATM Withdrawal:** Convenient, but may come with fees. It’s best to use a card that waives overseas transaction fees.

Avoid exchanging money at the airport, as the rates are typically poor.

## **4. How to Spot a Trustworthy Money Changer?**

- Look for authorized money changers with a green shield-shaped **PVA Berizin** sign.
- Check Google reviews—choose places with high ratings and plenty of feedback.
- Steer clear of shops offering rates that seem too good to be true—they’re almost certainly scams.
- **No commission** is another sign of a legitimate operator. Before exchanging, ask, "Any commission?" If they say yes, move on to another place.
- Our themed map lists "Authorized Money Changers" licensed by **Bank Indonesia**. Check it out [here](/map/money-changer/).

## **5. What should you watch out for when exchanging money?**

- Count your cash on the spot and make sure the amount is correct before leaving.
- Avoid exchanging money at night or in secluded areas.
- Watch out for any extra fees or commissions.
- Keep your exchange receipt for future reference.

## **6. Can I use credit cards or mobile payments?**

Yes, many shops, restaurants, and hotels in Bali accept credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) and mobile payments (such as GoPay, OVO). However, cash is still the primary payment method at small stalls or in remote areas.

## **7. Is it convenient to withdraw cash from ATMs?**

Bali has a widespread ATM network, with machines available in major cities and tourist areas. When using an international credit or debit card to withdraw cash, please note:

- Confirm that your bank card supports international withdrawals.
- Understand your bank's fees and exchange rate differences.
- Choose ATMs with security monitoring; bank-owned ATMs are the safest. Avoid withdrawing cash at night.

## **8. Will I be charged a fee when exchanging currency?**

- **Money changers:** Some may charge a fee, so it's best to ask before exchanging.
- **Banks:** Fees are usually lower, but the exchange rate may not be as favorable as at money changers.
- **ATM withdrawals:** You may incur two fees: the local bank's fee and your card-issuing bank's international withdrawal fee.

## **9. How much cash should I prepare?**

It's recommended to prepare cash based on your itinerary and spending habits. Generally:

- A daily budget of around **1,100,000 IDR to 1,280,000 IDR** (approximately USD$35 to USD$41) per person for meals, transportation, small purchases, and tips. Refer to the explanation below:

## **10. What if you run out of cash?**

- Use an ATM to withdraw cash, but be mindful of the fees.
- Use a credit card for a cash advance, though interest and fees are high.
- Ask hotel staff for help—they usually know nearby money changers or banks.
- Before you leave, confirm with your card issuer that your card has "**overseas withdrawal**" and "**cross-border transaction**" functions enabled. Otherwise, it won't work abroad.

## **11. Should you keep the receipt after exchanging money?**

Yes, it's recommended to keep the exchange receipt as proof in case any issues arise. Also, record the amount and exchange rate for each transaction to help with later financial management.

## **12. Do exchange rates in Bali fluctuate?**

Yes, exchange rates fluctuate with the market. It's advisable to monitor rate trends before your trip and exchange money when rates are favorable. Use rate tracking tools like XE.com or OANDA to stay updated.

## **14. Can you use other currencies in Bali?**

No, Bali only accepts Indonesian Rupiah as the official currency. Even if some shops accept US dollars, the exchange rates are usually less favorable for tourists.

## **15. Do I need to speak Indonesian when exchanging money?**

Bali is a well-established tourist destination, and staff at legitimate money changers can almost always communicate in **English**, so English alone is perfectly fine. Here are some useful phrases:

**Bargaining**

- Can you give me a better rate? "Can you give me a better rate?"
- If I exchange more, can I get a better rate? "If I exchange more, can I get a better rate?"

**Confirming the Amount**

- I'd like to exchange 200 US dollars. "I'd like to exchange 200 US dollars."
- How much Indonesian Rupiah will I get? "How much Indonesian Rupiah will I get?"
- Is there any commission or fee? "Is there any commission or fee?"

**Transaction Complete**

- Can I count the money here?
- Thank you, this looks correct.

## 17. **Credit Card Payments**

It's recommended to use a credit card that offers overseas spending rewards and low or no foreign transaction fees, especially at large hotels or big restaurants. This can be more cost-effective, allowing you to enjoy favorable USD exchange rates while maintaining flexibility! A quick tip: Some travelers have experienced credit card fraud at **small supermarkets**, so use your card cautiously!
