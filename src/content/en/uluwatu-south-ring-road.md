# Uluwatu Infrastructure Overhaul: The South Ring Road Set to Transform Bali's Traffic Landscape

Imagine a brand-new 12-kilometer coastal road connecting Uluwatu and Jimbaran, finally solving the 'eternal traffic jam' that has plagued countless tourists and locals for years. This isn't a dream—Uluwatu's infrastructure upgrade is now officially underway, and the changes are already happening.

## The South Ring Road: 12 Kilometers of Anticipation

Bali's South Ring Road has received final approval from the Bangli Regency government. This 12-kilometer coastal artery will link Uluwatu and Jimbaran, stretching from Pecatu Suluban to Jalan Raya Karang Mas. Crucially, most of the necessary land has already been acquired, including those tricky areas near Bingin Beach.

For those living in or frequently visiting Uluwatu, there's currently only one way in and out—the Jalan Raya Uluwatu, which passes in front of the GWK Cultural Park and is perpetually clogged with tourists and scooters. Once the South Ring Road is complete, this 'one-way-in, one-way-out' bottleneck will be a thing of the past.

## The Jimbaran Underpass: The End of the 'Eternal Traffic Jam'

The underpass project at the Jimbaran McDonald's intersection received final government approval on August 4 and is set to begin construction in 2026. Why is this project so critical? Because this intersection is the biggest bottleneck for all traffic entering and exiting Uluwatu—the root cause of the 'eternal traffic jam.'

This underpass integrates with the Southwest Ring Road and is expected to bring the most direct traffic improvements to Jimbaran, Kuta, and Uluwatu. On a broader scale, it will also ease traffic flow in Sanur, Nusa Dua, and Central Denpasar. The Nirmala-GWK intersection in Ungasan, in particular, will experience the most immediate benefits.

Meanwhile, new access roads will connect the GWK Cultural Park in Uluwatu, offering an alternative route bypassing the heavily congested Jalan Raya Uluwatu, including a viaduct spanning the Jimbaran River.

Don't think only major projects bring change. Starting June 1, 2026, the Bangli Transportation Department has rolled out a new traffic management plan targeting six of the most congested intersections in Ungasan and Pecatu. This is the government's 'preemptive move' before large-scale infrastructure construction begins, tackling the worst bottlenecks first.

## Road Widening: Substantial Traffic Flow Improvements

In addition to building new roads, several existing roads in Uluwatu will be widened. Some sections will be expanded to 24 to 30 meters to accommodate greater traffic volume. What does this mean? On the same routes, traffic flow will significantly improve, drastically cutting travel times for tourists and residents alike.

## Timeline: 2026-2027 Construction Period

The planned completion date is late 2026 to early 2027. This means:

- **June 2026**: New traffic plan goes into effect
- **Second half of 2026**: Major projects like the Jimbaran underpass begin construction
- **2026–2027**: Construction period for the Southern Ring Road and supporting infrastructure
- **By end of 2027**: Expected major completion

In the short term, construction may cause some traffic disruptions—a common growing pain for most major infrastructure projects. But in the long run, these inconveniences are a necessary price for a better future.

## What This Means for Visitors and Residents

**For travelers**, future trips to Uluwatu will no longer be defined by traffic jams. A drive that once took 45 minutes might now take just 20, giving you twice the time to explore Uluwatu Cliff Temple, Blue Point Beach, or other attractions. Your vacation becomes more fulfilling, not wasted in a line of cars.

**For local residents**, daily commutes will become more predictable and comfortable. No more leaving two hours early to battle traffic—work and life schedules can be far more balanced.

**For business owners in the service industry**, faster traffic means guests linger longer at restaurants and shops, and customer flow becomes more stable—because traffic is no longer a huge variable.

## Conclusion

Uluwatu is taking its infrastructure seriously. This isn't a political slogan or a distant dream—it's a reality where construction has already begun, land has been acquired, and timelines are set.

Whether you're planning your next trip to Bali or running a tourism-related business, these changes are worth noting. Because in Uluwatu, traffic improvements are redefining the area's appeal for both visitors and residents.
