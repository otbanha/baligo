**Bottom Line First**: With a 5-day, 4-night Bali trip, you actually get less than 3 full days of play after accounting for travel days at the start and end. Try to wake up early to avoid traffic jams; key pre-trip prep—e-Visa on arrival, tourist tax, eSIM data card (buy the day before and it's ready the next day), and a 220V hairdryer (for drying your hair after taking stunning water photos). Massage quality varies widely; picking one at random is a gamble but can also yield pleasant surprises.

Although this Bali trip was only 5 days and 4 nights, I really felt that after subtracting the travel time on the first and last days, the actual playtime was way too short! This itinerary leaned toward being tiring rather than a relaxing vacation. Bali's traffic tends to get congested, so I recommend waking up early to fit in more activities.

## Pre-Trip Preparations

1. E-Visa on Arrival: Remember to apply for an electronic visa on arrival before heading to Bali.
2. Tourist Tax: Be sure to pay the tourist tax.
3. Arrival Form: Fill out the arrival form.
4. Currency Exchange: It's recommended to bring US dollars and exchange them for Indonesian Rupiah locally.
5. Sun Protection: The sun in Bali is extremely strong; if you forget sunscreen, you'll easily get tanned.
6. Internet Access: I suggest using an eSIM card. I bought one from 'Global Travel Expert' on Shopee, and the speed was stable except in remote mountain areas. Buy it the day before and it's ready the next day—very convenient.
7. Water Fun & Photos: If you plan to take photos after playing in the water, remember to bring a hairdryer that supports 220V.
8. Massage Experience: No matter which massage place you go to, it feels like a lottery. Luckily, every massage we had was great, even at random roadside shops.
9. Photography Tips: To get those Instagram-worthy shots, I suggest researching poses in advance and having your travel buddy practice their photography skills. I'm the one who can't take good photos.
10. Tipping Customs: Bali is a country where tipping is customary. The basic tip is around 10,000 Rupiah (about USD$0.30). Avoid giving coins or very small bills, as it's more polite to give proper notes.
11. Power Adapter: Remember to bring a power adapter, or ask your hotel if they can lend you one.
12. Extra Taxes: Some restaurants and massage places charge additional taxes, and paying by card may incur an extra 3% fee.

**Underwater Walking**

Our first activity was underwater walking. Although it sounded fun, the actual experience was disappointing. When we first put on the helmet, it leaked water without any warning, and the water pressed directly against our faces, which was quite frightening. While walking underwater, we had to hold onto a railing, but the scenery wasn't particularly impressive.

**Parasailing**

Next up was parasailing. This activity was truly amazing, especially the sense of freedom we felt while soaring through the air. Perhaps because we had a great chat with the instructor, the whole experience was even more enjoyable. Parasailing took us from the air down to the water and back up again—this back-and-forth process was a lot of fun.

**Donut Ride**

Finally, we tried the donut ride (towable tube). It's said that the donut ride in Taiwan is more thrilling, especially if you sit in the back row for extra fun. Besides this, Nanwan offers a variety of other water activities to suit different preferences.

**Snacks and Tea**

After the activities, snacks and tea are provided, allowing everyone to take a break after all the excitement.

**Reminders**

- **Price Negotiation**: Activity prices are often quoted high, so it's recommended to haggle or purchase discounted tickets online in advance.
- **Photography Fees**: If your itinerary includes photography services, these require an additional fee. Vendors will provide a set of cloud photos and recorded video.
