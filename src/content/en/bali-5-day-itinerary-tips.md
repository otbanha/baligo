**Bottom Line First**: In a 5-day, 4-night Bali trip, after accounting for travel days at the start and end, you really only have less than 3 full days to explore. I recommend waking up early to avoid traffic jams. Key pre-trip preparations: e-Visa on Arrival, tourist tax, eSIM (buy it the day before and it's ready the next day), and a 220V hairdryer (for drying your hair after taking those stunning water photos). Massage parlor quality varies widely; picking one at random is a gamble, but it can also lead to pleasant surprises.

This Bali trip was only 5 days and 4 nights, but honestly, after factoring in the travel time on the first and last days, we still had way too little time to play! This itinerary was more on the tiring side—not exactly a relaxing vacation. Traffic in Bali can get congested easily, so I suggest getting up early to fit in more activities.

## Pre-Trip Preparation

1. E-Visa on Arrival: Remember to apply for an e-Visa on Arrival before heading to Bali.
2. Tourist Tax: Be sure to pay the tourist tax.
3. Arrival Form: Fill out the arrival form.
4. Currency Exchange: It's recommended to bring US dollars and exchange them for Indonesian Rupiah locally.
5. Sun Protection: The sun in Bali is incredibly strong; if you forget sunscreen, you'll easily get tanned.
6. Internet Access: I recommend using an eSIM. I bought one from 'Global Cross-Border Travel Expert' on Shopee. The internet speed was stable except in remote mountain areas. Buy it the day before and it's ready to use the next day—super convenient.
7. Taking Photos After Swimming: If you plan to take nice photos after swimming, remember to bring a 220V hairdryer.
8. Massage Experience: No matter which massage place you go to, it feels like a lottery. Luckily, we had great experiences every time, even at random roadside shops.
9. Photography Tips: To get those Instagram-worthy shots, I suggest researching poses in advance and having your travel buddy practice their photography skills. I'm the one who's terrible at taking photos.
10. Tipping Customs: Bali is a country where tipping is customary. The basic tip is around 10,000 Rupiah (about USD$0.65). Avoid giving coins or very small bills, as it's considered more polite.
11. Power Adapter: Remember to bring a power adapter, or check with your hotel first if they can lend you one.
12. Extra Taxes: Some restaurants and massage places charge additional taxes, and paying by card might incur an extra 3% fee.

**Underwater Walking**

Our first activity was underwater walking. Although it sounded fun, the actual experience was disappointing. When we first put on the helmet, water leaked in without any warning, and it pressed against our faces, which was quite frightening. While walking underwater, we had to hold onto a railing, but the scenery wasn't particularly impressive.

**Parasailing**

Next up is paragliding. This activity is truly amazing, especially when we're soaring through the air—that sense of freedom is unforgettable. Maybe because we had such a great chat with the instructor, the whole experience felt even more enjoyable. Paragliding takes you from the sky down to the water and then back up again; this back-and-forth process is incredibly fun.

**Doughnut Ride**

Finally, we tried the doughnut ride (towable tube). It's said that the doughnut ride in Taiwan is more thrilling, especially if you sit in the back row for extra fun. Besides that, Nanwan offers a variety of other water activities to suit different preferences.

**Snacks and Tea**

After the activities, snacks and tea are provided, giving everyone a chance to relax after all the excitement.

**Reminders**

- **Price Negotiation**: Activity prices are often set high, so it's advisable to haggle or purchase discounted tickets online in advance.
- **Photo Fees**: If your itinerary includes photography services, these come at an extra cost. Vendors will provide a set of cloud photos and recorded videos.

## Recommended Private Driver

Finding a good private driver is crucial when traveling in Bali, especially when you're exhausted and struggling to translate English. We recommend [Mang Loco](https://www.facebook.com/komang.rika.501/), a Balinese who used to work in Taiwan. ### Tip:

1. Itinerary planning: Roughly plan the attractions you want to visit and let the driver arrange the route.
2. Cost communication: Discuss extra fees for longer distances or overtime in advance.
3. Meal invitation: Drivers usually don't eat with guests, but if you like your driver, you can invite them and treat them to a meal.
