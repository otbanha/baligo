Island-wide relative humidity is forecast at 72–99%. During the rainy season, localized rainfall and weather changes may still affect outdoor activities.

According to BMKG's forecast for the southern waters of Bali, wave heights on November 19 are expected to range between 1.2–1.4 meters at certain times, falling within the low to moderate category.

**Travel Advisory:**

If you plan to visit Tanah Lot, Bedugul, Kintamani, or waterfall attractions tomorrow, it's advisable to bring rain gear and watch out for slippery roads.

Travelers heading to offshore islands like Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan should reconfirm ferry schedules and sea conditions before departure, especially regarding wind and wave changes in the southern waters.

Source: BMKG Bali Province Weather Forecast, BMKG Region III Southern Bali Waters Forecast.

## **🏆 Bali Wins 6 Asia Awards at the 2026 World Travel Awards! Waterbom, Mandapa, Ulaman, and More Make the List**

Bali has secured 6 awards at the 2026 World Travel Awards Asia ceremony, covering categories such as accommodation, family vacations, eco-friendly resorts, water parks, and wedding tourism.

The list of winners includes:

- **Asia's Leading Boutique Resort 2026**: Mandapa, a Ritz-Carlton Reserve (Ubud)
- **Asia's Leading Family Villa Resort 2026**: Anantara Uluwatu Bali Resort
- **Asia's Leading Green Resort 2026**: Ulaman Eco Luxury Resort
- **Asia's Leading Resort 2026**: Sofitel Bali Nusa Dua Beach Resort
- **Asia's Leading Water Park 2026**: Waterbom Bali
- **Asia's Leading Wedding Destination 2026**: Bali

Bali claimed six of Indonesia's eight regional awards in Asia, with the remaining two going to Bunaken Oasis Dive Resort & Spa in North Sulawesi and Bawah Reserve in the Riau Archipelago.

Founded in 1993, the World Travel Awards is one of the most recognized accolades in the international travel industry, covering hotels, resorts, destinations, and related services.

For travelers, this list serves as a reference when choosing accommodations and travel facilities, especially for those seeking boutique resorts, family villas, or eco-friendly stays.

However, travel awards don't guarantee a perfect fit for every traveler; actual choices should still be based on budget, location, room type, and recent guest reviews.

## **🌍 Bali Makes Fodor's 2027 No List Again! Overtourism, Air Pollution, and Land Development Under Scrutiny**

U.S. travel publication Fodor's Travel Guide has released its 2027 No List, with Bali once again named among eight global destinations urged to rethink tourism pressures.

This list is not meant to urge travelers to cancel their trips, but rather to highlight the potential pressure that tourism development in popular destinations can place on the environment and local residents.

Fodor's main concerns regarding Bali this time include:

- Air pollution and open burning of waste
- Land conversion due to tourism development
- Gradual reduction of farmland
- Pressure on water resources and infrastructure
- Impact of overtourism on residents' daily lives

Bali welcomed nearly 6.95 million international visitors in 2025, with the tourism industry generating substantial economic revenue for the region. However, the increase in tourists has also drawn more attention to issues such as waste management, traffic, land use, and water resources.

This is not the first time Fodor's has included Bali on such a list; it was also flagged in 2020 and 2025, though it did not make the list in 2026.

**This does not mean Bali is unsuitable for travel.** Fodor's No List represents the editorial perspective of a travel publication, not a government-issued travel ban or safety warning.

For travelers, this news is worth considering how to reduce the negative impact of tourism on local communities—such as choosing legal accommodations, supporting local businesses, minimizing single-use plastics, and avoiding leaving trash at popular attractions.

## **🪂 Tabanan Plans 8-Hectare Skydiving Facility! Kelating Could Become a Skydiving Tourism Hub**

Bali's Vice Governor, Giri Prasta, expressed support on November 18 for the development of the Skydive Bali aviation sports facility in Kelating Village, Kerambitan District, Tabanan Regency.

The project, proposed by units related to the I Gusti Ngurah Rai Air Force Base, is expected to cover approximately 8 hectares of land for skydiving and other aviation sports facilities.

Future plans include:

- Skydiving and related aviation facilities
- Domestic and international skydiving competitions
- Training for athletes of the Indonesian Aero Sports Federation (FASI)
- Supporting police-related skydiving training
- Developing sports tourism in the Tabanan area

The Balinese government believes that developing aviation sports can diversify tourism offerings, reducing reliance solely on beaches, villas, temples, and natural landscapes.

Located on the southwestern coast of Tabanan, Kelating has the potential to become Bali's new outdoor adventure destination once the facilities are officially built and opened.

**This is still in the planning and construction phase, with no official opening date, visitor booking methods, or complete operational information announced yet.** Interested travelers should not include it in their confirmed itineraries for now.

## **☕ Bali Promotes Geographical Indication Products Integrated with Tourism! 16 Specialty Items Could Become Cultural Experiences**

Bali's legal and intellectual property authorities aim to combine local geographical indication (Indikasi Geografis, IG) products with tourism experiences, turning specialty agricultural goods, handicrafts, and traditional culture into more than just commodities—they could become part of travel activities.

Bali currently has around 16 registered geographical indication products, covering:

- Coffee
- Salak (snake fruit)
- Traditional sea salt
- Handicrafts
- Traditional paintings
- Gemstones and local specialty products

Potential products being proposed for 2026 include:

- Coconut sugar (Gula Aren) from Buleleng's Pedawa Village
- Songket woven fabric from Klungkung's Gelgel Village

For 2027, further promotions are expected for:

- Red rice from Tabanan's Jatiluwih
- Cocoa products from Jembrana

Local authorities aim to develop cultural, agricultural, and handicraft experiences with local characteristics through collaboration with the tourism sector, local communities, and travel operators—such as coffee plantation visits, agricultural product processing, traditional weaving, and handicraft demonstrations.

Currently, these initiatives are focused on product promotion and tourism cooperation planning, with no complete official travel routes or package tours announced yet.

For travelers, beyond the popular attractions and beaches, it's also worth paying attention to Bali's coffee-growing regions, traditional craft villages, and unique agricultural products to gain a deeper understanding of the local culture.
