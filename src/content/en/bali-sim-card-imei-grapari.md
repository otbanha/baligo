If you're planning to be a digital nomad in Bali or stay longer than a month, don't just grab a SIM card anywhere. Street stalls and tourist shops may seem convenient, but my advice is simple: **go directly to the telecom company's official customer service center**, like Telkomsel's GraPARI.

## Why Buying a Card on the Street Often Backfires

### 1. Prices Are Over Double the Official Rate

Street vendors and tourist areas mostly sell packaged tourist plans, often costing much more than official stores. For the same data, you're just throwing money away.

### 2. Internet Cut Off After 30 Days

Indonesia strictly regulates foreign phone IMEI registration. Street-bought cards often only activate a 30-day tourist permit—once it's up, your phone's IMEI gets blocked. No matter how much you top up, your phone won't get any signal.

### 3. Thinking You Ran Out of Data, So You Buy Another Card

The worst part? Many people, when they lose internet, assume 'I must have run out of data' and buy a new card, wasting another USD$16 to USD$19. The real issue isn't data—it's the IMEI being blocked.

## 💡 Money-Saving and Hassle-Free Tips

**For those heading to Bali who haven’t left yet:**
Ask your driver to take you directly to an official store (like GraPARI) to activate your SIM on the spot—it’s the safest option (bring your passport). Rates are transparent, so you won’t have to worry about being overcharged as a tourist.

**For those who already bought a 30-day plan on the street:**
Don’t panic—there’s still hope! Before your 30 days are up, bring your passport to an official customer service center to extend your IMEI and renew your card. This way, you can keep using the same SIM and top up with affordable data. You’ll save several hundred New Taiwan Dollars and avoid buying a new card.

## Summary

For short-stay travelers, a tourist package might be fine. But if you’re staying more than a month, heading to an official store from the start is the most cost-effective and reliable way to avoid losing internet access midway. Just remember to bring your passport—one visit and you’re set for worry-free internet in Bali.
