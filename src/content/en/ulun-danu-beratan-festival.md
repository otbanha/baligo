### About the Water Temple

Perched on the shores of Lake Bratan, the Pura Ulun Danu Beratan Water Temple has honored the water goddess Dewi Danu since the 17th century, symbolizing Bali’s reverence for its lakes and rivers, and reflecting the vital role of water resources in agricultural irrigation, harvests, and the island’s food security. As one of the nine directional temples known as 'Kahyangan Jagat,' it serves not only as a spiritual center but also plays a practical role in managing irrigation channels, fostering harmony between heaven, earth, people, and gods through the principles of Āgama Tirta (Water Religion) and Tri Hita Karana. The temple complex also incorporates Buddhist stupa elements, showcasing a blend of diverse religious traditions, and its stunning lake-floating vista makes it a perfect intersection of Bali’s culture, spirituality, and natural beauty.

### **Ulun Danu Beratan Water Temple Teams Up with Blooms Garden for Daily Gebogan Parades**

Nestled in the scenic Bedugul highlands, the Ulun Danu Beratan Water Temple is one of Bali’s most iconic landmarks. From July to August 2025, the temple will join forces with nearby Blooms Garden to host daily Gebogan cultural parades, a grand event running nonstop for two months.

### **What Is Gebogan? A Deep Dive into This Balinese Tradition**

Gebogan is a traditional offering tower made of fruits and flowers, symbolizing gratitude and blessings. Every day, women from 18 villages carry these intricately stacked offerings on their heads as they move between temples, representing abundance and thankfulness. Female participants dress in traditional attire and parade with colorful, towering Gebogan, showcasing the unique charm of Balinese culture.

### **Different locations on weekdays and weekends, designed for traveler convenience**

To manage weekend crowds, the parade takes place at the Ulun Danu Beratan Temple on weekdays (Monday to Friday), and moves to the livelier Blooms Garden on weekends. This arrangement allows visitors to flexibly plan their itinerary and experience the culture anytime.

### **Kecak and Barong performances added for double the cultural appeal**

![同步推出Kecak與Barong表演，文化魅力加倍](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_b705323d70d8400b29ebbfa065a12e47.jpg)

In addition to the Gebogan parade, the organizers have also prepared traditional performances like Kecak and Barong dances, offering travelers a richer cultural experience. The detailed schedule will be announced gradually on official social media platforms.

### **Expected surge in visitors, with international travelers from around the world**

Currently, the average daily visitor count is around 1,700, rising to over 1,800 on weekends. The organizers anticipate a large influx of international tourists, and several school groups from East Java, Indonesia, have already booked visits.

## **FAQ**

### **What is Gebogan?**

"Gebogan" is an Indonesian word (pronounced: geh-BO-ahn) that, in Balinese culture, refers to an offering—typically a decorative arrangement of flowers, fruits, food, and other items used in religious ceremonies or festivals. In Indonesian, Gebogan can also mean a floral display or arrangement used to decorate stages or podiums. So, "Gebogan" can be understood as a "Balinese ceremonial offering," "floral decoration," or "flower display."

### **When does the event take place?**

From July 1 to August 31, 2025, parades are held daily.

### **Where can you watch the Gebogan Parade?**

It takes place at the Water Temple on weekdays and at Blooms Garden on weekends.

### **Do you need to book or buy tickets to attend?**

General visitors only need to purchase an entrance ticket to the attraction to watch the event; some performances require advance seat reservations.
