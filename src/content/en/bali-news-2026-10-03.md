# Top Topics & News for Bali Travelers | October 3, 2026

## ⚖️ New Developments in the Kelingking Glass Elevator Case: Bali Government Formally Appeals

The lawsuit surrounding the famous **Kelingking Beach glass elevator** on Nusa Penida has taken a new turn today.

The Bali government has filed an appeal against the previous ruling by the Denpasar Administrative Court (PTUN), seeking final legal certainty through the judicial process.

In its grounds for appeal, the government challenges the court's earlier findings on the nature of administrative actions, enforcement procedures, and warning protocols. The case is now in the process of submitting appeal documents.

For travelers, the key takeaway remains:

**Kelingking Beach is still a popular and open attraction, but the legal and administrative disputes over this large-scale facility are not yet fully resolved.**

So, if you come across social media posts claiming the glass elevator has been definitively canceled or is about to reopen immediately, it's best to check the latest official announcements rather than relying on short videos or travel group shares.

## 🛵 Suspected Illegal Motorcycle Riding Course Found in Serangan; Two Foreign Instructors Fled When Authorities Arrived

Bali's immigration office recently discovered a suspected motorcycle riding course targeting foreigners in **Serangan, Denpasar**.

When immigration enforcement officers arrived at the scene, two foreign nationals believed to be instructors fled immediately. Police and immigration authorities are still investigating their identities and activities.

This incident is linked to the Bali government's recent crackdown on foreigners engaging in motorcycle-related commercial activities.

For travelers, the key takeaway isn't that you can't learn to ride a motorcycle in Bali, but rather:

**If you plan to take lessons or rent a bike, it's best to verify whether the operator is legal, the instructor's identity, and whether the activity complies with Indonesian regulations.**

Especially as more foreign tourists choose to ride motorcycles in Bali in recent years, authorities are paying closer attention to related safety and legality issues.

## 📞 Bali Accelerates Integration of 112 Emergency Services; Reporting Incidents, Getting Medical Help, and Fire Services Expected to Become More Convenient

This news is quite useful for travelers.

The Bali government is accelerating the integration of the **112 single emergency service number**, aiming to connect fire, ambulance, police, and other emergency response resources.

BPBD Bali reiterated today the need to strengthen cross-agency cooperation and emergency response capabilities, so that incidents can be transferred to the appropriate units more quickly.

For foreign tourists, this single emergency number is highly significant.

Because in an unfamiliar country, you might encounter:

- Car accidents
- Drowning
- Fires
- Mountain incidents
- Other emergencies

Often, the biggest problem isn't 'not knowing who to ask for help,' but **not knowing which number to call or which agency to contact**.

Currently, the 112 system is still in a gradual integration and enhancement phase, so in real emergencies, you should still use the local emergency channels based on the situation on the ground.

## 🌊 Southern Bali Waters May Still See Waves Over 3 Meters—Don't Judge Sea Conditions by Land Weather

Although many parts of Bali have been sunny or partly cloudy recently, **sea conditions still require extra attention**.

The latest BMKG forecast shows that on October 2, southern Bali waters could still see waves of around **3 meters or higher**; wave heights in the southern sea are significantly higher than in the northern waters.

So, if you have upcoming plans for:

**Speedboats, snorkeling, diving, surfing, or other water activities around Nusa Penida or Nusa Lembongan, it's best to check with the boat company and the latest BMKG sea conditions on the day of departure.**

This is why I always advise travelers not to just look at 'whether it will rain in Bali today.'

**Good weather on land ≠ calm seas.**

## ❄️ Bali Welcomes Rare 'Ice Theater' in Late October: Alice in Wonderland Takes the Stage

If you've visited Bali many times and are looking for something different, this is worth noting.

**Alice in Wonderland on Ice** will debut on **October 31** at BLOCK 42 in Nuanu Creative City, with over 25 performances scheduled throughout November.

This is a large-scale production featuring a real ice rink, figure skating, music, and visual effects to bring *Alice in Wonderland* to life.

Ticket prices vary by seating, starting from:

**IDR 200,000 ~ 2,700,000**

Children aged 0–4 enter free.

The same season will also feature **Aladdin and the Famous Stories from 1001 Nights**, and **Dracula: The Story of Eternal Love**, suitable for audiences aged 16 and up.

For families with children, this type of indoor activity also offers an alternative to beaches, pools, and other attractions.
