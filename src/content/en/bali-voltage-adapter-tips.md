**Bottom line**: Bali uses **220V** voltage and **European two-round-pin sockets (Type C/F)**. Taiwanese appliances need an adapter (available at local supermarkets for about NT$30–40, roughly USD$1). Make sure your phone, camera, and laptop chargers **support 220V** (most 3C devices do, but high-wattage items like hair dryers and curling irons require extra attention to voltage range).

Indonesia and Bali operate on a standard voltage of **220 volts (220V)**—crucial info for travelers bringing electronics. Before packing, ensure your devices support international 220V standards to use them properly in Indonesia and Bali.

Beyond voltage, socket type matters too. Indonesia uses European-style two-round-pin sockets. Without the right adapter, many hotel outlets may be unusable.

## Do You Need an Adapter?

You can buy adapters at local supermarkets; they're called "Adaptor" (pronounced ah-dap-tor in Indonesian). You can also ask your hotel if they provide adapters, but not all do—so it's best to grab one at a local store to be safe. They're cheap, around NT$30–40 (about USD$1).

![你需要轉接頭嗎？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_cee2923f799f1f91f4f50a8eee72a53d.jpg)

Typically, you'll need at least one adapter plus a **220V-compatible** multi-outlet extension cord to power all your devices in Indonesia and Bali. When choosing an extension cord, don't skimp on quality—opt for a reputable brand to safely use all your electronics.

## How to Prepare a USB Charger?

Modern USB chargers that travelers bring abroad (like phone and tablet charging heads) typically support a wide voltage range of 100V-240V. As long as the input voltage range marked on the charger includes 220V, you can use it directly in Bali without an extra transformer. Before you go, check the small print on your charger—if it says "Input: 100-240V," it's good to go; if it only says 110V, you'll need to replace it.

![USB充電器如何準備？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_a1558c540adf790fc52dc3f5badfe3ae.jpg)

If you're planning to bring a multi-outlet extension cord like the one pictured above, first make sure it supports the 110~220V range. Second, check the plug type—those bought in Taiwan usually have two flat prongs, so you'll also need an adapter (as mentioned at the top) to use it safely in different hotels or villas. Finally, if your devices support PD or QC fast charging, it's best to choose a corresponding high-power charger to significantly cut charging time and reduce travel anxiety!

## 🔌 Different Socket Types Across ASEAN Countries

Socket and plug standards vary across Southeast Asia, so checking your destination's type before departure can save you a lot of hassle finding adapters locally. For example, Singapore commonly uses Type G sockets with three rectangular prongs, which are not directly compatible with the Type C and F plugs found in Bali. The Philippines, on the other hand, uses both Type A and Type B sockets: Type A has two flat pins, while Type B adds a grounding pin—both require attention to ensure compatibility with your devices.

If your trip includes both Bali and other ASEAN countries, it's a good idea to list all the adapters you'll need in your packing checklist and prepare them in advance for a smoother journey.

## Can I Bring My Own Hair Dryer or Curling Iron?

Most hotels provide hair dryers for guests, but some do not.

It's recommended to check with your hotel in advance to see if they offer a hair dryer. If not, or if you need a specific model or brand, you'll need to consider the solutions mentioned earlier, such as buying a local 220V hair dryer or using a voltage converter.

If you bring your own hair dryer from Taiwan, note that most are designed for 110V only. In Indonesia and Bali, where the standard voltage is 220V, a 110V hair dryer won't work properly and may even be damaged due to voltage mismatch.

For this situation, the safest and most convenient solution is to buy a hair dryer that supports 220V locally. This way, you can ensure it works properly in Indonesia and Bali, while avoiding damage caused by voltage mismatch.
