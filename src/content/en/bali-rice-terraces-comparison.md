## Introduction to Bali's Rice Terraces: Exploring the Two Major Terrace Regions

**Bottom line upfront**: **Tegalalang** is closer to Ubud, easy to reach, but more commercialized (including the famous Instagram swing); **Jatiluwih** is a UNESCO World Heritage site with more expansive and pristine scenery, though farther away and less crowded. Choose Tegalalang for Instagram-worthy photos, and Jatiluwih for grand nature and fewer crowds. For both, it's best to avoid visiting during midday.

Bali is renowned not only for its stunning beaches, vibrant cultural events, and rich natural landscapes, but its rice terraces are also iconic attractions. The unique stepped rice paddies offer not just spectacular visual enjoyment but also reveal the local farming culture and social structure. **Bali's two major terrace regions—Tegalalang Rice Terraces** and **Jatiluwih Rice Terraces**—are wonders of the world, allowing visitors to delve into Balinese rural life and personally experience the serene beauty of nature.

![峇里島梯田介紹：探索峇里島兩大梯田區](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_681188d17bd939f1995e2b811dd9ea8d.jpg)

## **What Are Bali's Rice Terraces?**

Bali has rice terraces, and so does Taiwan—so why go all the way to Bali to see them? Behind Bali's terraces lies an ancient and complex irrigation system known as the **Subak system**. Originating in the 11th century, it has been designated a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**. This system is not only the foundation of local agriculture but also an integral part of Balinese culture and social structure.

Below, we dive into the unique features and best ways to explore these two major terrace regions.

## **Tegalalang Rice Terrace**

Located north of Ubud, Tegalalang Rice Terrace is one of Bali's most iconic tourist terrace attractions. Its stunning and picturesque landscape, along with a wealth of nearby activities, draws countless visitors, making it a must-visit for photography enthusiasts and nature lovers.

![德格拉朗梯田（Tegalalang Rice Terrace）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_04c9efaa4e64a1161cd73552f0fa0eaf.jpg)

### **The Unique Charm of Tegallalang Rice Terraces**

The standout feature of the Tegallalang Rice Terraces is their **breathtaking landscape and unique location**. The terraces vary in height and are mostly carved into hillsides, with the Subak irrigation system channeling water smoothly through each layer of rice paddies. This creates stunning reflective effects on the water, especially at sunrise and sunset, when the scene feels almost dreamlike. Surrounding the terraces, a rich variety of tropical plants adds to the lush, green panorama, making you feel like you've stepped into a verdant paradise.

![德格拉朗梯田的特色](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_4b230e91b2e502eebc023b2f37cd16aa.jpg)

### **Tips for Visiting Tegallalang Rice Terraces**

- **Visit in the morning or at dusk**: It's best to go early in the morning or late in the afternoon, when the light is soft and the views are at their most spectacular. Plus, the cooler temperatures make for a more comfortable experience, avoiding the harsh midday sun.
- **Opening hours**: Daily from 6:00 AM to 7:00 PM.
- **Join a local guided tour**: Consider taking a guided tour to learn about the history and workings of the Subak system, and gain deeper insight into the local rice terrace culture.
- **Try the giant swing**: The "Bali Giant Swing" near the Tegallalang Rice Terraces has become a popular Instagram spot. You can enjoy the thrill of swinging while taking in the magnificent terrace views around you.
- **Watch your step on the trails**: The pathways at Tegallalang can be quite steep, so wear comfortable shoes and stay mindful of your footing.

### **Tickets and Transportation for Tegallalang Rice Terraces**

Currently, the entrance fee for the Tegallalang Rice Terraces ranges from about 10,000 to 50,000 Indonesian Rupiah (depending on the specific ticket booth area). If you're coming from Ubud town center, it's very convenient to get there by car or scooter, taking roughly 20 to 30 minutes.

## **Jatiluwih Rice Terraces**

![賈提魯維梯田（Jatiluwih Rice Terraces）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_03240ac24d35267825628f03033462da.jpg)

Jatiluwih Rice Terraces, located in central Bali, is a vast and well-preserved rice field area. Compared to Tegallalang, Jatiluwih is more expansive, with endless terraced landscapes that let visitors wander through a magnificent green ocean. The scenery here is rustic and perfect for travelers seeking tranquility and natural beauty.

### **Highlights of Jatiluwih Rice Terraces**

![賈提魯維梯田的特色](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_489574c7808e044a63eb6e36357de053.jpg)

Jatiluwih Rice Terraces are listed as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**, showcasing the essence of the Subak irrigation system through their harmonious natural layout. The terraces are spread widely, with broad fields surrounded by mountains, creating a peaceful and serene atmosphere. The rice paddies change colors with the seasons, from vibrant green to golden yellow, offering stunning natural beauty no matter when you visit.

### **Tips for Visiting Jatiluwih Rice Terraces**

- **Circular Trail Hiking**: Jatiluwih features several trails of varying lengths, perfect for hiking. You can pick a path and stroll through the rice fields, breathing in the fresh air and enjoying the beautiful scenery.
- **Bike Rental**: Local bike rentals are available, allowing you to cycle through the terraces while exercising and taking in the views—a favorite activity among foreign tourists.
- **Savor Local Cuisine**: Nearby traditional restaurants serve Balinese dishes, where you can dine while overlooking the terraced fields and experiencing the warm hospitality of the locals.

### **Jatiluwih Rice Terraces: Tickets & Transportation**

The entrance fee for Jatiluwih Rice Terraces is approximately 50,000 Indonesian Rupiah. From major tourist areas in Bali like Seminyak or Kuta, the drive takes about 2 hours. It's recommended to hire a private driver or join a local tour to save the hassle of transportation.

## **Tegalalang vs. Jatiluwih Rice Terraces: A Comparison**

Tegalalang and Jatiluwih rice terraces each have their own charm. Tegalalang is closer to Ubud, making it ideal for travelers with limited time, while Jatiluwih is more expansive, perfect for those who love deep exploration and serene nature. Whichever terrace you choose, you'll experience Bali's unique agricultural landscape and rich cultural heritage.

![德格拉朗與賈提魯維梯田的比較](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_ac65861b32e9c3e850c3f44aff108470.jpg)

### Best Season to Visit & Tips

Bali's rice terraces are actually **beautiful year-round**, showcasing different stunning scenery at various stages. However, it's important to note that the rice cultivation cycles at these two major terraces are quite different:

- **Tegalalang Rice Terraces (Multi-cropping):** Farmers here stagger their planting throughout the year, so no matter which month you visit, you can almost always see lush green seedlings, golden rice stalks, or water paddies reflecting the sky. There's no absolute dry season.
- **Jatiluwih Rice Terraces (Traditional Red Rice, 1-2 harvests per year):** This area preserves the most traditional farming calendar. Typically, **from February to April**, the rice seedlings grow rapidly, blanketing the entire valley in a carpet of tender green. By **June to July** (locally known as Sasih Sada), the rice turns golden, marking the busy harvest season for farmers—the best time to experience a traditional agricultural bounty.

**In summary:** If you want to avoid afternoon thunderstorms and capture terraced rice fields set against blue skies and white clouds, the **dry season from May to September** offers the most stable weather for your visit. However, nature's rhythm isn't set in stone—local farmers adjust their planting schedules based on the year's rainfall and climate, making each trip a unique encounter!

## **Photography Tips: How to Capture the Beauty of the Rice Terraces**

- **Choose the golden hours**: The warm, soft light at sunrise or sunset adds depth to your shots. The prime window from 8 to 10 AM offers moderate sunlight that creates gentle shadows without overexposing your photos. In the late afternoon around 4 to 5 PM, the golden sunset casts a warm glow over the terraces, turning the layered paddies a dreamy shade of gold.
- **Use a wide-angle lens**: The expansive landscape of the rice terraces is perfect for a wide-angle lens, helping you capture the full grandeur of the layered fields. If you're shooting with a phone, try the panorama mode to take in the sweeping views.
- **Incorporate foreground and composition**: Add elements like rice stalks or local farmers to the foreground to give your photos more storytelling depth and dimension. If you happen to catch farmers at work, seize those moments—capturing them in action adds a rich sense of daily life. You can also photograph nearby small temples, Subak irrigation channels, or ask a friend dressed in traditional Balinese attire to pose, enhancing the cultural vibe.
- **Leverage layering**: The hallmark of Bali's rice terraces is their stepped structure. Use the foreground, midground, and background to create a photo with rich depth.
- **Play with contrasting colors**: The vibrant green of the paddies against the blue sky and white clouds creates a striking contrast—a must-capture element in your composition.
- **Try low-angle shots**: Crouch down or shoot from a low angle to emphasize the rice stalks, adding a sense of dimension and highlighting the intricate details of the fields.
- **Respect the locals**: If you want to photograph farmers or villagers, always ask for permission first (a simple way to ask is: "Foto, foto?").

## Extra Insight: What Is the Subak System?

The Subak system mentioned above is a traditional irrigation method unique to Bali's rice terraces, with a history spanning over a thousand years. Since the 11th century, Subak has been the backbone of local agriculture and has been recognized by UNESCO as a **World Heritage Site**. It is far more than just a technical irrigation system—it embodies a unique cultural symbol integrating religious beliefs, social structure, and environmental conservation. Rooted in Buddhist and water deity worship, the Subak system emphasizes harmony between humans and nature, fostering a spirit of cooperation and sharing among farming communities.

![額外補充: 什麼是蘇巴克系統？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_0ca73e6ea28937fc21a3beff6cc1905f.jpg)

The Subak system is collectively managed by local farmers, ensuring fair water distribution to every terrace area. Farmers hold meetings through Subak organizations to coordinate water allocation and perform rituals each planting season to thank the water gods and mountain deities for their blessings. This system effectively prevents water disputes and strengthens the bonds of solidarity within farming communities.

![有名的Kanto Lampo瀑布並不是一個自然形成的瀑布，是因為蘇巴克灌溉系統的水溢出而形成](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_9ce193a07d5b914c7677aa9920e7b859.jpg)

The famous Kanto Lampo Waterfall is not a natural waterfall but is formed by overflow from the Subak irrigation system.

This spot is a must-visit for snapping stunning photos! For more details, check out Xiao Jie's [Kanto Lampo Waterfall Guide](https://youtu.be/-0KOzA9nPGY)

### Water Sources of the Subak System

The Subak system primarily draws water from Bali's volcanic mountain ranges, especially Mount Batur and Mount Agung. These volcanic areas receive abundant rainfall, providing a steady water supply to local streams and rivers. The entire Subak system cleverly harnesses this natural flow, channeling water to each terrace layer in sequence to ensure stable rice growth.

### Water Distribution Process

1. **Mountain Water Sources**: Rainwater collects in lakes within the volcanic mountain range and flows downhill through rivers.
2. **Main Irrigation Channels**: The Subak system uses rivers and streams to build main irrigation channels that divert water into the rice terraces.
3. **Distribution Channels**: The main water flow is distributed to each terrace area, with distribution channels ensuring every layer of the terraces receives water evenly.
4. **Lakes Supplying the Two Major Terrace Areas**: The Tegalalang rice terraces are primarily fed by Lake Batur (Danau Batur), while the Jatiluwih rice terraces draw their water mainly from Lake Beratan (Danau Beratan) at the Ulun Danu Temple.

![水神廟所在的布拉坦湖供應著賈提魯維梯田區的灌溉用水](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_cb2b1f1de756cb57dc9c603d2552ddba.jpg)

Lake Beratan, home to the Ulun Danu Temple, supplies irrigation water to the Jatiluwih rice terraces.

### Sacred Water Rituals

Subak is not just a technical irrigation system—it is deeply intertwined with local religious beliefs. At the start of each planting season, farmers pray to the water gods, holding ceremonies to thank nature for the gift of water and to pray for a bountiful harvest. These rituals reflect the farmers' reverence and gratitude toward nature, adding a layer of mystique to the terraced landscape.
