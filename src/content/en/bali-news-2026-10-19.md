## ⛽ Bali Gas Station Queues Gradually Easing, but Some Stations Still Short on Fuel

After nearly a week of fuel supply and distribution issues in Bali, signs of improvement are finally emerging.

Pertamina reported on October 17 that, following the implementation of the odd-even license plate fueling system, queues at some gas stations in Denpasar and Badung have started to ease. Wait times at certain locations are now around 15 to 30 minutes, though conditions vary by station, and fuel supply has not fully recovered everywhere.

### October 19 Fueling Rules: Odd License Plates

The temporary measures announced apply from October 17 to 19, with an odd-even license plate system for subsidized Pertalite fuel:

- October 17: License plates ending in odd numbers (1, 3, 5, 7, 9).
- October 18: License plates ending in even numbers (0, 2, 4, 6, 8).
- October 19: License plates ending in odd numbers (1, 3, 5, 7, 9).

The single purchase limit remains at 3 liters for motorcycles and 20 liters for four-wheeled vehicles. These are per-transaction caps, not guarantees that all vehicles can fill up to these amounts; actual supply still depends on gas station inventory.

### What should tourists be aware of?

If you need to rent a motorcycle, drive, or hire a car today or tomorrow, first check the vehicle's license plate last digit and fuel level. For rented vehicles, confirm whether they are subject to the same restrictions—don't assume foreign tourists or rental cars are automatically exempt.

Additionally, whether non-subsidized fuels like Pertamax are subject to the same limits should be based on on-site station announcements and the latest official updates. Don't simply apply Pertalite's rules to all fuel types.

What's clear for now is that queues are starting to ease, but that doesn't mean all areas of Bali have returned to normal. If you're heading to remote regions, it's wise to check fuel availability with your driver or rental provider in advance and allow extra travel time.

## 🚔 Bali Police Detain 21 Suspected of Reselling Fuel at Inflated Prices, Seize 753 Liters of Pertalite

During Bali's fuel shortage, some took advantage of long lines at gas stations by filling plastic bottles with subsidized gasoline and selling them online at high prices. On October 17, police announced they had taken action across multiple locations in Bali, detaining 21 individuals suspected of illegally reselling Pertalite.

Police noted that the suspects come from various backgrounds, including laborers, ride-hailing motorcycle drivers, ordinary citizens, and students. They sold gasoline through platforms like Facebook Marketplace, with each 1.5-liter bottle priced between IDR 50,000 and IDR 200,000.

The operation seized approximately 753 liters of Pertalite. Police stated that those involved will face administrative penalties, regular reporting, and other measures on a case-by-case basis—not all have been convicted by the courts.

On October 18, police announced another case in Tabanan Regency. A 22-year-old man is suspected of selling 10.5 liters of Pertalite via Facebook for a total of IDR 620,000. Police have detained him for investigation.

These cases show that the fuel crisis not only affects refueling times but also brings attention to illegal reselling and price gouging.

Reminder for travelers: Do not buy bottled gasoline from unknown sellers on social media platforms just because of long lines at gas stations. Besides the price potentially being much higher than the normal rate, the source and quality of the fuel are also hard to verify. It is recommended to refuel only through official gas stations or confirmed legal service channels.

## 🏛️ Bali Requires All Districts to Form Fuel Oversight Teams to Prevent Misuse of Subsidized Gasoline

In addition to stepping up police enforcement, Bali Governor Wayan Koster has also required all districts to form dedicated teams to oversee the sale and use of subsidized fuel.

The oversight teams are expected to include representatives from local governments, police, military, local law enforcement, transportation departments, gas station operator associations, and Pertamina. Their main tasks include inspecting subsidized fuel sales, handling illegal refueling, and preventing people from using non-compliant license plates or modified fuel tanks to obtain extra quotas.

Additionally, related management measures also involve conditions for refueling again. Some implementation arrangements require that a vehicle must have consumed at least 70% of its previous fuel purchase before it can buy subsidized fuel again, which may be verified through odometer readings or other methods.

The goal of this policy is to ensure that subsidized fuel is prioritized for those who truly need it, and to reduce queue issues caused by hoarding, reselling, and repeated purchases.

However, the odd-even license plate system and purchase restrictions were originally scheduled to last until October 19. Whether they will be extended, canceled, or modified still awaits an official announcement. Travelers should not assume that the temporary measures will definitely end on October 19.

## 🌐 International Internet Governance Conference Held in Nusa Dua, Approximately 2,500 Delegates Expected to Attend

The 87th Annual General Meeting of the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN87) is being held from October 17 to 22 at the Bali International Convention Centre in Nusa Dua, Bali.

This international conference is expected to attract approximately 2,000 to 2,500 delegates from 180 countries to discuss topics such as domain names, internet addresses, and global internet governance.

To ensure the smooth running of the conference, Bali police have deployed 215 personnel to handle security along the airport reception route, at the conference venue, and on surrounding roads. On October 18, police also dispatched bomb disposal units and canine teams to inspect the venue, finding no suspicious items.

For regular travelers, this conference does not mean a complete lockdown of Nusa Dua, but if you are staying in the ITDC area or arranging airport transfers, it is still advisable to allow extra travel time and follow on-site security checks and road control instructions.

## 🌤️ Bali Weather: Daytime highs around 33°C, still watch for sea conditions in southern waters

According to the weather forecast for October 18, daytime highs in Bali will be around 33°C, with some mountainous and inland areas possibly cooler at night. From the morning of October 18 to the morning of October 19, no rainfall warnings have been issued for any regencies or cities in Bali.

As for sea conditions, wave heights in southern Bali waters are forecast at 0.5 to 2 meters, and in northern waters at 0.25 to 1.25 meters. If wind speeds increase further, ferries and small vessels may still be affected.

If you have plans for boat trips, sea activities, or visits to outlying islands tomorrow, it's best to reconfirm with the ferry company and check the latest sea conditions before departure. Even without rainfall warnings, it doesn't mean there is no risk of waves and wind for sea activities.
