## **Visa Section**

I chose to apply for an e-visa. Since traveling with seniors and kids can easily take more time, I preferred to handle everything I could in advance. Not just the visa, but I also paid the tourist tax in Taiwan beforehand to minimize processing time upon arrival.

Bali's customs process is now quite digital, but I missed one thing—I didn't realize the departure declaration had also moved online. So I had to quickly fill it out on my phone on the spot, which took over ten minutes. Luckily, my family had already learned to relax, so this little hiccup didn't dampen the first day's mood. Instead, it made everyone appreciate the flexibility and spontaneity of travel even more.

Although there were a few bumps along the way, this trip deeply reminded me how important it is to stay flexible and patient when traveling with family. I'll continue sharing more details and insights from this Bali journey.

## **Flight Arrangements**

When it comes to flights during the Chinese New Year period, based on my decade-plus experience, the best strategy is to start monitoring prices closely a full year before your desired departure date. Once you spot a good deal, don't hesitate or wait for travel fairs or airline promotions, as fares during this peak season only tend to climb higher.

For this Bali trip, the original plan was Australia, but after discussing with family in March, we switched to Bali. While searching for flights over the Chinese New Year period, I unexpectedly found EVA Air business class round-trip tickets to Bali for just over NT$35,000 (about USD$1,129), while economy class was already approaching NT$20,000 (about USD$645). In comparison, the business class price seemed especially worthwhile, especially considering the nearly five-hour flight to Bali—enhanced comfort without the sky-high price tag.

## **Local Transportation**

Public transportation in Bali is not well-developed, so getting around mainly comes down to three options: hiring a private driver, taking taxis, or renting a car or scooter.

1. **Private Driver**: This is the most common and convenient option, especially for families traveling with elderly parents and children. With a private driver, you can tailor your itinerary flexibly, adjusting times and destinations. Drivers often double as guides, recommending local hidden gems and restaurants.
2. **Taxis**: For short trips, taxis are a good choice. It's best to use local ride-hailing apps like Grab or Gojek, which offer transparent pricing and convenience.
3. **Renting a Car or Scooter**: For travelers who love independence, renting a car or scooter is a great option. However, be aware that traffic in Bali can be chaotic, and driving habits differ from those in Taiwan. This option is best for experienced drivers.

This time, we chose a private driver, which not only made the trip more relaxing but also allowed my family to rest in the car and recharge for the adventures ahead. While Bali's transportation isn't as convenient as in big cities, with proper planning, the journey can still be smooth and enjoyable.

### **Taxis & Traffic Conditions**

We visited during the rainy season, which is pretty similar everywhere—ordering food or hailing a taxi is smooth on sunny days, but once it rains, everything changes. So, on days without a private driver, avoid planning trips that are too far; a 30-minute drive is the absolute limit. I suggest including popular attractions on days when you have a driver booked, otherwise, getting a ride back can be pricey, and in bad weather, you might not even find one.

We experienced an extreme case of ordering food on a rainy day: we placed an order at 11 AM, and by 1 PM, no driver had accepted it. Eventually, Grab gave up and let me cancel the order! Luckily, we had stocked up on instant noodles, vegetables, and eggs at the supermarket the day before, so we didn't go hungry.

Traffic in Bali is indeed far from ideal, mainly because the road planning is quite haphazard. Often, places that look close on the map require five times the travel time to reach by a long detour (sometimes I really feel like climbing over the wall!).

### **Grab Ride-Hailing Experience**

Most Grab drivers are decent, but we did encounter one who asked us to cancel the ride after picking us up, so he wouldn't have to pay the platform fee. When we arrived, he jokingly asked, 'No tip?' In such situations, just stay calm—every place has its own culture and habits.

This trip was all about kicking back and relaxing, so I focused on booking villas. Browsing through Airbnb felt like a tour of luxury homes, with all kinds of extravagant villas dazzling me. It took about three days to snap out of those dreamy options and start seriously considering practical needs.

In Bali, the more luxurious the villa, the more remote its location tends to be, which can mean less convenience compared to city-center stays. So, based on my family’s personalities and needs, I tried to strike a balance between "luxury" and "convenience." The villas I booked were all in areas where Grab food delivery or taxis were easy to arrange, ensuring we could handle meals and transportation when needed.

That said, this accommodation experience taught me a few lessons. Bali villas often design common spaces—like living rooms, dining areas, and kitchens—as outdoor or semi-outdoor areas to bring guests closer to nature. Next time, I’ll pay more attention and try to choose villas with air-conditioned, enclosed common spaces, so I can enjoy a comfortable environment without the bug problem.

The booking process went very smoothly. When it comes to accommodation, this villa stay made our whole family very satisfied. Especially being able to enjoy a leisurely vacation in a private space is truly ideal for a family trip with elderly parents and children.

## **Dining Experience**

Bali’s water is quite hard, which was a bit painful for someone like me who loves tea. Fortunately, a friend’s sister who lives in Bali reminded me that even bottled water from the supermarket produces tea far inferior to what I’d brew back in Taiwan. She suggested bringing tea bags made from broken leaves or stems, as they yield a more acceptable brew. Although I still brought some nice red oolong tea leaves, after the first day of using tea bags I brought from Taiwan, I gave up completely—I didn’t even use the travel gaiwan tea set I brought.

### Local Food

When it came to food, I tried local street stalls two or three times—things like crispy roast pork rice and satay—but quickly gave up. The locals' standards for ingredient quality didn't quite match our family's expectations, so afterward I mostly chose restaurants frequented by foreigners. These places tend to cater to foreign palates, even adapting local dishes to be safer in flavor, which is especially friendly for those who don't like spicy food.

### Dairy Products and Desserts

My travel companion loves dairy products and kept looking for local fresh milk. It's important to emphasize that Bali has no livestock industry, so there's no dairy production. If you apply Taiwan's standards to find good milk, you'll only waste money. Compared to twenty years ago when my first trip to Bali offered only powdered milk and UHT milk, now there are some products labeled 'Fresh milk' in English that come close, but they're far from ideal for drinking straight—only good for adding to coffee or tea.

Similarly, Bali doesn't produce flour, and locals' taste for sweets isn't very Westernized, with little emphasis on quality. So I wouldn't recommend spending time or money here looking for good Western-style pastries or bread. This is Bali, not Korea or Japan—adjust your expectations accordingly for such foods.

## **Transportation & Itinerary Planning**

As mentioned earlier, Bali's road design lacks comprehensive planning, resulting in traffic jams for more than two-thirds of the day, almost around the clock. So, if you have plans for the day—especially for attractions more than an hour's drive from your accommodation—**it's absolutely recommended** to set off by 7 or 8 AM. Otherwise, the traffic alone can drain half your energy, leaving you exhausted for the rest of the day.

When planning your itinerary, try to group attractions in the same area together. Don't fantasize about covering more than 40 kilometers in a single day! Because Bali's roads are narrow and the Lunar New Year falls during the island's [third peak season of the year](/en/blog/bali-peak-season-travel-tips/), a trip of less than 40 kilometers can easily take two hours. **Don't pack your schedule too tightly just because the distance looks short on the map**—you'll only end up stuck in traffic, frustrated, and lose all the joy of travel.

### Family Travel Tips

For family trips, especially with elderly and children, I highly recommend adopting a "**one day with a driver, one day off**" approach. This not only gives everyone enough time to rest and relax between activities but also helps avoid fatigue from long commutes.

For example, on the day you hire a driver, you can plan visits to farther attractions or popular activities like Ubud Market, the rice terraces, or Tanah Lot Temple. On the rest day, you can enjoy the private pool at your villa, get a spa treatment, or have a leisurely meal at a nearby restaurant. This pace keeps the elderly comfortable and gives kids enough time to burn off energy and rest.

## **Massage Experience**

I didn't have high expectations for massages in Bali. In my experience, whether budget or luxury, the pressure tends to be too light for Taiwanese people—like a gentle touch on the muscles. For those used to deep tissue work, it never feels quite enough. Even when I chose the strongest pressure and repeatedly asked the therapist to go harder, the effect was still limited.

This time in Ubud, I tried the Deep Tissue Massage at Nata Spa. The pressure was indeed heavier than typical Balinese massages, and it felt quite pleasant—I even dozed off for most of it. While it was much better than the "gentle touch" feeling I'd had before, it still left me wanting a bit more. However, our driver mentioned that massages in Kuta and Seminyak are more impressive, while Ubud's are relatively average, so I wasn't disappointed.

### Surprise Discovery: Hot Bamboo SPA

![驚喜發現：Hot Bamboo SPA](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_c72ed5775df8b39abb816fd5c7abd11d.jpg)

After arriving in Canggu, I started searching for nearby massage places and stumbled upon "Hot Bamboo SPA." After reading the reviews, I was immediately drawn to its uniqueness. They offer a massage using warm bamboo, hence the name "Hot Bamboo." The reviews were overwhelmingly positive, especially about the pressure—claiming it's the most intense massage in Bali.

So, I decided to go for it! However, this spa was unexpectedly popular and one of the few that require a deposit when booking, payable only in cash or via bank transfer to an Indonesian account. The massage fee also can't be paid by card—only cash. Luckily, with the help of our driver, I asked him to transfer the deposit, and we successfully secured the appointment.

### Simple Yet Professional Choices

Unlike the overwhelming menus at other massage places, Hot Bamboo SPA keeps it refreshingly simple with just three massage options:

1. **Relaxing Bali Aga** (gentle relaxation)
2. **Hot Bamboo** (warm bamboo massage)
3. **Strong Barefoot** (intense foot massage)

The pressure ranges from light to firm, and you can choose between 60, 90, or 120 minutes—straightforward and clear. The interior decor is clean and artsy, dominated by natural bamboo and linen tones, creating a cozy and natural ambiance.

### Strong Barefoot Massage Experience

Without hesitation, we went for the firmest option, the 'Strong Barefoot' massage, booking a 2-hour session. The pressure was truly on point—firm yet gentle. When the therapist worked on my back, I initially thought about asking for a lighter touch, but within a minute, I felt completely relaxed and even started to doze off. I couldn't bring myself to ask them to ease up.

This massage experience completely changed my view of Balinese massages. Hot Bamboo SPA's professionalism and unique techniques truly deliver a full-body and mind relaxation. If you enjoy firmer massages, this SPA is definitely worth a try!

## **Final Thoughts**

This family trip to Bali, though challenging with elderly parents and young children, brought us countless unforgettable memories and valuable experiences. From flights, accommodation, and transportation to food and massages, every detail deepened my understanding that travel is not just about exploring the world, but also about creating beautiful moments with family.

Bali's charm lies in its diversity and inclusiveness—whether it's luxurious villas, unique massage experiences, or nature-close landscapes, everyone can find their own way to unwind. While the traffic and food habits may require some adjustment, with advance planning and flexibility, these small challenges can actually make the journey richer and more enjoyable.

This trip taught me that the most important thing about traveling with family isn't how packed the itinerary is, but whether everyone feels comfortable and happy along the way. Bali's slow pace and warm hospitality provided the perfect space for us to relax. I hope this travelogue offers practical insights for future travelers to Bali, and I look forward to our next journey, creating more wonderful memories with my family!
