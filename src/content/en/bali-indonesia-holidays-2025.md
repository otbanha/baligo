- January 1st (New Year's Day) is a public holiday in almost every country, including Indonesia and Bali, with celebrations peaking on New Year's Eve (Dec 31). During this period, Indonesians enjoy a ~~spontaneous~~ "Nataru" holiday—see the festival section at the bottom of December for details.
- January 27th marks [Siwa Ratri], a key event in the Balinese calendar emphasizing introspection, meditation, and seeking forgiveness. Rituals include staying awake for 36 hours, fasting for 24 hours, and observing silence for 12 hours. Afterward, purification ceremonies often take place on beaches, along with rituals at major temples across the island.
- January 27th, 2025, also coincides with Indonesia's national holiday "Isra' Mi'raj" (the Prophet Muhammad's Ascension). This important Islamic festival commemorates the Prophet's journey to heaven, with Muslims gathering in mosques for prayer and worship.
- January 29th is our Chinese New Year! Indonesia has designated the first day of the lunar new year (Jan 29, 2025) as a national holiday. In Taiwan, this means a nine-day break from Jan 25 (Sat) to Feb 2 (Sun). While the 2025 Chinese New Year holiday doesn't directly align with Bali's calendar, it's a peak travel period for tourists from China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan. Plus, since Jan 27 and 29 are Indonesian holidays, and Jan 28 is a flexible day off, many locals enjoy a five-day break. If they take leave on Jan 30 and 31, they can stretch it to nine days, leading many Indonesians to vacation in Bali. So, our Lunar New Year is also a high season for Bali travel! ### **Day 23: Galungan & Kuningan (Balinese Hindu Festival)**

- In 2025, Indonesia's Eid al-Fitr holiday begins on April 1, with a nationwide break of about ten days. Eid is the most important holiday in Indonesia. While it celebrates the end of the Muslim fasting month of Ramadan, it's also a time for Indonesians to spend with family and friends, much like the Chinese Lunar New Year.
  Expect Bali to start seeing an influx of Indonesian locals from around March 24, with the island packed with domestic tourists for about two weeks. This is the busiest of Bali's five peak seasons—a holiday you're advised to avoid visiting Bali!

![往年的開齋節假期在峇里島的盛況](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_f3a789be6961d56dd62ec22568e70d10.png)

Scenes from past Eid al-Fitr holidays in Bali

The Eid al-Fitr period is also known as "Lebaran." For an introduction to Eid al-Fitr, check out Xiao Jie's video:

- April 18 is a Christian holiday. Under Indonesia's Pancasila principle of equality among five major religions, this major Christian holiday is also a national holiday in Indonesia.
- Starting April 23, Galungan & Kuningan marks one of Bali's most important festivals. Galungan on April 23 lasts until Kuningan ten days later, celebrating the creation of the universe and the triumph of good over evil. During this festival, towering penjor decorations bring a vibrant atmosphere to the streets, while abundant offerings, rituals, and celebrations offer a glimpse into the island's rich culture and spirituality. Families honor their ancestors at home, seeking their blessings, and hold ceremonies at temples to pay respect to the gods. According to the Balinese Pawukon calendar, Galungan is also the day of the universe's creation. Schools in Bali close for two weeks during this period, highlighting its significance! Be aware that transportation like ferries, private drivers, and taxis will be affected. Many local guides and drivers take a few days off during the main celebrations, so it's best to book in advance. The day after Galungan (Manis Galungan) is when locals visit relatives or go out together, so you might find the roads especially crowded. Large celebrations can also cause traffic congestion.

## FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions)

**1. Does Nyepi Day in Bali affect tourism?**

Yes, on Nyepi Day, all commercial activities and transportation come to a halt, and even the airport is closed. Travelers must stay inside their hotels, but it's a unique opportunity to experience Balinese culture.

**2. Do I need to buy tickets for Bali's festivals?**

Most festival events are open to the public and free of charge, especially ceremonies held within temples. However, participants are required to dress appropriately and observe proper etiquette.

**3. When is the best time to attend cultural festivals in Bali?**

The period from March to May and November to December each year is ideal for experiencing several major festivals, including Nyepi Day of Silence, Galungan, and Kuningan.

**4. How can I respectfully participate in Bali's festivals?**

It is recommended to wear a traditional sarong and sash, and to follow local customs and taboos, avoiding any inappropriate behavior.

**5. Are Bali's festivals suitable for families?**

Yes! Many festivals not only allow children to experience a different cultural atmosphere but also strengthen family bonds through participation. However, some ceremonies may last for extended periods, so families are advised to plan their schedules carefully to prevent children from getting too tired.

**6. Do Bali's festivals affect traffic?**

Indeed they do, especially during major festivals like Galungan or Kuningan, where some streets are temporarily closed for parades or activities. It's advisable to plan your itinerary early or opt for walking, which also allows you to enjoy the festivities up close.

**7. Is it allowed to photograph local festival ceremonies?**

Photography is generally allowed, but it's best to ask locals or your guide first as a sign of respect, especially in temples or during traditional ceremonies. Some ritual events may have restrictions, so staying respectful and cautious with your camera is a polite gesture.

**8. What special festival foods can you try in Bali?**

During festivals, travelers can enjoy many local traditional dishes, such as Babi Guling (suckling pig) and Lawar (a mix of vegetables and meat). These foods often carry deep cultural and ritual significance, making them well worth trying.

**9. How can you avoid cultural conflicts during festivals?**

Respecting local culture is crucial. It's recommended that travelers learn about festival-related taboos in advance—such as staying quiet on Nyepi Day or avoiding taking photos of forbidden scenes—and always observe local behavior to adapt accordingly.

**10. How do Balinese festivals strengthen community bonds?**

Festival events are often grand occasions for villages or communities, where residents prepare and participate together, deepening their emotional connections and fostering a stronger sense of unity. Joining these activities is not only a cultural experience but also a chance to feel the warmth and friendliness of the locals.
