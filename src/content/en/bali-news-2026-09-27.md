# Trending Topics & News Roundup for Bali Travelers | September 27, 2026

## 🚤 Nusa Penida Fully Suspended Tomorrow! September 27 is Nyepi Segara

If you had plans to visit **Nusa Penida** tomorrow, take note.

Because September 27 (Sunday) marks **Nyepi Segara (Day of Ocean Silence)** on Nusa Penida, all ferry services from Sanur to Nusa Penida and Lembongan will be suspended for the day.

According to the schedule, **the Sanur–Nusa Penida route will be closed all day on September 27**, including regular speedboats and other sea transport.

Nyepi Segara is an important religious tradition on Nusa Penida, during which all sea activities are halted and locals observe related rituals.

So if you had planned:

**9/27 Sanur → Nusa Penida → 9/27 return to Bali**

This itinerary will need to be rearranged.

If you've already purchased ferry tickets, it's best to contact the company directly to confirm a reschedule to **September 28 or September 29**.

This isn't just a 'reduced ferry schedule'—it's a **full-day suspension of service**. Don't show up at Sanur Harbor only to find out you can't go that day.

## 🚨 Russian Tourist Missing in Pecatu; Motorcycle and Helmet Found Near Cliff

The top safety news for travelers in Bali today comes from **Pecatu, South Kuta**.

A Russian national is reported missing in the **Tanjung Mebulu** area. Rescue teams were alerted at 5:40 AM on September 26, and according to friends, the individual has been uncontactable since **September 23**.

What has drawn the attention of search teams is a report from locals: an unidentified motorcycle and helmet were found near **Tebing Karang Boma**.

The Denpasar Search and Rescue Center dispatched seven personnel, working with police and local authorities to search the cliffs of Tanjung Mebulu, Karang Boma, and the surrounding coastline.

**As of now, rescue teams have not confirmed the whereabouts of the foreign national, nor can they conclude that a cliff fall has occurred.**

For travelers, this is also a very practical reminder: the Pecatu and Uluwatu area is full of cliffside views—don't get too close to unguarded cliff edges just for a photo angle.

## 🦎 Komodo National Park's Annual Quota Used Up Early; Only 500 Additional Visitors Allowed Per Day from September to December

Although Komodo National Park is not in Bali, many travelers head there from Bali to **Labuan Bajo and Komodo Island**, so this policy directly affects Bali's tourism market.

The latest update shows that **Komodo National Park's annual visitor quota of 365,000 for 2026 was already exhausted by August.**

Originally, the daily limit was **1,000 visitors**. Now, the management of Indonesia's Komodo National Park has decided to add extra slots from September to December, but only **500 additional visitors per day**.

In other words, from now until the end of the year, only 500 new visitors can enter Komodo National Park each day.

The management noted that one reason the annual quota was used up so quickly is that some tourists' **purchase dates don't match their actual entry dates**, so they are also working on improving the ticketing system.

It's important to note that this restriction is an entry policy for **Komodo National Park**, not a travel restriction for Bali.

If your itinerary is:

**Taiwan / Hong Kong → Bali → Labuan Bajo → Komodo**

It's best not to leave your Komodo itinerary until the last minute, especially during the peak season at year-end.

## 🌴 Sanur Is Changing: From a Traditional Fishing Village to a Mature Tourist Area

There's an interesting article about Sanur today too.

Actually, it's hard to imagine now, but Sanur was once primarily a **traditional fishing village** that relied on the sea for a living.

With the growth of tourism, hotels, restaurants, resorts, and various travel facilities have gradually appeared along Sanur's coast, transforming it from a traditional fishing settlement into a key tourist destination in Bali.

But one of the biggest differences between Sanur and places like Canggu or Seminyak is that it still retains much of its original community life and traditional culture.

For travelers, this is also why Sanur has been attracting more attention in recent years from **families, senior travelers, and those who enjoy a laid-back vacation**.

Especially after the improvements at Sanur Harbor, transportation to Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan has become more centralized, gradually transforming Sanur from a 'one-night beach area' into a true travel base where you can comfortably arrange a multi-day stay.
