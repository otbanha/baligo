To protect this fragile paradise, the Indonesian government has strictly capped daily visitors to Komodo National Park. The old days of showing up at the dock and hopping on a boat on a whim are over. Now, every encounter with the giant lizards requires precise reservations and advance planning.

## Only 1,000 People Allowed In Per Day

Komodo National Park (Taman Nasional Komodo) has officially announced a daily visitor cap, allowing a maximum of 1,000 tourists per day. That translates to an annual limit of 365,000 people.

This number isn't arbitrary. Back in 2018, research institutions assessed the park's ecological carrying capacity and determined the optimal annual visitor count to be around 366,000. At the time, there was no need for restrictions, as yearly visitors were under 200,000.

But by 2025, tourist numbers surged past 420,000, blowing right through that limit.

![每天只放 1,000 人進去](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-04/1776404897217-fdf1ae42c54b9b350a38e08a3ccc880d.jpg)

## Why the Cap Now?

To be honest, Komodo's meteoric rise in popularity over the past few years has been staggering. This year, it snagged [two first-place spots](/en/blog/komodo-national-park-global-award/) in Time Out's ranking of the 'World's Most Beautiful Destinations,' and the park's Pink Beach was even named the world's most beautiful beach by Forbes.

With that kind of media buzz, it's no wonder tourist numbers exploded.

Facing the pressure of 'overtourism,' the park authorities decided to directly reference the research data from eight years ago and officially implement a visitor cap mechanism. Although some critics argue that the data is outdated, the park's stance is clear: the data is objective, so they'll stick with it.

## How Does the Visitor Cap Work?

Currently, this policy is being implemented on a trial basis, and the rules may be adjusted based on the results. For now, it's not a park-wide restriction but rather targeted controls on specific areas.

In practice, if you show up last minute, the daily quotas for Padar Island or Komodo Island might already be full, and you'll be turned away at the gate.

The peak season runs from July to September each year, making it the hardest time to snag tickets on the spot.

## Is This Good or Bad for Travelers?

Honestly, for those who plan ahead, this is actually good news.

Fewer people, a better environment, and the Komodo dragon's habitat remains undisturbed—so when you visit, you'll see real wildlife instead of a crowd jostling on the viewing platform.

The ones most affected are travelers who prefer spontaneous trips without any preparation. In Komodo, that habit might need to change.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: How many people are allowed into Komodo National Park each day?**

A: The current daily cap is 1,000 visitors, with an annual limit of 365,000 people.

**Q: Does the visitor cap apply to the entire park?**

A: Currently, it's managed per specific area. For example, Komodo Island and Padar Island each have their own daily quotas, rather than a single shared limit for the whole park.

**Q: When is peak season? Are there any special considerations before going?**

A: Peak season is from July to September each year, when spots are hardest to secure. It's best to book at least 1 to 2 months in advance during peak season, and don't wait too long even in the off-season to avoid disappointment.

**Q: Is it possible to just show up and queue for entry?**

A: In theory, yes, if there are still spots available that day, but in practice, it's nearly impossible during peak season. We recommend not taking the risk and booking ahead to be safe.

**Q: Is this capacity control policy permanent?**

A: It is currently being implemented on a trial basis. The park will evaluate the results and decide whether to adjust the rules, so changes are possible in the future.

**Q: How do I get to Komodo National Park?**

A: The most common way is to fly from Bali to Labuan Bajo, then take a boat from there to the various islands. Most travelers opt for a private boat tour, either a day trip or a multi-day excursion.

**Q: How can I guarantee a spot on Komodo Island?**

A: The most reliable way is to book a package tour through a local tour operator, who will secure entry permits in advance—no need to scramble for them yourself. If you're planning independently, book through a travel agency with local resources at least 1 to 2 months ahead, especially during peak season (July to September).
