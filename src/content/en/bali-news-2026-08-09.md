# Top Topics & News Roundup for Bali Travelers (August 9, 2026)

Here are 5 Bali-related news stories, focusing on safety, policies, transportation, and activities that matter to travelers.

## 🧘 Australian Tourist Deported for Allegedly Teaching Yoga in Bali on a Tourist Visa

Bali's Singaraja Immigration Office reported that a 55-year-old Australian man was suspected of using a tourist visa for activities inconsistent with its purpose. Not only did he serve as a meditation and yoga instructor, but he also hosted a yoga retreat in Buleleng Regency. He was eventually deported by Indonesian authorities and recommended for a blacklist.

Traveler's tip: If you're just sightseeing or joining a legitimate class, you're generally fine. But if you plan to openly recruit students, charge fees, or host events in Bali, make sure your visa and activity permits comply with regulations.

## 🚨 Two Indian Nationals Caught at Ngurah Rai Airport with Cannabis Resin Worth IDR 1.5 Billion

Two Indian nationals, a man and a woman, were intercepted at Ngurah Rai International Airport with approximately 10 kg of cannabis resin hidden in their luggage, valued at around IDR 1.5 billion. Customs officials said the suspects packed the drugs inside children's toy boxes and stuffed them into two suitcases to avoid detection. The pair has been handed over to police for further investigation.

Traveler's tip: Never carry any packages, toys, food, or luggage for strangers, and don't accept any unverified shipping requests. Indonesia enforces strict drug laws, and foreign travelers are not exempt.

## 📊 3.2 Million International Tourists Visited Bali in the First Half of the Year, Still Indonesia's Strongest Tourism Magnet

According to data from the Bali Provincial Statistics Bureau, a total of 3,203,156 international tourists visited Bali from January to June 2026, a slight 2.42% decrease compared to the same period last year, but the overall numbers remain impressive. In terms of source countries, Australian tourists still rank first, followed by Chinese tourists.

Traveler Tip: Peak seasons like summer vacation and year-end holidays will continue to see high demand. It's advisable to book popular hotels, villas, private drivers, and island-hopping tours well in advance to avoid last-minute unavailability.

## 🏛️ Museum Bali in Denpasar Will Close for Renovations from August 10 to End of November

Museum Bali has announced it will close to the public starting August 10, 2026, with renovations expected to last until the end of November. The renovation primarily focuses on roof replacements for three buildings: Gedung Buleleng, Tabanan, and Karangasem. During the closure, general visitors will not be able to enter, but administrative, library, and research services will remain open.

Traveler Tip: If you have Museum Bali on your itinerary, be sure to avoid dates after August 10. To explore Balinese cultural history, consider visiting other museums or traditional village attractions instead.
