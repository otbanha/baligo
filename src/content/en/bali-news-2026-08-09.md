# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup (August 9, 2026)

Here are 5 Bali-related news stories, focusing on safety, policies, transportation, and events that travelers care about.

## 🧘 Australian Tourist Suspected of Violating Visa Rules by Teaching Yoga in Bali, Deported

Bali's Singaraja Immigration Office reported that a 55-year-old Australian man was suspected of using a tourist visa to engage in activities inconsistent with its purpose. Not only did he serve as a meditation and yoga instructor, but he also organized a yoga retreat in Buleleng Regency. He was eventually deported by Indonesian authorities and recommended for a blacklist. Traveler advice: If you're just a regular tourist or attending legitimate classes, you're usually fine. However, if you plan to openly recruit students, charge fees, or host events in Bali, make sure to check visa and activity permit requirements first.

## 🚨 Two Indian Travelers Caught with Cannabis Butter Worth IDR 1.5 Billion at Ngurah Rai Airport

Two Indian nationals, a man and a woman, were caught at Ngurah Rai International Airport with approximately 10 kilograms of cannabis butter hidden in their luggage, valued at around IDR 1.5 billion. Customs officials stated that the suspects had packed the drugs inside children's toy boxes and then stuffed them into two suitcases in an attempt to evade detection. The pair has been handed over to the police for further investigation. Traveler advice: Never carry any packages, toys, food, or luggage for strangers, and do not accept any consignment requests from unknown sources. Indonesia enforces strict drug laws, and foreign travelers are not exempt.

## 📊 Bali Welcomes 3.2 Million International Visitors in First Half of 2026

According to data from the Central Statistics Agency (BPS), Bali recorded 3.2 million international tourist arrivals in the first half of 2026. The top five source countries are Australia, China, India, the United Kingdom, and the United States. This figure represents a 15% increase compared to the same period last year, indicating a strong recovery in Bali's tourism industry. Traveler advice: With the surge in tourists, popular spots like Ubud, Canggu, and Seminyak can get crowded. It's recommended to book accommodations and activities in advance.

## 📊 3.2 Million International Tourists Visited Bali in H1, Still Indonesia's Strongest Tourism Magnet

According to data from the Bali Provincial Statistics Office, 3,203,156 international tourists visited Bali from January to June 2026. Although this represents a slight 2.42% decline compared to the same period last year, the overall numbers remain impressive. In terms of source countries, Australian tourists still rank first, followed by Chinese tourists.

Travel Tip: Demand remains high during summer and year-end peak seasons. It's recommended to book popular hotels, villas, private drivers, and island excursions early to avoid last-minute unavailability.

## 🏛️ Museum Bali in Denpasar Will Close for Renovations from August 10 to End of November

Museum Bali has announced it will close to the public starting August 10, 2026, with renovations expected to last until the end of November. The renovation focuses on roof replacements for three buildings: Gedung Buleleng, Tabanan, and Karangasem. During the closure, general visitors will not be allowed inside, but administrative, library, and research services will remain open.

Travel Tip: If you have Museum Bali on your itinerary, avoid visiting after August 10. To explore Balinese cultural history, consider alternative museums or traditional village attractions instead.
