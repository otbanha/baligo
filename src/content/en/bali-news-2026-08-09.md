# Bali Hot Topics & News Roundup (2026/8/9)

Here are 5 Bali-related news stories, focusing on safety, policies, transportation, and events that travelers care about.

## 🧘 Australian Tourist Deported for Allegedly Teaching Yoga in Bali on a Tourist Visa

Bali's Singaraja Immigration Office reported that a 55-year-old Australian man was suspected of using a tourist visa for activities inconsistent with its purpose. He not only served as a meditation and yoga instructor but also hosted a yoga retreat in Buleleng Regency. He was eventually deported by Indonesian authorities and recommended for a blacklist. Traveler advice: If you're just traveling normally or attending legitimate classes, you're usually fine. But if you plan to openly recruit students, charge for lessons, or host events in Bali, make sure your visa and activity permits comply with regulations.

## 🚨 Two Indian Nationals Caught with Cannabis Butter Worth IDR 1.5 Billion at Ngurah Rai Airport

Two Indian nationals, a man and a woman, were caught at Ngurah Rai International Airport with approximately 10 kg of cannabis butter hidden in their luggage, valued at around IDR 1.5 billion. Customs officials said the suspects packed the drugs inside children's toy boxes and then stuffed them into two suitcases in an attempt to avoid detection. The pair has been handed over to the police for further investigation. Traveler advice: Never carry any packages, toys, food, or luggage for strangers, and don't accept any consignment requests from unknown sources. Indonesia enforces strict drug laws, and foreign tourists are not exempt.

## 📊 Bali Welcomes Over 2.5 Million International Tourists in First Half of 2026

According to data from the Bali Central Statistics Agency, Bali received 2,538,000 international tourists in the first half of 2026, a 12% increase compared to the same period last year. The top five source countries are Australia, China, India, the UK, and the US. Traveler advice: With the surge in tourist numbers, popular spots like Ubud, Canggu, and Seminyak can get crowded. It's recommended to book accommodations and activities in advance.

## 📊 3.2 Million International Tourists Visited Bali in H1, Still Indonesia's Strongest Tourism Magnet

According to data from the Bali Provincial Statistics Office, 3,203,156 international tourists visited Bali from January to June 2026. Although this represents a slight 2.42% decline compared to the same period last year, the overall numbers remain impressive. In terms of source countries, Australian tourists still rank first, followed by Chinese tourists.

Travel Tip: Demand will remain high during summer and year-end peak seasons. It's recommended to book popular hotels, villas, private drivers, and island-hopping tours early to avoid last-minute sellouts.

## 🏛️ Museum Bali in Denpasar Will Close for Renovations from August 10 to End of November

Museum Bali has announced it will close to the public starting August 10, 2026, with renovations expected to last until the end of November. The renovation focuses on roof replacements for three buildings: Gedung Buleleng, Tabanan, and Karangasem. During the closure, general visitors will not be allowed inside, but administrative, library, and research services will remain open.

Travel Tip: If you have Museum Bali on your itinerary, be sure to avoid dates after August 10. To explore Balinese cultural history, consider visiting other museums or traditional village attractions instead.
