# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup (2026/8/9)

Here are 5 Bali-related news stories, focusing on safety, policies, transportation, and events that travelers care about.

## 🧘 Australian Tourist Deported for Allegedly Teaching Yoga in Bali in Violation of Visa Rules

Bali's Singaraja Immigration Office reported that a 55-year-old Australian man was suspected of using a tourist visa to engage in activities inconsistent with its purpose. Not only did he serve as a meditation and yoga instructor, but he also hosted a yoga retreat in Buleleng Regency. He was eventually deported by Indonesian authorities and recommended for a blacklist. Traveler advice: If you're just traveling normally or attending legitimate classes, you're usually fine. But if you plan to openly recruit students, charge fees, or host events in Bali, make sure to confirm your visa and activity permits comply with regulations.

## 🚨 Two Indian Nationals Caught with Cannabis Butter Worth IDR 1.5 Billion at Ngurah Rai Airport

Two Indian nationals, a man and a woman, were caught at Ngurah Rai International Airport with approximately 10 kg of cannabis butter hidden in their luggage, valued at around IDR 1.5 billion. Customs officials stated that the suspects had packed the drugs inside children's toy boxes and then stuffed them into two suitcases in an attempt to avoid detection. The pair has been handed over to the police for further investigation. Traveler advice: Never carry any packages, toys, food, or luggage for strangers, and do not accept any consignment requests of unknown origin. Indonesia enforces strict drug laws, and foreign travelers are not exempt.

## 📊 Bali Welcomes Over 2.9 Million International Visitors in First Half of 2026

According to data from the Bali Central Statistics Agency, Bali received 2,931,000 international visitors in the first half of 2026, a 20.4% increase compared to the same period last year. Australia remains the largest source market, followed by India, China, the UK, and the US. Traveler advice: The peak season is approaching, so it's recommended to book flights and accommodations early. If you want to avoid crowds, consider visiting during the shoulder season.

## 📊 3.2 Million International Visitors to Bali in First Half of the Year, Still Indonesia's Strongest Tourism Magnet

According to data from the Bali Provincial Statistics Office, 3,203,156 international tourists visited Bali from January to June 2026. Although this represents a slight 2.42% decline compared to the same period last year, the overall numbers remain impressive. In terms of source countries, Australian tourists still rank first, followed by Chinese tourists.

Traveler Tips: Demand will remain high during summer and year-end peak seasons. It's recommended to book popular hotels, villas, private drivers, and island-hopping tours well in advance to avoid last-minute sellouts.

## 🏛️ Museum Bali in Denpasar to Close for Renovations from August 10 to End of November

Museum Bali has announced it will close to the public starting August 10, 2026, with renovations expected to last until the end of November. The renovation primarily involves roof replacements for three buildings: Gedung Buleleng, Tabanan, and Karangasem. During the closure, general visitors will not be allowed inside, but administrative, library, and research services will remain open.

Traveler Tips: If you have Museum Bali on your itinerary, be sure to avoid dates after August 10. To explore Balinese cultural history, consider visiting other museums or traditional village attractions instead.
