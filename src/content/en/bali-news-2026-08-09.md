# Bali Hot Topics & News Roundup (August 9, 2026)

Here are 5 Bali-related news stories, focusing on safety, policies, transportation, and events that travelers care about.

## 🧘 Australian Tourist Suspected of Violating Visa Rules by Teaching Yoga in Bali, Deported

Bali's Singaraja Immigration Office reported that a 55-year-old Australian man was suspected of using a tourist visa for activities inconsistent with its purpose. Not only did he serve as a meditation and yoga instructor, but he also hosted a yoga retreat in Buleleng Regency. He was eventually deported by Indonesian authorities and recommended for a blacklist.

Traveler Advice: If you're just traveling normally or attending legitimate classes, you're usually fine. But if you plan to openly recruit students, charge fees for teaching, or host events in Bali, make sure to check visa and activity permit requirements first.

## 🚨 Two Indian Nationals Caught with Cannabis Butter Worth IDR 1.5 Billion at Ngurah Rai Airport

Two Indian nationals, a man and a woman, were caught at Ngurah Rai International Airport with approximately 10 kilograms of cannabis butter hidden in their luggage, valued at around IDR 1.5 billion. Customs officials stated that the suspects had packed the drugs inside children's toy boxes and then stuffed them into two suitcases in an attempt to evade detection. The pair has been handed over to the police for further investigation.

Traveler Advice: Never carry any packages, toys, food, or luggage for strangers, and do not accept any consignment requests from unknown sources. Indonesia enforces strict drug laws, and foreign travelers are not exempt.

## 📊 3.2 Million International Visitors to Bali in First Half of the Year, Still Indonesia's Strongest Tourist Magnet

According to data from the Bali Provincial Statistics Office, 3,203,156 international tourists visited Bali from January to June 2026. Although this represents a slight 2.42% decrease compared to the same period last year, the overall number remains impressive. In terms of source countries, Australian tourists still rank first, followed by Chinese tourists.

Traveler Tip: Demand remains high during summer and year-end peak seasons. It's recommended to book popular hotels, villas, private drivers, and island-hopping tours early to avoid last-minute unavailability.

## 🏛️ Museum Bali in Denpasar to Close for Renovations from August 10 to End of November

Museum Bali has announced it will close to the public starting August 10, 2026, with renovations expected to last until the end of November. The renovation primarily involves roof updates for three buildings: Gedung Buleleng, Tabanan, and Karangasem. During the closure, general visitors will not be allowed inside, but administrative, library, and research services will remain open.

Traveler Tip: If you have Museum Bali on your itinerary, be sure to avoid dates after August 10. To explore Balinese cultural history, consider visiting other museums or traditional village attractions instead.
