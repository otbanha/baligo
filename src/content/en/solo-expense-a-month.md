# **How much does it cost to solo travel in Bali for a month? How comfortable can US$1,800–2,400 get you?**

If you've ever thought, 'Why not just stay in Bali for a month instead of just visiting?'—how much budget would you actually need?

Here's a realistic breakdown:

**Around 30 years old, not trying to pinch pennies, but not chasing luxury either—wanting a comfortable stay, eating out daily, keeping up with exercise, and occasionally enjoying the Bali lifestyle.**

For this kind of lifestyle, I'd estimate:

## **US$1,800–2,400 per month**

You don't need to stay in five-star hotels every night, nor survive on instant noodles to save money.

**A quick note: All of the following budgets are for one person.**

This estimate is based on a **solo traveler in their 30s staying in Bali for a full month**.

It's not a backpacker budget mode, nor a five-star resort vacation. Instead, it's about living comfortably, eating regular meals, having space for exercise and work, and occasionally treating yourself to a massage, surf session, sauna, or other experiences on weekends.

**All costs—accommodation, food, transportation, fitness, and coworking spaces—are calculated for one person.**

If you're traveling with a partner, accommodation and some living expenses can be split, usually bringing the per-person cost down a bit. But for a family trip, you'd need to factor in kids, room types, activities, and transportation separately.

So, the **US$1,800–2,400/month** below can be understood as:

**"How much does it cost for one person to live comfortably in Bali for 30 days?"**

Here's the breakdown.

## **🏠 Accommodation: US$800–1,200/month**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787753525566-stay.jpg)

Let's start with the most important part: where you'll stay.

For a month-long stay in Bali, I wouldn't recommend thinking of your accommodation budget as a "per-night" cost. Instead, look for **monthly rental apartments**.

In areas like Canggu, where daily amenities are more complete, you can find standard apartment-style accommodations.

For about **US$800/month**, you can find a one-bedroom apartment.

It may not have any luxurious decor, but it covers basic living needs like a bedroom, bathroom, air conditioning, and Wi-Fi.

More importantly, these apartments usually come with a **shared large swimming pool**.

Swim in the morning, take a dip in the afternoon, and head out for dinner in the evening—this lifestyle feels completely different from staying in a regular hotel.

And if you choose a good location, there are usually restaurants, cafes, convenience stores, supermarkets, and gyms nearby.

If you're willing to raise your accommodation budget to **US$1200/month**, you can look for a more comfortable **one-bedroom, one-living-room** unit.

In addition to a separate bedroom, you get a proper living room and kitchen.

This difference is actually quite significant.

Because staying for a month is different from staying for just three to five days.

For short stays, you might be out exploring attractions every day, with the room serving only as a place to sleep.

But after staying for a month, you'll start craving a comfortable sofa, your own workspace, a kitchen to store things, and even a living room to scroll through your phone at night instead of always being stuck in bed.

So if your budget allows, I personally recommend:

**US$800: Sufficient, practical, and includes a pool.**

**US$1200: One-bedroom with a living room, noticeably improving your quality of life.**

As for wanting a private pool, villa, ocean view, or hotel-style service—that's a whole different budget level.

## **🍛 Dining: US$2–32 per meal**

One of the biggest perks of long-term stays in Bali is the incredible flexibility when it comes to eating.

If you're in the mood for local Indonesian cuisine, a meal costs around **US$2–4** and will leave you fully satisfied.

Local dishes like fried rice, noodles, chicken rice, and satay are quite affordable.

But if you're craving Western food, brunch, or dining at a more photogenic restaurant, budgeting around **US$10–20** per meal is more realistic.

So you can totally adjust based on your own preferences.

If you want to save a bit today, just eat at a local Warung.

And tomorrow, if you feel like sitting down for coffee and a nice brunch, that's perfectly fine too.

If you eat out every day, your monthly food expenses will likely range from **US$300 to 500**, depending on what you choose.

## **🥊 Fitness: US$150/month**

If you have a regular exercise routine, Bali is actually a great place for it.

There are plenty of gyms, boxing gyms, yoga studios, and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu academies here.

For example, a monthly membership for boxing plus Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu costs around **US$150**.

Some plans offer unlimited classes.

If you attend one or two classes daily, it's actually very cost-effective over a month.

What's more interesting is that some venues include **saunas and ice baths** in their membership plans.

So for those who already enjoy working out, this isn't just a gym—it becomes a daily part of life.

## **💻 Coworking Spaces: US$65–130/month**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787753778088-_workmatescanggu_.webp)

If you're a digital nomad or just need to handle work occasionally, Bali's coworking spaces are also very convenient.

A full-day monthly pass costs around **US$130/month**.

If, like me, you mainly need to work in the afternoon or evening, you can opt for a half-day plan for about **US$65/month**.

No need to hunt for outlets at cafés every day or worry about noisy neighbors at the next table.

Spending a little for a stable work environment is well worth it for a long stay, in my opinion.

## **👕 Clothing: There's Really No One-Size-Fits-All Answer Here**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787753778088-_workmatescanggu_.webp)

One easily underestimated expense in Bali is:

**Shopping.**

Especially in places like Canggu and Seminyak, where designer brands, curated boutiques, and clothing stores are everywhere.

You might just plan to buy a T-shirt.

But it can quickly turn into:

"This one's nice too."

"This bag could work as well."

"Well, I'm in Bali anyway."

And then, when you're heading home, you start figuring out how to cram everything into your suitcase.

So shopping really depends on the individual—I won't force it into the basic living expenses.

## **🧺 Laundry: US$5–6 per load**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787754117052-blog-best-laundry-seminyak.webp)

Living long-term in Bali comes with another lovely little perk:

**You don’t have to do your own laundry.**

Drop off a week’s worth of clothes, and someone will wash, dry, iron, and fold them for you.

It costs around **US$5–6 or less** per load.

Pick them up and put them straight into your closet.

For someone staying just a month, this service really makes life a lot easier.

## **🛵 Transportation: Around US$1–2 per day**

For short trips around town, a scooter is still super convenient.

Whether you ride your own scooter or use a ride-hailing service like Go-Ride, short-distance trips usually cost under **US$2**.

And if you choose your accommodation wisely, many daily needs won’t require traveling far at all.

Restaurants, cafés, gyms, and supermarkets are all nearby, so transportation costs naturally stay low.

## **🧖‍♂️ Weekend Entertainment: US$20–100+, Depending on How You Play**

Spending a month in Bali doesn’t mean working, eating, and going home every day.

Weekends are for going out.

Surfing, diving, massages, spa treatments, saunas, ice baths, or even day trips to Ubud, Uluwatu, or Nusa Penida are all on the table.

If you only arrange this kind of activity occasionally, it won't put too much strain on your budget.

But if you go diving every weekend, get a massage every day, and hit up high-end beach clubs every few days, the budget will be a completely different story.

## **💰 What kind of life can US$1,800–2,400 afford you in Bali?**

Adding up all the expenses above, you'll find:

**US$1,800–2,400 per month isn't Bali's 'extreme budget'—it's closer to a 'comfortable living' budget.**

You can live in a neighborhood with good amenities.

For around US$800, you can find a basic apartment with a shared pool; if you're willing to go up to about US$1,200, you can get a one-bedroom unit that makes living much more comfortable.

You don't have to cook all three meals a day—eat Indonesian food at local warungs when you want, or grab a burger, pasta, or brunch.

You can hit the gym, a boxing gym, or a Brazilian jiu-jitsu academy on a regular basis.

When you need to work, spend half a day at a coworking space.

Buy a few clothes if you feel like it.

If you don't feel like doing laundry, just send it out.

Take a motorbike taxi to the beach at night for a coffee, and on weekends, arrange surfing, diving, massages, spa, or a sauna.

This kind of life is completely different from 'visiting Bali for a week.'

### **🌴 The Real Difference: You Start Living Like a Local**

When traveling and staying in a hotel, every day feels like 'I have to do something today.'

But after staying for a month, your pace of life gradually shifts.

You might exercise in the morning, grab lunch at a Warung, work for a few hours in the afternoon, head to the beach in the evening, and find a favorite restaurant at night.

Sometimes you don't even have any plans for the whole day.

But you'll realize—this is what it truly means to 'live in Bali.'

Instead of rushing through a checklist of attractions every day.

### **⚠️ However, USD$2,400 isn't an all-powerful budget either**

If you want to include high-end villas, five-star hotels, fine dining, beach clubs, private drivers, diving, and spa treatments all in one trip, USD$2,400 can quickly fall short.

Especially **accommodation**, which directly determines your overall cost of living.

From basic apartments to luxury villas, prices can vary enormously.

So if you're trying to control your budget, the key isn't really about saving a dollar or two each day—it's this:

**Choose the right accommodation and living area first.**

Staying in a convenient spot cuts down on transportation costs; having enough restaurants nearby gives you more dining options; and with a gym, café, and supermarket close by, you can even reduce unnecessary trips.

### **🧳 If you have a month, I'd actually suggest not filling every day with plans**

Bali is great for short trips, but it's also perfect for slow living.

If you only have 5 or 6 days, of course you'll want to pack in the sights.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787754778596-b80bd8219a7ebbf52f5a6af73db77f28.jpg)

But if you have a full month, I'd actually suggest:

**Don't rush to see all of Bali.**

Stay in Canggu for a while to soak up the beach life; when you want a change of scenery, spend a few days in Seminyak, Ubud, or another area.

You'll gradually discover your favorite cafes, restaurants, and gyms, and even start getting to know the locals.

In the end, you might realize:

**The most comfortable Bali trip isn't about how many attractions you visit, but waking up one day and suddenly feeling like you're no longer a tourist.**

So, if you've always wanted to try 'living in Bali, not just visiting Bali,' start with a month.

**Prepare around USD$48–65 and give yourself 30 days.**

No need for luxury, and no need to scrimp either.

Eat what you love, do the activities you enjoy, occasionally head to the beach to unwind, and spend the rest of your time just living well.

Maybe after a month, you'll start seriously thinking:

**'A month... it really doesn't seem like enough.'** 😆
