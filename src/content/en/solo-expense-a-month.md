# **How much does it cost to stay in Bali for a month as a solo traveler? How comfortable can US$1,800–2,400 get you?**

If you've ever thought, 'Why not just stay in Bali for a month instead of a short trip?'—how much budget do you actually need?

Here's a realistic breakdown:

**Around 30 years old, not trying to pinch pennies but not chasing luxury either—wanting a comfortable stay, eating out every day, keeping up with exercise, and occasionally indulging in Bali's lifestyle experiences.**

For this kind of lifestyle, I'd estimate:

## **US$1,800–2,400 per month**

You don't have to stay in five-star hotels every night, and you don't need to survive on instant noodles to save money.

**A quick note: All of the following is a 'solo' budget.**

This estimate is based on a **solo traveler in their 30s staying in Bali for a full month**.

The lifestyle isn't about backpacking on a shoestring budget or five-star luxury, but rather living comfortably—eating regular meals, having a dedicated space for exercise and work, and occasionally treating yourself to massages, surfing, saunas, or other experiences on weekends.

**All costs—accommodation, meals, transportation, fitness, coworking spaces, etc.—are calculated for one person.**

If you're traveling with a partner, accommodation and some living expenses can be split, usually bringing the per-person cost down a bit. But for a family trip, you'd need to factor in kids, room types, activities, and transportation separately.

So, the **US$1,800–2,400/month** figure below can be understood as:

**"How much does it cost for one person to live comfortably in Bali for 30 days?"**

Here's the breakdown.

## **🏠 Accommodation: US$800–1,200/month**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787753525566-stay.jpg)

Let's start with the most important part: where you stay.

For a month-long stay in Bali, I wouldn't recommend thinking of accommodation as a "per-night" cost. Instead, look for **monthly rental apartments**.

In areas like Canggu, where daily amenities are more complete, you can find standard apartment-style accommodations.

For about **US$800/month**, you can find a one-bedroom apartment.

It may not have luxurious decor, but basic needs like a bedroom, bathroom, air conditioning, and Wi-Fi are all covered.

More importantly, these apartments usually come with a **shared large swimming pool**.

Swimming in the morning, taking a dip in the afternoon, and heading out for dinner at night—this kind of lifestyle feels completely different from staying in a regular hotel.

And if you choose a good location, there are usually restaurants, cafes, convenience stores, supermarkets, and gyms nearby.

If you're willing to raise your accommodation budget to **US$1200/month**, you can look for a more comfortable **one-bedroom-plus-living-room** setup.

In addition to a separate bedroom, you get a proper living room and kitchen.

This difference is actually quite significant.

Because staying for a month is different from staying for just three to five days.

When you're staying for a short time, you might be out exploring attractions every day, and the room is just for sleeping.

But once you stay for a month, you'll start wanting a comfortable sofa, your own workspace, a kitchen to store things, and even a living room to sit and scroll through your phone at night instead of always being in bed.

So if your budget allows, I personally recommend:

**US$800: Adequate, practical, with a pool.**

**US$1,200: One-bedroom with a living room, noticeably better living experience.**

As for wanting a private pool, villa, ocean views, or hotel-style service, that's a whole different budget level.

## **🍛 Dining: US$2–32 per meal**

One of the biggest advantages of long-term stays in Bali is the flexibility when it comes to eating.

If you're in the mood for local Indonesian food, a meal costs around **US$2–4** and can be very satisfying.

Local dishes like fried rice, noodles, chicken rice, and satay are all quite affordable.

But if you're craving Western food, brunch, or dining at a nicer restaurant, budgeting around **US$10–20** per meal is more realistic.

So you can totally adjust based on your own preferences.

Want to save today? Grab a meal at a local Warung.

Feel like sitting down for coffee and a beautiful brunch tomorrow? No problem at all.

If you eat out every day, your monthly food budget can be around **US$300–500**, depending on what you choose.

## **🥊 Fitness: US$150/month**

If you have a regular exercise routine, Bali is actually a great fit.

There are plenty of gyms, boxing gyms, yoga studios, and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu academies here.

For example, a monthly membership for boxing plus Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu is about **US$150**.

Some plans offer unlimited classes.

If you take one or two classes a day, it's actually very cost-effective over a month.

What's more interesting is that some venues even include **sauna and ice baths** in their membership plans.

So for those who already enjoy exercise, this isn't just a gym—it becomes a daily part of life.

## **💻 Coworking Spaces: US$65–130/month**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787753778088-_workmatescanggu_.webp)

If you're a digital nomad or just need to work occasionally, Bali's coworking spaces are also quite handy.

A full-day monthly pass costs about **US$130/month**.

If you're like me and mainly need to work in the afternoon or evening, you can opt for a half-day plan, around **US$65/month**.

No more hunting for outlets at cafes every day or worrying about noisy neighbors.

Spending a bit for a stable work environment—I think it's well worth it for a long stay.

## **👕 Clothing: There's really no standard answer here**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787792606143-nirvana-article-sep-2.webp)

One easily underestimated expense in Bali is:

**Shopping.**

Especially in places like Canggu and Seminyak, there are tons of designer brands, concept stores, and clothing shops.

You might just plan to buy one T-shirt.

But it can quickly turn into:

"This one's nice too."

"This bag seems like a good idea."

"Well, I'm in Bali anyway."

And then, when heading home, you start figuring out how to cram everything into your suitcase.

So shopping is entirely up to you—I won't squeeze it into the basic living expenses.

## **🧺 Laundry: US$5–6 per load**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787754117052-blog-best-laundry-seminyak.webp)

Living in Bali long-term comes with another lovely little perk:

**You don't have to do your own laundry.**

Just drop off a week's worth of clothes, and someone will wash, dry, iron, and fold them for you.

It costs around **US$5–6 or less** per load.

When you pick it up, it's ready to go straight into your closet.

For someone staying just a month, this service really makes life a lot easier.

## **🛵 Transportation: Around US$1–2 per day**

For short daily trips, a scooter is still a super convenient option.

Whether you ride your own scooter or use a ride-hailing service (Go-Ride motorbike taxi), short trips can usually be covered for **under US$2**.

And if you choose your accommodation wisely, many daily needs won't require traveling far at all.

Restaurants, cafés, gyms, and supermarkets are all nearby, so transportation costs naturally stay low.

## **🧖‍♂️ Weekend Entertainment: US$20–100+, Depending on How You Play**

Spending a month in Bali doesn't mean just working, eating, and going home every day.

You still need to go out on weekends.

Surfing, diving, massages, SPA, saunas, ice baths, or even a day trip to Ubud, Uluwatu, or Nusa Penida are all on the table.

If you only arrange these activities occasionally, it won't put too much strain on your budget.

But if you're diving every weekend, getting massages daily, and hitting up high-end beach clubs every few days, the budget will naturally look completely different.

## **💰 US$1,800–2,400: What Kind of Life Can You Live in Bali?**

Adding up all the expenses above, you'll find:

**US$1,800–2,400 per month is not Bali's 'extreme budget'—it's closer to a 'comfortable living' budget.**

You can stay in an area with good amenities.

For around US$800, you can find a basic apartment with a shared pool; if you're willing to go up to about US$1,200, you can get a one-bedroom unit that makes living space much more comfortable.

You don't have to cook all three meals yourself—enjoy Indonesian dishes at local warungs, or grab burgers, pasta, and brunch whenever you like.

You can regularly hit the gym, boxing gym, or Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu academy.

When you need to work, spend half a day at a coworking space.

Buy clothes whenever you feel like it—just a few pieces.

If you don't feel like doing laundry, just send it out.

In the evening, take a motorbike taxi to the beach for a coffee, and on weekends, arrange surfing, diving, massages, spa, or a sauna.

This kind of life is actually completely different from 'coming to Bali for a week.'

### **🌴 The Real Difference: You Start Living Like a Local**

When you travel and stay in a hotel, every day brings a sense of 'I have to do something today.'

But after staying for a month, your daily rhythm gradually shifts.

You might exercise in the morning, grab lunch at a Warung, work for a few hours in the afternoon, head to the beach in the evening, and then find a favorite restaurant at night.

Sometimes you don't even have any specific plans for the entire day.

But you'll realize—this is what it truly means to 'live in Bali.'

Instead of rushing through a checklist of attractions every day.

### **⚠️ However, US$2,400 isn't an unlimited budget**

If you plan to include luxury villas, five-star hotels, fine dining, beach clubs, private drivers, diving, and spa treatments all at once, US$2,400 can easily fall short.

**Accommodation**, in particular, will directly determine your overall cost of living.

Prices can vary dramatically, from a basic apartment to a high-end villa.

So if you're looking to control your budget, the key isn't pinching pennies every day—it's this:

**Choose your accommodation and lifestyle area wisely first.**

Living in a convenient spot cuts down on transportation costs; having plenty of nearby restaurants gives you more dining options; and access to a gym, café, and supermarket can even reduce unnecessary trips.

### **🧳 If you have a month, I'd actually suggest not filling every day with plans**

Bali is perfect for short trips, but it's also ideal for slow living.

If you only have 5 or 6 days, by all means pack in the sights.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787754778596-b80bd8219a7ebbf52f5a6af73db77f28.jpg)

But if you have a whole month, I'd suggest something different:

**Don't rush to see all of Bali.**

Stay in Canggu for a while to soak up the beach life; when you want a change of scenery, move to Seminyak, Ubud, or another area for a few days.

You'll gradually discover your favorite cafés, restaurants, and gyms, and even start getting to know the locals.

In the end, you might realize:

**The most comfortable Bali trip isn't about how many attractions you visit, but waking up one day and suddenly feeling like you're no longer a tourist.**

So, if you've always wanted to try 'living in Bali, not just visiting Bali,' start with a month.

**Budget around US$1,500–2,000 and give yourself 30 days.**

No need for luxury, and no need to pinch pennies either.

Eat what you love, do the activities you enjoy, occasionally head to the beach to unwind, and spend the rest of your time simply living well.

Maybe after a month, you'll start seriously thinking:

**'A month... might actually not be enough.'** 😆
