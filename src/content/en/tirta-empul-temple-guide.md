Tirta Empul (meaning "Holy Spring") is one of Bali's oldest and most spiritually profound water temples, situated in Tampaksiring Village, Gianyar. The temple is renowned for the water purification ritual called "Melukat," as devotees believe the spring water here has the power to cleanse negative energy.

## What Is the Melukat Purification Ritual? Can Non-Hindus Participate?

![Melukat 淨化儀式是什麼？非印度教徒可以參加嗎？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_5ba2abc7963542e2253bb38ebb3360ef.jpg)

Melukat is a purification ritual in Balinese Hinduism, where devotees pass through 26 fountains in sequence, cleansing their spirit through splashing water and prayers.

Non-Hindus can also participate, as long as they respect the culture and etiquette.

**Guidelines for Participation:**

- Wear a Sarong and sash (provided free by the temple)
- You may prepare [Canang Sari](/en/blog/bali-canang-sari-explained/) (offerings), available for purchase outside the temple
- Remain quiet, humble, and respectful
- Follow the fountain sequence for purification (you can ask a guide for assistance)

### The Correct Procedure and Sequence for the Holy Spring Purification Ritual

The holy spring purification ritual may seem simple, but it follows a specific order and rules—please adhere to them:

1. **著裝與準備**：更換好傳統綠色 Sarong（沙龍）與腰帶，將隨身物品放入置物櫃後前往聖泉池區。
2. **開始淨化流程**：面對聖泉池時，**請從左側水池**開始，按照出水口的順序逐一進行祈禱與淨化。
3. **跳過兩個特定出水口（Tirta Pengentas）**：請**略過左側水池倒數第二與第三個出水口**，這兩個是專為亡者的淨靈儀式設置，非喪事者不應使用。
4. **完成左側水池後，前往右側水池**：右邊的聖泉池只需進行第一個出水口的淨化儀式即可，不必逐一進行。
5. **每個出水口的淨化步驟如下：**
6. - 雙手合十，面對出水口默念心中願望或祈求。

- 2. **Start the Purification Process**: Facing the holy spring pool, **begin from the left pool** and perform prayers and purification at each spout in order.
- 3. **Skip Two Specific Spouts (Tirta Pengentas)**: **Skip the second-to-last and third-to-last spouts on the left pool**—these are reserved for purification rituals for the deceased and should not be used unless you are in mourning.

4. **After Completing the Left Pool, Move to the Right Pool**: For the right holy spring pool, only perform the purification ritual at the first spout—no need to go through each one.

The main area for purification rituals features two large pools and 26 springs, each representing a different spiritual wish.

**Significance of Key Springs:**

- Tirta Sudamala: Soul purification
- Tirta Penglukatan: Removal of negative energy
- Tirta Panegtegan: Strengthening courage and confidence

Note: Some springs (such as Tirta Pengentas) are reserved exclusively for death ceremonies—please avoid using them.

## Dress Code and Visiting Etiquette

![衣著規範與參拜禮儀](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_de538df8b6d201321be97e49b49d502a.jpg)

**Required Attire:**

- Sarong and sash are provided free of charge
- Shoulders must be covered; avoid wearing tank tops or shorts
- Men may wear a Udeng headband (optional)

**Prohibitions:**

- Women are not allowed to enter the main temple during menstruation
- Do not step on altars or offering tables
- Maintain silence and respect those performing rituals
- Littering and loud behavior are strictly forbidden

## Other Hidden Holy Water Temples

![Dalem Pingit聖泉有著圖左的瀑布灌頂/Pura Gunung Kawi聖泉有壯觀的石壁雕刻](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_a0e641fa72cd5443fbfe00f02e4c1894.jpg)

Dalem Pingit Holy Spring features a waterfall shower on the left / Pura Gunung Kawi Holy Spring boasts stunning rock-carved walls

Most travel blogs only mention the most famous holy water temple in Ubud, Pura Tirta Empul, but as far as I know, there are at least three holy water temples in Ubud!

Beyond the well-known Pura Tirta Empul in Ubud, there are two temples hidden in quiet corners, not too far from each other, waiting to be discovered by explorers. These two holy water temples not only let you experience Bali’s unique religious culture but also offer a sincere and peaceful bathing experience in a relatively serene environment.

Here are two other holy water temples we recommend to you:

**Dalem Pingit Holy Spring** is a relatively secluded temple. With its distinctive architecture, this temple’s sacred spring is deeply revered by local devotees. In this tranquil setting, you can feel the authenticity and serenity of Bali’s original religion. Dalem Pingit is especially known for its beautiful waterfall-fed holy spring.

**Pura Gunung Kawi Holy Spring** is a temple famous for its rock carvings. Beyond the ancient statues, there is also a hidden holy spring where devotees and visitors can bathe to receive blessings. This peaceful temple area is filled with a sense of mystery, making you feel as if you’ve traveled back in time to experience Bali’s ancient sacredness.

These two different holy water temples each have their own unique charm and cultural depth, while also offering a relatively quiet bathing environment, allowing you to find peace and purification in Bali’s sacred atmosphere.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can non-Hindus participate in the Melukat ceremony at Tirta Empul?

Yes. Just respect the local culture and ritual guidelines, and maintain a polite attitude to participate with peace of mind.

### 2. Are sarongs provided at the entrance?

Yes. Sarongs and sashes are provided free of charge upon entering the temple, so you don't need to bring your own.

### 3. Can the spring water be drunk?

It is not recommended to drink it directly. The spring water is primarily used for ceremonial purposes and has not been purified.

### 4. Can I explore the temple on my own without a guide?

Yes, you can. However, if you wish to gain a deeper understanding of the water's significance and the ritual procedures, it is advisable to hire a local guide.

### 5. Is photography allowed inside the temple?

Photography is allowed, but please avoid capturing people during ceremonies and refrain from disturbing worshippers at prayer.
