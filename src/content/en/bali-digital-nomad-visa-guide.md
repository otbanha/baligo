## Newly Introduced E33G Remote Worker Visa

The E33G is currently the closest option to a 'digital nomad visa' and falls under the KITAS residence visa category:

- Allows multiple entries and a maximum stay of up to 1 year in Indonesia.
- Permits remote work for companies outside Indonesia, not limited to Bali—you can live anywhere in Indonesia.
- Does not allow working for companies registered in Indonesia.
- Cannot be directly renewed; you must leave Indonesia and reapply after expiration, and can obtain it once more for a total of 2 years.

### Application Requirements

- Passport valid for at least 6 months.
- Personal bank statements for the last 3 months showing at least USD $2,000.
- Proof of annual income of at least USD $60,000, derived from an employment contract.
- Employment contract signed with a company outside Indonesia.
- Recent photo and resume.

Freelancers are not eligible, and the conditions lack flexibility.

Fee: IDR 7,000,000 (approximately USD$226), non-refundable.

Tax: Staying in Indonesia for 183 days makes you a tax resident. It's advisable to consult an accountant familiar with international tax laws.

It's recommended to apply while you're abroad. If you're already in Bali, you'll usually need to use an agent, which costs more.

## Short-term / Long-term Options

### If You Meet E33G Requirements

Apply directly for E33G

### If Your Income Doesn't Qualify, or You Want to Try Living First

Tourist Visa (VoA/e-VOA, 30 days, extendable once) — allows remote work for overseas employers, but you cannot be employed by an Indonesian company.

### 'Golden Visa' for Investors

In 2023, the Indonesian government launched the 'Golden Visa' program, aimed at attracting foreign investment and stimulating the local economy. This program requires a minimum investment of USD$2.5 million, with a minimum term of 5 years. Applicants can obtain residency for 5 to 10 years. Individual investors who invest USD$2.5 million and establish a company will receive a 5-year residency, while increasing the investment to USD$5 million extends the residency period to 10 years.

For corporate applicants, the minimum investment is USD$25 million, allowing directors and assigned personnel to obtain a 5-year residency. If the company doubles the investment amount, the residency period also extends to 10 years.

## **Practical Life Tips**

You've sorted out work, accommodation, and visas. Now let's talk about the practical side—how to manage daily life?

**Transportation:** Renting a scooter: Renting a scooter in Bali is relatively cheap and easy (but remember, you must have an international driver's license). You'll see many people riding without helmets, but don't forget to wear yours. Police are cracking down on helmetless riding, and more importantly, Bali's roads can be dangerous—wearing a helmet is a key way to protect yourself.

**Use ride-hailing apps like Grab and Gojek:** These apps are convenient for booking cars, scooter taxis, and food delivery. However, due to pressure from local taxi companies, these services may not be available at some island attractions or in remote areas.

**Taxi Tips:** Some taxi drivers in Bali may refuse to use the meter or claim it's "broken" to negotiate a higher fare. Whenever possible, use Blue Bird taxis—they're the most reliable and always use the meter.

**Stay Connected:** Buy a local SIM card for the most affordable data plans and reliable coverage. Telkomsel and XL are the two best mobile network providers in Bali. Also, investing in a VPN service is worthwhile for connecting to public Wi-Fi and bypassing Indonesia's internet restrictions on certain sites.
