**Bottom line**: Solo female travel in Bali is generally safe. Just keep basic precautions—**don't get into strangers' cars, avoid eye contact or conversation with street harassers**. Most locals are friendly and warm. You can also pre-book airport transfers via Klook (drivers wait patiently for about 2 hours), which greatly reduces solo travel anxiety.

I want to give back to this community. In March, I traveled solo to Bali for 8 days. Since there's not much info online about women traveling alone in Bali, I'm here to share my itinerary and insights.

![獨自闖蕩峇里島8天 - 女子獨旅體驗分享](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6152213cb48cec104b8463302a71617d.jpg)

Actually, you'll see plenty of foreign women traveling solo in Bali. From my experience, as long as you keep basic safety awareness—like not getting into strangers' cars, avoiding eye contact or conversation with street harassers—most locals are warm, friendly, and safe.

Here's my 8-day solo Bali itinerary:

## **Saturday, March 17**

**09:15-14:45** Flew into Bali. Pre-booked airport transfer via Klook; the driver waited patiently for 2 hours. On the way, I asked the driver to stop at a money changer—I went straight to BMC Money Changer. Then headed to my accommodation for the night: [The Eight](https://www.agoda.com/partners/partnersearch.aspx?pcs=1&cid=1961347&hid=23144116) Resort.

> Related: [Indonesia/Bali Currency Exchange Guide](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-guide/)

On Nusa Penida, the only option is to hire a private driver. I booked a ferry ticket + private driver package on Klook, and I was very satisfied overall. The driver company contacted me in advance to confirm the itinerary and helped communicate with the driver. As soon as I got off the boat and met the driver, he even helped carry my luggage. Throughout the trip, he was a dedicated guide—when I climbed down to Kelingking Beach, he followed me all the way down. I'm truly grateful to him. At every stop, he took photos and reported back to the company, which made me, a solo female traveler, feel much safer. I really appreciated the driver's help with luggage from top to bottom and his company for photos—he was very responsible.

## **Final Thoughts**

Overall, I found Bali's environment perfect for solo travel and photography. The attractions are spacious, so I could easily set up a tripod and take photos by myself. Except for the airport transfer and the Nusa Penida car hire, almost all my beautiful shots were taken using a tripod and remote control. Even in spots where a tripod wasn't possible—like uneven ground or in waterfall pools—locals often offered to take photos for a reasonable price, which was very thoughtful.

Bali's natural scenery is absolutely stunning! This solo trip gave me an unforgettable experience and showed me the warmth and hospitality of the locals. Solo travel isn't scary at all; instead, it allowed me to fully immerse myself in nature and exotic vibes. If I get another chance, I'd love to visit this dreamy island again! I look forward to sharing more travel stories with everyone.
