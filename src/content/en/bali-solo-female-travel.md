**Bottom line**: Solo female travel in Bali is generally safe. Just keep basic precautions—**don't get into strangers' cars, avoid eye contact or conversation with street harassers**. Most locals are friendly and warm. You can pre-book airport transfers via Klook (drivers patiently wait about 2 hours), which greatly eases solo travel anxiety.

I want to give back to this community. In March, I traveled solo to Bali for 8 days. Since there's not much info online about women traveling solo in Bali, I'm here to share my itinerary and insights.

![獨自闖蕩峇里島8天 - 女子獨旅體驗分享](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6152213cb48cec104b8463302a71617d.jpg)

Actually, I saw many foreign women traveling alone in Bali. From my experience, as long as you maintain basic awareness—like not getting into strangers' cars and avoiding eye contact or conversation with street harassers—most locals are warm, friendly, and safe.

Here's my 8-day solo Bali itinerary:

## **Saturday, March 17**

**09:15-14:45** Arrived in Bali by flight. Pre-booked airport transfer via Klook; the driver waited patiently for 2 hours. On the way, I asked the driver to stop at a money changer—I went straight to BMC Money Changer. Then headed to tonight's stay at [The Eight](https://www.agoda.com/partners/partnersearch.aspx?pcs=1&cid=1961347&hid=23144116) resort.

> Further reading: [Indonesia/Bali Travel Currency Exchange Guide](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-guide/)

On Nusa Penida, the only way to get around is by private car. I booked a ferry + car charter package on Klook and was very satisfied overall. The charter company contacted me in advance to confirm the itinerary and coordinated with the driver. When I got off the boat, the driver helped carry my luggage, and throughout the trip, he was a dedicated guide. He even followed me down to Kelingking Beach—I really appreciated that. At every stop, he took photos and reported back to the company, which made me, as a solo female traveler, feel much safer. I'm truly grateful to the driver for helping with my luggage from start to finish and accompanying me for photos. He was incredibly responsible.

For transportation, aside from the airport transfer and the trip to Nusa Penida—both pre-booked private cars—I used Grab or Gojek for everything else. It was very convenient, and most drivers were quiet, allowing me to relax and rest during the rides.

{{block:包車}}

## **Final Thoughts**

Overall, I found Bali's environment perfect for solo travel and photography. The attractions are spacious, making it easy to set up a tripod for selfies. Except for the airport transfer and the Nusa Penida private car, almost all my beautiful photos were taken by myself using a tripod and remote shutter. Even in spots where a tripod wasn't possible—like uneven ground or in waterfall pools—locals often offered to take photos at reasonable prices, which was very thoughtful.

Bali's natural scenery is absolutely stunning! This solo trip gave me an unforgettable experience and showed me the warmth and hospitality of the locals. Solo travel isn't scary at all; instead, it allowed me to fully immerse myself in nature and exotic vibes. I'd love to visit this dreamy island again if I get the chance! I look forward to sharing more travel stories with everyone.
