**Bottom line**: Bali is generally safe for solo female travelers. Just keep basic precautions—**don't get into strangers' cars, avoid eye contact or conversation with roadside harassers**. Most locals are friendly and welcoming. You can also pre-book airport transfers via Klook (drivers will patiently wait up to 2 hours), which greatly reduces solo travel anxiety.

Taking from the community, I want to give back. In March, I traveled solo to Bali for 8 days. Since there's not much info online about women traveling solo in Bali, I'm here to share my itinerary and insights.

![獨自闖蕩峇里島8天 - 女子獨旅體驗分享](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6152213cb48cec104b8463302a71617d.jpg)

Actually, in Bali, you'll see many foreign women traveling alone too. Based on my experience, as long as you keep basic precautions—like not getting into strangers' cars and avoiding eye contact or conversation with men who harass women on the streets—most locals are warm, friendly, and safe.

Let me share my 8-day solo Bali itinerary:

## **March 17, Saturday**

**09:15-14:45** Arrived in Bali by flight. Pre-booked airport transfer via Klook; the driver waited patiently at the airport for 2 hours. After getting in the car, I asked the driver to take me to a money changer first, so I went straight to BMC Money Changer. Then headed to my accommodation for the night, [The Eight](https://www.agoda.com/partners/partnersearch.aspx?pcs=1&cid=1961347&hid=23144116) Resort.

> Further reading: [Indonesia/Bali Currency Exchange Guide](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-guide/)

On Nusa Penida, the only option is to hire a private driver. I booked a ferry ticket + private car package on Klook, and overall I was very satisfied. The car company contacted me in advance to confirm the itinerary and helped communicate with the driver. As soon as I got off the boat, the driver met me and even helped carry my luggage. Throughout the trip, he was a dedicated guide—when I climbed down to Kelingking Beach, he followed me all the way. I was truly grateful. At every stop, he took photos to report back to the company, which made me, a solo female traveler, feel much safer. I really appreciated the driver's help from start to finish, carrying luggage and accompanying me for photos—he was very responsible.

## **Final Thoughts**

Overall, I found Bali's environment perfect for solo travel and photography. The attractions are spacious, making it easy to set up a tripod and take photos. Except for the airport transfer and the Nusa Penida private car, almost all my beautiful shots were taken by myself using a tripod and remote control. Even in spots where a tripod wasn't possible—like uneven ground or in waterfall pools—locals often offered to take photos for me at reasonable prices, which was incredibly thoughtful.

Bali's natural scenery is absolutely stunning! This solo trip gave me an unforgettable experience and showed me the warmth and hospitality of the locals. Solo travel isn't scary at all; instead, it allowed me to fully immerse myself in nature and exotic vibes. If I get the chance, I'd love to visit this dreamy island again! I look forward to sharing more travel stories with everyone.
