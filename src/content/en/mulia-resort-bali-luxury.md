### **Art Collection and Spatial Design at the Resort**

Mulia Resort is renowned not only for its luxurious amenities but also for its unique integration of global contemporary art into every corner, transforming the entire resort into an open-air art space. These works, created by artists from China, India, and beyond, are thoughtfully placed in guest rooms, restaurants, lobbies, walkways, and pool areas, crafting a vacation experience that blends cultural sophistication with visual delight.

### **Serene Artistic Companionship in Guest Rooms**

Stepping into a guest room, your eyes are drawn to licensed paintings by Nanjing artist Yu Qiping. Yu specializes in traditional Chinese literati painting, interpreting classical aesthetics with a restrained contemporary touch. His subjects often appear lost in contemplation, surrounded by lotus flowers, still waters, or birds in flight—these artworks never demand attention but quietly accompany you during moments of rest, infusing the private space with tranquility and depth. Amid Bali's vibrant resort atmosphere, this serenity perfectly embodies the essence of true luxury: a calm that comes from within.

### **Contemporary Creative Dialogue in the Lobby**

The Mulia Villa lobby showcases a monumental work by Shanghai-based artist Pianzi. Born in Yunnan and long residing in Shanghai, Pianzi creates with a humorous and symbolic perspective, using her signature cat motifs and layered symbols to build scenes that balance imagination and order. Another piece transforms a traditional abacus into a structure composed of human faces, turning 'calculation' into a metaphor for the relationship between the individual and the collective—a restrained commentary on commerce, order, and social rhythms, inviting guests to ponder the systems behind everyday life.

### **Narratives of Power at Table8 Restaurant**

At the resort's Table 8 Chinese restaurant, works by Guangzhou artist Huang Zhongyang reinterpret historical themes through a cross-cultural lens. His creation centered on Empress Dowager Cixi uses the motif of mirrors to explore the literal and metaphorical meanings of 'reflection,' presenting power with subtle, delicate strokes—not declaring, but questioning. The subtle interplay between Cixi and a monkey in the painting silently probes the structures of power. In this space where tradition and precise culinary artistry converge, art leaves ample room for thought beyond taste.

### **The Labor and Service Tribute of the Pathways**

Along the resort's pathways, Indian contemporary artist Valay Shende crafts human figures from metal discs and clock parts, exploring themes of labor, collaboration, and collective identity. Inspired by Mumbai's famous dabbawala system, the body made of watch faces symbolizes time and reliability, while the stomach, shaped like a lunchbox, points to nourishment and need. This sculpture subtly captures the essence of hotel service: not a performance, but a steady, consistent fulfillment.

### **Pool Area Blessings of the Water Goddess**

In the courtyard pool, ocean pool, and oasis pool areas, three female statues draw inspiration from Bali's water goddess Dewi Danu, symbolizing water, fertility, and the cycle of life. The lotus in the courtyard pool represents devotion and purity; the water bowl in the ocean pool signifies purification; and the rice and water in the oasis pool embody nourishment and renewal. These custom pieces become key markers of the space, seamlessly connecting architecture, landscape, and local beliefs, allowing every moment by the pool to be enriched with cultural and spiritual depth.

The Mulia Resort's curation philosophy emphasizes experience and meaning over mere display. Art need not be understood all at once but unfolds gradually during your stay. Amid contemporary luxury's relentless pursuit of expression and scale, Mulia chooses a more restrained path—art doesn't assert its presence but naturally integrates into the space. This is true luxury: born from clear choices and values that stand the test of time.
