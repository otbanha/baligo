**Bottom Line First (Updated July 2024)**: Errors in your e-VOA **can no longer be corrected**—Indonesia's immigration authority stopped accepting correction requests as of July 2024. The right approach is: **double-check every detail before payment**, including your name, place of birth, and passport number. If you spot an error, don't pay—just reapply. If you only notice the mistake after payment, you'll have to apply for a new e-VOA. The email-based correction method described in the first half of this article is outdated and no longer valid, kept here for reference.

Recently, a group member named Mike Chiu ran into trouble while applying for an Indonesia e-Visa (e-VOA). He asked for advice in our Facebook group. Mike said he correctly entered his place of birth as 'TAIWAN' on the application form and double-checked it. However, when he received the final e-Visa, the place of birth appeared as 'Talwan'—the 'i' had turned into an 'l'. Unsure if this was acceptable, he asked whether he should re-edit the application or take another step.

In response, another traveler, Wei Chih Wen, suggested Mike note down the e-Visa number, then write to the relevant department explaining the need to correct the place of birth error, and wait patiently for a reply. Wei Chih Wen also mentioned having faced a similar issue before and successfully received a corrected response.

In the end, Mike Chiu followed the advice and quickly got an official reply, resolving the issue smoothly. This experience serves as a reminder: if you encounter similar problems during the visa application process, contacting the authorities for corrections can help avoid disruptions to your travel plans.
