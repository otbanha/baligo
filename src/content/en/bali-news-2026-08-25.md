# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup｜August 25, 2026

## 🦟 US Issues Zika Travel Advisory for Bali, But No Confirmed Cases Yet on the Island

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recently issued a **Level 2 "Practice Enhanced Precautions" travel health notice** for Bali, following reports of travelers returning from Bali with Zika virus infections. The CDC advises visitors to Bali to step up mosquito bite prevention and notes that Zika can also be transmitted through sexual contact.

However, the Bali Provincial Health Office stated today that, based on monitoring from district health agencies and hospitals, **no confirmed Zika virus cases have been found within Bali itself**.

So there's no need to interpret this as a "Zika outbreak in Bali."

For those planning to visit Bali, the key takeaway is to take mosquito precautions seriously—use repellent, wear appropriate clothing, and don't let your guard down even during the daytime.

Additionally, Zika is especially concerning for pregnant women, as the CDC warns the infection can cause severe harm to a developing fetus.

## 🔥 Dry Season Peaks! 13 Villages in Bali Listed as High-Risk for Forest Fires

The dry season in Bali continues, with **13 villages** in Klungkung now classified as high-risk zones for forest and land fires (Karhutla), covering approximately **26,362.88 hectares** of land.

Local BPBD officials are urging residents to conserve water and avoid clearing land by burning, as this could spark fires in the dry weather.

This isn't just a concern for local residents.

If you have outdoor plans in drier areas like **Nusa Penida, Klungkung, or East Bali** in the near future, it's also advisable to avoid starting fires or engaging in activities that could ignite one.

Moreover, Bali is currently at the peak of the dry season, and these risks are expected to persist for some time.

## 🦌 Drought and Water Scarcity: Artificial Ponds in West Bali National Park Become Wildlife 'Watering Holes'

The dry season doesn't just affect people.

**West Bali National Park (Taman Nasional Bali Barat)** has recently stepped up patrols and installed artificial ponds within the park to provide water for wildlife.

The ponds quickly attracted wild animals, with local **deer** and other species already using these artificial water sources.

In addition to replenishing the water supply, the park is also enhancing patrols through smart patrol systems, primarily to address both the risk of wildlife dehydration due to drought and the threat of forest fires.

If you're planning a trip to West Bali National Park soon, this seasonal change is worth noting.

## 🌊 Southern Bali Waves Around 2.2 Meters—Be Mindful of Sea Conditions at Beaches and for Water Activities

The latest BMKG marine data shows **southern Bali waters** currently have **moderate waves of about 2.2 meters**, with wind speeds around 15–16 knots and potential gusts. This forecast covers August 24–27.

This may not significantly affect travelers strolling along Kuta, Seminyak, or Canggu beaches, but if you're planning:

**Surfing, snorkeling, diving, water sports, or taking a boat to nearby islands**

It's advisable to check the latest sea conditions before heading out—don't assume it's safe just because the weather looks sunny.

Currently, northern Bali waters have relatively lower wave heights, around **1.2 meters**.

So even within Bali, **sea conditions can vary greatly between the north and south coasts**.

## 🚢 Heavy Traffic at Gilimanuk Ferry Terminal—Travelers Heading to Java Should Be Aware of Ferry Disruptions

On August 23, the **Movable Bridge II dock (Dermaga Movable Bridge II)** at Gilimanuk Port was temporarily closed, causing vehicle queues for entering and exiting the port, with traffic stretching about **1 kilometer**.

Gilimanuk is a key ferry hub connecting Bali and Java, so if you're planning to: 

**Bali → Java**

Especially if you're driving, hiring a car, or taking a bus to Ketapang, it's wise to check the latest ferry and port conditions before departure and allow extra time.

This kind of transportation issue doesn't affect most travelers staying in Kuta or Ubud, but it's crucial for those planning a cross-island itinerary combining Bali and East Java.
