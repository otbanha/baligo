## Where to buy home crafts in Bali? Two local routes away from tourist spots, at half the price!

When visiting Bali, many people want to bring home some home decor. But the markets and shops around tourist spots often come with higher price tags. Today, I’m sharing two shopping routes that aren’t in popular tourist areas—places locals frequent. **Prices are friendly, roughly half of what you’d find in tourist zones, and you can still haggle** (thanks to my driver who helped me score great deals 🥰).

### Why are these two routes worth exploring?

Neither of these routes sits on the main tourist trail. Most shops are either run by artisans themselves or serve as direct outlets for workshops. Some travelers online have noted that these shops tend to offer better starting prices and more room for negotiation. It’s a more relaxed shopping experience, without the pressure of shopkeepers chasing you down.

### Route 1: Jl. Raya Andong, Ubud

**Location:** About a 15-minute drive from Ubud Palace
**Google Maps street name:** Jl. Raya Andong

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789892846449-mixcollage-20-sep-2026-02-55-pm-8586.jpg)

This road is known as the “Craft Highway,” lined with unique little shops that feel like a treasure hunt. Here’s what I actually found:

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789892872937-mixcollage-20-sep-2026-02-57-pm-7192.jpg)

- Stone figures
- Woven lamps
- Chairs
- Bags
- Bamboo baskets
- Shell lamps

Bamboo and rattan woven storage baskets, trays, and placemats make great souvenirs to bring home, adding a vacation vibe to your space. When you're tired from shopping, there are also some coffee shops along the street to rest.

### Route 2: Seminyak Jl. Gn. Athena

**Location:** About a 15-minute drive from Seminyak Shopping Square
**Google Maps street name:** Jl. Gn. Athena

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789892930093-mixcollage-20-sep-2026-03-12-pm-6179.jpg)

The shops here are even more upgraded, making it feel like browsing home decor showrooms, but **prices are still very affordable**! This street has many stores selling furniture, antique-style decorations, and wood carvings. If you love tasteful home decor, this is a must-visit.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789892957452-mixcollage-20-sep-2026-03-13-pm-6929.jpg)

### Shopping Tips

1. **Make good use of Google Maps:** Just enter the street names above and you'll find them. Neither road has a specific endpoint—both are perfect for leisurely strolling along.
2. **Politely bargain:** Compare prices at a few shops, communicate politely, and there's usually room for negotiation.
3. **Ask your driver for help:** A driver who speaks the local language is your best ally—their bargaining power is amazing.
4. **Leave space in your luggage:** Lamps and rattan items take up a fair amount of space, so it's wise to leave room or ask about packaging in advance.

### For Shopaholics Like You

I've personally discovered and visited many quality shops, and **my wallet truly took a hit** 😂. If you're heading to Bali soon and love treasure hunting or decorating your home, these two routes are perfect to add to your itinerary.
