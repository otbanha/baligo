# Latest Bali News: Gas Station Queues Improve, Mount Agung Closed, International Conference in Nusa Dua

## ⛽ Bali Fuel Supply Gradually Recovers! Odd-Even Plate Rule Extended to October 23, Government Deploys 7 Additional Fuel Tankers

Bali's week-long fuel distribution crisis is finally showing signs of improvement. Governor Wayan Koster stated on October 19 that after implementing the odd-even license plate fueling system, queues at gas stations in Denpasar and some other areas have shortened significantly, with some stations no longer seeing the long lines from before.

However, to prevent further supply issues, the provincial government has extended the odd-even plate measure, originally set to end on October 19, until **Friday, October 23**. If fuel distribution returns to normal earlier, the government may reassess and lift the measure ahead of schedule.

Indonesia's Minister of Energy and Mineral Resources, Bahlil Lahadalia, announced on the same day that the government has increased Bali's fuel reserve target from approximately 11 days to 15 days, and has boosted the number of ships responsible for fuel delivery from 3 to 7. He also denied rumors that Bali's fuel quota had been cut by nearly half, stating the issue primarily stemmed from transportation and distribution delays rather than an official reduction in supply.

The head of Pertamina once again apologized to the public for the gas station queue issues, pledging to improve fuel distribution and operational management.

### Can travelers now arrange transportation normally?

There are indeed signs of improvement, but conditions at gas stations may still vary by area. It's not safe to assume the entire island's supply is fully stable just because some stations have returned to normal.

Travelers renting motorcycles, driving, or hiring cars in Bali recently are still advised to check fuel levels before departure, especially when heading to North Bali, East Bali, or making long-distance cross-region trips.

The odd-even license plate measure is expected to be extended until October 23; rental car travelers should also confirm the latest rules with their providers. Don't assume rental vehicles are automatically exempt.

**The most telling indicator to watch right now is the actual fuel supply and queue conditions at gas stations across the island from October 20 to 23, not just the increase in fuel tanker shipments.**

## 🏔️ Mount Agung Climbing Suspended from October 21 to November 5, All Routes Affected

Bali's highest peak, Mount Agung (Gunung Agung), will suspend all climbing activities from **October 21 to November 5, 2026**, for a period of 16 days.

This closure is in conjunction with the Pujawali religious ceremony at Pasar Agung Temple in Sebudi Village, Karangasem Regency, aimed at preserving the sanctity of the sacred mountain and ensuring the smooth conduct of the rituals.

During the closure, all climbing routes to the summit of Mount Agung will be closed; guides, trekking operators, and travelers are not permitted to arrange or engage in any climbing activities. Those who have already booked a climb should contact their operator immediately to reschedule.

This closure is a temporary measure tied to a religious ceremony and should not be confused with volcanic eruption warnings or abnormal geological activity.

If you originally planned to climb Mount Agung in late October, it's best to reschedule; if you're just visiting the Karangasem area for sightseeing, follow local road and attraction announcements—this doesn't mean all of East Bali is off-limits for tourism.

## 🌐 ICANN87 International Internet Governance Conference Kicks Off in Nusa Dua, Expected to Attract 2,500 Delegates

The 87th Annual General Meeting of the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN) is being held from October 17 to 22 at the Bali International Convention Centre in Nusa Dua, Bali.

This international conference is expected to attract approximately 2,000 to 2,500 delegates from around the world to discuss topics such as domain names, internet addresses, and global internet governance.

Given the involvement of international delegates and a key conference venue, Bali police have stepped up security measures around the Nusa Dua venue, including site inspections, police deployments, and related traffic management.

For regular travelers, this does not mean the entire Nusa Dua resort area is closed. However, if you are staying in the ITDC area or need to go near the conference venue, it's advisable to allow extra travel time and cooperate with on-site security checks and staff instructions.

Such international conferences also highlight that Bali continues to host large-scale international business and professional events, beyond just the leisure travel market.

## 🌤️ Bali Weather: Mostly cloudy across many areas, with daytime highs around 30 to 31°C

According to the weather forecast released on October 19, many parts of Bali will see mostly cloudy skies. Daytime temperatures in Denpasar, Badung, and Klungkung can reach around 30 to 31°C, while mountain areas will be relatively cooler at night.

This weather is still suitable for beach outings, shopping, café visits, and outdoor sightseeing, but midday sun and perceived temperatures may be high, so it's wise to apply sunscreen and stay hydrated.

If you plan to visit Ubud, Kintamani, or other mountainous areas, be sure to check the local real-time weather. Mountain weather can differ from the southern coast, so don't rely solely on Denpasar's forecast to judge conditions across all of Bali.

If you are taking a boat to the outlying islands, be sure to check sea conditions and ferry company announcements separately; general city weather forecasts are not a substitute for maritime safety information.

## 🐟 Kusamba's Traditional Salted Fish Industry Faces Off-Season Pressure, Female Workers Remain Steadfast Year-Round

In Kusamba, Klungkung Regency, eastern Bali, the area is renowned for its traditional fish processing and salted fish industry. Local female workers have long been engaged in fish handling, sustaining the daily operations of this local traditional industry.

A local report on October 18 documented the work of these women. One worker, Ni Luh Ernayati, has been in the fish processing industry for 28 years, working most days except during major holidays like Nyepi and Galungan.

However, the local industry is also affected by seasonal fluctuations in fish catch, with off-season periods putting pressure on raw material supply and workers' income. This reflects that Bali's economy isn't just about tourism—traditional fishing and food processing are also vital livelihoods for some coastal communities.

For travelers visiting Kusamba or the Klungkung area, it's worth learning more about the local traditional industries and food culture. However, this report does not indicate any new tourism activities or fixed tour itineraries in the area, so these workplaces should not be treated as open tourist attractions.
