## ⛽ Bali Gas Station Panic Continues: Pertamina Adds Fuel Supply, Governor Urges Public Not to Hoard

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DebJyOKJEhz/?utm_source=ig_web_button_share_sheet

On October 13, long queues persisted at many gas stations across Bali, with some stations running out of Pertalite (RON 90) and Pertamax. The queues are no longer limited to Denpasar and Badung Regency but have spread to Buleleng, Gianyar, Klungkung, and Karangasem. Some roads have become congested as waiting cars spill onto the streets.

Bali Governor Wayan Koster stated on October 13 that the public's rush to fill up, driven by fears of running out of fuel, has caused a sudden spike in demand. He emphasized that fuel supplies still exist, but one issue is that distribution from storage terminals to gas stations can't keep up with on-site demand, with traffic congestion also slowing replenishment. The governor expects supply to gradually return to normal within one to two days.

Pertamina, meanwhile, reported that ships carrying Pertalite and Biosolar arrived at the Sanggaran fuel terminal in Denpasar on the morning of October 13, with deliveries expected to begin that afternoon based on each station's needs. An additional total of 26,200 kiloliters of fuel is scheduled to arrive in Bali on October 12, 14, and 16.

### Practical Impact for Bali Travelers

If you're renting a scooter, driving yourself, or using a private driver in Bali these days, here's what to keep in mind:

- Don't wait until your tank is nearly empty to refuel. If you spot a station with supply, top up early when it's reasonable to do so.
- Allow extra travel time. Gas station queues may extend onto main roads, especially in Denpasar, Badung, and along routes toward Gilimanuk.
- Don't hoard gasoline. Avoid panic buying or storing fuel in non-compliant containers based on social media rumors.
- Check with your driver or accommodation first. If you have a long-distance trip the next day, confirm your vehicle's fuel level and the situation at nearby gas stations.

The most accurate take right now is: Bali is facing fuel distribution disruptions and heightened public panic. Some gas stations are indeed out of stock, but authorities say overall supply is still being replenished. It's not accurate to say the entire island is completely out of gas, nor should anyone guarantee that all areas will return to normal at the same time.

This situation differs from the localized gas station queues seen in early October. This is a new wave of supply issues and queuing that escalated from October 12 to 13, with subsequent developments including a new ship arrival, additional fuel deliveries, and a public response from the governor.

## 🌵 BMKG Places Parts of Bali Under Highest Drought Alert, Urges Water Conservation

Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) has placed parts of Bali under an Awas (Alert) level in its second-ten-day October meteorological drought warning—the highest drought alert level in the system. BMKG urges water conservation during the dry season and advises avoiding activities that could spark fires.

Currently, about 76% of Indonesia remains in the dry season, and parts of Bali need to watch for the effects of prolonged low rainfall. Beyond daily water use, the dry conditions also increase the risk of wildfires from burning fields, brush, or trash.

For travelers, this doesn't mean all of Bali is experiencing water shortages or that hotel water supplies will definitely be disrupted. However, if you're staying at a villa or guesthouse in a remote area, it's worth checking if your accommodation has water-saving measures in place.

Travelers are advised to follow local water conservation practices—avoid running water for long periods and don't burn trash near dry grasslands or forests. If you have outdoor activities planned, stay mindful of the heat and keep hydrated.

## 🌏 A 5.0 Magnitude Earthquake Strikes Southern Java, Felt by Some Residents in Denpasar

Around noon on October 13, a 5.0 magnitude earthquake struck the southern waters of Java, with brief tremors felt by residents in the Renon area of Denpasar. According to BMKG data, the epicenter was located approximately 112 kilometers southwest of Jembrana at a depth of about 10 kilometers. BMKG stated that this earthquake does not pose a tsunami risk.

The key takeaway here is that while the quake was indeed felt by some residents, current reports indicate no significant damage in Bali.

For travelers, if you experience an earthquake while in Bali, stay calm and move away from windows and large furniture that could fall. If indoors, protect your head and follow on-site safety instructions.

If you're near the coast, rely on official warnings from BMKG and local authorities rather than social media rumors to decide whether to evacuate.

## 🅿️ Nusa Penida Attraction Parking Fees May Double; Diamond Hill's Price Hike Plan Under Scrutiny

The Klungkung Regency Transportation Agency has asked the management of Diamond Hill's, a tourist attraction on Nusa Penida, to explain its proposed parking fee adjustments. According to a report on October 12, the operator plans to raise motorcycle parking fees from IDR 5,000 to IDR 10,000, and car parking fees from IDR 10,000 to IDR 20,000, with the new rates originally set to take effect on October 15.

The Transportation Agency noted that dedicated parking lots can set fees according to regulations, but any price adjustment must follow proper procedures and meet licensing requirements. Authorities have requested the operator to explain the reasons for the increase and will review the matter further.

Thus, this news should be understood as the proposed parking fee hike being reviewed by authorities, not as a confirmed rate change already in effect.

If you're planning a day trip to Nusa Penida soon, especially if you intend to rent a motorcycle or drive to Diamond Hill's, it's wise to confirm the current parking fee with the attraction beforehand and bring enough cash, as on-site charges may differ from online information.

## 🚶 Kuta Continues Pedestrian Path Upgrades, Future Plans to Extend to Kartika Plaza

The Badung Regency government is pushing forward public space improvements in the Kuta tourist area, including pedestrian walkways, parking facilities, and a better walking environment, aiming to restore Kuta as a cleaner, more walkable tourist district.

Regent I Wayan Adi Arnawa stated on October 12 that after completing the walkway improvements around Kuta Beach, the next phase will extend to Jalan Kartika Plaza.

The focus of this project is to enhance pedestrian access, improve traffic order, and upgrade the overall environment of the tourist area. However, actual progress on the ground should be taken as the benchmark, and it cannot be assumed that the entire stretch of walkway is already finished.

For travelers, if you're staying near Kuta Beach, Waterbom Bali, or Kartika Plaza, be mindful of construction zones, temporary pathways, and vehicle access points when walking in the near future. If you've arranged airport transfers or private car services, it's also advisable to confirm pickup and drop-off locations with your driver to avoid roadwork or temporary traffic changes.

In my view, if Kuta can continue improving its walking environment, it will be much more convenient for travelers who don't rent scooters and prefer to explore and dine on foot. However, the ultimate outcome will still depend on construction quality, road management, and ongoing maintenance.
