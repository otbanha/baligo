# Key Topics and News Roundup for Bali Travelers｜October 17, 2026

## ⛽ Bali Gas Station Crisis Escalates! Odd-Even License Plate Fueling Starts October 17

Bali's fuel shortage continues, and the provincial government has decided to implement odd-even license plate fueling for subsidized fuel across the island from October 17 to 19, 2026, aiming to reduce gas station queues and gradually restore fuel distribution and refueling services to normal.

According to measures announced on October 16, the schedule is as follows:

- October 17 (Saturday): Vehicles with license plates ending in an odd number can refuel.
- October 18 (Sunday): Vehicles with license plates ending in an even number can refuel.
- October 19 (Monday): The odd-even system remains in effect; specific application details should follow official announcements and gas station instructions on the day.

These regulations mainly apply to subsidized fuel, particularly Pertalite. Pertamina has stated it has received the relevant official letter from the Bali Governor, will coordinate with the Indonesian military and police for on-site order, and has added 16 additional fuel tanker trucks to speed up distribution from fuel terminals to gas stations.

### What Should Travelers Do?

If you rent a motorcycle or car between October 17 and 19, first check the last digit of the license plate—don't assume that rental vehicles are exempt from the rules.

It's especially important to note that the odd-even license plate rule and the fuel purchase limit are two separate measures; even if your plate is allowed on a given day, you still must follow the gas station's purchase restrictions.

Additionally, this measure was announced on October 16, and implementation details may be adjusted based on subsequent official notices. Before heading out, it's best to confirm with your rental company, driver, or accommodation to avoid arriving at a gas station only to find you can't refuel.

## ⛽ Motorcycles: Max 3 liters per purchase, Cars: 20 liters—fuel purchase limits take effect simultaneously

Alongside the odd-even license plate rule, Pertamina has also announced that, until Bali's fuel supply returns to normal, the following purchase limits apply per refueling:

- Motorcycles: Up to 3 liters per purchase.
- Four-wheeled vehicles: Up to 20 liters per purchase.

This restriction has been approved by the Governor of Bali and will remain in effect until fuel supply normalizes, with adjustments to follow the Governor's directives.

This means that even if you rent a car or motorcycle, you can't assume you'll be able to fill the tank in one go. If you're planning long-distance charters or cross-region trips, it's advisable to check the vehicle's fuel level with the driver in advance and allow time for refueling stops.

The official announcement only specifies a single refueling cap, which does not mean all gas stations have sufficient fuel. Travelers should still rely on real-time availability at the stations.

## 🚚 200,000 Liters of Fuel Arrived? Correction: 20,000,000 Liters of Pertalite Reaches Bali, Distribution Accelerates

On October 16, two fuel tankers arrived at Bali's fuel terminals, bringing a total of approximately 20 million liters of Pertalite:

- MT Pangkalan Brandan arrived at the Manggis fuel terminal in Karangasem, carrying about 16 million liters of Pertalite.
- MT Asian Rigel arrived at the Sanggaran fuel terminal in Denpasar, carrying about 4 million liters of Pertalite.

Additionally, Pertamina has deployed 108 tanker trucks and 465 staff for distribution, with both terminals operating 24/7. In Denpasar and Badung, 66 gas stations offer round-the-clock service—42 in Denpasar and 24 in Badung.

Notably, this fuel arrival marks significant progress in restoring the supply chain, but it still takes time to deliver from terminals to individual stations. Pertamina reports that queues in some areas have begun to ease, though overall distribution continues to be adjusted.

For travelers, the key concern isn't just whether fuel has arrived at the port, but whether your destination area has been restocked and if vehicles can refuel as per regulations.

## 🛵 Denpasar Launches Bottled Fuel Delivery Service, Providing About 5,000 Liters of Pertalite Daily

To reduce queues at gas stations, Pertamina has also introduced a per-liter fuel sales service at some stations in Denpasar, primarily serving motorcycle riders.

This service provides approximately 5,000 liters of Pertalite daily, with a maximum purchase of 3 liters per person, paid in cash. Service locations include:

- Teras Ayung
- Nangka Utara
- Kesiman
- Trenggana
- Hayam Wuruk
- Antasura
- Renon
- Imam Bonjol

The above locations may be adjusted based on queue conditions and actual demand, so you should confirm whether the service is still available on the day before heading there.

This is not a universally available bottled gasoline service across Bali, nor does it mean travelers can freely buy gasoline from roadside vendors. If you need to refuel, it is still recommended to prioritize official gas stations or authorized service points to avoid purchasing fuel of unknown origin.

Given the varying supply situations across different areas, travelers can refer to [Bali Fuel Watch](https://balifuelwatch.vercel.app). This is a map-based website that shows community-reported fuel availability, allowing you to check the supply status of Pertalite, Pertamax, and diesel at different locations.

When using this resource, keep in mind that the information relies on user reports, may not be real-time, and there's no guarantee fuel will still be available when you arrive. It's best to confirm with gas stations, rental agencies, or your accommodation before heading out.
