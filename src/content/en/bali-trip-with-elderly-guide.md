**Key Takeaways**: Practical tips for traveling Bali with seniors — ① Complete the health QR code, e-visa, and customs declaration online before arrival (now integrated into the All Indonesia entry card for one-stop processing); ② **Hire a private driver for the entire trip**, communicate the itinerary with the driver in English via LINE, and add a Chinese-speaking guide before departure; ③ Focus the itinerary on **Ubud's mountain and cultural experiences**, keep a slow pace with ample rest, and only move to a southern beach hotel at the end for relaxation before heading to the airport.

> The following content is adapted from a travelogue shared by group member [Claire Chen](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/17yjQ17JHi/):

On this Bali trip with my elderly parents, the main focus was on mountain scenery and local culture, so we spent most of our time in the Ubud area. Only toward the end of the trip did we gradually move toward the airport, choosing to relax at a hotel near the southern beaches. The itinerary covered several must-visit attractions, and I deliberately planned relaxing activities and ample rest time for the seniors.

The Bali entry process can be prepared before departure. There are many online guides for the application steps. I've organized the post-customs procedures below for your reference:

## 【Tips for Communicating About Private Car Hire and Itinerary Planning】

For this private car hire arrangement, we first communicated with the driver in English via LINE for initial discussions. As the departure date approached, we also invited a Chinese-speaking guide named Ani to assist with our itinerary. When discussing with your private car driver, it's recommended to provide the following information in advance so they can better help with planning:

![（Claire Chen提供）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_696ef9532aca81d419e61d7f96d61b1b.jpg)

(Provided by Claire Chen)

1. **Basic Information**
2. - **Number of travel days**, **hotel locations**, **flight details**, and **group size**, etc., so the driver can help plan routes and arrange pickups.
3. **Itinerary Discussion Mode**
4. - **Initial Planning**: This is the approach I used. First, I browsed popular Bali itinerary ideas within the community, sketched out a rough travel framework, and booked hotels separately. Then, I provided the accommodation details and preliminary itinerary to the driver for confirmation and a quote.

- **Leave it entirely to the guide**: Ideal for those without a specific plan. Just provide your **trip length**, **preferences**, and **group details**—such as the age range of participants—and the guide will tailor the arrangements accordingly.

5. **Itinerary Preferences**
6. - **Travel Companions' Age Group**: Different combinations like families with kids, seniors, or friends will influence itinerary preferences. You can inform the driver and guide in advance.

- **Special Activity Requests**: For example, mountain excursions (such as relaxing, hiking, or sunrise volcano tours) or water activities (like snorkeling, diving, or river rafting). This information helps the guide incorporate suitable experiences into the itinerary.
- **Restaurant Preferences**: If you have specific dining preferences (such as wanting to try local cuisine or unique restaurants), let the guide know in advance so they can recommend restaurants along the route.

7. **Language Choice**
   Our private car drivers primarily offer English service. If you're confident in English communication, there's no need to hire a Chinese-speaking guide. On my trip, the first and last days were relaxed, so the driver sufficed. For the middle days with longer distances, I hired a Chinese-speaking guide named Ani for full-day assistance. During pre-trip communication, you can flexibly use ChatGPT or Google Translate to ensure both sides clearly understand each other's needs.

With this kind of preparation and communication, a private car trip will go more smoothly and better align with your travel needs, delivering a fulfilling Bali experience.

## 【Recommended Private Driver and Tour Guide Services for First-Time Bali Travelers】

This is my first time writing a recommendation, especially for those visiting a Southeast Asian island for the first time, particularly travelers who want to skip the hassle of detailed itinerary planning. The local drivers and guides in Bali were incredibly helpful, making the entire trip relaxed and enjoyable. Here are my tips and insights:

1. **SIM Card and Currency Exchange Needs**
   If you need a local SIM card or currency exchange, you can let your driver or guide know anytime. I had already taken care of these details in Taiwan, so I could start the trip right upon arrival.
2. **Itinerary Planning Tips**
   Some locations in Bali have long travel times between them. It's best to prioritize your accommodation when planning your itinerary to avoid unnecessary back-and-forth. Arranging your itinerary around your stay can make the trip much more relaxed.
3. **Activity Requests**
   If you have specific day trips or other activities in mind, you can discuss them with your driver or guide. They'll help arrange everything smoothly, so you can just follow along without worrying about the details.
4. **Transparent Pricing**
   The private driver fees for this trip were clear and transparent, adjusted based on the daily route, and settled each day. Entrance fees and additional day trip costs were calculated separately and could be discussed and confirmed before departure.
5. **Thoughtful Company of Chinese-Speaking Guide Ani**
   On the tours Ani accompanied, she stayed with us throughout and introduced Bali's culture, history, and customs along the way. This companionship was invaluable for me and my elderly family members! Also, if we wanted to buy souvenirs, Ani would take us directly to purchase them or buy them on our behalf based on photos we provided. It's best to let her know in advance so she can arrange it properly.
6. **Attentive Communication and Photography**
   Ani and the driver not only helped take photos throughout the trip but also assisted with communicating accommodation details at the hotel front desk, so we didn't have to worry about language barriers.
7. **Local Traffic and Driver Skills**
   Traffic in Bali can sometimes be congested, especially in the city, but the driver's skills were very steady, making us feel safe even on mountain roads. We were also lucky to encounter almost no traffic jams during this trip.
8. **Companionship Like Family**
   Throughout the journey, the driver and guide's care made them feel like family or friends, not only making the trip smoother but also bringing immense ease. I'm grateful for their company and support on this trip.
