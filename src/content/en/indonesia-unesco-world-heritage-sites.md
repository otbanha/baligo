## 🌋 Must-Visit World Heritage Sites in Indonesia

To date, Indonesia has **10** UNESCO-recognized World Heritage sites (including 6 cultural and 4 natural sites). Here is the complete list:

### 🏛️ Cultural Heritage

1. **Borobudur Temple Compounds**
    - **Year of Inscription**: 1991
    - **Location**: Central Java
    - **Overview**: Built in the 8th and 9th centuries, this magnificent Mahayana Buddhist monument is the largest Buddhist temple complex in the world, standing alongside Cambodia's Angkor Wat and Myanmar's Bagan as one of Southeast Asia's three great Buddhist wonders.
2. **Prambanan Temple Compounds**
    - **Year of Inscription**: 1991
    - **Location**: Border of Central Java and Yogyakarta
    - **Overview**: Constructed in the 9th century, this is Indonesia's largest and most awe-inspiring Hindu temple complex. Its towering main shrines and intricately carved bas-reliefs depicting Hindu epics are truly breathtaking.
3. **Sangiran Early Man Site**
- **Year of Inscription**: 1996
- **Location**: Central Java Province
- **Overview**: One of the oldest and most important paleoanthropological sites in the world, this location has yielded more than half of all known Homo erectus (Java Man) fossils, making it crucial for the study of human evolution.
4. **Cultural Landscape of Bali Province: the Subak System**
- **Year of Inscription**: 2012
- **Location**: Bali
- **Overview**: A cooperative water irrigation system rooted in the traditional Balinese philosophy of "Tri Hita Karana" (harmony between humans and God, humans and each other, and humans and nature). It encompasses terraced rice fields, irrigation canals, and iconic water temples such as Pura Ulun Danu Batur and Pura Taman Ayun.
5. **Ombilin Coal Mining Heritage of Sawahlunto**
- **Year of Inscription**: 2019
- **Location**: West Sumatra Province
- **Overview**: A complete industrial heritage system built during the Dutch colonial period from the late 19th to early 20th century, including mining areas, railway networks, and port facilities, documenting the history of modern industrialization in Southeast Asia.
6. **Cosmological Axis of Yogyakarta and its Historic Landmarks**
    - **Year of Inscription**: 2023
    - **Location**: Special Region of Yogyakarta
    - **Overview**: This north-south spatial axis embodies the profound Javanese traditional understanding of cosmology and the cycle of life, connecting Mount Merapi, the Yogyakarta Palace (Kraton), and the Indian Ocean to the south.

### 🌿 Natural Heritage

7. **Komodo National Park**
    - **Year of Inscription**: 1991
    - **Location**: East Nusa Tenggara Province
- **Overview**: Composed of volcanic islands including Komodo, Rinca, and Padar, this is the only habitat in the world where the Komodo dragon lives, and it boasts rich marine biodiversity.
8. **Ujung Kulon National Park**
    - **Year of Inscription**: 1991
    - **Location**: Banten Province (westernmost tip of Java)
    - **Overview**: Encompasses the historically significant Krakatoa volcanic area, preserving lowland tropical rainforests and mangrove ecosystems, and serves as the last refuge for the critically endangered Javan rhinoceros.
9. **Lorentz National Park**
    - **Year of Inscription**: 1999
    - **Location**: Papua Province
    - **Overview**: The largest nature reserve in Southeast Asia and one of the few places globally that features a complete vertical ecosystem, from equatorial snow-capped mountains (glaciers) and alpine meadows to tropical coastal mangroves.
10. **Tropical Rainforest Heritage of Sumatra**
- **Year of Inscription**: 2004
- **Location**: Sumatra Island
- **Overview**: Comprising three major national parks—Gunung Leuser, Kerinci Seblat, and Bukit Barisan Selatan—this site serves as a vital sanctuary for rare species such as orangutans, Sumatran tigers, Sumatran elephants, and the Rafflesia arnoldii flower.
