# Topics of Concern for Bali Tourists + News Roundup (Published Tomorrow Morning)

## 🚨 Flores 7.7 Earthquake: Aftershocks May Continue for 2–3 Weeks, BMKG Urges Vigilance

The **M7.7 Flores earthquake** on August 15 triggered a tsunami warning, which has since been lifted. However, Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) warns that aftershock activity may persist for some time, with the natural decay process potentially lasting **2 to 3 weeks**.

As of the latest updates, numerous aftershocks have been detected, some of which are still strongly felt. This means travelers heading to Flores, Labuan Bajo, or Komodo in the near future should not interpret the lifting of the tsunami warning as a sign that the area has fully returned to normal.

While Bali is not the primary affected area from this earthquake, Flores is a common extension destination for many travelers visiting Bali. If you have plans for a **Bali + Labuan Bajo/Komodo** trip, it's advisable to closely monitor the latest announcements from BMKG, airlines, and local attractions.

## 🆘 NTT Declares 14-Day Emergency State, Rescue and Disaster Response in Full Swing

In response to the M7.7 earthquake, the East Nusa Tenggara provincial government has declared a **14-day emergency response status** to expedite rescue, medical aid, supplies, and post-disaster recovery.

The affected area currently faces road closures, landslides, and damage to communication and infrastructure, with rescue efforts in some regions hindered by transportation obstacles. The Indonesian government has deployed a large number of police and military personnel to assist in disaster relief.

The death toll reported by international media continues to rise, increasing from an initial several dozen to over 50.

**Traveler Advisory:** If you're staying on Bali's main island, there's no need to cancel your itinerary due to this earthquake panic. However, if your next stop is Flores or Labuan Bajo, reconfirm transportation and site conditions.

## 🇮🇩 Indonesia's Independence Day Tomorrow: Flag-Raising and Celebrations Across Bali

August 17 marks Indonesia's **81st Independence Day (National Day)** , and various locations across Bali will hold flag-raising ceremonies and local celebrations tomorrow.

In Denpasar, **Niti Mandala Renon** serves as the main provincial celebration site, with preparations complete for 72 Paskibraka flag-raising team members.

For travelers, the biggest impact isn't being unable to travel, but rather potential traffic controls or congestion near some government offices, roads, and city events.

If you have plans in Denpasar city center tomorrow, especially around Renon, allow extra travel time.

## 🎭 GWK's "Pesta Rakyat" Continues Tomorrow: Perfect for Culture and Entertainment

If you're in South Bali tomorrow, GWK Cultural Park's **Pesta Rakyat GWK 2026** is still ongoing.

The event runs from **August 16 to 17**, coinciding with Indonesia's Independence Day, featuring cultural performances, entertainment, food, and traditional folk activities.

Unlike events that have already ended in the past few days, **August 17 is still the final day** of this activity, so publishing it tomorrow morning still holds practical travel value.

If you're staying in Jimbaran, Nusa Dua, or South Kuta, consider adding GWK to your itinerary for tomorrow.

## 🌾 TeBA Majalangu Festival Runs Through the 17th, Denpasar Also Hosts Local Cultural Events

Beyond GWK, the **TeBA Majalangu Festival II 2026** in Kesiman Kertalangu, Denpasar, also continues until **August 17**.

Running from August 15, the festival blends Subak agricultural culture, local community activities, and Indonesian Independence Day celebrations at the TeBA Majalangu Eco-Education Tourism Area.

For travelers who have already explored the beaches and major attractions of South Bali, these community-driven events offer a more authentic glimpse into Balinese local culture.
