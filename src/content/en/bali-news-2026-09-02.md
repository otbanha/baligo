# Hot Topics & News Roundup for Bali Travelers | September 2, 2026

## 🚋 Kuta–Canggu Tram Officially Approved! Aiming for Operations in Q4 2028

There's finally major progress on Bali's southern traffic woes.

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786015897605-trem.jpeg)

On September 1, the Badung Regency government, Bali Provincial government, and Indonesian state railway PT KAI signed a cooperation agreement to push forward the tram system connecting Ngurah Rai International Airport, Kuta, Legian, Seminyak, and Canggu.

The current plan covers approximately 13.1 kilometers, with a target to begin commercial operations in Q4 2028.

The first phase will prioritize the route from Ngurah Rai Airport to Canggu, with gradual extensions to the network later.

The main goal of this project is to tackle the worsening traffic congestion in southern Bali, while also supporting the Bali Low Emission Zone Initiative (BLEZI) to create a lower-carbon transport system.

For travelers, this line is highly significant.

Because it directly connects:

**Airport → Kuta → Legian → Seminyak area → Canggu**

This is currently the most popular—and most traffic-prone—tourist corridor in Bali.

However, note that **2028 is the target operational date**, so it can't be used as a current transportation option just yet.

## 💰 Nusa Penida's 'One Spot, One Fee' System Sees Massive First-Day Volume! OGOD Records 11,836 Transactions on Day One

Nusa Penida officially launched its new **One Gate One Destination (OGOD)** tourism fee system yesterday.

And the first day saw remarkably high transaction volume.

According to NusaBali, on the first day of implementation, September 1, the system recorded **11,836 attraction fee transactions**.

The system's key feature is standardizing entry fees across Nusa Penida's attractions, requiring visitors to pay the designated retribusi when entering different tourist sites.

This policy was originally scheduled to take effect on August 12, but was postponed due to concerns from tourism operators about the fee structure and implementation details, and is now confirmed to launch on September 1.

This means that day-trippers heading to Nusa Penida now need to **factor attraction entrance fees into their budget, in addition to transportation, ferry tickets, and tour costs.**

If you're planning a trip to Nusa Penida soon, it's best to stop relying on the old assumption that 'one fee covers the entire island.'

## 📈 European Visitors Surge 47% in July! Bali's Peak Season Is Stronger Than Expected

According to the latest data from BPS, **Bali welcomed 200,182 European tourists in July 2026, a 47.12% increase** compared to June.

The main reason is the start of Europe's summer holiday season.

However, looking at the bigger picture, Bali's total foreign tourist arrivals haven't seen broad, dramatic growth.

The latest BPS data shows that **cumulative foreign tourist arrivals from January to July 2026 are still down 1.98% compared to the same period last year.**

In other words, the numbers for July this year are indeed impressive, but not enough to fully reverse the cumulative performance of the earlier months.

For travelers, this data reflects a very practical reality:

**In August and September, popular areas in Bali may still be quite lively.**

Especially in Seminyak, Canggu, Ubud, and the southern beach areas—don't expect a sudden drop in tourists just because it's late August.

## 🥋 Bali Steps Up Tourism Staff Training: Not Just Scenic Views, But Ensuring Visitor Safety

Bali's tourism authority has highlighted a noteworthy direction today:

**Bali's tourism industry cannot rely solely on 'beautiful scenery'; it must also enhance the professional skills of its workforce.**

The Bali Provincial Tourism Office states that the capabilities, professionalism, and safety awareness of tourism service personnel are key factors in ensuring a quality travel experience for visitors.

Especially as Bali has rapidly developed tourism products like water sports, hiking, diving, and outdoor adventures in recent years, **whether staff have proper safety handling skills** is becoming increasingly important beyond the attractions themselves.

Officials emphasize that tourism safety cannot depend solely on the natural environment; it requires a comprehensive safety system built through staff training, skill enhancement, and professional standards.

This direction is a positive move for Bali.

Because today's travelers want more than just 'a great photo'; they seek:

**Beauty + Convenience + Safety.**
