**"In Bali, every bite is a symphony of spices!"**

**Bottom line**: Bali's top 10 must-try Indonesian foods are led by Bebek Betutu (smoked duck) and Babi Guling (suckling pig), with the soul of the seasoning coming from the spice paste **Basa Gede (Basa Rajang)**. If you're worried about spice, just say "**Tidak pedas**" (not spicy) or "**Sedikit pedas**" (a little spicy)—most restaurants are happy to adjust the heat and recipe for tourists.

As the brightest gem among Indonesia's 17,000 islands, Bali offers not only stunning ocean views but also an astonishing culinary universe. Local cuisine boldly uses dozens of spices like turmeric, lemongrass, and chili, paired with unique slow-roasting techniques to create richly layered Southeast Asian flavors. Follow this food map and embark on your taste adventure!

🍚 **Decoding Bali's Food Culture**

Balinese believe that "rice is the source of life," and even temple offerings include rice (locally called Nasi). Strolling the streets, you'll find three basic types of rice: white rice (Nasi Putih), red rice (Nasi Merah), and turmeric-infused yellow rice (Nasi Kuning). The soul of all these flavors comes from a mysterious spice paste—**Basa Gede or Basa Rajang** (a blend of chili, shrimp paste, garlic, and more, pounded into a fragrant paste).

⚠️ Quick tips:

- Spice levels vary significantly; when ordering, say "Tidak pedas" (not spicy) or "Sedikit pedas" (a little spicy)
- Bali isn't a vegetarian paradise! Pork and seafood are common, but nearly all restaurants are happy to adjust recipes for visitors

## 🏆 **Top 10 Must-Try Balinese Classic Dishes**

### 1. **Nasi Campur (Mixed Rice)**

![1. Nasi Campur（綜合飯）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_ba61bd1cca025325f216cead628b0be6.jpg)

Bali's version of a "buffet"! Pick your base—white, red, or yellow rice—and pair it with dozens of side dishes: spicy scrambled eggs, fish head curry, sweet soy-braised chicken, fried tempeh, and more. Each Warung (food stall) has its own unique combo. **Don't skip the Sambal chili sauce**—if you can handle the heat, dare to try "Sambal Matah," a raw chili sambal!

### 2. **Babi Guling (Suckling Pig)**

![2. Babi Guling（烤乳豬）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_8c8b1c1ab0f2a6f07b153fbf717d6fa1.jpg)

A Bali exclusive! (Most of Indonesia is Muslim and avoids pork.) The suckling pig is stuffed with spices and slow-roasted until the skin shatters like glass, while the innards are turned into blood sausage (Urutan). For the best experience, head to a specialty eatery.

🍽️ How to eat: Order the "Special" set, which includes rice, crispy skin, vegetables, and spiced soup.

### 3. **Bebek Betutu (Spiced Smoked Duck)—Bali's 24-Hour Slow-Cooked Ritual**

![3. Bebek Betutu（香料燜鸭）—— 峇里島的24小時慢火儀式](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_424d54a38db06d3ba8596b74b05187fa.png)

In the pantheon of Balinese cuisine, **Bebek Betutu** is the ultimate "labor of love"! This traditional dish, which takes an entire day to prepare, is like Indonesia's answer to "beggar's chicken," but with ten times the complexity of flavors—

**🔥 Decoding the "Betutu" Craft**

**1️⃣ Spice Massage SPA**

The whole duck or chicken is marinated with **30+ spices**, including:

- Base spices: Turmeric (Kunyit), Galangal (Lengkuas), Lemongrass (Sereh)
- Secret weapons: **Candlenuts (Kemiri)** and **Indonesian bay leaves (Salam)**
- Fiery soul: Bird’s eye chilies (Cabe Rawit) freshly pounded into a paste

**2️⃣ Banana Leaf Mummy-Style Cooking**

- The marinated duck is **wrapped layer by layer in banana leaves**, secured with bamboo skewers
- Slow-cooked in a traditional **wood-fired clay oven** for 24 hours (modern restaurants use low-temperature ovens to mimic this)
- Each batch yields 20-30 ducks, with an aroma that fills the entire street!

**3️⃣ The Art of Serving**

- The waiter brings over an **unopened banana leaf parcel**, and when it’s cut open on the spot, steam bursts out
- The duck meat is a deep reddish-brown, and a gentle touch with a fork makes it **fall off the bone**
- Served with **yellow turmeric rice (Nasi Kuning)** and two sauces:
- - **Sambal Matah** (raw chili sauce)
  - **Peanut sauce mixed with duck fat** (sinfully delicious!)

### 4. **Sate (Satay) — Bali’s Smoky Soul Skewers**

On the streets of Bali, an irresistible charcoal-grilled aroma always hangs in the air—that’s definitely **Sate** (satay) dancing over the fire! This is more than just grilled skewers; it’s Indonesia’s “national social food,” found everywhere from street stalls to wedding banquets.

![高級版沙爹 Sate Lilit](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_ad43250bb9775984fb58c3e834abd564.jpg)

Premium Satay: Sate Lilit

**🔥 The Three Secrets of Satay**

**1️⃣ The Art of Skewering**

- **Bamboo Skewers vs. Lemongrass Stems**:
- - The standard version uses thin bamboo skewers (perfect for popping one after another)
  - **The premium version uses lemongrass stems** (Sate Lilit), which infuse the meat with a fragrant lemongrass aroma after grilling!
- **Meat Encyclopedia**:
- - **Sate Ayam** (Chicken) | **Sate Sapi** (Beef)
  - **Sate Babi** (Pork) | **Sate Lilit Ikan** (Fish mixed with coconut)
  - **Hidden Gem**: Sate Kambing (Goat meat, with a stronger gamey flavor)

**2️⃣ The Irresistible Sauces**

- **Peanut Sauce**: Freshly ground peanuts, palm sugar, and garlic simmered to a thick, chocolate-like consistency
- **Sweet Soy Sauce** (Kecap Manis): A caramel-hued, dark brown sauce that's sweet with a hint of saltiness
- **Sambal Chili Sauce**: If you can handle the heat, ask for "Pedas extra!" (extra spicy)

**3️⃣ The Charcoal-Grilled Ritual**

- Vendors typically grill **30-50 skewers** at a time, and during festivals, hundreds are grilled together in a spectacular display
- **Banana leaves are used to fan the coals**, infusing every fiber of the meat with smoky aroma

### 5. **Iga Babi Panggang (Honey-Glazed Pork Ribs) — Bali's Guilty Pleasure**

![5. Iga Babi Panggang（蜜汁烤豬肋排）—— 峇里島的罪惡快樂](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_4ee77298a14e6cba766ff1f7336a0f17.jpg)

If you think suckling pig (Babi Guling) is too traditional, **Iga Babi Panggang** (pork ribs in English) is Bali's take on "American BBQ ribs" — but sweeter, more aromatic, and with more layers of flavor!

**🔥 Why These Pork Ribs Drive People Wild?**

**1️⃣ 24-Hour Spice SPA**

1. - First, marinated with **Basa Genep (Balinese mixed spice paste)**
   - **Palm sugar (Gula Jawa)** and **tropical honey** are added to create a sweet base
   - Secret weapon: **Tamarind paste** brings a fruity, tangy kick

**2️⃣ Double Grilling Technique**

1. - First, slow-roast at low heat for 4 hours to tenderize the meat
   - Then, finish with direct charcoal grilling to create a caramelized crispy crust

### 6. **Nasi Goreng: Bali's 'National Fried Rice'**

![6. Nasi Goreng 峇里島「國民炒飯」](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_1285e1be061d17bb96ecb35f176c3aec.jpg)

How could we forget Indonesia's soul dish—**Nasi Goreng (Indonesian Fried Rice)**? This isn't just any fried rice; it's a 'dark and smoky' delight stir-fried with **sweet soy sauce (Kecap Manis)** and **sambal (Sambal)**, boasting a wok hei that rivals Macau's clay pot rice!

🍳 **Three Highlights of Nasi Goreng**

**1️⃣ Spice Code**  
Sauté **shallots, garlic slices, and candlenuts (Kemiri)** until fragrant, then add the signature sweet soy sauce to create grains of rice with a 'salty-sweet-spicy' trinity of flavors—completely distinct from Thai pineapple fried rice or Hong Kong Yangzhou fried rice!

**2️⃣ Luxurious Toppings**  
The basic version includes **shredded chicken, shrimp, and a fried egg**, while upgraded versions might feature:

1. - **Sate Ayam** (chicken satay skewers)
   - **Rendang** (beef in coconut milk)
   - **Krupuk (shrimp crackers)** placed on the plate as garnish

**3️⃣ Secret Eating Style**  
Locals love to poke the **sunny-side-up egg** to let the yolk coat the rice, then squeeze some **lime juice** to cut through the richness!

### **7. Mie Goreng (Indonesian Fried Noodles): The King of Late-Night Street Food**

![7.Mie Goreng（印尼炒麵）—— 平民宵夜之王](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_f312682b08f56f3ab8dd0284244c584e.jpg)

If Nasi Goreng is the king of Indonesian fried rice, then **Mie Goreng** is its "noodle sibling"—equally beloved as a daily street food staple in Bali!

**🍳 What Makes It Special**

**1️⃣ Noodle Types**:

- Common yellow noodles, or instant noodles like Indomie
  - Upscale restaurants may use hand-pulled fresh noodles
  - Also available in thick or thin rice vermicelli versions

**2️⃣ The Soul Sauce**: Sweet soy sauce (Kecap Manis) + sambal chili paste (Sambal) + fried garlic for an aromatic kick

**3️⃣ Classic Toppings**:

- Basic: Chicken, shrimp, cabbage, bean sprouts
  - Deluxe: Add satay, fried shrimp crackers (Krupuk), and a sunny-side-up egg

### 8. **Bebek Bengil (Dirty Duck) — Bali's Most Legendary Countryside Feast**

![8. Bebek Bengil（髒鴨餐）—— 峇里島最傳奇的田園饗宴](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6c3ebab1d089d9493682b46c684d8502.jpg)

Nestled amid Ubud's lush rice terraces lies a legendary dish that haunts the taste buds of countless food lovers—**Bebek Bengil** (English: Dirty Duck). Voted by CNN as a "must-eat in Bali," this classic is far more than a gimmicky name; it's a ritual of pure flavor!

**🌾 Why Is It Called "Dirty Duck"?**

The name originated in the 1990s on a rainy day when a group of ducks wandered into the restaurant's muddy yard, getting completely covered in mud. The owner had a stroke of inspiration and turned 'Bengil' (dirty) into a signature. Today, this **original Ubud location** has become a culinary landmark, with its rice field view seats being an absolute highlight!

**🔍 Unraveling the Three Secrets of Dirty Duck**

**1️⃣ Premium Duck Selection**

- Only uses **free-range Balinese ducks** raised for 45 days, with firm, gamey meat
- Freshly slaughtered daily, never frozen (prep starts at 4 AM)

**2️⃣ Spice Infusion Technique**

- 1. First, rub with salt and kaffir lime to remove any gamey smell
  2. Massage with **Basa Rajang** spice paste (containing 13 spices like turmeric and candlenut)
  3. Finally, brush with palm sugar syrup

**3️⃣ Traditional Cooking Method**

- Wrapped in banana leaves and **smoked over coconut husks**
- Modern versions are first slow-cooked then fried, creating a 'crispy outside, melt-in-your-mouth inside' texture

**💡 Fun Fact:**

The authentic way to eat is to tear the duck by hand, then shape crispy skin, tender meat, rice, and sambal into a rice ball using a technique locals call '**Cremes**'!

### **9. Sop Buntut (Indonesian Oxtail Soup)**

![9.Sop Buntut（印尼牛尾湯）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_4627aa4329d41d2364576f0df857fb02.jpg)

When a tropical downpour suddenly hits Bali, or when your palate needs a break from spicy dishes, a steaming bowl of **Sop Buntut** is a godsend! Originating from Javanese royal cuisine, this rich soup perfectly blends 'char-grilled aroma, spice soul, and collagen sweetness'—you can even suck the marrow out of the bones!

**🔥 What Makes This Soup Special?**

**1️⃣ Prep Work:**

- - Oxtail marinated with **turmeric + black pepper** for 12 hours
  - Grilled over coconut shell charcoal 'until caramelized'—this is the key to the flavor!

**2️⃣ Simmering Magic:**

- - Slow-cooked for 8 hours with **10 spices**, including cloves and nutmeg
  - **Roasted tomatoes** added for natural umami

Oxtail soup is believed to have traveled from Java to Bali, but the two versions have distinct flavors:

### **10. Bubur Injin (Black Rice Pudding)**

![10.Bubur Injin（黑米布丁）](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_ac3ba68004d65e62711e83be4a0b29f9.jpg)

After indulging in Bali's bold and spicy cuisine, a bowl of glistening **Bubur Injin** is the perfect sweet and wholesome finale no dessert lover or health enthusiast should miss! Made from ancient 'purple-stem rice,' this dessert is more than just a sweet treat—it's a nourishing heirloom passed down through Balinese generations for centuries.

**🌾 The Secret of Black Glutinous Rice**

- **Rice Variety**:  
  Uses Indonesia's unique **black glutinous rice** (Ketan Hitam), which contains three times more anthocyanins than regular purple rice  
  Natural antioxidants are twice that of blueberries  
  Coconut sugar (Gula Jawa) has a glycemic index 40% lower than white sugar

**🍧 Deconstructing a Top-Tier Bubur Injin**

**1️⃣ Base Preparation**

- Soak black glutinous rice overnight in **coconut water**
- Simmer with **pandan leaves** and **cinnamon sticks** for 2 hours until the grains burst open into a deep purple-black hue

**2️⃣ Golden Ratio Sauces**

- **Coconut cream sauce**: Freshly squeezed coconut cream with a pinch of sea salt, gently boiled
- **Caramel sauce**: Coconut sugar and palm sugar in a 1:1 ratio, simmered to an amber color

Black glutinous rice dessert simmered with coconut sugar, drizzled with coconut cream and topped with banana slices—a healing finale where aromas blend together!

**🌿 Cultural Trivia**：

In Bali, postpartum women swear by Bubur Injin, believing it helps "Ngejukin rahina" (restore their complexion). Locals jokingly call it an "edible black pearl face mask"!

Next time your legs give out from exploring Ubud, skip the coffee—find a family-run Warung and order a warm bowl of black glutinous rice pudding. When the coconut milk and caramel melt on your tongue, your taste buds will sing: "Enak pol!" (Freaking delicious!) 💜

## 🌿 **Vegetarian-Friendly Options**

- **Gado-Gado**: Warm salad with peanut sauce, topped with fried tofu, boiled eggs, and Indonesia's beloved Krupuk shrimp crackers
- **Tempeh Manis**: Fermented soybean cake stir-fried in sweet soy sauce until caramelized—packed with protein!

## ☕ **Drinks to Note**

- **Kopi Luwak**: The world's most expensive civet coffee—best enjoyed at ethical plantations to avoid animal welfare concerns
- **Jamu**: A traditional turmeric and honey tonic, Bali's go-to "wellness bubble tea"
- **Bintang Beer**: Indonesia's national beer, a perfect match with satay

**🗺️ Food Hunter Tips:**

- Street stalls and small eateries are more authentic than restaurants! Look for Warungs with a local queue
- For roasted pork or duck dishes, **buy before noon**—they often sell out later
- Spot "Pedas" on the menu = spicy; "Manis" = sweet
