### Pre-Trip Planning: Don't Underestimate the Importance of Itinerary Planning

The biggest pitfall when traveling with young kids is thinking, 'We'll figure it out when we get there.' While Bali is rich in travel resources, many things for toddlers aren't readily available or require a special search. Before you go:

- Confirm your flight choice (the difference between a long direct flight and a layover can be huge for young children)
- Confirm if your accommodation provides a crib and has children's play facilities
- Plan the daily pace in advance—don't rush, as toddlers need nap time.

Budget planning is also crucial; traveling with kids costs more than adults, especially for medical needs and children's supplies, which are often pricier if bought on the island than in Taiwan. (Read more: [How Much Cash to Bring for Bali Travel?](/en/blog/bali-travel-cash-budget-guide/))

### The Flight Hurdle: Packing for Your Toddler

The flight is often the most stressful part of traveling with a young child, but being well-prepared can significantly reduce the pressure.

In-flight comfort tools: For kids aged 1-3, consider bringing an inflatable footrest so they can lie down and rest, or a ride-on suitcase that doubles as a bed (like the JetKids). However, not all airlines allow these, so it's best to check in advance. For infants under one year old, you can reserve a bassinet seat, but this needs to be requested a few weeks ahead.

In-flight hydration: The cabin air is very dry, so remember to bring baby lotion and saline nasal spray to keep your baby's nasal passages moist. Ear pressure changes are noticeable during takeoff and landing—nursing or offering a bottle is the most effective way to relieve it; for older kids, you can give them candy.

Packing hack: Prepare a small bag with multiple pockets, stuffed with snacks, small toys, stickers, and mini books. Let your child explore and rummage through it themselves—the process of discovery alone can kill quite a bit of time.

Baby carrier: When moving through the airport and around Bali, a good-quality carrier made of natural fabric is incredibly useful. Many places in Bali aren't stroller-friendly, so a carrier offers much more flexibility.

### Baby Food and Formula: How Much to Bring and Can You Buy It Locally?

This is a major concern for many moms. To be honest, you can find quite a few things in Bali, but there are definitely some pitfalls to avoid.

**Formula**: If your baby drinks a special formula (hydrolyzed protein, hypoallergenic), it's strongly recommended to bring enough from home, as options on the island are very limited. Standard formula can be found in supermarkets and pharmacies, with common brands including Nutrilon, Lactogen (Nestlé), and Enfamil—Enfamil also has a lactose-free version. Australian organic brands like Bellamy's (cow's milk) and Karihome (goat's milk) are available but tend to be pricey.

**Jarred baby food**: It's advisable to bring enough meat purees, especially turkey, which is nearly impossible to find in Bali. Some imported supermarkets (like Pepito) occasionally carry Heinz or Bellamy's fruit purees, but these are mostly fruit and vegetable flavors and cost about twice as much as in Taiwan. If your baby is still on purees, a more practical approach once you arrive in Bali might be to buy a blender and make purees from local ingredients.

**Dairy Products**: The overall quality of dairy products in Bali is not ideal—they tend to be high in sugar or overpriced. If your child is used to drinking yogurt or kefir, consider bringing starter cultures to make your own. Plant-based milk options are plentiful, and lactose-free milk brand Milk Life is also available.

**Health Food Stores**: Bali Buda is a chain of organic health food stores across the island. You can find a variety of grains (including buckwheat, which is rare in Taiwan), gluten-free and dairy-free foods—making it a great supply stop for families with young children.

### Diapers & Hygiene Products

**Diapers**: The brand selection differs from Taiwan. Better options include Korean and Japanese brands like Genki, Makuku, and Merries. Some places also carry biodegradable Ecoboom. Note that Merries sold on the island is mostly locally produced in Indonesia, with a noticeable quality difference. The genuine Japanese-made white packaging is only available at select retailers (such as Clandy's or Bali Hypermart), or you can order online via Shopee/Tokopedia. Swim diaper pants are almost exclusively available online, so it's best to bring enough from Taiwan.

**Sizing Tip**: Many diaper brands run narrow, so consider buying one size up.

**Other Baby Supplies**: Offline stores include Balonku, Genyc, and Mothercare for baby products. Baby furniture can be found at Informa, and tableware and storage items at IKEA (order in-store or via the app, with delivery available).

**Special Skincare**: If your child has specific skin conditions requiring particular shampoos or creams (e.g., Mustela), bring them from Taiwan—the selection on the island is limited.

**Hats**: A sun-protective hat for toddlers is essential. It's best to bring one from home that your child is used to—while hats in Bali's markets are cheap, many are too thick or have poorly designed straps that won't stay on your child's head.

### Medications: Please Prepare This List Carefully

Most pharmacies in Bali sell generic drugs; brand-name ones are available but cost two to three times more, and many require a prescription. If your child is on long-term medication, be sure to bring enough.

Other recommended over-the-counter medications to bring:

- **Digestive**: Simethicone for gas relief (e.g., Gas-X), probiotics, intestinal adsorbents, digestive enzymes
- **Wounds**: Antiseptic spray, healing ointment, band-aids, liquid skin adhesive
- **Throat & Cold**: Throat anti-inflammatory spray, throat lozenges, saline nasal rinse, fever reducer and pain reliever
- **Eyes**: Artificial tears or anti-inflammatory eye drops
- **Nose**: Saline nasal spray, vasoconstrictors
- **Allergies & Insects**: Antihistamines (e.g., cetirizine/Zyrtec), anti-itch gel
- **For Toddlers**: Teething gel, nasal aspirator
- **Other**: COVID/flu rapid tests, thermometer

**Most important: Purchase travel medical insurance before departure.** Private hospitals in Bali are decent, but extremely expensive—without insurance, an emergency can really hurt your wallet.

## **A Few Extra Tips:**

- Bring your child's favorite toys and activities on the plane to help keep them happy.
- In hot weather, make sure your child drinks plenty of water and protect them from the sun.
- Before visiting temples or other religious sites, teach your child to respect the local culture.
- Learn some basic Indonesian phrases, like “thank you.” Check out Xiao Jie’s [video on how to pronounce it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuG8s-u_I0Q&t=106s)

Bali is truly a great place to bring toddlers—as long as you prepare well.
