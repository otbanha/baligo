### Pre-Trip Planning: Don't Underestimate the Importance of Itinerary Planning

The biggest pitfall when traveling with young children is thinking, 'We'll figure it out when we get there.' While Bali is rich in travel resources, many things needed for little ones aren't readily available or require a special search. Before you go:

- Confirm your flight choice (the impact of a long direct flight vs. a layover on young children is huge)
- Verify if your accommodation provides a crib and has children's play facilities
- Plan a daily rhythm in advance—don't rush; toddlers need nap time

Budget planning is also key. Traveling with kids costs more than with adults alone, especially for medical needs and children's supplies, which are often pricier if bought on the island. Related reading: [How Much Cash Do You Need for a Bali Trip?](/en/blog/bali-travel-cash-budget-guide/)

### Surviving the Flight: How to Pack for Toddlers

The flight is often the most stressful part of traveling with young children, but being well-prepared can significantly reduce the pressure.

Flight aids: For children aged 1-3, consider bringing an inflatable footrest so they can lie flat and rest, or a ride-on suitcase that doubles as a bed (like JetKids). However, not all airlines allow these, so check beforehand. For infants under one year old, you can reserve a bassinet seat, but you need to apply a few weeks in advance.

In-flight hydration: The cabin air is very dry, so remember to bring baby lotion and saline nasal spray to keep your baby's nasal passages moist. During takeoff and landing, ear pressure changes are noticeable; breastfeeding or offering a bottle is the most effective relief, and older kids can have candy.

Packing hack: Prepare a small bag with multiple pockets, stuffed with snacks, small toys, stickers, and little books. Let your child explore it on their own—the process of discovery itself can kill quite a bit of time.

Baby carrier: When moving around the airport and Bali, a good-quality carrier made of natural materials is very practical. Many places in Bali aren't great for strollers, so a carrier offers much more flexibility.

### Baby food and formula: How much to bring, and can you buy it locally?

This is a big worry for many moms. To be honest, you can find most things in Bali, but there are some pitfalls to avoid.

**Formula**: If your baby uses special formula (hydrolyzed protein, hypoallergenic), it's strongly recommended to bring enough, as options on the island are very limited. Standard formula is available at supermarkets and pharmacies, with common brands including Nutrilon, Lactogen (Nestlé), and Enfamil, which also has a lactose-free version. Australian organic brands like Bellamy's (cow's milk) and Karihome (goat's milk) are also available but pricey.

**Baby food pouches**: It's best to bring enough meat purees, especially turkey, which is nearly impossible to find in Bali. Some import supermarkets (like Pepito) occasionally carry fruit purees from Heinz or Bellamy's, but they're mostly fruit and vegetable flavors and cost about twice as much as in Taiwan. If your baby is still on purees, buying a blender in Bali and making food from local ingredients might be a more practical approach.

**Dairy products**: The overall quality of dairy in Bali isn't ideal—either high in sugar or expensive. If your child is used to yogurt or kefir, consider bringing starter cultures to make your own. Plant-based milk options are plentiful, and lactose-free cow's milk like Milk Life is also available.

**Health food stores**: Bali Buda is a chain of organic health food stores across the island. You can find various grains (including buckwheat, which is rare in Taiwan), gluten-free and dairy-free foods—a great supply stop for families with young children.

### Diapers & Toiletries

**Diapers**: The brand selection is quite different from Taiwan. The better options are Korean and Japanese brands like Genki, Makuku, and Merries. Some places also carry biodegradable Ecoboom. Note that Merries sold on the island is mostly locally produced in Indonesia, with a noticeable drop in quality. The genuine Japanese-made white packaging is only available at a few retailers (such as Clandy's or Bali Hypermart), or you can order online via Shopee/Tokopedia. Swim diaper pants are almost exclusively available online, so it's best to bring enough from Taiwan.

**Sizing Tip**: Many diaper brands run narrow, so consider sizing up.

**Other Baby Supplies**: Offline stores like Balonku, Genyc, and Mothercare carry baby products. For baby furniture, check out Informa. For tableware and storage, IKEA is a good option (order in-store or via the app, with delivery available).

**Special Skincare**: If your child has specific skin conditions requiring particular shampoos or creams (e.g., Mustela), bring them from Taiwan—the selection on the island is limited.

**Hats**: A sun-protective hat for toddlers is essential. It's best to bring one from home that you know fits well—while hats at Bali's markets are cheap, many are too thick or have poorly designed straps that won't stay on your child's head.

### Medications: Take This List Seriously

Most pharmacies in Bali sell generic drugs; brand-name options are available but cost two to three times more and often require a prescription. If your child is on long-term medication, be sure to bring enough.

Other recommended medications to bring:

- **Digestive**: Anti-gas medication (e.g., Gas-X), probiotics, intestinal adsorbents, digestive enzymes
- **Injuries**: Antiseptic spray, healing ointment, band-aids, liquid skin adhesive
- **Throat & Cold**: Throat anti-inflammatory spray, throat lozenges, saline nasal rinse, fever and pain relievers
- **Eyes**: Artificial tears or anti-inflammatory eye drops
- **Nose**: Saline nasal spray, vasoconstrictors
- **Allergies & Insects**: Antihistamines (e.g., cetirizine/Zyrtec), anti-itch gel
- **For Toddlers**: Teething gel, nasal aspirator
- **Other**: COVID/flu rapid tests, thermometer

Most importantly: **Make sure to purchase travel medical insurance before departure**. Private hospitals in Bali are decent, but costs are very high—without insurance, an emergency can seriously drain your wallet.

## **A few extra tips:**

- In hot weather, make sure kids drink plenty of water and stay protected from the sun.
- Before visiting temples or other religious sites, teach children to respect local customs.

Bali is truly a great destination for traveling with toddlers—as long as you prepare well. These days, most basic necessities are available on the island, though the brand selection is limited, some items need to be ordered online, and a few things cost more than in Taiwan.
