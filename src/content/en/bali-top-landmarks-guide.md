## GWK Cultural Park

![GWK Cultural Park 神鷹廣場](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_fe7847c15db88a7191a1f728c33d7bbe.jpg)

GWK Cultural Park is one of the few popular attractions in Bali backed by government support. Located near Jimbaran, it's famous for its grand sculptures, impressive architecture, and Balinese cultural elements. The most iconic feature is the 121-meter-tall statue of Vishnu riding the Garuda, the fourth tallest in the world. The park also hosts cultural events and performances, where you can enjoy traditional dance and music. There's a cultural center offering deeper insights into Balinese culture.

## Pura Besakih — The Mother Temple

![Pura Besakih 百沙基母廟](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_145940526f93b2b01a3a93bf4e89daf3.jpg)

Pura Besakih is one of Bali's most important landmarks and the largest and holiest Hindu temple on the island, perched on the slopes of Mount Agung. Revered as the spiritual heart of Bali, it is also known as the 'Mother Temple,' featuring multiple shrines and altars dedicated to various deities.

The grand temple complex of Pura Besakih sits amidst stunning valleys and lush green rice terraces, offering breathtaking views. Visitors can soak in the centuries-old religious atmosphere, explore the intricate temple architecture, and feel the sacred ambiance. It also serves as a key venue for many Balinese religious and cultural events, such as festivals and ceremonies.

## Monkey Forest & Pura Taman Saraswati — Ubud's Monkey Forest and Water Palace

![Monkey Forest & Pura Taman Saraswati 猴子森林 & 烏布水皇宮](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_bf494c9ed8153f01ba76c1699bb373a3.jpg)

Both located in the heart of Ubud, the Monkey Forest and the Ubud Water Palace are unmissable landmarks.

Ubud's Monkey Forest is a unique attraction that draws visitors for close encounters with monkeys. With dense trees and rich biodiversity, it serves as a natural habitat for these primates. Guests can observe various monkey activities and interact with them for photos. It's also a great place to unwind—stroll along forest paths and enjoy the natural beauty and peaceful atmosphere.

## Tirta Empul Temple

https://youtube.com/shorts/fiYs5GKdINU

Beyond Bali's most famous Tirta Empul Temple, there are two other holy spring temples hidden in serene corners, all within a short distance from each other, waiting to be discovered by explorers. These two sacred sites not only let you experience Bali's unique religious culture but also offer a sincere and tranquil bathing experience in a relatively quiet environment.

Here are the two other holy spring temples we recommend:

**Dalem Pingit Holy Spring** is a relatively secluded temple. It boasts unique architecture, and its sacred spring is deeply revered by local devotees. In this peaceful setting, you can feel the authenticity and serenity of Bali's original religion. Dalem Pingit is known for its beautiful waterfall-fed holy spring.

**Pura Gunung Kawi Holy Spring** is a temple famous for its rock carvings. Beyond the ancient statues, there is also a hidden holy spring where devotees and visitors can bathe to receive blessings. This tranquil temple area is filled with a sense of mystery, making you feel as if you've traveled back in time to experience Bali's ancient sacredness.

These two different holy spring temples each have their own unique charm and cultural depth, while also offering a relatively quiet bathing environment, allowing you to find peace and purification in Bali's sacred atmosphere.

## Tanah Lot Temple

![Tanah Lot Temple 海神廟](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_4627595c4f7e1a1a61c20ebb613256f0.jpg)

Bali's Tanah Lot is a famous landmark, renowned for its stunning sea temple. Built atop a massive rock, it is one of Indonesia's most iconic Hindu temples. Every sunset, visitors gather here to admire the breathtaking view as the sun sets, making it one of Bali's top ten landmarks.

However, due to its immense popularity, if you plan to visit in the evening, be mindful of transportation to and from the area.

## Pura Ulun Danau Bratan

![Pura Ulun Danau 烏倫達努水神廟](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_7b504fc83d34c26e674b57a1c762bf58.jpg)

Pura Ulun Danau Bratan, completed in 1633, sits on the shores of Lake Bratan in central Bali, and is also known as the 'Lake Bratan Temple.' Since Lake Bratan is the primary irrigation source for central Bali, this temple was once the site of ceremonies honoring Dewi Danu, the goddess of water, lakes, and rivers. Additionally, the 11-tiered Pura Ulun Danau Bratan is dedicated to Shiva and Parvati, and a statue of Buddha is also enshrined here, making it a temple that blends Hinduism and Buddhism.

Notably, this Balinese water temple is featured on Indonesian currency, appearing on the second-largest denomination in circulation: the 50,000 Indonesian Rupiah note, which is worth about USD$3.

## Jatiluwih Rice Terrace

![Jatiluwih Rice Terrace 加蒂盧易梯田](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6b26a35c2746bdb8c6ba7f898bc97822.jpg)

The terraced rice fields around Ubud are a highlight of Bali, offering a picturesque landscape that has earned UNESCO World Heritage status. Ubud has two main rice terrace areas:

1. Tegalalang Rice Terrace is the most accessible and popular rice terrace area near Ubud. Its unique layout features winding paths through the terraces, creating a green canvas that showcases nature's vitality. Here, the rice paddies ripple like waves, forming a moving agricultural poem that blends natural beauty with farming culture.
2. The less commercialized Jatiluwih Rice Terrace is farther from Ubud, requiring a 90-minute drive from Ubud Palace, but it offers a more authentic experience of the pristine terraced fields!

The best time to capture the beauty of the rice terraces is in the early dawn, just as the sun rises. At this moment, golden sunlight spills across the terraces, casting rich layers of shadow and creating a mesmerizing play of light and shadow. It's the favorite time for photography enthusiasts and travelers alike, as the terraces seem to be immersed in a golden dream.
