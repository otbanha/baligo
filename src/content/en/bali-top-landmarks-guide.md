## GWK Cultural Park

![GWK Cultural Park 神鷹廣場](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_fe7847c15db88a7191a1f728c33d7bbe.jpg)

GWK Cultural Park is one of the few government-supported attractions in Bali, located near Jimbaran. It’s famous for its grand sculptures, stunning architecture, and Balinese cultural elements. The highlight is the 121-meter-tall statue of Lord Vishnu riding the Garuda—the fourth tallest in the world. The park hosts cultural events and performances, including traditional dance and music shows. There’s also a cultural center offering deeper insights into Balinese heritage.

## Pura Besakih

![Pura Besakih 百沙基母廟](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_145940526f93b2b01a3a93bf4e89daf3.jpg)

Pura Besakih is one of Bali's most important landmarks and the largest and holiest Hindu temple on the island, perched on the slopes of Mount Agung. Revered as the spiritual heart of Bali, it is often called the 'Mother Temple,' featuring numerous shrines and altars dedicated to various deities.

The grand temple complex of Pura Besakih sits amidst stunning valleys and lush green rice terraces, offering breathtaking views. Visitors can soak in the ancient religious atmosphere, explore the intricate temple architecture, and feel the sacred ambiance. It also serves as a key venue for many of Bali's religious and cultural events, such as festivals and ceremonies.

## Monkey Forest & Pura Taman Saraswati

![Monkey Forest & Pura Taman Saraswati 猴子森林 & 烏布水皇宮](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_bf494c9ed8153f01ba76c1699bb373a3.jpg)

Both located in the heart of Ubud, the Monkey Forest and Ubud Water Palace are must-visit landmarks.

Ubud's Monkey Forest is a unique attraction that draws visitors for close encounters with monkeys. With dense trees and rich biodiversity, it's a natural habitat for these primates. Guests can observe various monkey activities and interact with them for photos. It's also a great spot to unwind, strolling along forest paths while enjoying the natural beauty and peaceful atmosphere.

## Tirta Empul Temple (Holy Water Temple)

![Tirta Empul Temple 聖泉寺](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_2671370a86c84863c3c5f1ca502e8e1e.jpg)

Beyond the famous Pura Tirta Empul, Bali hides two holy spring temples in serene corners, both within easy reach, waiting for explorers to discover. These two sacred springs not only let you experience Bali's unique religious culture but also offer a sincere and peaceful bathing experience in a relatively tranquil setting.

Here are two other holy spring temples we recommend to you:

**Dalem Pingit Holy Spring** is a relatively secluded temple. This temple not only boasts a unique architectural style, but its holy spring is also deeply revered by local devotees. In this quiet environment, you can feel the sincerity and tranquility of Bali's original religion. Dalem Pingit is famous for its beautiful waterfall-fed holy spring.

**Pura Gunung Kawi Holy Spring** is a temple renowned for its rock carvings. Beyond the ancient statues, there is also a hidden holy spring here, offering devotees and visitors a place to bathe and receive blessings. This peaceful temple area is filled with a sense of mystery, making you feel as if you've traveled back in time to experience Bali's ancient sanctity.

These two different holy spring temples each have their own unique charm and cultural depth, while also providing a relatively quiet bathing environment, allowing you to find peace and purification in Bali's sacred atmosphere.

## Tanah Lot Temple

![Tanah Lot Temple 海神廟](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_4627595c4f7e1a1a61c20ebb613256f0.jpg)

Bali's Tanah Lot is a famous landmark, renowned for its stunning seaside temple. Built on a massive rock, this temple is one of Indonesia's most famous Hindu temples. Every sunset, visitors gather here to admire the magnificent scenery illuminated by the setting sun, making it one of Bali's top ten landmarks.

However, because this place is so famous, if you choose to visit in the evening, be mindful of transportation to and from the site.

## Pura Ulun Danau Beratan

![Pura Ulun Danau 烏倫達努水神廟](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_7b504fc83d34c26e674b57a1c762bf58.jpg)

Pura Ulun Danu, completed in 1633, sits on the shores of Lake Bratan in central Bali and is also known as the "Lake Bratan Temple." Since Lake Bratan is the primary irrigation source for central Bali, Pura Ulun Danu once served as a venue for ceremonies honoring Dewi Danu, the goddess of water, lakes, and rivers. Additionally, this 11-tiered temple is dedicated to Shiva and Parvati, and a statue of Buddha is also enshrined here, making it a fusion of Hindu and Buddhist traditions.

Remarkably, this Balinese water temple is featured on Indonesia's currency, specifically the second-largest denomination in circulation: the 50,000 Indonesian rupiah note, which is worth about USD$3.

## Jatiluwih Rice Terrace

![Jatiluwih Rice Terrace 加蒂盧易梯田](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6b26a35c2746bdb8c6ba7f898bc97822.jpg)

The terraced rice fields around Ubud are a highlight of Bali, offering a breathtaking landscape that has earned UNESCO World Heritage status. There are two main terrace areas near Ubud:

1. Tegalalang Rice Terrace is one of the most accessible and popular terraced areas near Ubud. Its unique layout features winding paths between the terraces, creating a green canvas that vividly showcases nature's vitality. Here, the rice paddies ripple like waves, forming a moving agricultural poem that blends natural beauty with farming culture.
2. The less commercialized Jatiluwih Rice Terrace is farther from Ubud, about a 90-minute drive from the Ubud Palace, but it offers a more pristine experience of the terraced fields.

The best time to capture the beauty of the rice terraces is at dawn, just as the sun rises. At this moment, golden sunlight spills across the terraces, creating layered shadows and a mesmerizing play of light and shadow. It's a favorite time for photographers and travelers, as the terraces seem to be immersed in a golden dream.
