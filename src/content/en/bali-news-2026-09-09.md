## 💎 Diamond Beach Reopens! Nusa Penida's 'Diamond Beach' Welcomes Visitors Again

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_ca4724a8eeb3ab1c2e10fa29582c9546.jpg)

Great news from Nusa Penida!

**Diamond Beach**, which was temporarily closed due to permit issues, has now reopened. The latest videos on social media announce the return of this popular beach with the phrase 'Diamond Beach is back.'

Located on the eastern side of Nusa Penida, Diamond Beach is famous for its **crystal-clear turquoise waters, towering cliffs, white sandy shores, and a staircase that descends along the cliffside**, making it one of the most iconic spots on the island.

This reopening is certainly welcome news for travelers planning a trip to Nusa Penida in the near future.

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdA4edSTDQm/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==

But here's an important heads-up:

**"Reopening" doesn't mean you can swim freely without restrictions.**

Diamond Beach has seen tourist drowning incidents in the past, with dangerous waves and rip currents. For now, you must follow on-site safety rules and avoid getting too close to the waves just for a photo.

Also, this reopening news mainly comes from local communities and tourism operators. The previous closure was tied to permit issues, so if you're heading there tomorrow, **it's best to have your driver or local guide double-check the current conditions on the ground.**

## ✈️ Bali Airport Gradually Resuming! Flights to Jakarta, Tangerang, and Bandung Reopen

After being affected by ash from Anak Krakatau, flights at Bali's airport have drawn a lot of attention from travelers over the past couple of days.

The latest update: **Ngurah Rai International Airport has gradually resumed flights to Jakarta, Tangerang, and Bandung.**

According to detikBali, these routes were back in operation on September 8, and the impact of flight cancellations from the volcanic ash in previous days is slowly being resolved.

On the other hand, major airports across Indonesia are also gradually reopening. Reuters reported today that airports like Soekarno-Hatta have reopened after the ash situation improved, though airlines are still assessing flight safety.

So, if you're **flying from Bali to Jakarta on a domestic flight tomorrow, then transferring to an international flight**, don't let the news that 'the airport is back to normal' give you complete peace of mind.

The safest approach is still:

**Check the latest flight status directly on the airline's app or website before you leave.**

## 🚕 Nusa Lembongan's Unique Transport Exposed! 'Pickup Taxi' Becomes a Must-Try for Foreign Tourists

If you've been to Nusa Lembongan, you're probably familiar with this mode of transport.

The local **Pickup Taxi**—essentially a small truck with an open-air passenger area converted from the cargo bed—has become the island's signature ride.

detikBali highlighted today that this Pickup Taxi has become a key transport option for many foreign tourists upon arriving in Nusa Lembongan.

The current price is around **150,000 Indonesian Rupiah per trip**. Besides getting you from point A to B, its open views make it feel like an 'island experience' for many travelers.

However, if you're carrying a lot of luggage, traveling with seniors, or have young children, the comfort and safety may not be as good as a regular car.

Here's my take:

**For short trips with light luggage, it's fun to try once; but for longer journeys, a regular car is much more comfortable.**

## 🌊 Nusa Lembongan Seaweed Prices Rise, but High Heat Slashes Harvests

Today, there's a rare piece of travel news from Nusa Lembongan.

Local seaweed farmers are facing a paradox:

**Seaweed prices are up, but actual harvests are down.**

Currently, seaweed is sold to collectors at around **IDR 31,000 per kilogram**, higher than the usual price of about IDR 25,000 per kilogram.

The problem is that recent hot weather has raised seawater temperatures, and there's less land available for seaweed farming. As a result, some farmers' yields have dropped from around 500 kilograms per harvest to just 200–300 kilograms.

This actually reflects an interesting shift on Nusa Lembongan.

In the past, it was an important seaweed farming area in Bali, but with the rapid growth of tourism, some seaweed cultivation zones have gradually been converted into docks, tourist facilities, and other uses.

So now when you visit Nusa Lembongan, besides enjoying the sea, snorkeling, and diving, you can also take note of the traditional seaweed farming culture that still exists on the island.

## 🚨 Chinese National Deported from Bali for Illegal Work

Bali's Immigration Office announced a new enforcement case today.

A Chinese national was deported by Bali immigration authorities after being found **illegally working as a project supervisor**.

The man's residence permit did not allow him to engage in such work, but during monitoring, immigration officers discovered his actual activities did not match the permitted scope, ultimately leading to deportation.

This is part of Bali's ongoing efforts to tighten immigration oversight on foreigners.

While it has little impact on regular tourists, this case is worth noting for foreigners planning to **stay long-term, work remotely, open a business, or take on freelance projects** in Bali.

Especially don't:

**"You can stay in Bali"**

mistake it for:

**"You can work in Bali."**

Under Indonesian immigration law, these are two completely different things.
