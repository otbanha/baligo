## When is Bali's Rainy Season? (Monthly Rainfall Comparison)

**Bali's rainy season runs from November to March, and the dry season from April to October.** The wettest month is January (about 388 mm of rain, roughly 19 rainy days), while the driest is August (about 16 mm, roughly 2 rainy days).

But the numbers don't capture what Bali's rain actually looks like: except for the peak in January–February, it's mostly a heavy afternoon downpour that stops within an hour or two, not a constant drizzle all day. Mornings are usually sunny. That's why many people still visit in November, December, and March—just schedule outdoor activities in the morning and keep afternoons flexible.

| Month | Average Rainfall | Rainy Days | Season |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | 388 mm | 19 days | Rainy season · Wettest month |
| February | 299 mm | 17 days | Rainy season |
| March | 214 mm | 13 days | Rainy season, easing up |
| April | 115 mm | 9 days | Transition to dry season |
| May | 73 mm | 7 days | Dry season |
| June | 51 mm | 5 days | Dry season |
| July | 30 mm | 3 days | Dry season · Peak season |
| August | 16 mm | 2 days | Dry season · Driest month |
| September | 41 mm | 3 days | Dry season |
| October | 68 mm | 5 days | Transition to rainy season |
| November | 149 mm | 10 days | Rainy season begins |
| December | 299 mm | 16 days | Rainy season · Christmas & New Year peak |

The above figures are long-term averages for southern Bali (Denpasar). Ubud and the central highlands are noticeably wetter, while the southern Uluwatu peninsula and the northern coast around Lovina are relatively drier.

## Exact Date When the Rainy Season Ends?

According to the latest official news from Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) released from March to early April 2026, there is currently **no single nationwide 'exact end date'** (such as a specific day of the month). Instead, predictions are made in phases based on each 'Seasonal Zone' (ZOM).

Here is a summary of the specific time points mentioned in media reports:

### **Key Timeline for the Seasonal Transition**

Based on statements from BMKG head Teuku Faisal Fathani, the climate turning points for 2026 are as follows:

- **Official End of Rainy Season / Start of Dry Season:**
    - **April:** Approximately **16.3%** of regions will enter the dry season first, starting from eastern **Nusa Tenggara**.
    - **May:** About **26.3%** of regions (including **Bali** and parts of Java) will enter the dry season.
    - **June:** The remaining **23.3%** of regions will follow.
- **Peak of Dry Season:** Around **61.4%** of all Indonesian regions will reach their driest peak in **August 2026**.

### **Why Is There No Exact Date?**

BMKG notes that the seasonal transition across Indonesia progresses 'from east to west,' so the dates vary:

- **Eastern regions (e.g., Lombok, Sumbawa):** Begin turning dry in early April.
- **Central regions (e.g., Bali, East Java):** Expected to complete the transition from mid-to-late April to early May.
- **Western regions (e.g., Sumatra, West Java):** May not fully enter the dry season until late May or June.

### **Special Climate Warnings for 2026**

Media outlets have particularly highlighted how this year differs from previous ones:

- **Early Onset:** Nearly half (46.5%) of the country's regions will see the dry season start **earlier** than usual.
- **Extreme Dryness:** With the weak La Niña ending in February and a possible **weak El Niño** emerging in July, BMKG predicts this year's dry season will be longer and drier than in 2025.
- **Transition Warning:** Early April is currently in the **Pancaroba (transition season)**. While the rainy season is ending, strong afternoon thunderstorms, high winds, and even hail are still possible. BMKG reminds the public to stay alert for sudden extreme weather during this transition period.

If you're planning activities in **Bali** or Java, media reports citing BMKG suggest that **after mid-April**, the weather will become noticeably more stable, with rainfall frequency dropping significantly, marking the start of a season ideal for outdoor activities.

## 📅 2026 Indonesia Transition Season FAQ

#### Q1: When is the 2026 rainy season expected to end in Indonesia?

A: According to BMKG forecasts, the end of the rainy season varies by region. About 16.3% of the country will enter the dry season in **April**, while most areas (around 50%) will transition between **May and June**. Overall, this year's dry season is arriving earlier than usual.

#### Q2: Which areas will enter the dry season first?

A: The climate shift will start in eastern Indonesia and move westward.
First wave (April): Nusa Tenggara Islands (e.g., Lombok, Komodo).
Second wave (May): Bali, East Java, and Central Java.
Third wave (June): West Java, Sumatra, and Kalimantan.

#### Q3: What's special about this year's dry season?

A: This year is expected to be an **"extremely dry"** year. With La Niña ending and a potential weak El Niño on the horizon, BMKG predicts that about 46.5% of regions will see an **early start to the dry season**, with rainfall below the historical average.

#### Q4: Is it a good time to travel to Java or Bali now (early April)?

A: We're currently in the Pancaroba (transitional season). Although the rainy season is ending, the atmosphere remains unstable, with brief but intense afternoon thunderstorms possible. It's best to schedule outdoor activities in the morning and stay alert for sudden strong winds or heavy rain warnings.

#### Q5: What's the best time to travel in 2026?

A: Mid-to-late April: Ideal for travelers who love lush green scenery but want less rain.

May to September: The most stable weather period across Indonesia, with the lowest chance of rain. This is the golden window for Bali's beaches or climbing Java's volcanoes like Bromo and Ijen.

#### Q6: What should I watch out for when traveling during this period?

A: 1. Sun protection and hydration: As the dry season approaches, UV rays intensify significantly—be sure to protect your skin.

2. Temperature shifts: During the dry season, highland areas in Java can see nighttime temperatures drop sharply (even near 0°C). Hikers need to pack enough warm clothing.
