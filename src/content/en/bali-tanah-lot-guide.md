## 1. The History and Religious Significance of Tanah Lot

The history of Tanah Lot dates back to the 16th century, when it was reportedly built by the Javanese priest Nirartha. During his travels, Nirartha was drawn to the spiritual energy of this sea-bound islet and established a temple dedicated to the sea god, seeking blessings for local fishermen and villagers. Since then, Tanah Lot has become a vital part of local faith and culture.

The temple's architecture is simple yet powerful, especially during high tide when it appears to float on the sea, adding an air of mystery and solemnity. At low tide, visitors can walk up to the temple's front to admire its intricately carved structure. As an important religious site in Bali, Tanah Lot also hosts special ceremonies, the most notable being the 'Piodalan' festival, which typically takes place four days after the Balinese holiday of Hari Raya Kuningan. Visitors can witness the locals' devout religious practices firsthand.

## 2. Top Activities to Explore at Tanah Lot

Tanah Lot is more than just a place to see an ancient temple; it offers a variety of activities for visitors to enjoy. Here are some not-to-be-missed experiences:

1. **Admire the Stunning Views of Tanah Lot and the Rocky Islet**
   The rocky islet where Tanah Lot Temple sits is a natural wonder, appearing and disappearing with the tides. Many visitors walk over during low tide to get close to the temple and witness the dramatic waves crashing against the rocks. This iconic islet, known as Tanah Lot, offers a unique vantage point that makes it a favorite spot for capturing Bali's scenic beauty.
2. **See the Sacred Snakes at the Snake Temple**
   Another unique feature is the "Sacred Snake Cave" near the temple, home to snakes locals call "Ular Suci" (sacred snakes). These black-and-yellow snakes with flat, fish-like tails are believed to be incarnations of Nirartha, the founder of Tanah Lot Temple, and are thought to have a divine duty to protect the site. Visitors can observe these snakes under the guidance of local guides, experiencing the locals' reverence for these sacred animals.
3. **Capture the Magical Sunset at Tanah Lot**
   The sunset at Tanah Lot is hailed as one of the best in Bali. As the sun dips below the horizon, the entire temple is bathed in golden light, creating a serene and romantic atmosphere. To find the best viewing spot for the "golden sunset," it's recommended to arrive early and scout out the ideal angle. This magical time draws crowds of photography enthusiasts, making it a prime moment for shooting.

## 3. How to Get to Tanah Lot? Travel Time and Transport Options from Each Area

**Key Point First**: Tanah Lot is located in the **Tabanan** area, with no direct public transport—you'll need to rely on **private car hire, ride-hailing, or self-driving**. The nearest base is **Canggu**, about a 30-minute drive away; from Ubud, allow at least 1.5 hours. Sunset is the busiest time of day, and **the return trip is especially prone to traffic jams**, so be sure to build in extra time.

Travel times are for reference only. Bali's roads are narrow and busy, so **actual times vary greatly depending on traffic**. If your goal is to catch the sunset, plan to leave at least 1.5–2 hours early.

### How to Choose Among Four Transport Options

1. **Private Car Hire (Most Recommended)**
   A typical visit to Tanah Lot lasts about 1–2 hours. The biggest advantage of hiring a private car is that **your driver can wait for you in the parking lot**, so you don't have to worry about the return trip. Rates are roughly **10 hours / 5-seater / south of Ubud: Rp 600,000–800,000 (about US$40–53), charged per car, not per person**; from July 1, 2026, the base rate stays the same, but an additional fuel surcharge applies per trip (Rp 50,000 for half-day, Rp 100,000 for full-day). Whether cross-zone trips or extra stops cost more should be confirmed with your driver when booking. For detailed fee structures, see [Bali Private Car Hire Guide: FAQs, Costs, and Booking](/en/blog/bali-private-car-hire-guide/), and for the latest updates, check [2026 Private Car Hire Rate Adjustment Notice](/en/blog/bali-car-charter-rates-2026/).

2. **Gojek / Grab Ride-Hailing**
   Getting a ride from Canggu or Seminyak to Tanah Lot is generally easy and cheaper than private car hire. **But be extra careful on the return trip**—the parking lot area has its own local transport ecosystem, and ride-hailing apps often face restrictions on picking up passengers. During sunset, when everyone leaves at once, you may wait a long time or even fail to get a ride. If you plan to use ride-hailing both ways, confirm your return plan in advance, or simply ask your driver to wait. For tips on using ride-hailing apps, see [Gojek Full Guide](/en/blog/gojek-bali-guide/).
3. **Renting a Scooter**
   Riding from Canggu takes about 30 minutes, offering the most flexibility and easy parking (scooter parking fee is only 3,000 IDR). However, some stretches of road are in poor condition, and **after sunset, it gets dark and traffic becomes chaotic, making night riding risky for beginners**—not recommended for those without riding experience.
4. **Joining a Day Tour**
   Tanah Lot is often combined with Ubud, Taman Ayun Temple, or Ulun Danu Beratan Temple on a western Bali day tour, ideal for those who don't want to arrange their own transport. The downside is that the stop time is fixed, and you can't adjust it if the tide isn't ideal.

### Upon Arrival

The parking lot is a short walk from the temple entrance, along a pedestrian street lined with souvenir shops on both sides. It takes about 5–10 minutes to reach the ticket booth and the seaside platform. **Wear sturdy, non-slip shoes**—when the tide is low, the rocky steps are slippery and covered in moss, making it easy to slip in crowded conditions.

## 4. Travel Tips: Ticket Info, Tide Schedules, and Nearby Accommodation

Before visiting Tanah Lot, knowing the ticket and transportation details can make your trip smoother.

Tanah Lot official website: [www.tanahlot.id](https://www.tanahlot.id/)

The latest 2026 ticket prices for Tanah Lot are:

**Indonesian Nationals**

- Adults: 30,000 IDR
- Children: 20,000 IDR

**Foreign Visitors**

- Adults: 75,000 IDR
- Children: 40,000 IDR

There are also corresponding parking fees: 3,000 IDR for motorcycles, 5,000 IDR for cars, and 10,000 IDR for large buses. The temple is open daily from 6:00 AM to 7:00 PM.

The best time to visit Tanah Lot Temple usually depends on the tides. It's recommended that travelers check the [Tanah Lot tide schedule](https://www.tideschart.com/Indonesia/Bali/Tabanan/Weekly/) before heading out, so you can plan your visit during low tide. This allows you to get a close-up view of the temple while avoiding the dangers of high tide.

Note that sunset hours are peak times with large crowds; if you want to avoid the rush, it's best to arrive early.
