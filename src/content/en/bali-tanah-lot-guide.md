## 1. History and Religious Significance of Tanah Lot Temple

The history of Tanah Lot Temple dates back to the 16th century, when it was said to have been built by the Javanese priest Nirartha. During his travels, Nirartha was drawn to the spiritual energy of this small sea islet and established a temple dedicated to the sea god, praying for the protection of local fishermen and villagers. Since then, Tanah Lot has become a vital part of local faith and culture.

The architecture of Tanah Lot Temple is simple yet powerful, especially during high tide when the entire temple appears to float on the sea, adding a mysterious and solemn atmosphere. At low tide, visitors can walk up to the temple's front to admire its intricately carved structure. As an important religious site in Bali, Tanah Lot also hosts special religious ceremonies, the most significant being the 'Piodalan' festival, which typically takes place four days after the Balinese holiday 'Hari Raya Kuningan.' Visitors can witness devout local religious activities firsthand.

## 2. Top Activities to Explore at Tanah Lot Temple

Tanah Lot Temple is more than just a site to admire ancient architecture—it offers a range of activities for visitors to enjoy. Here are some must-try experiences:

1. **Admire the Stunning Views of Tanah Lot and Its Rocky Islet**
   The rocky islet where Tanah Lot Temple sits is a natural marvel, appearing and disappearing with the tides. Many visitors choose to walk over during low tide to get close to the temple and witness the dramatic sight of waves crashing against the rocks. This iconic islet, known as Tanah Lot, offers a unique vantage point that makes it a favorite spot for capturing Bali's scenic beauty.
2. **Visit the Sacred Snake Cave**
   Another unique feature is the "Sacred Snake Cave" near the temple, home to holy snakes locals call "Ular Suci." These black-and-yellow snakes, with flat, fish-like tails, are believed to be incarnations of Nirartha, the temple's founder, and serve as sacred guardians. Under the guidance of local guides, visitors can observe these snakes and experience the deep reverence locals hold for these holy creatures.
3. **Capture the Magical Sunset at Tanah Lot**
   The sunset at Tanah Lot is hailed as one of the best in Bali. As the sun dips below the horizon, the temple is bathed in golden light, creating a serene and romantic atmosphere. To secure the best spot for viewing this "golden sunset," it's recommended to arrive early and find your ideal angle. This magical hour draws crowds of photography enthusiasts, making it a prime time for stunning shots.

## 3. How to Get to Tanah Lot: Travel Times and Transport Options from Each Area

**Key Point First**: Tanah Lot is located in the **Tabanan** region, with no direct public transport—you'll need to rely on **private car hire, ride-hailing, or self-driving**. The nearest base is **Canggu**, about a 30-minute drive away; from Ubud, allow at least 1.5 hours. Sunset is the busiest time of day, and **the return trip can be especially congested**, so be sure to build in extra time.

### Four Transport Options: How to Choose

1. **Private Car Hire (Most Recommended)**
   A visit to Tanah Lot typically lasts 1–2 hours. The biggest advantage of hiring a private car is that **your driver can wait in the parking lot**, so you don't have to worry about the return trip. Typical rates are **10 hours / 5-seater / south of Ubud area: Rp 600,000–800,000 (approx. US$40–53), charged per vehicle, not per person**; from July 1, 2026, the base rate remains the same, but a fuel surcharge applies per trip (Rp 50,000 for half-day, Rp 100,000 for full-day). Whether cross-zone trips or extra stops cost more should be confirmed with your driver when booking. For detailed pricing, see [Bali Private Car Hire Guide: FAQs, Costs, and Booking](/en/blog/bali-private-car-hire-guide/), and for the latest updates, check [2026 Private Car Hire Rate Adjustment Notice](/en/blog/bali-car-charter-rates-2026/).

2. **Gojek / Grab Ride-Hailing**
   Getting a ride from Canggu or Seminyak to Tanah Lot is generally easy and cheaper than private car hire. **But be cautious on the return trip**—the parking area has its own local transport ecosystem, and ride-hailing pickups are often restricted. During sunset, when everyone leaves at once, you may face long waits or even fail to get a ride. If you plan to use ride-hailing both ways, confirm your return plan in advance, or simply ask your driver to wait. For app tips, see [Gojek Full Guide](/en/blog/gojek-bali-guide/).
3. **Renting a Scooter**
   Riding from Canggu takes about 30 minutes, offering the most flexibility and easy parking (scooter parking fee is only 3,000 IDR). However, some stretches of road are in poor condition, and **after sunset, with dim lighting and chaotic traffic, riding back at night can be risky for beginners**. Not recommended for those without riding experience.
4. **Joining a Day Tour**
   Tanah Lot is often included in western Bali day tours that also cover Ubud, Taman Ayun Temple, or Pura Ulun Danu Bratan. This option is great for those who don't want to arrange transport themselves. The downside is fixed stop times—you can't adjust your visit to match the tides.

### After Arrival

The parking lot is still a short walk from the temple entrance, leading through a pedestrian street lined with souvenir shops on both sides. It takes about 5–10 minutes on foot to reach the ticket booth and the seaside platform. **It's recommended to wear comfortable, non-slip shoes** — when the tide goes out and you walk down the rocky path, the reef surface is slippery and covered in moss, making it easy to slip when crowded.
