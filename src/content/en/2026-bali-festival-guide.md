Before diving into the details below, it's important to understand that most festivals in Indonesia and Bali follow the Islamic calendar, the Balinese calendar, or the Chinese lunar calendar. As a result, their dates change each year and don't align with fixed Gregorian dates. So next year (or any given year) won't necessarily have the same festivals on the same days!

### **Isra Mi'raj (Prophet Muhammad's Ascension)**

- - **Date:** January 16, 2026 (Indonesian national holiday)
    - **Description:** A nationwide Islamic holiday in Indonesia commemorating the Prophet Muhammad's night journey and ascension to heaven.

- - **Date:** January 17, 2026
    - **Description:** An important Hindu festival in Bali where devotees fast and meditate through the night, seeking forgiveness and strength from Lord Shiva, rooted in the legend of the hunter Lubdaka.

### **Omed-Omedan, Sesetan Denpasar (The Kissing Ritual)**

- - **Date:** March 20, 2026
    - **Description:** A unique ritual held in Sesetan village after Nyepi Day, one of Bali's most exhilarating festivals. On this day, large groups of unmarried youth gather for a mass kissing event. Two groups, men and women, stand opposite each other, then embrace and kiss. As onlookers cheer them on, they splash water on the participants. Foreign tourists are welcome to watch but should avoid joining in.

### March 18–24: Idul Fitri Holiday

- March 18–24, 2026: Indonesia's Idul Fitri (1447 Hijriah)—the most important national holiday.

The entire country takes about ten days off. Idul Fitri marks the end of the Muslim fasting month of Ramadan, but it's also a time for Indonesians to spend with family and friends, much like the Chinese Lunar New Year. Expect Bali to start seeing an influx of domestic tourists from around March 13, with crowds of Indonesians everywhere for about two weeks. This is the busiest of Bali's five peak seasons—a holiday you should avoid visiting Bali.

### **Galungan and Kuningan**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_c8fb868428b648667e4ff2774ba1d282.jpg)

- **Dates:** June 17 and June 27, 2026
    - **Description:** One of Bali's most important religious festivals, celebrating the victory of good over evil, when ancestral spirits descend to Earth. This is one of Bali's most significant festivals, with Galungan on 6/17 lasting until Kuningan 10 days later, celebrating the creation of the universe and the triumph of good over evil. During this festival, towering penjor decorations bring a vibrant atmosphere to the streets, while abundant offerings, rituals, and celebrations offer a glimpse into the island's rich culture and spirituality. People make offerings at home to honor their ancestors and seek their blessings, while also holding ceremonies at temples to pay respect to the gods. Galungan, according to the Balinese Pawukon calendar, is also the day of the universe's creation. During this period, schools in Bali close for two weeks, highlighting its importance! Note that transportation such as ferries, private cars, and taxis will be affected! Many local guides and drivers take a few days off during the main celebrations, so it's best to book in advance. The day after Galungan (Manis Galungan) is when locals visit relatives or go on outings together, so you may find the roads particularly crowded. Large celebrations can also cause traffic congestion.

### **Bali Arts Festival - Pesta Kesenian Bali** [**Official Website**](https://pestakesenianbali.id/)

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_15ea6a6aa9cd30d097d8adf78a25f3cb.png)

- **Dates:** June 13 – July 11, 2026
    - **Description:** A month-long annual arts extravaganza showcasing dance, music, theater, and handicrafts. Every June, Bali holds the Bali Arts Festival, the largest cultural event in Bali, Indonesia, aimed at celebrating, preserving, and developing Balinese art and culture. This festival typically begins on the second Saturday of June and lasts for a month. It has been held since 1979, making it one of Indonesia's longest-running arts festivals. The Bali Arts Festival includes various exhibitions, performances, and art-related activities designed to showcase Balinese culture and art. During the festival, many towns are decorated with traditional bamboo and coconuts to celebrate. Numerous outdoor stages and pavilions are set up across Bali to host various talent performances. On these stages, you can enjoy dance, puppet, and music performances. The festival usually kicks off with a parade in Denpasar, followed by traditional music, then painting, sculpture, and photography exhibitions open to the public, along with handicraft displays and food stalls offering Balinese cuisine.

### **Christmas (Kelahiran Yesus Kristus)**

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_601fcd7028841bd9bfdec12156d64e54.jpg)

A national holiday in Indonesia, with December 24 and 25 as consecutive days off. December 25 is Christmas, a national holiday in Indonesia. For Christians, Christmas is an important celebration, and nearly 10% of Indonesia's population is Christian. Since Christmas and New Year's are close together, many Indonesians go to great lengths to take leave and connect the two holidays into an extended break. This gives them the chance to enjoy a longer vacation with family and friends, traveling to other cities or countries. Indonesians have even coined the term "Nataru," meaning the "Christmas-New Year holiday," combining "Natal" (Christmas) and "tahun baru" (New Year) to represent this festive period.

## FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions)

**1. Does Nyepi Day in Bali affect tourism?**

Yes, on Nyepi Day, all commercial activities and transportation cease, and even the airport closes. Visitors must stay within their hotels, but it's a unique opportunity to experience Balinese culture.
  **2. Do I need to buy tickets for Bali's festivals?**

Most festivals are open to the public and free, especially ceremonies held at temples. However, you must dress appropriately and observe etiquette when attending.
  **3. When is the best time to experience Balinese cultural festivals?**

The periods from March to May and November to December are ideal for experiencing several major festivals, including Nyepi Day, Galungan, and Kuningan.
  **4. How can I respectfully participate in Balinese festivals?

It is recommended to wear a traditional sarong and sash, and to observe local customs and taboos to avoid inappropriate behavior.
  **5. Are Bali's festivals suitable for families?**

Yes! Many festivals not only allow children to experience a different cultural atmosphere but also strengthen family bonds through participation. However, some ceremonies may last for extended periods, so it's advisable for families to plan their schedule to prevent children from getting too tired.
  **6. Do Bali's festivals affect traffic?**

Yes, they do. Especially during major festivals like Galungan or Kuningan, some streets are temporarily closed for parades or events. It's best to plan your itinerary early or choose to walk, which also allows you to enjoy the festivities up close.
  **7. Is it allowed to photograph local festival ceremonies?**

Photography is generally permitted, but it's best to ask locals or your guide first as a sign of respect, especially in temples or during traditional ceremonies. Some ritual occasions may have restrictions, so being respectful and careful with your camera is a courteous practice.
  **8. What special foods can be enjoyed during Bali's festivals?**

During festivals, visitors can sample many traditional dishes, such as Babi Guling (suckling pig) and Lawar (a mix of vegetables and meat). These foods often carry cultural significance tied to the festival rituals and are well worth trying.
  **9. How can cultural conflicts be avoided during festivals?**

Respecting local culture is crucial. It's recommended that visitors learn about festival-related taboos in advance, such as staying quiet during Nyepi or not photographing forbidden scenes, and always observe the behavior of locals to adapt accordingly.
  **10. How do Bali's festivals strengthen community bonds?**

Festival activities are often major events for villages or communities, where residents prepare and participate together, deepening their emotional connections and making the community more cohesive. Joining these activities is not only a cultural experience but also allows visitors to feel the warmth and friendliness of the locals.
