Facebook group member [Mei Chen](https://www.facebook.com/mei.co.nz/) and her family share their eight-day, seven-night Bali travelogue, specially provided for us to edit and republish:

***

**Key Highlights**: This is a real-life travelogue of a family of 6 adults and 1 child (2 years old) over eight days and seven nights, covering **Jimbaran, Uluwatu, Nusa Lembongan, Sanur, Ubud, Mount Batur sunrise, Tanah Lot, and Seminyak**. Two practical lessons: ① Visa on arrival is safer than e-visa (worried about data entry errors and fines; on-site queue took about 10 minutes, but credit card payments incur an extra 2% fee); ② **Baggage claim may take longer than getting the visa** (they waited nearly 2 hours, missing the Jimbaran sunset), so plan your itinerary with buffer time.

Thanks to the "[Go Bali Go Facebook Group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/baligo)" for the pre-trip help; I hope my info can also assist others.

## Travel Info & Pre-Trip Preparation

Trip Dates: May 18 - May 25

This Bali trip was a family vacation with 6 adults and 1 child (a two-year-old boy).

### Flights

We chose China Airlines' direct flight, with round-trip tickets costing about USD$419 per person. Since we booked more than three months in advance, we got a good deal.

### Visa

We opted for the visa on arrival, but paying by card incurs an extra 2% fee. We initially considered the e-visa, but it required filling out a lot of information, and we saw reports before departure of people being fined for writing 'TAIWAN,' so we worried about mistakes and decided to go with the visa on arrival instead. The visa-on-arrival process in Bali is relatively straightforward, with many queue counters, each line holding about five people. We were lucky and completed the visa process in about ten minutes. However, our luck ran out at baggage claim—we waited nearly two hours for our luggage, missing the chance to watch the sunset in Jimbaran.

> [1-Minute Guide: Latest Process from Landing to Customs to Baggage Claim](/en/blog/bali-all-indonesia-arrival-guide/)

### SIM Card

We pre-ordered two XL SIM cards from a highly-rated seller on Shopee. But since XL's signal is reportedly poor, we set aside some budget to buy another SIM card locally. As it turned out, XL's signal was indeed terrible, with almost no internet access. We later bought a Telkomsel SIM card locally—24GB for about USD$12—and finally got a stable connection.

### Currency Exchange

We exchanged some US dollars in Taiwan first, then converted to Indonesian rupiah upon arriving in Bali. We chose exchange counters that provided receipts and were certified. In practice, rates at all legitimate exchange places were similar; only shops without receipts offered slightly better rates, but we worried about scams and stuck with official ones. We noticed that rates were best from noon to around 3 PM, sometimes worsening later in the afternoon.

How to choose a legitimate exchange counter? Check out: [Indonesia/Bali Travel Currency Exchange Guide](/en/blog/bali-currency-exchange-guide/)

### Airport to Jimbaran Hotel

We booked two cars via Grab, with a wait time of about 20 minutes. On-the-spot pricing showed Grab was cheaper than Gojek and also cheaper than pre-booking airport transfers on Klook (a HiAce-style car was about USD$23). Given potential delays with the visa on arrival, we opted to book on-site. The cost for two Grab cars was around USD$13, but you have to wait on-site—so weigh your options. In comparison, airport transfer via private car is the priciest choice, as the driver only makes one trip and rents the vehicle, with no other itinerary possible. Unless you have other destinations to visit that day, a private car is not recommended.

### Itinerary

Since we wanted to visit all the famous spots, we planned our itinerary in advance and then found a private driver to match our schedule.

## Saturday, May 18 — Day 1: Exploring Jimbaran

We took off from Taiwan at 9:10 AM, and after a five-hour flight, arrived in Bali at 2:40 PM.

### FOX Hotel Jimbaran Beach

Upon arrival, we headed straight to FOX Hotel Jimbaran Beach to drop off our luggage. The hotel is within walking distance of Jimbaran Beach. It features a rooftop infinity pool and a children's splash area, making it a great choice for families.

### Jimbaran Beach

We originally planned to watch the sunset at Jimbaran Beach, but a long wait for luggage at the airport made us miss the beautiful sunset. Still, the beach scenery was delightful, and we got to try the local specialty: grilled corn. The corn was sweet and buttery, absolutely delicious and memorable.

### Dinner — Lia Cafe

For dinner, we chose Lia Cafe, which charges an additional 10% service fee and accepts credit cards. While waiting for our food, a local band came by and sang songs based on our nationality. However, they aggressively demanded tips and seemed unhappy when we didn't give enough, which soured the dining experience a bit. (Editor's note: This band is not affiliated with the restaurant. A tip of around 50,000 Indonesian Rupiah for your group would have been more than enough to keep everyone happy!)

### Breakfast – Bull's Coffee Jimbaran

Our first stop was Bull's Coffee Jimbaran, where we hoped to try their famous avocado sandwich, but unfortunately it wasn't available that day. The café accepts card payments and has a cozy atmosphere, making it a great spot to kick off the day.

Afterward, we grabbed another breakfast—Bali's iconic chicken rice at Warung Nasi Ayam Ibu Oki. The dish was delicious and authentic, leaving a lasting impression on us.

After breakfast, we headed to Pantai Batu Barak, a fantastic spot for photos. The natural scenery here is stunning and perfect for snapping Instagram-worthy shots.

Next, we made our way to Pandawa Beach. While the beach itself isn't particularly breathtaking, we had fun trying out kayaking, which added a playful touch to the visit.

For lunch, we chose the One Eighty Bali Cliff Bar. The food here is relatively affordable, and we ordered quite a few dishes to meet the minimum spend. ### Private Driver

We hired a private driver multiple times during our trip, including on May 19 and from May 21 to May 24. Initially, we searched online for recommended drivers, but most offered only small vehicles. Since our group of seven plus luggage required a larger car, we posted our itinerary on an English-language Bali community forum. Eventually, we found a driver who provided 12-hour service at a price cheaper than others' 8-hour rates. His fee covered everything: driver, fuel, tolls, and parking, and he even provided one bottle of water per person daily. The driver was very friendly, with 15 years of experience and excellent driving skills—even on very narrow roads, he handled the car masterfully. His English was good, and he answered our questions without being overly chatty, maintaining a professional demeanor. We were very satisfied with his service. On the last day, we asked for his contact information, and he gladly shared it. He even mentioned that if needed, he could recommend other drivers who speak Chinese. For [Gdbokink driver](https://www.facebook.com/Bokink/), this was his first time driving guests who changed areas daily, but we were thoroughly pleased with his service.

## Post-Trip Reflections & Takeaways

This trip was mainly planned for the adults, but we still made time each day for both kids and adults to enjoy the water. We didn't choose to stay in high-end villas or resorts because our itinerary was packed tight, leaving little time to truly relax and enjoy them—so why spend the extra money? Instead, we opted for well-reviewed, high-value hotels. 😆

If you're not particularly interested in temples, consider cutting some temple-related activities to free up more time for massages, water fun, and shopping. That said, we approached this trip with a 'maybe once in a lifetime' mindset, so we crammed in all the famous sights. Overall, it was a rich and unforgettable journey.

**Family Feedback**: Traffic in Bali is incredibly congested. While we managed to visit almost all the major attractions, the schedule can't be too tight. Aside from staying in a 'haunted house,' everything else went well. In the future, we might cut some temple visits in favor of more relaxing activities. As for shopping, the experience of inflated prices followed by haggling wasn't great.

**Would We Visit Bali Again?**: Although Bali's beaches are beautiful, they fall slightly short compared to those on other islands. Still, Bali's unique cultural landscape is well worth a visit! However, the world is vast, and we want to explore every corner of it—so we probably won't return to Bali. But the memories we made there will stay with us forever.
