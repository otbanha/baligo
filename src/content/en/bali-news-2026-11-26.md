# Bali Latest News | November 26: Rainy Season Weather, New Kuala Lumpur Route, and Year-End Price Alert

TransNusa announced a direct Bali to Kuala Lumpur route starting in December, offering travelers more options for Southeast Asian stopovers and multi-country trips.

Meanwhile, Bank Indonesia has begun warning of potential price increases during the year-end holidays, and Bali's immigration office has disclosed a case of a transnational fake investment scheme involving foreigners.

Based on the forecast, Tabanan, Gianyar, and Bangli require special attention for rainfall, while other areas cannot rule out afternoon showers either.

**Sea Conditions:**

BMKG marine weather forecasts indicate that on November 26, wave heights in the Indian Ocean south of Bali will range from approximately 1.9 to 2.1 meters, classified as moderate sea conditions; other areas around Bali will see waves around 0.8 to 1.2 meters.

For travelers, the main concerns are island excursions and water activities.

If you have plans for Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, or water activities along the southern coast, it's advisable to check with boat operators for the day's sea conditions and sailing schedules.

In the mountainous regions, including Ubud, Kintamani, and Bedugul, it's also recommended to keep your itinerary flexible, especially for waterfalls, mountain trails, and outdoor activities.

There are currently no reports of widespread closures across the island's attractions, but actual conditions should be confirmed with the latest local weather and sea condition advisories.

## 💰 Bank Indonesia Warns of Year-End Price Hikes! Christmas, New Year, and the 2027 Galungan Festival

On November 25th, the Bank Indonesia Bali representative office warned that Bali could face significant inflationary pressure from late this year into early next year.

The main reasons are the 2026 Christmas and New Year holidays (Nataru), followed by the traditional Galungan and Kuningan festivals in January 2027.

The combined consumer demand during these periods may strain the supply of food and daily necessities.

Achris Sarwani, the Bank Indonesia Bali representative, noted that Bali's permanent population is around 4.5 to 5 million, but tourist arrivals from outside Bali could approach 20 million, creating a massive consumer demand that challenges local price management.

**Here are some key figures to note:**

| Item | Data |
| --- | --- |
| Cumulative inflation rate from Jan to Oct 2026 | 2.74% |
| Inflation target ceiling | 3.5% |
| Remaining target margin | 0.76 percentage points |
| Market stabilization measures implemented | Approximately 377 times |

The central bank also pointed out that existing stocks of some food items can only meet demand for less than two weeks, urging the Regional Price Control Team (TPID) to strengthen market supply and price monitoring.

**Key Period to Watch:**

December 10, 2026, to January 31, 2027—about 7 weeks.

During this time, besides the Christmas and New Year travel peak, Bali will also observe the important Galungan and Kuningan festivals, which may further increase demand for local food and household goods.

**Impact on Travelers:**

This warning primarily targets food and daily essentials supply, not an official announcement of widespread price hikes on hotels, flights, or attraction tickets.

However, if you're planning a long stay around year-end, renting a villa to cook your own meals, or frequently buying ingredients at local markets, it's wise to keep an eye on supermarket and market prices.

Additionally, for the late December to January travel peak, it's still recommended to book accommodations and transportation early rather than leaving it to the last minute.

## ⚠️ Bali Immigration Uncovers Fake Investment Case Involving Foreigners! 20 Suspects Allegedly Part of Transnational Fraud Network

On November 25, Bali's Ngurah Rai Immigration Office announced a series of recent enforcement actions against foreigner violations, uncovering 20 foreigners allegedly involved in a transnational fake investment network targeting other foreigners in Bali.

According to Immigration Office head Muhammad Novyandri, these 20 foreigners were caught during a joint operation on November 5 in the Ungasan area of South Kuta (Kuta Selatan).

This case was included in the immigration department's deportation of 39 foreigners carried out between November 16 and 20.

In addition to suspected fake investment activities, this immigration enforcement also involved other violations, including:

- Holding a visitor stay permit while engaging in activities inconsistent with the permit's purpose.
- Overstaying the permitted period.
- Involvement in other illegal or improper business activities.
- Using invalid or non-compliant residence or travel documents.

The immigration department stated that it will continue to cooperate with the Bali police to strengthen supervision of foreigner activities.

It should be noted that the 20-person fake investment case should currently be understood based on official suspicions and investigation results, and not all involved individuals should be considered convicted by a court.

**Reminder for foreigners traveling or residing long-term in Bali:**

If someone recommends high-return investments to you through social media, private gatherings, or messaging apps—especially claiming special channels, guaranteed profits, or asking for transfers to personal accounts—you should first verify the company's background and legitimacy.

Don't easily trust investment offers just because the other person is also a foreigner, or lives in a Bali villa or co-working space.

During your travels, avoid participating in business activities you don't fully understand, to prevent violating local laws or visa regulations.
