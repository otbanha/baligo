A recent report from travel media [The Bali Sun](https://thebalisun.com/staying-connected-while-on-vacation-in-bali-is-easier-than-ever-but-tourists-ask-if-offline-vacation-is-still-possible/) offers an intriguing observation: staying connected in Bali is now easier than ever—booking rooms, hailing rides, making payments, and navigating all rely on your phone. But precisely because of this, more travelers are asking themselves, 'Can I still take a completely offline vacation?'

These represent two entirely different ways to experience Bali, and we'll explore both.

## Staying Connected: How Convenient Can Your Phone Be in Bali?

To be honest, Bali's mobile network infrastructure has improved rapidly in recent years. As long as you choose the right SIM card or eSIM, signal strength in urban areas is almost on par with Taiwan. Using Gojek, navigating with Google Maps, or booking restaurant tables online all require internet access; going offline can actually derail your itinerary.

In short:

- **Under 5 days**: Install an eSIM before departure (Klook and Airalo are both convenient), and you'll have internet as soon as you turn on your phone upon arrival.
- **7+ days**: Buying a Telkomsel physical SIM card directly at the airport is more cost-effective, with the widest coverage.

## Want to Disconnect: Bali Also Has 'Deliberately Inconvenient' Options

As mentioned in The Bali Sun article, if you truly want to disconnect from your phone, the simplest approach is to hand it over to the hotel front desk upon check-in, or stay at a place specifically designed for 'digital detox' travelers—these silent retreats typically restrict or even ban phone use, pairing it with meditation, yoga, and traditional Balinese healing sessions to help you truly unwind.

If your itinerary doesn't allow for a full digital detox, you can also schedule just one or two 'offline days': leave your phone in the hotel safe, and book a villa in the Ubud hills where the signal is naturally weak for a night—that's usually enough to give your brain a breather.

## You Can Have Both

In fact, that's Bali's advantage: you can stay fully connected for navigation, restaurant bookings, and photo uploads if you want, or find a place to completely disconnect if you need to. Instead of agonizing over which option to choose, plan by segmenting your itinerary—use the internet on days when you need efficiency, and stow your phone away on days when you want to unwind.

Sort out your internet plan before you go, and leave the decision of whether to disconnect to your mood in the moment.
