**Bottom Line**: Ubud Royal Palace (Puri Saren Agung) is located in the heart of Ubud at No. 8 Jalan Raya Ubud, featuring **19th-century Balinese architecture**. When people think of Bali, beautiful beaches and stunning natural landscapes often come to mind, but the island's cultural richness is equally impressive! Ubud Royal Palace (Puri Saren Agung) is a fantastic place to dive into traditional Balinese culture. As the historic home of Bali's royal family, this palace displays Balinese architectural styles from the 19th century and reflects the history, traditions, and artistic heritage of the Balinese monarchy.

## Location and How to Get to Ubud Royal Palace

Ubud Royal Palace is situated in the center of Ubud, Bali, at No. 8 Jalan Raya Ubud, right in the heart of Gianyar Regency. Its location is very convenient. It's about a one-hour drive from downtown Denpasar. For public transport, you can take the Trans Dewata bus on the K4B route, which passes through several key Ubud attractions, making it easy for travelers to reach the palace.

It is said that the interior of Ubud Royal Palace is not open to the public.

It's worth noting that although the palace grounds are extensive, only a small portion is open to visitors. As a result, guests can mainly explore the outer courtyards and some palace buildings. Yet even this limited area offers a glimpse into the beauty of traditional Balinese architecture. The buildings feature intricate carvings rich in Balinese religious and cultural symbols, making it an excellent spot for photographers to capture the essence of Bali.

## Highlight of Ubud Royal Palace: Art Performances

For culture enthusiasts, Ubud Palace hosts a traditional Balinese dance performance every Thursday evening, including the mystical "Kecak Fire Dance" show. These performances are among Bali's most iconic artistic events, drawing visitors from around the world. Tickets cost 100K Indonesian Rupiah, and the event typically runs from 7 PM to 8 PM every Thursday, offering a visual feast best enjoyed under the sunset glow. For travelers eager to delve deeper into Balinese culture, this is an unforgettable experience.

## Visiting Information for Ubud Palace

Ubud Palace does not charge an entrance fee, allowing visitors to explore the open areas for free. However, tickets are required to enjoy the art performances, and it's advisable to arrive early to choose a good seat for the entire show. The palace is open daily from 7 AM to 5 PM, giving visitors ample time to leisurely explore this historically rich venue.

## Exploring Ubud Palace and Nearby Attractions: A Perfect Blend of Culture, Shopping, and Nature

Ubud is not only home to the historically rich palace but also offers many nearby attractions to explore, including the lively Ubud Art Market, the beautiful Water Palace, trendy shops on Monkey Forest Road, and the nature-rich Sacred Monkey Forest. Whether you're immersing yourself in local culture, enjoying shopping, or getting close to Bali's natural ecosystem, these spots will make your Ubud trip truly memorable!

**5. Can you wear shorts and a tank top into the Ubud Palace?**

- The Ubud Palace doesn't have a strict dress code, but it's recommended to dress neatly and respectfully to honor local culture. When visiting religious sites or temples, more modest clothing is usually required, and some places provide sarongs for visitors to use.

**6. What types of traditional dance performances are held at the palace?**

- Every Thursday evening, the Ubud Palace features traditional Balinese dances such as the Barong Dance and Legong Dance. These performances showcase Bali's rich cultural heritage, blending unique music and dance artistry, and are well worth watching.

**7. Can you take photos inside the Ubud Palace?**

- You can take photos in the palace's open courtyard areas, but it's best to respect local customs by not using flash during performances or near religious structures. If a local ceremony or event is in progress, keep a low profile when taking pictures to show respect.

**8. What are the transportation options to get to the Ubud Palace?**

- You can rent a scooter or hire a private driver to get there. Bali also offers ride-hailing services like Grab or Gojek taxis. If you're staying in central Ubud, the palace is easily reachable on foot.

**9. Are there any recommended restaurants or cafes near the palace?**

- There are many well-known restaurants and cafes near the Ubud Palace, offering traditional Balinese cuisine and creative dishes, making them great spots for visitors to rest and dine.

**10. Do I need to book in advance to visit Ubud Palace?**

- No reservation is needed for daytime visits, but if you want to catch the evening traditional dance performances, it's wise to buy tickets in advance to secure a seat, especially during peak tourist season when tickets can sell out quickly.

**11. Does the Balinese royal family still live in Ubud Palace?**

- Yes, Ubud Palace remains the residence of the Balinese royal family. Although they no longer hold actual political power, the royal family still lives within the palace and plays a key role in preserving traditional culture, participating in many religious and cultural events.
