# Top Topics and News for Bali Travelers｜August 23, 2026

## 🏃‍♂️ Maybank Marathon Takes Off! 14,100 Runners Challenge Themselves, Traffic Affected Across Bali

The 2026 Maybank Marathon will be held on **Sunday, August 23** in Bali, with **14,100 runners** participating this year—an increase of about 500 from last year. The event attracts runners from Indonesia and abroad, featuring full marathon, half marathon, and other categories.

Many participants have chosen to 'level up' this year; for instance, some who previously ran only the 21-kilometer half marathon are now tackling the 42.195-kilometer full marathon for the first time.

One runner began training about six months in advance for their first full marathon, incorporating running, swimming, fitness, and strength training. Medical professionals also remind that long-distance events require special attention to injuries like ankle sprains.

**For regular travelers, the key concern is traffic.**

Starting early tomorrow morning, areas around Gianyar, Sukawati, and Ida Bagus Mantra will see a surge of runners and traffic controls, with some coastal roads also affected.

If you plan to travel from **Sanur or Denpasar to Ubud, Gianyar, or East Bali** tomorrow, allow extra time and confirm your route with your driver in advance.

## 💳 Maybank Marathon: More Than Just a Run, Driving Bali’s Local Economy and Social Initiatives

This year, the Maybank Marathon goes beyond the race itself, continuing to weave in elements of local community and social good.

Maybank is promoting financial education in schools along the Gianyar route, teaching teachers and students how to manage money and warning young people about issues like **online lending and internet gambling**.

Additionally, some participants have joined the Run for Charity program this year, raising funds for social projects, with a portion going toward initiatives like nature conservation and children’s programs.

So for Bali, the Maybank Marathon is no longer just an international race—it’s gradually becoming a major event that blends **tourism, the local economy, the environment, and community welfare**.

## 🏡 Bali Prepares a Full Inventory of Tourist Accommodations, Stepping Up Crackdowns on Illegal Villas

The Balinese government is working to compile a more comprehensive **list of tourist accommodations**, including hotels, villas, and guesthouses.

This move is tied to the ongoing issue of “illegal villas” in Bali. The government wants to know exactly which accommodations are hosting tourists and whether they hold proper business licenses.

This has a direct impact on travelers, too.

Especially in recent years, a flood of private villas has appeared on platforms like Airbnb and Booking.com—**just because a place looks beautiful and cheap doesn’t mean it’s legal**.

The Bali government has recently required online travel platforms to remove accommodations without legal permits, and continues to strengthen enforcement against illegal lodging. ## 🌾 Can you eat 'vegetables grown by Balinese farmers' even at a five-star hotel? The government pushes for 80% local ingredients.

The Bali Provincial Council is drafting a new policy that would require hotels and restaurants to increase the proportion of ingredients sourced from local Balinese farmers and fishermen.

The proposed ratio currently ranges from **40% to 80%**, but whether it will ultimately be set at 80% depends on the upcoming legislative process. The relevant bill is still in the justification and policy argument stage, with lawmakers hoping to finalize it by the end of this year.

If the policy is implemented, the impact will go beyond just hotel procurement.

For travelers, staying at a Bali hotel, eating breakfast at a resort, or even ordering at a fine dining restaurant could mean seeing more **local vegetables, fruits, meats, and seafood** on the menu.

This is also part of Bali's recent push for 'high-quality tourism'—not just attracting more visitors, but ensuring that tourism revenue stays more within local agriculture and communities.

## 🔥 Fire Breaks Out at 10 Bamboo Bars on Canggu Beach, Firefighters Rush to Contain Blaze

A fire recently broke out in the **Pantai Perancak** area of Canggu, affecting about 10 bars built from materials like wood and bamboo.

Due to the extensive use of flammable materials in these structures, the fire spread rapidly. Firefighters arrived at the scene after receiving reports to bring the situation under control.

There's no need to interpret this incident as a 'large-scale fire in Bali's tourist area,' but it serves as a reminder: many beaches, bars, and temporary commercial facilities in Bali use large amounts of wood and bamboo, **so fires can spread very quickly**.

When visiting beach bars or nighttime entertainment spots, it's reassuring to keep an eye on exits and escape routes.
