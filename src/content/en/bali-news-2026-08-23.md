# Bali Travel Highlights & News Roundup｜August 23, 2026

## 🏃‍♂️ Maybank Marathon Takes Off Tomorrow! 14,100 Runners Challenge Themselves, Traffic Impact Across Bali

The Maybank Marathon 2026 will be held on **Sunday, August 23** in Bali, with **14,100 runners** participating this year—an increase of about 500 from last year. The event attracts runners from Indonesia and abroad, featuring full marathon, half marathon, and other categories.

Many participants are also choosing to 'level up' this year, such as those who previously ran only the 21 km half marathon now tackling the full 42.195 km marathon for the first time.

One runner began training about six months in advance with running, swimming, fitness, and strength exercises to prepare for their first full marathon. Medical professionals also remind that long-distance events require special attention to injuries like ankle sprains.

**For regular travelers, the bigger concern is traffic.**

Starting early tomorrow morning, areas around Gianyar, Sukawati, and Ida Bagus Mantra will see a surge of runners and traffic controls, with some coastal roads also affected.

If you're planning to travel from **Sanur or Denpasar to Ubud, Gianyar, or East Bali** tomorrow, allow extra time and confirm your route with your driver in advance.

## 💳 Maybank Marathon: More Than Just a Race, Driving Bali’s Local Economy and Social Impact

This year, the Maybank Marathon goes beyond the race itself, continuing to weave in community and charitable elements.

Maybank is promoting financial education in schools along the Gianyar route, teaching teachers and students how to manage money and warning young people about issues like **online lending and internet gambling**.

Additionally, some participants have joined the Run for Charity initiative this year, raising funds for social projects, with a portion going toward nature conservation and children’s programs.

So for Bali, the Maybank Marathon is no longer just an international race—it’s evolving into a major event that blends **tourism, local economy, environment, and community welfare**.

## 🏡 Bali Prepares a Full Inventory of Tourist Accommodations, Stepping Up Crackdown on Illegal Villas

The Balinese government is working to compile a more comprehensive **list of tourist accommodations**, including hotels, villas, and guesthouses.

This move is tied to the ongoing issue of “illegal villas” in Bali. Authorities want to know exactly which properties are hosting tourists and whether they hold valid business licenses.

This has a direct impact on travelers, too.

Especially with the surge of private villas on platforms like Airbnb and Booking.com in recent years—**just because a place looks beautiful and cheap doesn’t mean it’s legal**.

The Bali government has recently required online travel platforms to remove accommodations without legal permits, and continues to strengthen enforcement against illegal lodging. ## 🌾 Can you eat 'vegetables grown by Balinese farmers' even at a five-star hotel? The government pushes for 80% local ingredients

The Bali Provincial Council is drafting a new policy that would require hotels and restaurants to increase the proportion of ingredients sourced from local Balinese farmers and fishermen.

The proposed ratio currently ranges from **40% to 80%**—whether it will ultimately be set at 80% still depends on the upcoming legislative process. The relevant bill is still in the phase of justification and policy argumentation, with lawmakers hoping to finalize it by the end of this year.

If the policy is ultimately implemented, the impact will go beyond hotel procurement.

For travelers, staying at a Bali hotel, having breakfast at a resort, or even ordering at a fine dining restaurant could mean seeing more **local vegetables, fruits, meats, and seafood** on the table.

This is also part of Bali's recent push for 'high-quality tourism'—not just attracting more visitors, but ensuring that more of the tourism industry's revenue stays within local agriculture and communities.

## 🔥 Fire Breaks Out at 10 Bamboo Bars on Canggu Beach, Firefighters Rush to Extinguish

A fire recently broke out in the **Pantai Perancak** area of Canggu, affecting about 10 bars built from materials like wood and bamboo.

Due to the heavy use of flammable materials in these structures, the flames spread rapidly, prompting firefighters to rush to the scene after receiving reports.

There's no need to interpret this incident as a 'large-scale fire in Bali's tourist zone,' but it serves as a reminder: many of Bali's beaches, bars, and temporary commercial facilities are built with plenty of wood and bamboo, **so fires can spread very quickly**.

When visiting beach bars or nighttime entertainment spots, it's reassuring to keep an eye on exits and escape routes.
