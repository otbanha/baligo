# Bali Is Getting an Electric Tram! Direct Airport-to-Canggu Route, Operations Could Start as Early as 2028

If you've ever been stuck in Bali traffic—where the short hop from the airport to Canggu feels like an eternity—this news might be just what you've been waiting for.

**Bali is officially moving forward with an electric tram system!** 🇮🇩🚋

On September 1, 2026, the Bali provincial government, Badung Regency government, and Indonesia's state railway company PT Kereta Api Indonesia (KAI) signed a formal cooperation agreement to advance a battery-powered electric tram project, with an initial route connecting **Ngurah Rai International Airport to Canggu**.

https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dcw_N3BgrN3/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&igsi=MzRlODBiNWFlZA==

The core goal of this project is straightforward: **to ease the worsening traffic congestion in southern Bali while offering travelers and locals a new public transit option.**

{{block:kuta}}

***

## 🚋 Not MRT, Not LRT—It's an 'Electric Tram'

The most notable aspect this time is that Bali did not opt for the more familiar MRT or LRT, but instead KAI is promoting a **battery-powered electric tram**.

The reason is actually quite easy to understand.

Unlike highly urbanized cities like Jakarta, Bali has narrow roads and dense buildings in some popular tourist areas, and must also consider temples, traditional villages, hotels, residences, and tourist attractions.

Therefore, KAI believes that the scale and operation of the tram are better suited to Bali's current environment and needs.

Moreover, this electric tram **does not require traditional overhead cables along the route**, as its power is mainly supplied by batteries. Officials also emphasize that it can reduce noise and environmental impact. The vehicle size is relatively compact, with the goal of providing public transportation without disrupting Bali's original landscape.

***

## 📍 Connecting from the airport all the way through Kuta, Legian, Seminyak to Canggu?

Currently, different official sources have slightly varying descriptions of the 'first phase,' but the Badung Regency government's published plan further indicates that the overall concept runs from **Ngurah Rai International Airport → Kuta → Legian → Seminyak → Canggu**.

According to Badung Regency government data, the planned total length is approximately **13.1 kilometers**, divided into two phases:

**Phase 1A: Airport to Legian, approximately 6.6 kilometers**
Targeting commercial operations to begin in the fourth quarter of 2028.

**Phase 1B: Legian – Seminyak – Canggu, approximately 6.5 km**
Target completion: Q1 2031.

However, according to the latest press releases from the Bali Provincial Government and KAI, the overall project is described as an 'Airport–Canggu' electric tram, with the first phase expected to launch in 2028 and the full extension to Canggu targeted for 2029. In other words, **it is still in the technical research and planning stage, and the final route and timeline may still change.**

This is important for travelers: **you can't count on this being a rideable option by 2028 just yet.**

***

## ⚡ 30 minutes from the airport to Canggu? The official plan is quite appealing

The preliminary concept released so far is ambitious.

The electric tram is expected to:

- 🚋 Battery-powered
- 📏 Route approximately 12–13 km
- ⏱️ Airport to Canggu in about 30–40 minutes
- 🚉 Service intervals of about 10 minutes
- 👥 Maximum daily capacity of approximately 60,000 passengers
- 🌱 Low noise, reduced environmental impact
- 🔌 No need for overhead cables along the entire route

The '60,000 passengers per day' figure is estimated under peak operating conditions, with officials hoping it can handle about 40% of traffic along the route.

If they really can achieve a 10-minute frequency in the future, it would be a huge change for tourists.

Imagine:

**Get off the plane → Take the Tram → Kuta → Seminyak → Canggu**

Instead of:

**Get off the plane → Get in a car → Get stuck in traffic → Watch your arrival time on Google Maps keep jumping further back.** 😅

## 🚧 Construction begins March 2027, first section opens in 2028

According to the current timeline, after the cooperation agreement was signed in September 2026, the next steps involve detailed **technical studies, feasibility assessments, community outreach, and participation of traditional villages (Desa Adat)**.

If everything goes according to the current plan:

**March 2027:** Groundbreaking expected
**2028:** First section begins operations
**2029:** Original goal to complete the full airport–Canggu route

However, the latest data from Badung Regency sets a later target of **Q1 2031** for the second phase extension to Canggu. For now, it's more realistic to view 2028–2031 as the 'current planned timeline' rather than a confirmed opening date.

***

## 🌴 The hardest part in Bali isn't actually 'building the railway'

I think what's truly worth watching about this project isn't the tram itself, but **whether it can be completed without undermining Bali's unique character.**

Bali Governor Wayan Koster has specifically emphasized that transportation development must not prioritize speed and modernization alone, but must also consider Bali's culture, environment, landscape, and the daily lives of local residents.

KAI also emphasized that from the planning stage, **culture, the environment, and local social life** are taken into consideration.

This is actually very important.

Because if the goal is simply to 'make cars go faster,' the answer might be easy; but if it also has to:

**reduce traffic jams + be convenient for residents + serve tourists + not spoil the landscape + not disrupt traditional culture**

the difficulty level becomes completely different.

***

## 🧳 What Does This Mean for Taiwanese Travelers?

If you're planning a trip to Bali in the next few years, **there's absolutely no need to change your itinerary because of this Tram** for now.

In 2026, Bali's transportation will still rely mainly on cars, taxis, Grab, Gojek, and private drivers.

But if this project is eventually completed successfully, the impact could be huge.

The **Airport–Kuta–Legian–Seminyak–Canggu** corridor is one of Bali's most important tourist arteries and also the area most prone to traffic jams.

For independent travelers, the biggest draw in the future might be:

**No longer having to factor 'traffic time' into your itinerary.**

For travelers staying in Canggu, Seminyak, or Kuta, being able to use the Tram to get to and from the airport could also reduce reliance on private transfers and car rentals.

## 🧐 This Might Be a Better Fit for Bali Than 'Building Another Road'

Bali's traffic problem isn't just about 'roads not being wide enough' anymore.

With more cars and more tourists, if every traffic issue is solved by widening roads, it's easy to fall into a cycle of 'wider roads → more cars → more traffic jams.'

The electric tram completed by Indonesian railway company KAI:

So we believe that developing public transportation is an issue Bali will inevitably have to face sooner or later.

And this time, choosing a small, battery-powered tram instead of building a massive MRT system from the start seems, conceptually, to be a better fit for Bali's urban scale and tourism environment.

Of course, this is still in the cooperation and planning stage. The real challenges will come with coordinating routes, land, funding, construction, and the concerns of residents and businesses along the way.

If these issues can all be resolved, then one day in the future, you might actually be able to step off the plane and hop on an electric tram, winding through Kuta, Seminyak, and all the way to Canggu—

When that happens, Bali's transportation could truly enter a whole new era.
