**Bottom line**: The only way to avoid Bali's traffic jams is a simple formula — **'Stay where you play'**: Break your itinerary into zones (e.g., first two nights in Seminyak or Canggu, then head to Ubud, and finish in the southern beach area). Within each zone, use Gojek/Grab for rides; only hire a private driver for mountain or cross-zone days. This minimizes time stuck in traffic. Our 8-day, 7-night guide follows this logic, covering transportation, accommodation, attractions, food, and SPA — feel free to adjust based on your trip length.

Bali is a popular Indonesian destination known for its stunning beaches, rich culture, and history. However, traffic issues are a major headache for travelers, potentially disrupting plans or even causing you to miss flights.

To make your Bali trip more enjoyable, we've prepared this travel guide. You can tailor it to your own schedule. We've carefully factored in traffic when planning, ensuring you can explore Bali at a relaxed pace.

## Day 3 to Day 4: Sanur or Nusa Penida

From Seminyak or Canggu, it takes about an hour by car to reach the east coast of southern Bali. Depending on your preferences, you can choose [Option 1] to explore the beach vibes of Sanur, or [Option 2] to head to Nusa Penida for its stunning natural landscapes and underwater world.

### Option 1: Sanur

- Sanur is a laid-back beach town on Bali, brimming with a relaxed coastal charm.
- It boasts serene and beautiful beaches with a seaside promenade stretching for several kilometers, perfect for sunbathing and leisurely strolls.
- The promenade is lined with eclectic beachfront restaurants, shops, and markets blending exotic and Balinese influences, where you can pick up local handicrafts and souvenirs.
- Sanur Beach offers spectacular sunrise views—wake up early to soak in the tranquility and beauty of Bali.
- It's also a hotspot for water activities like kayaking, stand-up paddleboarding, and windsurfing.
- The harbor for island hopping is right here, making it easy to catch an early boat to Nusa Penida or Lembongan.

## **Day 5 to Day 6: Ubud**

About an hour's drive from Sanur, you'll reach Ubud, Bali's most famous mountain town.

- Before heading to Ubud, consider a slight detour to explore [Bali's largest art wholesale market](/en/blog/kumbasari-art-market-shopping/).
- Ubud was once the capital of a Balinese kingdom and remains the island's cultural and artistic heart.
- Ubud is the most uniquely artistic and culturally rich area in Bali.
- Stay at a resort villa or boutique hotel in Ubud to soak in its distinctive atmosphere and charm.
- Visit nearby attractions like the Sacred Monkey Forest, UNESCO-recognized rice terraces, and stunning waterfalls.
- Join local cultural experiences, such as Ubud's handicraft workshops or traditional Balinese cooking classes.

## **Day 7 to Day 8: Kuta or Jimbaran**

To tackle Bali's traffic challenges, we recommend spending your last one or two nights in Kuta or Jimbaran. Both areas are close to the airport, and as long as you don't stay too far north or south, you can usually reach the airport within about 30 minutes on your final day. This setup ensures you won't have to arrive at the airport too early out of boredom, nor worry about traffic jams causing you to miss your flight. Whether you choose Kuta or Jimbaran, there are plenty of stunning spots to explore, giving your trip a perfect ending.

**Option One: Kuta**

- Kuta is one of Bali's most bustling commercial districts, packed with shops, markets, and malls. Here, you can find exquisite Balinese crafts, clothing, jewelry, and all sorts of souvenirs.
- Kuta Beach is one of Bali's most famous beaches, known for its golden sand and impressive waves. It's a paradise for surfers and a fantastic spot for sunbathing and watching the sunset.
- Kuta offers a wide range of dining options, from [local traditional dishes](/en/blog/bali-top-10-foods/) to international cuisine. You can savor flavors from around the world to satisfy your taste buds.
- Kuta's nightlife is vibrant, with numerous bars, clubs, and entertainment venues. After dark, you can dive into Bali's lively party scene.
- At Kuta Beach, you can try surfing and water sports—it's world-famous as a great beach for beginners. [Video of Xiao Jie's first surfing experience](https://youtu.be/gECdNvG16ww).

## Absolutely Avoid vs. Absolutely Do

### How You Absolutely Should NOT Plan Your Bali Itinerary

1. **Overcrowded and rushed schedule**: Avoid packing every day with attractions and activities. Instead, give yourself time to relax and soak in Bali's beauty and laid-back vibe. A good rule of thumb is to schedule no more than four attractions/activities plus lunch/dinner restaurants per day.
2. **Rushing between regions**: Although Bali isn't huge, traffic jams are severe. Moving between regions can waste a lot of time and energy, leading to exhaustion and inconvenience. When planning your itinerary, try to focus your exploration and activities within one area. This saves time and energy while allowing you to dive deeper into the local culture and attractions.
