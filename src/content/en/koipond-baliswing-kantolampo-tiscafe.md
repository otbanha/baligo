## What Does This Tour Include?

The core advantage of this tour is **private car + hotel pickup + English-speaking guide**, covering Ubud's top five classic spots in one day:

### 1. Koi Pond — A Serene Zen Photography Haven

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DW1B6p2CaG8/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=MzRlODBiNWFlZA==

The Koi Pond has quickly become one of Ubud's hottest new attractions in recent years. In the early morning, as mist still blankets the lush green rice terraces, koi fish glide leisurely across the water's surface, set against traditional Balinese architecture and natural landscapes—it's a dream location for photographers.

The best thing about this spot is that it's **relatively off the beaten path** — unlike the crowded Jungle Swing, you have a chance to take clear photos without too many strangers in the frame. If you're a solo traveler or in a small group, it's highly recommended to arrive early; the most beautiful light is usually between 7 AM and 9 AM.

### 2. Bali Swing — An Adrenaline-Pumping Photo Spot

https://www.instagram.com/reel/Da4vDKSuulw/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==

This is one of the most Instagrammed spots in Ubud. A giant swing hanging high above the jungle, with a backdrop of lush green treetops and a thrilling sensation, makes anyone look like a model in an ad.

But honestly, the biggest issue with the Bali Swing is **the crowds**. During peak hours (9 AM to 2 PM), you often have to queue for half an hour just to get your turn for a photo. The reason this tour is worth joining is that the guide will help you avoid the busiest times or arrange the pace so you can capture relatively clean shots.

### 3. Tegallalang Rice Terrace — Ubud's Green Carpet

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdAoI6zzLb3/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==

If you haven't seen the Tegallalang Rice Terraces, you can't really say you've been to Ubud. This layered expanse of green terraces is the quintessential image of Bali. Whether you're looking down from above or walking through the paddies, every angle yields postcard-worthy photos.

This spot is also crowded, but its advantage lies in **its vast size**. As long as you're willing to walk a bit further away from the main path, you can find quieter corners with fewer people. The guides on this tour usually know where these secret spots are.

### 4. Kanto Lampo Waterfall — Bali's Most Photogenic Waterfall

https://youtube.com/shorts/w-gzis-G6OI

The name Kanto Lampo means 'radiance' and 'sparkle' in Balinese, and once you see its cascading water, you'll understand why. This waterfall is famous for its unique layered volcanic rock terrain—water gently flows down natural stone steps, like a staircase waterfall carefully designed by nature.

**Why is it so photogenic?**

Kanto Lampo is the most Instagram-worthy waterfall in Ubud. The water flow isn't too strong, creating a soft, layered curtain of water. In the early morning light, sunlight filters through the leaves, casting a gentle glow on the surface. Strike a pose in a pretty dress in front of the waterfall, and you'll capture magazine-worthy shots.

If you come early (before 8 AM) or after 3 PM, you'll have a better chance of snapping photos with fewer people and a cleaner backdrop.

### 5. Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary — Up Close with Wild Monkeys

The Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary is one of Ubud's oldest cultural attractions. Home to over 700 long-tailed macaques roaming freely, you can wander through the lush jungle and get up close and personal with the monkeys.

https://youtube.com/shorts/tpPWsvsH-Eo

**A quick tip**: While the monkeys are adorable, they're wild animals and quite mischievous. Keep your glasses, phone, hair clips, and other small items secure. Never tease the monkeys or tempt them with food. Your guide will show you the right way to interact.

### 6. Tis Cafe in the Ubud Rice Terraces — A Relaxing Break

After a full day of sightseeing marathon, the perfect ending is to visit Tis Cafe, a coffee shop overlooking the Tegalalang rice terraces.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789207928888-_2026-09-12_17.03.06.png)

#### **Why Choose Tis Cafe?**

Located in the rice terrace area, about a 20-minute drive from central Ubud, Tis Cafe boasts an unbeatable position—you can sit in the cafe and gaze out over the lush green terraces, enjoying the bamboo design and comfortable ambiance.

The dining environment here is especially thoughtful. Tis Cafe features warm bamboo-woven design and a pool overlooking the greenery, allowing you to immerse yourself in nature while savoring your meal. It's the perfect spot to pause after exploring the terraces, sipping coffee and snapping photos.

#### **Menu Recommendations**

Tis Cafe's signature dish is Bebek Goreng Ngetis (crispy duck rice), made with authentic Balinese spices and cooking techniques. If you prefer pork, the Nasi Goreng Crispy Pork Belly is also a popular choice.

For coffee lovers, Tis Cafe offers a variety of specialty coffees. Sitting here, sipping a latte art coffee with the undulating green rice terraces before you—it's arguably Bali's most poetic coffee moment.

**A little tip**: Tis Cafe has become a popular spot in Ubud in recent years, but it feels more like a leisure retreat than a typical restaurant. If you're just looking for a quick coffee, you might have to wait for a seat; but if you want to sit back, relax, and soak in the terrace views, it's absolutely worth your time. The best time to visit is between 4-6 PM, when crowds are thinner and the light is soft.

## Practical Tips: How to Make the Most of This Day

### 1. Dress Comfortably

With a full 10-hour itinerary, comfortable clothing is essential. Here's what to wear:

- Lightweight, breathable clothes (Ubud gets hot at midday)
- Non-slip casual shoes or hiking shoes (water crossings at the waterfall)
- A sun-protective shirt or light jacket (mornings in the mountains can be a bit chilly)
- A change of clothes (waterfalls will splash)

### 2. Bring enough sunscreen and water

Although Ubud is in the mountains, the UV rays are still strong. Bring high-SPF sunscreen, preferably waterproof. Also bring plenty of water, especially in the waterfall and rice terrace areas, where you'll be walking a lot.

### 3. Prepare your camera

If you have a drone, ask your guide in advance whether the destination allows flying (some spots prohibit it). Bring a power bank to charge your phone, since taking photos and videos all day will drain the battery quickly.

### 4. Respect local culture

When entering temples (such as small temples near some rice terraces), remember to take off your shoes or dress modestly. Be patient with the monkeys and avoid using flash photography that might startle them.

### 5. Start early

If your schedule allows, aim for the earliest departure time. Why? Because:

- The morning light is most beautiful over the koi pond and rice terraces
- The fewest crowds at the attractions
- You have more time to linger at each spot

### 6. Bring Cash for Tips

Tipping is common in Balinese culture. If your guide has taken you around all day and you're satisfied with their service, it's polite to give a tip (Rp. 50,000–100,000). This amount is usually not included in the tour fee.
