## 🚌 Bali Welcomes Its First 11 Electric Buses! Trans Sarbagita Expected to Launch in December

Bali's public transportation is taking a new leap forward! On October 22, the Bali government officially launched an electric bus cooperation project, securing 11 electric buses provided with support from South Korea, along with 6 charging stations, which will be integrated into the Trans Sarbagita public transport system.

Each of these electric buses has around 20 seats and a range of over 200 kilometers on a single charge. According to current plans, the fleet is expected to begin service in December 2026, with free trial rides offered before the official launch.

The first phase will use Trans Sarbagita's Route 1, connecting GOR Ngurah Rai in Denpasar to GWK (Garuda Wisnu Kencana) in Jimbaran, passing through the Udayana University area. The final route, stops, and operating hours are subject to official announcements from the transportation authorities.

### What Does This Mean for Travelers?

This initiative is worth noting because Bali's tourism has long relied on motorcycles, car rentals, and private drivers, with limited public transport options.

If the electric buses run smoothly as planned, travelers heading to downtown areas and southern attractions may have an additional transportation option in the future. However, it's not yet a ready-to-use travel shuttle service.

For those planning a trip to Bali at the end of the year, keep an eye out for announcements on free trial ride dates, as well as the schedule and ticket prices once the service officially launches.

## ✈️ Melbourne Direct Flights to Bali Launch! Plans to Increase to Daily Flights from November 15

Indonesian airline TransNusa launched direct flights from Melbourne to Bali on October 20, further strengthening air connectivity between Australia and Bali.

The new route initially operates four times a week, on Mondays, Wednesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays. According to current plans, **flights will increase to daily service starting November 15, 2026**.

This route offers Australian travelers more options for Bali getaways and enhances Bali's role as a regional aviation hub.

Australia remains one of Bali's most important international source markets. As of the end of September 2026, Australian visitors to Bali reached approximately 1.3 million, accounting for about a quarter of total international arrivals.

## 📈 Bali's Annual Foreign Visitors Expected to Exceed 7.2 Million! Government Steps Up Crackdown on Unlicensed Accommodations

Bali Governor Wayan Koster estimates that foreign visitor arrivals in 2026 could reach 7.1 to 7.2 million, surpassing the approximately 7.05 million recorded in 2025.

According to released data, from January to September 30, 2026, Bali had already welcomed around 5.2 million foreign visitors, an increase compared to the same period last year.

The Governor believes that despite ongoing global uncertainties, Bali's tourism market remains robust overall. However, the growth in visitor numbers also brings challenges in accommodation management, environmental capacity, and tourism service quality.

The government is particularly focused on accommodations operating without full business licenses, including those sold through online travel agency (OTA) platforms, aiming to create a fairer market environment and prevent unfair competition against legitimate operators.

### What to Check Before Booking a Villa or Homestay?

For travelers, the most practical takeaway from this news is that when booking accommodations, beyond comparing prices, photos, and reviews, you should also check whether the property has a valid business license.

Especially when booking a villa through social media or private channels, it's advisable to verify the host's identity, actual address, payment terms, and cancellation policy.

The government's current focus is on strengthening accommodation management and legality checks, which doesn't mean all villas or homestays booked through OTAs have issues. Travelers don't need to cancel confirmed bookings because of this news.

## 🛕 Bali Religious Groups Call for Enhanced Cultural Training for Police in Tourist Areas, Foreign Visitor Misconduct Under Spotlight Again

The Bali Hindu Council recently met with the Bali Police to discuss public safety in tourist zones, cultural respect, and issues involving foreign visitors' misconduct.

During the meeting, they proposed that police stationed in tourist areas improve their English skills and deepen their understanding of Balinese traditions, local culture, and Hindu beliefs, enabling better communication and coordination when handling disputes between tourists and locals.

The police also stated they will further discuss cases of foreign visitor misconduct and law enforcement cooperation with immigration authorities.

It's important to note that this news refers to proposals and future collaboration directions from the meeting, not newly announced regulations or enforcement deadlines for foreign visitors.

### What Should Travelers Be Aware Of?

Bali's temples, religious ceremonies, and traditional villages each have their own etiquette. When entering a temple, follow the dress code and on-site instructions; before photographing a religious ceremony, always confirm that you have permission.

If a local resident reminds you or stops you from doing something in a public place, stay calm and avoid arguing or posting the conflict on social media.

Respecting local culture isn't just about staying out of trouble—it's also key to making your trip smoother.

## 🌏 Bali's Tourism Competitiveness Faces New Challenges! Governor Calls for Central Investment in Transport, Water Supply, and Waste Management

Bali's governor recently emphasized again that the island's tourism competitiveness cannot rely solely on beaches, culture, and hotel facilities—it also needs better infrastructure.

During events related to Indonesia's 2026 Tourism Marketing Week, local authorities proposed that the central government provide infrastructure funding more in line with Bali's tourism contributions, prioritizing improvements in inter-regional transport, waste management, water supply, and other public facilities.

The governor noted that Bali generates significant tourism revenue for Indonesia, but if traffic congestion, waste management, and water resource issues are not addressed simultaneously, the long-term quality of tourism and international competitiveness could suffer.

This discussion also reflects the core challenge facing Bali's tourism development: how to continue attracting visitors while improving residents' living conditions and maintaining the destination's quality.

### Impact on Travelers

This doesn't mean Bali is about to roll out new traffic or waste management regulations across the board—rather, local authorities are once again highlighting the need for infrastructure investment.

For travelers, the most practical impacts at this stage remain travel time, water supply conditions in accommodation areas, and the quality of environmental management in different regions.

When planning a trip to Bali, it's advisable not to just look at the distance between attractions, but also consider road conditions during peak hours; if staying at a remote villa, it's wise to check water supply and waste disposal arrangements in advance.
