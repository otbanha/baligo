# Bali Independent Travel Packing List: 21 Essential Tips Before You Go

Heading to Bali for your first independent trip? Packing can be a headache—bring too much and you'll be weighed down, forget something and you'll regret it once you're there. Honestly, this anxiety is something every traveler experiences. Today, I want to share some often-overlooked packing tips that can really affect your trip quality.

## Documents to Confirm Before Departure

Before diving into specific items, passports and visas should be your top priority. This isn't just a formality—I've seen people turned away at the boarding gate because of passport validity issues, and it's just not worth it. Beyond that, travel insurance is worth serious consideration; after all, unexpected situations can arise during any trip.

## Clothing and Sun Protection Details

Located near the equator, Bali's tropical climate is famously intense. Sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses are must-haves, but you'll find plenty of options at local supermarkets, often at better prices.

When it comes to clothing, lightweight and comfortable is key. If you tend to get cold, bring a light jacket—temperatures can drop significantly at night or in air-conditioned spaces. And don't underestimate footwear—Bali's attractions involve lots of stairs, and comfortable shoes are crucial for beaches, hikes, or water activities.

## Small Items You Might Forget Until It's Too Late

Bring basic toiletries like a toothbrush, toothpaste, and shampoo. But here's a special mention: toilet paper. Yes, bring your own toilet paper. This isn't a joke—it's real experience.

For electronics, Indonesia's plug type differs from Taiwan's, and the voltage is 220V. Check compatibility before bringing any devices. You can buy adapters at local supermarkets, but a multi-outlet extension cord that supports 220V is even more convenient.

## Water and Health

Staying hydrated is crucial in the tropics. While bottled water is cheap at convenience stores and supermarkets, bringing a refillable water bottle is more eco-friendly and economical.

Don't forget to pack mosquito repellent. Mosquitoes aren't just annoying—they can also bring dengue fever, which is no joke. Local supermarkets have plenty of effective and affordable options. Also, bring a first-aid kit with common anti-inflammatories, band-aids, and painkillers, just in case.

## For Safety and Convenience

A waist pouch is essential—keep your passport and ID documents on you at all times. A waterproof one is even better, since rain in Bali can come out of nowhere, and you'll likely be near the water often.

## For the Beach and Water Activities

Swimsuits and travel towels are a must. Bali has a great variety of stylish swimwear, so you can also pick some up locally.

Waterproof bags and cases are incredibly practical. Not just for beach fun—Bali can suddenly pour rain, or you might venture into a waterfall hideaway. Waterproof gear will save your camera and phone.

## Don't Forget at the End

An umbrella or lightweight raincoat is essential. Though it's tropical, rain is quite frequent, especially in the mountains. **Bring a shopping bag too**—this is due to Bali's plastic ban policy. Many places don't provide plastic bags, so bringing your own is both eco-friendly and saves you money.
