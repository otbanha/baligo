# Bali Independent Travel Packing List: 21 Essential Tips to Check Before You Go

Heading to Bali for your first solo trip? Figuring out what to pack can be a headache—bring too much and you'll be weighed down, forget something and you'll regret it later. Honestly, that anxiety is something every traveler experiences. Today, I want to share those easily overlooked packing tips that can really make or break your trip.

## Documents to Double-Check Before Departure

Before diving into specific items, passports and visas should be your top priority. This isn't just a formality—I've seen people turned away at the boarding gate because of passport validity issues, and it's just not worth it. Beyond that, travel insurance is worth serious consideration—after all, unexpected situations can pop up on any trip.

## Clothing and Sun Protection Details

Located near the equator, Bali's tropical climate is notoriously intense when it comes to sun exposure. Sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses are absolute must-haves—though you'll find plenty of options at local supermarkets, often at even better prices.

When it comes to clothing, lightweight and comfortable is key. If you tend to feel the cold, pack a light jacket, as temperatures can drop significantly in the evenings or in air-conditioned spaces. And don't underestimate the importance of footwear—many of Bali's attractions involve lots of stairs. Whether you're hitting the beach, hiking, or doing water activities, comfortable shoes are essential.

## Little Things You Only Think of When It's Too Late

Bring basic toiletries like a toothbrush, toothpaste, and shampoo. But here's a special mention: toilet paper. Yes, you absolutely need to bring your own. This isn't a joke—it's real experience talking.

For electronics, Indonesia's plug sockets and voltage (220V) differ from Taiwan's. Make sure your devices are compatible before you go. You can buy adapters at local supermarkets, but bringing a multi-outlet extension cord that supports 220V will make life much easier.

## Water and Health

Staying hydrated is crucial in the tropics. While bottled water is cheap at convenience stores and supermarkets, bringing a refillable water bottle is more eco-friendly and economical.

Don't forget to pack mosquito repellent. Mosquitoes aren't just annoying—they can also bring dengue fever, which is no joke. Local supermarkets have plenty of effective and affordable options. Also, bring a first-aid kit with common anti-inflammatories, band-aids, and painkillers, just in case.

## For Safety and Convenience

A travel waist pouch is essential—keep your passport and ID documents on you at all times. A waterproof one is even better, since rain in Bali can come out of nowhere, and you'll likely be spending time at the beach.

## For the Beach and Water Activities

Swimsuits and travel towels are a must, of course. Bali has a great selection of stylish swimwear, so you can also shop for them locally.

Waterproof bags and phone pouches are incredibly practical. Not just for playing at the beach—Bali can surprise you with a sudden downpour at any time, or you might find yourself hiking into a hidden waterfall. Waterproof gear can save your camera and phone.

## Don't Forget at the End

An umbrella or a lightweight raincoat is essential. Even though it's tropical, rainfall is actually quite frequent, especially in the mountains. Also bring a shopping bag—Bali has a plastic bag ban, and many places don't provide them. Bringing your own is both eco-friendly and saves you money.
