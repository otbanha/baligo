# Key Topics and News Roundup for Bali Travelers (August 12, 2026)

## 🌊 Tidal Flood Alerts Issued for Popular Bali Beaches; Southern Coast Areas Advised to Stay Vigilant

[https://www.instagram.com/p/Db5YTZQTpwp/?igsh=cnF3NW9nNzR0YnU0](https://www.instagram.com/p/Db5YTZQTpwp/?igsh=cnF3NW9nNzR0YnU0)

Indonesia's BMKG weather agency has warned that from August 11 to 17, several coastal areas in southern Bali face a risk of tidal flooding, including the southern shores of Gianyar, Tabanan, Badung, Denpasar, and Klungkung. Popular beaches like Kuta, Seminyak, Legian, Jimbaran, and Uluwatu are within the affected zones. Travelers spending time on the sand should closely monitor changes in the tide line.

Traveler tip: If you're planning beach relaxation, photos, or surfing in the coming days, check the latest sea conditions first, and keep your loungers, towels, and bags well back from the water's edge.

## 🏖️ Chinese Female Tourist Harassed by Local Man in Lovina; Incident Sparks Social Media Attention

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786445851151-2006--8-12.png)

A video filmed in Lovina has recently circulated on social media, showing a Chinese female tourist being harassed by a local man in the Buleleng area of Lovina. Reports indicate the incident began with what seemed like a handshake greeting but escalated into inappropriate touching, drawing concern from netizens and local authorities.

Traveler tip: When visiting Bali's beaches, night markets, or crowded attractions, if you encounter uncomfortable approaches or physical contact, keep your distance immediately and seek help from shopkeepers, security, or police if needed.

## 🩺 Rabies in Bali Still Requires Vigilance: Dinkes Reports an Average of Around 205 Bite Cases Daily

According to NusaBali, reports of HPR (rabies vector animal) bites in Bali average about **205 cases per day** through the first half of 2026. As of June this year, there have been **34,561 cases** in total, with **26,106 people** receiving the VAR vaccine. Additionally, five deaths have been reported in Bali up to June this year, highlighting that rabies prevention remains a critical issue for travel safety.

Traveler advice: Untreated rabies has a near 100% fatality rate! If bitten or scratched by an animal, immediately clean the wound and seek medical attention as soon as possible—don't wait for symptoms to appear.

## 🌠 Watch the Perseid Meteor Shower in Bali in Mid-August, with Better Viewing Conditions in the Early Morning Hours

Bali.live reports that the Perseid meteor shower will peak over Bali on the **night of August 12 to 13**. BBMKG notes that viewing conditions are favorable this year due to low moonlight interference, making it ideal to head to areas away from city lights for observation.

Traveler advice: If you're staying in Ubud, Kintamani, Lovina, or other areas with less light pollution, look toward the north or northeast at night. Treat this meteor shower as a bonus to your itinerary—no telescope needed, it's visible to the naked eye.
