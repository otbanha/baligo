## 1. Where is Bali and how do I get there?

Bali is an island belonging to Indonesia, located in Southeast Asia. Surrounded by sea, it sits just west of Java Island and east of Lombok. With its unique character and diverse landscapes, Bali has become a top Southeast Asian vacation destination, attracting travelers from around the world every year.

## 2. What language is spoken in Bali?

In Bali, locals primarily use Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia), the official language of Indonesia. However, due to Bali's unique culture, locals also speak Balinese, a language that is quite common in daily life and can be considered the mother tongue for some residents. Balinese is mainly used in family settings, religious ceremonies, and traditional activities, playing a significant role in local religious and community life.

## 3. Do Balinese people speak English?

Generally speaking, Bali's tourism industry is highly developed, and many locals working in tourism-related jobs can speak basic English. Whether in restaurants, hotels, shops, or at tourist attractions, most service staff can communicate in simple English, especially in southern areas like Kuta, Seminyak, and Jimbaran. However, when talking to elderly locals or residents in remote villages, there may still be some language barriers. Some older generations only speak Balinese.

For travelers, learning a few simple Indonesian phrases like "Halo" (Hello) and "Terima kasih" (Thank you) can make communication more fun and smoother.

## 4. What currency is used in Bali?

The currency used in Bali is the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Travelers can easily exchange for Indonesian Rupiah at airports, banks, hotels, or currency exchange booths in the city. It's recommended to carry some cash for use at small shops, street stalls, or traditional markets, allowing for more flexibility with small payments.

## 5. What is Bali's climate like? What is the best time to visit Bali?

Located near the equator, Bali has a **tropical monsoon climate**, warm and humid year-round. The climate is divided into two main seasons: the **dry season** and the **rainy season**. Each season offers unique characteristics, making Bali suitable for travel throughout the year, though the experience varies slightly depending on the time of visit.

![5.峇里島的氣候如何？什麼月份拜訪峇里島最好？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6e7fac42f701496457dff00feb523003.png)

**Dry Season (May to October)**

The dry season is Bali's peak tourist season, with moderate temperatures, lower humidity, abundant sunshine, and clear skies. Daytime temperatures typically range from **27°C to 32°C**, cooling down slightly at night. This is the ideal time for outdoor activities like beach sunbathing, snorkeling, diving, and hiking, as well as water sports and capturing stunning sunsets.

**Rainy Season (November to April)**

The rainy season is Bali's low season. While rainfall is more frequent, it usually comes in short, intermittent bursts rather than continuous downpours. Daytime temperatures are similar to the dry season, but humidity is higher. Temperatures still hover around **26°C to 30°C**. Rain typically falls in the afternoon and evening, leaving mornings and late nights suitable for activities.

https://youtube.com/shorts/bqtCP1YoKCM

Bali's average temperature usually stays between 26-32°C. During the rainy season, it can occasionally be cooler. For packing, it's best to bring mostly short-sleeved clothing—no heavy jackets needed, just a light jacket or windbreaker.

## 7. Is Bali Better for Independent Travel or a Guided Tour?

**Advantages of Independent Travel:**

- Flexible itinerary, allowing for in-depth exploration based on your interests
- Generally more affordable than guided tours
- Lets you relax at your own pace
- Offers a wider variety of dining options

However, independent travel requires plenty of preparation:

- Transportation can be complex, with limited public transit, so you'll need to research private drivers or rental options
- Attractions are scattered, making route planning tricky—you'll need to study distances and visit sequences
- You'll need to consider whether the character of each accommodation area (e.g., lively Kuta, artistic Ubud) suits your needs
- You must understand weather, food hygiene, and local etiquette

**Pros of joining a tour:**

- Eliminates the hassle of arranging transportation and accommodation
- A guide provides explanations and assistance
- Itinerary is well-planned, ideal for travelers with limited time
- No need to worry about language barriers

**We recommend considering the following factors when deciding:**

1. Language ability - English is widely used in tourist areas, but if you don't speak any foreign language, a tour is advisable
2. Trip duration - For short stays, a tour allows you to quickly visit key spots
3. Budget planning - Independent travel lets you adjust expenses flexibly, while tours are mostly all-inclusive, so you don't have to worry about costs
4. Travel style - Whether you prefer in-depth exploration or a quick tour of attractions

1. **When to Use Credit Cards:**

2. - High-end restaurants, shopping malls, five-star hotels, and beach clubs typically accept international credit cards (Visa, MasterCard, Amex).

5. **Recommendations:**

6. - **Carry Indonesian Rupiah Cash:** Cash is most convenient for small transactions or tips, especially in remote areas or street shopping.

## 38. Are Bali's traditional dance performances worth watching?

![38.峇里島的傳統舞蹈表演值得看嗎？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_82b8c46b8f153469d536f48be8633bf8.jpg)

Visiting Bali without experiencing its traditional dances is a missed opportunity! These dances are not just artistic expressions but are deeply rooted in religious and cultural significance. Through intricate costumes, graceful movements, and vivid expressions, they perfectly showcase Bali's unique cultural landscape.

Here are a few traditional Balinese dances worth watching:

1. **Kecak Dance**

Also known as the 'Monkey Dance,' this is one of Bali's most iconic traditional dances. Inspired by the Hindu epic Ramayana, performers sit in a circle and chant 'chak-chak-chak' to mimic a troop of monkeys, creating a truly electrifying atmosphere. The most famous venue for the Kecak Dance is at Uluwatu Temple at sunset, where the golden sky and stunning ocean views provide a breathtaking backdrop for both sight and sound.

[Top 6 Kecak Fire Dance Locations in Bali](/en/blog/bali-kecak-fire-dance-locations/)

2. **Barong Dance**

The Barong Dance tells a story of the struggle between good and evil, featuring the benevolent mythical beast Barong battling the malevolent Rangda, symbolizing balance and harmony. Dancers wear elaborate costumes and showcase intricate body language, making this performance especially popular with families.

3. **Legong Dance**

Considered the most elegant dance in Bali, the Legong Dance is often performed by young girls who use highly refined hand gestures and eye movements to interpret captivating mythological tales. Known for its tight rhythm and precise movements, this dance is typically staged at Ubud Palace or local art villages, making it a favorite among art enthusiasts.

The Barong Dance and Legong Dance can be enjoyed on the indoor stage at Balerung Stage. For details, please see [here](/en/blog/balerung-stage-cultural-show/).

**Why It's Worth Watching**

1. **Cultural Experience**: The dances are deeply intertwined with Balinese religious rituals, offering you a deeper understanding of local culture.
2. **Visual Feast**: Performances are usually accompanied by traditional musical instruments, with exquisite costumes and delicate movements that are a true delight to the eyes.
3. **Fun for All Ages**: Most dance performances are suitable for everyone, whether you're a family with kids or a couple, there's something to enjoy.
