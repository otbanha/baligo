## 1. Where is Bali and how do I get there?

Bali is an island belonging to Indonesia, geographically located in Southeast Asia. Surrounded by sea, it borders Java Island to the west and Lombok Island to the east. With its unique character and diverse landscapes, Bali has become a popular Southeast Asian vacation destination, attracting travelers from around the world every year.

## 2. What language is spoken in Bali?

In Bali, locals primarily use Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia), the official language of Indonesia. However, due to Bali's unique culture, they also speak Balinese, a language commonly used in daily life and considered the mother tongue for many residents. Balinese is mainly used at home, in religious ceremonies, and during traditional activities, playing a significant role in local religious and community life.

***

## 3. Do Balinese people speak English?

Generally speaking, Bali's tourism industry is highly developed, and many locals working in tourism-related jobs speak basic English. Whether at restaurants, hotels, shops, or attractions, most service staff can communicate in simple English, especially in southern areas like Kuta, Seminyak, and Jimbaran. However, when talking to elderly locals or residents in remote villages, there may still be some language barriers. Some older generations only speak Balinese.

For travelers, learning a few simple Indonesian phrases like 'Halo' (Hello) and 'Terima kasih' (Thank you) can make interactions more enjoyable and friendly.

## 4. What currency is used in Bali?

The currency used in Bali is the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Travelers can easily exchange for Indonesian Rupiah at airports, banks, hotels, or currency exchange offices in the city. It's recommended to carry some cash for use at small shops, street stalls, or traditional markets, allowing for more flexibility with small payments.

## 5. What is the climate like in Bali? What is the best time to visit?

Located near the equator, Bali has a **tropical monsoon climate**, warm and humid year-round. The climate is divided into two main seasons: the **dry season** and the **rainy season**. These distinct climatic features make Bali suitable for travel throughout the year, though the experience varies slightly depending on the season.

![5.峇里島的氣候如何？什麼月份拜訪峇里島最好？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6e7fac42f701496457dff00feb523003.png)

**Dry Season (May to October)**

The dry season is Bali's peak tourist season, with moderate temperatures, lower humidity, abundant sunshine, and clear skies. Daytime temperatures typically range from **27°C to 32°C**, with slightly cooler evenings. This is an ideal time for outdoor activities like beach sunbathing, snorkeling, diving, and hiking, as well as for water sports and capturing stunning sunsets.

**Rainy Season (November to April)**

The rainy season is Bali's low season. Although rainfall is higher during this period, showers are usually brief and intermittent, not lasting for days on end. Daytime temperatures are similar to the dry season, but humidity is higher and rain is more frequent. Temperatures still hover around **26°C to 30°C**. Rain typically falls from afternoon to evening, leaving mornings and late nights open for activities.

## 7. Is it better to travel Bali independently or with a tour group?

**Advantages of independent travel:**

- High itinerary flexibility, allowing in-depth exploration based on personal interests
- Generally more affordable than group tours
- Lets you vacation at your own relaxed pace
- Offers a wider variety of dining options

However, planning an independent trip requires considerable effort:

- Transportation can be complicated, with limited public transit; you'll need to research private driver or car rental options
- Attractions are scattered and route planning is tricky, requiring study of distances and visiting order between sites
- You must consider whether the character of each accommodation area (e.g., lively Kuta, artistic Ubud) suits your needs
- You need to understand weather, food safety, and local etiquette

**Advantages of Group Tours:**

- Eliminates the hassle of arranging transportation and accommodation
- Includes a guide for explanations and assistance
- Offers a complete itinerary, ideal for travelers with limited time
- No need to worry about language barriers

**We recommend choosing based on the following considerations:**

1. Language skills - English is common in tourist areas, but if you don't speak any foreign language, a group tour is advisable
2. Trip duration - For short stays, a group tour allows quick visits to key sites
3. Budget planning - Independent travel lets you adjust expenses flexibly, while group tours are mostly all-inclusive with no need to worry about costs
4. Travel style - Whether you prefer in-depth exploration or a quick tour of attractions

## 21. Is it convenient to use credit cards in Bali?

The credit card payment environment in Bali is quite mature, but there are a few things to keep in mind:

1. **Where cards are accepted:**
2. - High-end restaurants, shopping malls, five-star hotels, and beach clubs typically accept international credit cards (Visa, MasterCard, Amex).

- Many car rental companies and travel agencies also accept credit cards, but may charge an additional fee (around 1.5–3%).

3. **Where cards are not accepted:**
4. - Street stalls, small local eateries (Warung), traditional markets, and remote areas may only accept cash.

- Some restaurants have a minimum spending amount for card payments; transactions below that threshold cannot be made with a card.

5. **Recommendations:**
6. - **Carry Indonesian Rupiah cash:** Cash is most convenient for small transactions or tips, especially in remote areas or when shopping at street stalls.

- **Bring multi-currency credit cards:** Visa and MasterCard are the most widely accepted.
- **Use digital payments:** GoPay, an e-wallet in Bali, is becoming increasingly popular. Consider installing the relevant app. For reference: [Comprehensive Guide to Registering, Using, and Topping Up Gojek and GoPay in Indonesia/Bali](/en/blog/indonesia-gojek-gopay-guide/)

## 38. Are Bali's Traditional Dance Performances Worth Watching?

![38.峇里島的傳統舞蹈表演值得看嗎？](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_82b8c46b8f153469d536f48be8633bf8.jpg)

Visiting Bali, experiencing the local traditional dances is an absolute must! Balinese dance is not just an art form; it's deeply rooted in religious and cultural significance. Through intricate costumes, graceful movements, and expressive gestures, these dances perfectly showcase Bali's unique cultural landscape.

Here are several traditional Balinese dances worth watching:

1. **Kecak Dance**

This is one of Bali's most iconic traditional dances, also known as the 'Monkey Dance.' Inspired by the Hindu epic Ramayana, performers sit in a circle and chant 'chak-chak-chak' to mimic a troop of monkeys, creating a truly electrifying atmosphere. The most famous performance takes place at sunset at Uluwatu Temple, with a backdrop of golden skies and stunning ocean views—a feast for both the eyes and ears.

[Top 6 Kecak Fire Dance Locations in Bali](/en/blog/bali-kecak-fire-dance-locations/)

2. **Barong Dance**

The Barong Dance tells a story of the struggle between good and evil, featuring the benevolent mythical beast 'Barong' battling the malevolent 'Rangda,' symbolizing balance and harmony. Dancers wear elaborate costumes and showcase refined body language, making this performance especially popular with families.

3. **Legong Dance**

The Legong dance is considered the most elegant dance in Bali, often performed by young girls who interpret captivating mythological stories through extremely intricate hand movements and eye contact. Known for its tight rhythm and precise movements, it is typically staged at the Ubud Palace or in local art villages, making it a favorite among art enthusiasts.

The Barong and Legong dances can be enjoyed on the indoor stage at Balerung Stage. For details, please see [here](/en/blog/balerung-stage-cultural-show/).

**Reasons to Watch**

1. **Cultural Experience**: The dances are closely tied to Balinese religious ceremonies, offering you a deeper understanding of local culture.
2. **Visual Delight**: Performances are usually accompanied by traditional musical instruments, with exquisite costumes and delicate movements that are a feast for the eyes.
3. **Family-Friendly**: Most dance performances are suitable for all ages, whether you're a family with kids or a couple, everyone can find enjoyment.
