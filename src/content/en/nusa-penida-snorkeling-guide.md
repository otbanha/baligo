## Can You Snorkel Everywhere in Bali?

Although mainland Bali is surrounded by coastline, there are actually only a few places truly suitable for snorkeling or diving. The most famous spots—Blue Lagoon, Amed, and Tulamben—are all in the remote eastern region, requiring two to three hours of driving from the airport. Below is a video of Xiao Jie snorkeling in Amed:

https://youtu.be/qdS8Td7QPYY?si=J-jy5ieS7-eAsl0E

If you want to reach good snorkeling or diving spots faster, we recommend taking a car from the airport to Sanur, staying there for one or two nights, then catching a 30-minute speedboat to Nusa Penida early in the morning. This is a more relaxed option that saves you from long road trips.

## Crystal Bay

![Crystal Bay](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_233401d4c81f691e18d2a6149a0d9830.jpg)

Crystal Bay is one of the most popular spots on Nusa Penida—a secluded cove on the island’s west coast with a beach stretching about 200 meters. You can reach it by road. Besides its charming palm-fringed shoreline, this bay is best known as Nusa Penida’s premier snorkeling and diving destination. The water here is crystal clear (hence the name), and divers rave about the excellent underwater visibility, which can reach up to 30 meters.

At Crystal Bay, you’ll not only see a dazzling variety of colorful corals and tropical fish, but it’s also one of the few places in the world where you can get up close with the ocean sunfish (mola-mola, known as manbo fish in Taiwan and Japan). These massive migratory fish can weigh up to 2,000 kilograms and reach 3 meters in length. If you dive here between July and October, you might just encounter them and swim alongside.

## Gamat Bay

![Gamat Bay](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-03/1774881880928-gamatbay1.jpeg)

Gamat Bay, along with Crystal Bay, is considered one of the two best snorkeling spots on Nusa Penida. The shallow waters here are incredibly clear, making it perfect for snorkeling and observing the coral reef ecosystem.

At Gamat Bay, you can see a variety of hard corals, such as staghorn coral, flowerpot coral, and large brain coral. Many types of algae, including sea anemones and sea fans, also thrive here. Small tropical fish like double-saddle butterflyfish, snapper, and surgeonfish dart among the coral formations. Occasionally, you might spot small reef sharks.

Another highlight is the sea turtles. In the shallow waters near the shore, you can often observe different species, such as black turtles and green turtles. From June to October, it’s turtle nesting season, and you might even see mother turtles coming ashore to lay their eggs.

Due to its proximity to the coast and calm currents, Gamat Bay is ideal for children and beginner snorkelers. Just put on a life jacket, mask, and fins, and you can safely enjoy the magical underwater garden.

## Manta Point

![Manta Point](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_20761005e11f28ee9b74adf674bab312.jpg)

Manta Point is one of the most distinctive spots on Nusa Penida Island. At this dive site, you can get up close and personal with giant manta rays (Manta birostris), offering a truly unique and breathtaking experience.

Commonly known as 'devil fish,' the giant manta ray can reach up to 7 meters in length and weigh around 3,000 kilograms—pretty huge, right? With such size and weight, it's natural to feel intimidated, but don't worry. Despite their massive size, manta rays are not dangerous at all. They are large yet friendly to humans. There are two species of manta rays: the reef manta and the oceanic manta.

The manta rays at Manta Point on Nusa Penida are of the reef manta type (Manta alfredi). This species is the second largest after the oceanic manta, with an average size of 3 to 3.5 meters. These beautiful creatures are filter feeders, meaning they eat plankton, crustaceans, and small fish. Although their tails are long, they are not venomous, making swimming with manta rays very safe. Besides their long tails, manta rays have the largest brain-to-body ratio among all fish. They are highly interactive with humans, making Manta Point on Nusa Penida a top choice for snorkelers and divers seeking close encounters with these majestic rays.

A reminder: when we say 'contact,' we don't mean actually touching these wild creatures!! The main reason is to protect the manta ray's skin—they have a layer of mucus that helps shield their skin from environmental harm. If touched by visitors, this mucus layer can be removed, increasing the risk of skin infections. Although manta rays are gentle, they are still wild animals. Unauthorized touching may startle them or trigger instinctive reactions that could injure people nearby. Most manta ray diving spots worldwide have strict rules against touching the fish, and visitors should follow these regulations.

For the best chance to see manta rays, the recommended months are **between April and November**, especially during the dry season (April to October), when the water is clear, currents are stable, and visibility is higher—making it the prime time for snorkeling and diving.

Among these, **August to October** is the peak season for encountering manta rays, as currents during this period carry abundant plankton, attracting large numbers of them to feed. In southern Bali, **Manta Point and Manta Bay on Nusa Penida Island** are popular spots for viewing manta rays.

Note: Manta ray sightings are influenced by weather and currents, so they cannot be 100% guaranteed.

## Toyapakeh Wall Point

Toyapakeh Wall Point is actually a steep cliff, located directly beneath the Adiwana Warnakali dive center.

This dive site features a small coral terrace at 4 to 6 meters deep, which is the best spot for snorkeling. Beyond the coral terrace, there are two layers: the first is about 12 to 15 meters deep, and the second is around 40 meters deep. It's a very popular dive site due to its clear waters and thriving coral.

![Toyapakeh Wall Point](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-03/1774881808430-toyapakeh_wall_point.jpg)

In front of the Adiwana Warnakali dive center lies an area protected by large soft corals and sea fans. These soft corals form stunning structures, and the entire area is rich in biodiversity: various schools of fish, soft corals, and healthy hard corals. Snorkeling or diving here, just admiring the scenery is a feast for the eyes!

## Tips Before You Go

- Check the weather and sea conditions before snorkeling, as visibility and wave conditions vary daily.
- If you're prone to seasickness, take motion sickness medication beforehand.
- If riding a scooter, be extra cautious—road conditions on the island are rough, so proceed at your own risk.
- If it's your first time, consider staying overnight on the island—snorkel during the day and take a leisurely stroll in the evening.
