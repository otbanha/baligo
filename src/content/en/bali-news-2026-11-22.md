# Latest Bali News | November 22: Rainy Season Weather, Kintamani Road Collapse, and New Tourism Management Policies

The latest updates from Bali on November 21 focus on rainy season traffic safety, tourism carrying capacity management, and maritime tourism safety. BMKG forecasts moderate to heavy rain and lightning risks in some areas on November 22, while an alternative road in Kintamani remains unrepaired after a collapse from previous heavy rains. Additionally, the Bali government has begun studying a reasonable international tourist carrying capacity for the future, and the issue of legal licenses for accommodation operators continues to draw attention.

## **🌦️ BMKG Forecasts Local Rainfall on November 22! Southern Sea Waves May Reach 2 Meters**

The Indonesian Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BMKG) Bali Region III Meteorological Center has released the weather forecast for November 22, predicting mainly light rain, but eastern, central, and western parts should still be cautious of moderate to heavy rain and lightning.

**November 22 Weather Overview:**

| Item | Forecast |
| --- | --- |
| Overall Weather | Light Rain |
| Temperature | 20–34°C |
| Relative Humidity | 60–95% |
| Wind Direction | East to South |
| Wind Speed | 4–37 km/h |
| Northern Sea Wave Height | 0.5–1.25 meters |
| Southern Sea Wave Height | 0.75–2 meters |

BMKG has issued a special rainfall alert, noting that parts of eastern, central, and western Bali may experience moderate to heavy rain accompanied by lightning.

In terms of sea conditions, the following areas should be cautious of wave heights of 2 meters or more:

- Southern Bali Strait
- Southern Lombok Strait
- Badung Strait (Selat Badung)
- Southern Bali Waters

**Impact on Travelers:**

If you have plans to visit mountain areas like Ubud, Kintamani, or Bedugul, it's advisable to watch for afternoon rainfall and road conditions, avoiding steep slopes or landslide-prone sections during heavy rain.

Travelers heading to Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, or other offshore islands should confirm the latest sea conditions with boat operators. Don't just check the weather at the departure port—sea conditions along the route and at the destination are equally important.

This is a localized weather advisory, not a blanket closure of all attractions on the island. Actual conditions should be assessed based on the latest local forecasts.

## **🚧 A Collapsed Alternative Road to Kintamani! Jalan Pulasari Kawan Remains Unrepaired**

An alternative road to Kintamani in Bangli Regency, Bali, collapsed due to heavy rains in early November and remains completely impassable.

According to a detikBali report on November 20, the affected road sections are:

**Jalan Pulasari Kawan**

Location: Pulasari Village, Tembuku District, Bangli Regency.

This road was hit by heavy rain in early November, causing severe collapse of the road surface and making the entire section impassable.

On November 20, after a field inspection by the relevant committee of the Bangli Regency Council, local authorities were urged to prioritize repair work and conduct a technical assessment to determine the cause of the collapse and the subsequent construction plan.

However, this road is only one of the alternative routes to Kintamani, and not all roads leading to Kintamani are blocked; other alternative routes remain usable.

**Reminders for travelers:**

- When heading to Kintamani to enjoy views of Mount Batur and the lake, it is recommended to use the main roads.
- Do not blindly follow navigation into unfamiliar rural roads just to save time.
- Travelers with private drivers can ask their driver in advance to confirm the road conditions for the day.
- If encountering heavy rain, avoid traveling through hillsides, landslide-prone areas, and flooded sections.

The exact completion date for the repair of this road has not yet been announced.

## **🌍 Bali Studies International Tourist Carrying Capacity! Future Entry Management Regulations May Be Established**

Bali Governor Wayan Koster stated on November 19 that the provincial government will study the number of international tourists Bali can sustainably accommodate in the long term, and consider drafting relevant local regulations to balance tourism development with environmental, infrastructure, and cultural preservation.

This study primarily evaluates:

- Bali's natural environment carrying capacity
- Road, transportation, and public facility capacity
- Water resources and waste management capacity
- The impact on Bali's traditional culture and residents' daily lives

Koster stated that based on the study results, relevant local regulations (Perda) will be prepared as a policy basis for tourist management.

**However, it's important to note that no official daily entry quota for foreign tourists has been announced, nor have any new entry restrictions or implementation dates been declared.** At this stage, it remains in the research and policy preparation phase, and should not be interpreted as foreign travelers being unable to freely visit Bali anytime soon.

On the other hand, the Bali government continues to require online travel agencies (OTAs) to stop selling accommodations that lack valid business licenses.

The governor stated that around 5,000 villa operators have applied for licenses between 2025 and 2026. The government aims to improve market competition and accommodation management through the legalization of lodging businesses.

For travelers, this policy has a practical impact: when searching for accommodations on platforms like Agoda, Booking.com, and Airbnb, properties that do not meet local business license requirements may no longer be available for booking.

This does not mean Bali is banning villa stays, nor are all private villas illegal. When booking, travelers are advised to choose accommodations with clear and legitimate business information, especially for long-term stays or entire villa rentals—be sure to verify the operator's business credentials.

Bali's future tourism policy direction is clearly shifting from simply chasing visitor numbers to focusing on environmental capacity, accommodation legalization, and tourism quality management.

## **🚢 Benoa Cruise Terminal Tightens Security Checks! Preparing for the Christmas and New Year Travel Peak**

The Benoa Port in Denpasar, Bali, has recently stepped up security management for passengers and cruise operations in preparation for the 2026 Christmas and 2027 New Year travel season.

According to a Bali Post report on November 20, the Denpasar police and Benoa Port police units have increased dock patrols and strengthened cooperation with port authorities, customs, Pelindo, and quarantine agencies.

Key management measures include:

- Monitoring of cruise ships and passenger vessels entering and leaving the port
- Passenger and luggage inspection
- Cargo and logistics management
- Preventing drugs, dangerous goods, and prohibited items from entering
- Strengthening security patrols around the port area

Benoa is an important maritime gateway to Bali, serving both domestic passenger ships and international cruise passengers.

Pelindo has also been continuously improving cruise service facilities, with the eastern cruise terminal extended from 350 meters to 500 meters, capable of berthing two large cruise ships simultaneously.

Currently, 76 cruise ships have registered plans to dock at Benoa in 2026, indicating that Bali's cruise tourism market is still developing.

For independent travelers, this measure will not directly change airport entry procedures; however, if arriving in Bali by cruise ship, you may encounter more intensive port security checks in the near future.

No new passenger entry restrictions or additional fees have been announced.

## **🏖️ Kuta Selatan Beaches Step Up Police Patrols! Reminding Tourists Not to Ignore Sea Conditions and Safety Signs**

The southern Kuta area of Bali, including several cliffside beaches and popular coastal spots, has recently intensified both sea and land patrols.

According to reports from Bali Post and NusaBali, the Denpasar Police Maritime Unit (Satpolairud) continued patrol operations in the waters and beaches of Kuta Selatan on November 18.

Patrol methods include:

- Using police boats to inspect the waters
- Conducting land patrols along accident-prone coastal areas
- Monitoring tourists, fishermen, and maritime activities
- Providing warnings and necessary safety measures when dangerous situations arise

Police are particularly focused on maritime accidents, risky tourist activities, and public order on the beaches.

Some beaches in Kuta Selatan have unique terrain, especially those beneath cliffs, making emergency rescue and evacuation more challenging than on typical sandy shores.

Police remind tourists:

- Follow safety signs and no-swimming warnings on the beach.
- Do not force yourself into the water when waves or currents are noticeably stronger.
- Do not enter restricted areas without permission.
- When visiting cliffside beaches, be mindful of the uphill/downhill terrain and the time needed for your return.

This patrol does not mean a total swimming ban on Kuta Selatan beaches, nor has any new unified closure measure been announced—it is part of ongoing safety monitoring.

Especially during the rainy season and periods of unstable sea conditions, travelers should not just look at how beautiful a beach is, but also pay attention to the waves, rip currents, and rescue conditions on site.
