# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup | October 5, 2026

## 🎟️ Kintamani 'Digital Ticket' Canceled This Year, Single-Entry Fee Delayed Until 2027

The planned **digital single-entry ticket system (one gate system)** for the Kintamani tourist area has been confirmed as a no-go for this year.

Bangli Regency officials had hoped to centralize tourism fees across Kintamani's attractions through a digital payment system, making it easier to track visitor numbers—with an estimated capacity of around 3,000 tourists per day once fully operational.

However, due to incomplete preparations, the system will **not go live in 2026**.

For travelers, the immediate takeaway is:

**Visiting Kintamani this year? You won't encounter the new digital single-entry fee system.**

So if you've seen social media posts claiming 'Kintamani has fully switched to digital tickets,' that's not accurate for now.

If this system is actually implemented in the future, it could actually be a good thing for tourists, as it would centralize the fee collection process at scenic spots and reduce the confusion caused by separate charges at different entrances.

## 🛡️ Ubud Catus Pata Enhances Tourist Safety, Police Assist with Traffic and Visitor Flow

Gianyar police have recently stepped up tourist safety operations around **Ubud Catus Pata**.

In addition to maintaining public order, local tourist police are collaborating with the transportation department, pecalang (traditional security), and market security to assist in popular areas with:

- Helping tourists cross the road
- Directing traffic flow
- Maintaining order around the market
- Handling potential traffic congestion

The police stated that this effort not only ensures public safety but also aims to make tourists feel safer when moving around popular areas in Ubud.

Catus Pata itself is a major traffic hub in Ubud, and with nearby popular attractions like **Ubud Palace** and **Ubud Market**, the area has long struggled with a mix of people and vehicles.

If you're planning a walking itinerary in Ubud city center soon and see police or pecalang directing traffic, it's best to follow their on-site instructions.

## 🛕 Klungkung Akah Holds Grand Festival After 37 Years, 500 Penjor Line the Streets

![](https://akcdn.detik.net.id/community/media/visual/2026/10/04/ratusan-penjor-menghiasi-jalan-desa-adat-akah-dalam-gelaran-karya-pamungkah-tawur-agung-di-klungkung-bali-minggu-4102026-fatih-1791095191629_169.jpeg)

**Desa Adat Akah** in Klungkung presents a spectacular sight today.

The local community is holding the **Karya Pamungkah Tawur Agung**, a major religious festival, with the last large-scale event taking place **37 years ago**.

The most eye-catching feature is nearly **500 Penjor** lining both sides of the road for about 1.5 kilometers, enveloping the entire village in traditional bamboo decorations and a festive atmosphere.

The festival began on October 4th, with related religious activities continuing.

If you're heading to **Klungkung** soon, such a large festival may cause localized traffic congestion, but it's also a rare opportunity to witness Bali's traditional cultural landscape.

However, a reminder:

**Penjor is not a decorative item for tourists—it is a sacred symbol with religious significance.**

When taking photos, keep your distance, do not touch the offerings, and avoid standing in the middle of the ceremonial procession for a photo.

## 🇹🇱 Bali and Timor-Leste Deepen Tourism Cooperation, with Approximately 46,000 Travelers in the First 9 Months

The Bali–Timor-Leste Business Forum was recently held in Denpasar, with the Balinese government aiming to expand collaboration in **tourism, agriculture, fisheries, and trade** between the two regions.

Bali Governor Wayan Koster stated that as of **September 30, 2026**, Bali has welcomed approximately **4.6 million foreign tourists** this year, with about **30,000 coming from Timor-Leste**.

Timor-Leste also hopes to learn from Bali's experience in tourism and agriculture.

For now, this is more of a government and industry partnership, but from a tourism perspective, it's worth noting:

**The Timor-Leste market is gradually becoming a neighboring country tourism market that Bali can tap into.**

Given the proximity between the two destinations, along with their historical and geographical ties, there is still room for growth in future travel exchanges.

## 🌊 Southern Bali Sees Waves Up to 3 Meters—Stay Cautious Near the Coast

According to the latest BMKG forecast, **on October 4, southern Bali waters may still experience waves up to about 3 meters**, while northern waters will see around 0.5 to 1.5 meters.

On land, Bali will be mostly sunny to partly cloudy today, with a high of around 32°C. No island-wide rain alert has been issued, but sea conditions in the south still warrant caution.

So if you have plans for:

**Sanur → Nusa Penida / Nusa Lembongan**

or any water activities like snorkeling, diving, or surfing, be sure to check the latest sea conditions before heading out.

The Buleleng search and rescue team also reminds visitors today not to ignore **safety signs and restricted areas** at the beach—don't assume calm waters mean it's safe to enter risky zones.
