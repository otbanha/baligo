### 20. Steps from Landing to Exiting the Airport:

After landing in Bali, you'll need to follow this sequence for immigration:

1. Follow signs to disembark and enter the terminal.
2. Follow the 'Arrival' signs to the immigration hall.
3. Apply for a Visa on Arrival (VoA): If you haven't obtained an e-Visa, queue at the 'Visa on Arrival' counter to pay. Those with an eVisa/eVoA can skip this step.
4. Immigration inspection (passport stamping) queue: Those with a VoA need to queue for inspection.
5. Electronic gate: Those who have applied/paid for an eVisa can skip the manual stamping and use the 'automatic gate'.
6. Collect luggage: After passing immigration/automatic gates, head to the carousel for your flight number.
7. Prepare your health declaration and customs declaration form (now integrated into All Indonesia).
8. Pay the Bali tourist tax.

### 23. Understanding Local Currency Conversion:

Bali uses the Indonesian Rupiah. Currently, one US dollar exchanges for around 16,000 Rupiah. Compared to the New Taiwan Dollar, the rate is roughly 1:500, meaning one TWD gets you 500 Rupiah; 1,000 TWD ✖️ 500 equals 500,000 Rupiah.

A quick way to convert is to drop the last three zeros from Indonesian Rupiah, then multiply by 2, and you'll get roughly the amount in New Taiwan Dollars.

### 27. Watch Out for Extra Charges:

When dining in Indonesia, pay close attention to the additional fees on your bill. The food and beverage tax is added separately, usually 11%, which goes to the government. On top of that, many restaurants also charge a service fee of 4% to 10%. So if a menu item is priced at 100,000 Indonesian Rupiah, your final bill will be 100,000 IDR + 11% tax + the service fee. Keep these extras in mind when ordering to avoid overspending.
