### 20. From Deplaning to Exiting the Airport: The Process

After flying into Bali, you'll need to follow these steps for immigration and customs:

1. Follow signs to deplane and enter the airport terminal.
2. Follow the "Arrival" signs to the immigration hall.
3. Apply for a Visa on Arrival (VoA): If you haven't applied for an e-Visa in advance, head to the "Visa on Arrival" counter to queue and pay. Those with an eVisa/eVoA can skip this step.
4. Immigration inspection (passport stamping) queue: Those with a VoA need to queue for inspection.
5. Electronic clearance: Those who have applied for and paid for an eVisa in advance can skip the manual stamping queue and use the "automatic gate."
6. Collect luggage: After passing through immigration/automatic gates, head to the baggage carousel based on your flight number.
7. Prepare your health declaration and customs declaration form in advance (now integrated into All Indonesia).
8. Pay the Bali tourist tax.

### 23. How to Convert Local Currency:

Bali uses the Indonesian Rupiah. Currently, one US dollar exchanges for around 16,000 Rupiah. Compared to New Taiwan Dollars, the rate is roughly 1:500, meaning one TWD gets you 500 Rupiah; 1,000 TWD × 500 equals 500,000 Rupiah.

A quick conversion trick is to drop the last three zeros from the Rupiah amount, then multiply by 2 to get an approximate TWD value.

### 27. Watch Your Bill:

When dining in Indonesia, pay close attention to the extra charges on your bill. The food and beverage tax is added separately, typically 11%, which goes to the government. Additionally, many restaurants tack on a service charge of 4% to 10%. So, if an item on the menu is priced at 100,000 Indonesian Rupiah, your final bill will be 100,000 IDR plus 11% tax plus the applicable service fee. Keep these surcharges in mind when ordering to avoid any budget surprises.
