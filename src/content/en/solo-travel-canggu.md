# Will solo travel in Canggu, Bali be boring?

Having lived in Bali for years, I often get DMs asking: 'I want to go to Canggu alone—will I feel lonely? Is it safe?'

## Why Canggu is great for solo travel

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-11/1794493745133-15617.jpg)

Let me cut to the chase: whether you want to meet people, work remotely, or just be alone, Canggu has you covered.

There are plenty of social entry points here. Surf schools, yoga classes, beach clubs, group tours, and coworking spaces—join any of them, and you'll have a chance to strike up a conversation. If you just want to zone out on your own, no one will bat an eye at you sitting solo in a café.

How long to stay: If Canggu is just one stop on your Bali itinerary, I'd recommend 3 to 5 days. If you're spending your entire trip here, you can easily stretch it to two weeks or more.

## Where to stay depends on how social you want to be

Hostels: Budget-friendly, often host events, easy to make friends, and many also offer private rooms.

Homestay or guesthouse: You get your own room, with shared garden or kitchen—not too noisy, not too lonely.

Hotel or villa: Maximum privacy. But a double room costs the same whether one or two people stay, so factor that into your budget.

## When to Go

Dry season (roughly April to October): Sunny, low humidity, best for surfing and beach clubs, but crowded and pricier.

Rainy season (roughly November to March): Rain often falls but usually in short bursts; fewer crowds and lower prices.

We're just entering the rainy season now. Don't stay cooped up in your room when it rains—we've put together [rainy day alternatives](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/2026-bali-indoor-activities/), including a trampoline park, silver jewelry workshops, and yoga studios in Canggu.

## Solo Travel Safety Tips

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-11/1794493677955-15615.jpg)

- Download Grab, Gojek, and offline Google Maps before you depart.
- If staying more than a few days, buy a local SIM card
- For longer solo day trips, let family or friends know your itinerary first
- After dark, stay in crowded, well-lit areas
- Never let your drink out of sight, and don’t accept drinks from strangers
- Keep valuables secure and only carry the cash you need for the day
- Dress modestly when entering temples
- Be sure to purchase comprehensive travel insurance

## In the End

For most solo travelers, the fear isn't danger—it's having no one to talk to.

Canggu is the opposite. In the morning, someone invites you to surf; after yoga class, someone asks if you want to grab a meal together; in the evening, you find a spot on the beach, and chances are the person next to you is also traveling alone. You don't have to actively make friends—they just show up.

So stop wondering, 'Will it be weird to go alone?' Download Gojek/Grab, get travel insurance, and on your first day, plan nothing—just head to Batu Bolong to watch the sunset.
