This summer, there's a Bali event worth noting: the Festival Jatiluwih. It's not your typical sightseeing—it invites visitors into the fields to harvest rice alongside farmers. Such experiences are quite rare in Bali.

## Large-Scale Rice Harvesting Experience

The festival's core concept is to let visitors experience harvesting firsthand. Coincidentally, from June 20 to 22 this year, the rice terraces of Jatiluwih (Subak Jatiluwih) enter the harvest season, so DTW Jatiluwih has chosen this time for the festival.

Organizer I Ketut 'John' Purna says visitors can truly feel what a farmer's daily work is like. Wear light clothes, step into the lush green terraces, bend down, and cut rice alongside the locals—no special skills needed, but the sensation of your feet sinking into soft mud, feeling the blazing sun, and working together with the community is something only firsthand experience can bring.

## More Than Just Farm Work

The festival offers more than just rice cutting. There are also music performances and Balinese cultural shows, plus a 'Bali Tourism Run' organized by the Indonesian Tourism Association (ASITA) (official site: [Bali Tourism Run](https://balitourismrun.id/)). The run is 5 kilometers, expected to attract 2,000 participants, and the route follows the slow-walking paths within the DTW area.

For running enthusiasts, this is an intriguing option—jogging while taking in Bali's most stunning rice terrace scenery.

## Electric Bike Adventure Tour

![電動單車探險之旅](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1781403086604-_2026-06-14_09.05.14.png)

Beyond the festival activities, another fantastic way to explore the Jatiluwih Rice Terraces is by joining an electric bike tour. Hop on an e-bike and ride through lush jungles, bamboo groves, and local villages all the way to this UNESCO World Heritage site.

Accompanied by a friendly English-speaking guide, you'll gain insight into the daily lives of local residents, with stops along the way to admire the best terrace views. After the ride, enjoy a delicious traditional Indonesian meal, a perfect finish to your nature adventure. This approach lets you experience the rural landscape without overexertion, ideal for travelers seeking a relaxed tour of the terraces.

## Getting There & Logistics

Anticipating a large influx of visitors, DTW Jatiluwih has prepared two parking zones. The western parking area is concentrated around Pura Petali, while the eastern side stretches from Desa Gunung Sari to the administrative service post near the Yeh Ba River bridge.

To protect farmers' crops, the park has deployed numerous security personnel, including traditional guards (pecalang) and local villagers, ensuring visitors do not wander into the terrace edges or pick rice.

## Why It's Worth Visiting

This festival is part of Bali's celebration of the '100-Year Tourism Commemoration.' For travelers who want to dive deeper into Balinese rural life beyond just snapping photos, this is a rare opportunity. In June, the rice terraces are at their best in terms of light and climate.

If you're planning a trip to Bali in June, set aside June 20 to 22 to experience Jatiluwih in a whole new way.
