There are plenty of day passes in Ubud, but Bambu Indah stands out because of the journey itself. You have to take a bamboo elevator to reach the riverside pool.

## Ride the Bamboo Elevator Down to the River: A Day Trip to Bambu Indah’s Ubud Spring Pools

Ubud has no shortage of day passes, but this time I want to highlight Bambu Indah. The reason is simple: to get to the pool, you first have to ride a bamboo elevator.

### First, Take the Bamboo Elevator

Bambu Indah is nestled in the Sayan Valley on the outskirts of Ubud. The property hugs a lush, rocky gorge that descends all the way to the Ayung River, Bali’s longest river.

The way down is unique. This bamboo elevator drops along a 20-meter-deep earthen shaft, and when the door opens, you’re in a world beneath the valley floor. If you prefer to walk, there’s also a spiral staircase leading down, ending at a row of spring pools beside the rushing river.

### What the Riverside Looks Like

According to the official website, there are over fifteen spring pools, one after another, with water flowing from underground springs on the mountain, passing through volcanic rock and soil layers before streaming into the river. The site describes these pools shimmering beneath the forest canopy, with cool water that feels softer due to its mineral content.

The park says this area by the river was actually rice fields many years ago. After a bamboo elevator was built, it suddenly became easy to reach, and once you settle in, you won't want to leave.

By the pools, there are cabanas, loungers, and hanging moon chairs. The constant backdrop here is the gentle murmur of the Ayung River all day long.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-11/1795339087306-15817.jpg)

### Eating at River Warung

Follow the pebble-strewn path to the riverside restaurant, River Warung. It's an open-air space shaded by forest trees, overlooking the pool—described on the Klook page as a bamboo arch with a copper roof.

The park mentions that the interior uses earthy tones and low benches reminiscent of a traditional Balinese kitchen. Guests pass by the kitchen upon entry, where they can watch chefs prepping ingredients and chopping vegetables. The official site says the menu features Indonesian cuisine, along with craft cocktails and cold-pressed juices.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-11/1795330942738-15806.png)

### Things to Know Before You Go

- The pool is closed for cleaning on Mondays, with operating hours from 11:00 to 16:00; other days, it's open from 11:00 to 17:00.
- Daily slots are limited, so first come, first served.
- Children are allowed entry but must be supervised by an adult.
- The address is Banjar Baung, Sayan, Ubud. When booking a ride, it's safest to share the map pin directly.
