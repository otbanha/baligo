# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup｜August 20, 2026

## 🌋 2,850 Aftershocks in Flores, Residents Remain Anxious

Since the M7.7 earthquake on August 15, Flores Island has experienced a steady stream of aftershocks.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787141452551-sebanyak-2850-gempa-susulan-terjadi-di-flores-sejak-sabtu-1582026-1787107710612_169.jpeg)

As of **8:00 AM on August 19**, a total of **2,850 aftershocks** have been recorded, **83 of which were felt by residents**; the largest reached **M6.2**, with 24 additional aftershocks exceeding M5.

Local residents remain uneasy about the ongoing tremors. This is why travelers heading to **Flores, Labuan Bajo, and Komodo National Park** should not assume everything is back to normal just because the tsunami warning has been lifted.

**If you have upcoming plans combining Bali and Labuan Bajo, it's advisable to reconfirm flights, ferries, and site conditions before departure.**

This is especially important for trips to Padar Island, Komodo, and other destinations that require boat travel and outdoor activities—stay updated on the latest local announcements.

## 🇮🇩 Bali Continues to Support Flores; Travelers to NTT Should Reconfirm Itineraries

After the Flores earthquake, Bali continues to provide aid to East Nusa Tenggara (NTT), while the tourism industry also reminds travelers to review their itineraries if they had planned trips to Flores or Labuan Bajo in the near future.

Let's emphasize this again:

**A strong earthquake in Flores does not mean Bali is off-limits.**

Current travel advisories mainly target **the affected areas in NTT**, not Bali itself.

So if your itinerary is:

Kuta → Ubud → Canggu → Uluwatu → Nusa Dua

There's no need to cancel on your own due to this earthquake.

But if it's:

**Bali → Labuan Bajo → Komodo**

It's recommended to double-check transportation and outdoor activity conditions.

## 🚨 Canggu Beauty Salon Dispute Escalates, Foreign Tourist Found Overstaying Visa by 34 Days

A Norwegian female tourist, who sparked online discussion over a **dispute at a Canggu beauty salon**, was subsequently detained by Indonesian immigration authorities.

Immigration officials discovered her visa had **overstayed by 34 days**, leading to her transfer to the Denpasar Immigration Detention Center (Rudenim Denpasar).

According to reports, she initially gained online attention due to a **dispute over a 600,000 Indonesian Rupiah beauty service fee**, after which immigration authorities further checked her identity and residency status.

The key takeaway for regular travelers from this news is simple:

**Don't treat your visa validity as 'a few days over is probably fine.'**

VoA, e-VoA, or other stay permits have clear expiration dates; overstaying can result in fines, detention, or even deportation.

## 🏝️ Rising Demand for Seaview Accommodations on Nusa Lembongan Boosts Island Tourism

According to the latest travel observations from The Bali Sun, demand for **seaview accommodations on Nusa Lembongan** has been steadily increasing, indicating that more travelers are expanding their stay options from mainland Bali to surrounding islands.

Compared to Nusa Penida, Lembongan offers a more relaxed pace of island travel and transportation, with its beaches, snorkeling, sunsets, and seaview accommodations being major draws.

If you find Canggu and Seminyak already too crowded, but don’t want to tackle a hardcore Nusa Penida itinerary, **Lembongan is actually a great middle-ground option worth considering.**

## 🛂 Bali Continues to Tighten Foreigner Management, Visa Purpose Back in the Spotlight

Recently, Bali’s immigration authorities have stepped up checks on foreigners’ stays and activities. Beyond overstaying, they’re now paying closer attention to whether foreigners are using tourist visas for purposes that don’t match the visa’s intent.

Lately, there have even been checks targeting foreign content creators, business activities, and other non-tourist work.

For regular travelers, there’s no need to overstress.

**Normal sightseeing, accommodation, dining, shopping, and joining legal tour activities are all perfectly fine.**

What you really need to watch out for: don’t overstay your visa, and don’t use a tourist visa to do work or business activities that require other permits.
