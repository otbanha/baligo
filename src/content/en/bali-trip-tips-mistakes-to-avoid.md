Today I want to share my travel experience in Bali. Known as the 'Island of the Gods,' Bali draws travelers from around the world with its stunning natural scenery, rich cultural heritage, and friendly locals. On this trip, we experienced everything from visa processing and private driver services to major attractions and restaurants, soaking in the local vibe—though we did encounter a few hiccups. I hope my insights can offer some useful tips for those planning a trip to Bali, making your journey smoother and more enjoyable.

## Visa:

Since many people in our group reported issues with the electronic visa (eVOA), we decided to go with the visa on arrival instead. After arriving at the airport, the queue took about 15–20 minutes, which was fairly reasonable. However, even though the visa process was relatively quick, baggage claim still took a while, leaving us feeling a bit helpless while waiting for our luggage 😅. So if you opt for a visa on arrival, make sure to budget enough time for potential delays.

## Private Driver:

When booking a private driver, don't put too much trust in online reviews. Based on a recommendation from a Facebook group, we booked the 'Brother X Driver Team,' but the reality didn't live up to expectations 🥲. The driver assigned to us, a certain 'Te X,' was not very proactive—he replied to messages very slowly, his responses were vague, and he put little effort into planning the itinerary. Every time we reached a destination, he wouldn't let us know unless we asked.

The driver said the fee included gas and parking, but when we arrived at the cliffside road, we were asked to pay Rp5,000 for parking at the entrance, and we ended up covering it ourselves. Since the driver handled all communication with the ticket staff, there's no way he didn't know about this parking fee. It made us feel the driver wasn't entirely honest, which was really frustrating 😅.

Another time, on the day we settled the bill, the driver told us it was a holiday and we needed to pay an extra Rp50,000. This was very confusing because I had confirmed the price twice before departure, and the driver said there would be no changes. This last-minute price hike left us dissatisfied. So, when booking a private car service, make sure to double-check the prices and terms to avoid similar issues.

## Souvenirs:

![Li Wallis 提供](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_101ac5a2786fcaa5db6ac954775e1089.jpg)

Photo by Li Wallis

The souvenirs at Ubud Market are almost all identical, with nothing that really caught our eye. On the flip side, we had more fun browsing supermarkets, even though they were slightly pricier. We stumbled upon Bali Galeria Mall, which was a fantastic find! It has a supermarket, souvenir shops, and restaurants—everything you need. We originally wanted to mail postcards from the post office, but it was already closed 🥲. So, if you're planning to visit the post office, make sure to go early. In the end, we bought stamps at the airport and found a mailbox.

## Other Tips

- Some restrooms charge a fee—when nature calls urgently, Rp3,000 is a price you just have to pay 😅😂
- Prices at the airport are very high, so I recommend buying all your essentials and souvenirs before heading there. For example, bubble tea costs Rp65,000, a Vietnamese-style bag is Rp120,000, and a large bottle of Aqua water is Rp20,000.
- Don't forget to bring a reusable bag! We only found out while shopping at the supermarket that **Bali doesn't provide plastic bags**, and ended up buying a reusable bag for Rp15,000—so expensive 🥺.
