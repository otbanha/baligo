Nusa Lembongan (also called Lembongan Island) and its neighboring islands—Nusa Ceningan and Nusa Penida—attract a more niche crowd of travelers with their crystal-clear turquoise waters, relaxed coastal village atmosphere, and unique island adventure experiences. But now, these three offshore islands near Bali have become must-visit destinations for any vacation. Whether you opt for a day trip to Nusa Lembongan and Nusa Ceningan or a two-day deep dive into Nusa Penida, these islands will add endless color to your Bali getaway.

Nusa Lembongan is an offshore island southeast of Bali, reachable by a 30-minute boat ride from Sanur Harbor. Covering just 8 square kilometers, its main economic activities are tourism and seaweed farming.

Arriving on Nusa Lembongan, you'll feel a completely different, relaxed vibe compared to the busy beaches of Kuta, Seminyak, or Canggu. Here, you can choose to actively explore the island (island hopping) and join activities, or simply unwind by your accommodation's pool or at a restaurant. It's a great way to take a short break and recharge, allowing you to fully relax before returning to daily life.

## Nusa Lembongan's Enchanting Attractions

Spanning about 6.15 square kilometers, Nusa Lembongan is just a 30-minute speedboat ride away. For travelers short on time, it's an ideal choice—you can explore the entire island in a day. In contrast, Nusa Penida requires at least two days to cover the main sights on its east and west coasts. As the most developed of the three islands, Nusa Lembongan boasts a variety of resorts, restaurants, and beach clubs, making it easy to relax and soak up the sun on the sand.

Upon arriving at Nusa Lembongan, visitors are typically dropped off at the island's main areas—Jungut Batu Beach or Mushroom Bay—which are dotted with plenty of accommodations and eateries. If you plan to stay overnight, booking ahead is a must. That way, you can drop your bags and start exploring right away.

## Beach Exploration

Heading south by bike, you'll discover some intriguing beaches like Sandy Bay, Devil's Tear, and Dream Beach. Sandy Bay is the best spot for sunset views, so save it for last.

![Sandy Bay有個beach club](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_60e81c23058acdc6be657d2a801fb230.jpg)

Sandy Bay has a beach club

Devil's Tear, known in Chinese as 'The Devil's Tears,' is a rocky cliff that meets the ocean. Standing here, you can take in a stunning panoramic view of the sea from above. When the timing is right, big waves crash against the rocks, sending up sprays of water—a perfect Instagram photo spot. However, be careful not to stand too close, as sudden large waves can be incredibly powerful. There have been several tragic incidents where people were swept into the sea, so stay safe!

Dream Beach is a popular spot outside the main area of the island, and it can get a bit crowded. A morning visit makes for a much more pleasant experience. Stroll over to the rocky areas, where you'll find interesting photo opportunities like rock formations or small caves. There's also a cliff jumping spot here, but it's best to ask a local guide to make sure it's safe to jump.

## Mangrove Tour

Heading north on your bike, you'll reach the serene mangrove area of Nusa Lembongan. It's usually less crowded than the southern part, making it a perfect spot for those who love peace and quiet.

https://youtube.com/shorts/yerwXithouw?si=nDV7gK4eyEVeKb16

Once you arrive on Nusa Lembongan, you'll find several ways to explore this enchanting mangrove forest. You can rent a kayak and paddle leisurely through the calm waterways, soaking in the cozy atmosphere surrounded by mangroves while listening to the natural symphony of birdsong and sea breeze. If you want a deeper understanding of this ecosystem, consider joining a small-group guided boat tour.

Generally, when visitors arrive on Nusa Lembongan, locals will enthusiastically recommend various mangrove boat tours. It's best to opt for a small private boat, which typically holds 3-4 people and comes with an experienced boatman. If you can negotiate the price to under 100,000 Indonesian Rupiah per person, that's considered a reasonable deal. During the half-hour journey, you can get up close to the stunning scenery of the pristine mangroves, spot various water birds, and even catch glimpses of crabs scuttling among the roots.

A special tip: it's best to go during **low tide**, as the mangrove roots are fully exposed above the water, revealing the most captivating landscape. The boatman can also navigate through shallower waters, allowing you to observe this unique ecosystem up close.

## A Leisurely Day

Spending a lazy afternoon at a beachfront restaurant in the Jungut Batu or Mushroom Bay area is the ultimate comfort. There are plenty of reasonably priced seaside eateries here. Since these are also the main arrival and departure points—with the harbor located in both spots—you can watch the comings and goings of people throughout the day.

![悠閒的一天](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_a1f56eb2c4193e81b53d33fffa1b1f7c.jpg)

At sunset, Jungut Batu is a fantastic spot to watch the sun go down. If you've spent the entire afternoon here, transitioning from the bustling activity to complete tranquility is a simple yet pure delight.

For a different perspective on the sunset, take a short hike to Panorama Point, where you can admire the beautiful views from above.

## Surfing

If you can't resist the call of the waves, there are three popular surf breaks near Jungut Batu Beach: **Playgrounds**, **Lacerations**, and **Shipwrecks**. You can find guidance and rental services at the surf center on Jungut Batu Beach.

## Best Choices for Diving and Snorkeling

For adventurers who love the underwater world, Lembongan Island and its neighboring islands, Ceningan and Nusa Penida, offer a wealth of diving and snorkeling spots. Mangrove Point is the closest snorkeling site to the shores of Lembongan, perfect for travelers looking to quickly glimpse the underwater beauty. Around Ceningan and Nusa Penida, there are also numerous snorkeling and diving locations, such as the famous Manta Point, where you can swim alongside majestic and graceful manta rays—an unforgettable experience.

https://youtube.com/shorts/a4mL69nASLY?si=UciZXsalBlU3fOjo

A half-day snorkeling trip on Lembongan Island, including hotel pickup, snorkeling gear, boat fees, and three dive spots, costs around 150,000 Indonesian Rupiah (about USD$10) per person.

## Water Activities and Adventures

Beyond diving and snorkeling, Lembongan Island offers a variety of water activities to suit all tastes. You can try waterskiing, stand-up paddleboarding (SUP), banana boat rides, or even the unique experience of seawalking. You can also join an island-hopping boat tour to admire the island's stunning scenery from different angles while enjoying close contact with the water and the charm of the ocean.

## Getting Around

The best way to explore Lembongan and Ceningan is by bicycle or renting a scooter. Like Nusa Penida, these islands do not have ride-hailing services such as Gojek or Grab. If you prefer not to ride, another unique local transport option is the modified pickup truck shuttle service.

![當地的 pick-up truck](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_38697b346c428fcbc1d2bff17b0e777d.jpg)

Local pickup truck

Transportation costs on Lembongan:

- Scooter rental: 75,000–100,000 IDR per day (150–200 TWD / $5–$7 USD)
- Pickup truck: 50,000 IDR per trip (100 TWD / $3 USD)
- Private car hire: Limited options and more expensive, around 800,000 IDR for 8 hours (1,600 TWD / $51 USD)

Renting a scooter is not only budget-friendly but also gives you the freedom to explore every corner of the island at your own pace. Alternatively, the truck shuttle service offers a fun local experience, especially for those who aren't comfortable riding or are traveling with more luggage.

**Friendly reminder**: When renting a scooter, make sure to check its condition and keep the receipt. If you opt for the truck shuttle, it's a good idea to ask about the price in advance and confirm your destination with the driver to avoid any miscommunication.

## The Future of Nusa Lembongan

Nusa Lembongan is set to become Bali's most digitally advanced tourist destination. In 2024, the local government and business sector launched the "Nusa Lembongan Digital Island" initiative, aiming to enhance the travel experience and boost the local economy through a digital payment system. The plan covers transportation, dining, accommodation, and shops, fully supporting cashless payments. Additionally, Bank Indonesia will provide financial education to integrate digital technology into daily life. This project is expected to improve the quality of tourism on Nusa Lembongan, increase visitor stay duration, and drive growth in the original local revenue (PAD) of the Klungkung Regency.

In the future, Nusa Lembongan will become Bali's most digitally connected tourist destination. Through the "Nusa Lembongan Digital Island" initiative, the area will gradually implement a fully digital payment system—from booking ferry tickets and island transportation to attraction entry fees, hotels, restaurants, and shops—making it easier for travelers to plan and organize their trips. Moreover, strengthening digital infrastructure will address current signal dead zones, ensuring stable network connectivity across all areas. By driving these changes, Nusa Lembongan will become a world-class travel destination, extending visitor stays and integrating the local community into the digital economy, boosting economic growth and quality of life throughout the region.

## Nusa Lembongan FAQ

### 1. **Is Nusa Lembongan worth visiting?**

Absolutely! Nusa Lembongan is beloved for its serene atmosphere. Compared to Bali, it's much quieter and packed with places to explore. Add nearby Nusa Ceningan and Nusa Penida, and you can easily fill a week-long itinerary. The island offers spectacular marine activities like swimming with manta rays and cliff jumping, plus plenty of beautiful beaches to enjoy.

### 2. **What is Nusa Lembongan known for?**

Nusa Lembongan is famous for the Yellow Bridge, seaweed farms, white sand beaches, crystal-clear waters, stunning sunsets, breathtaking scenery, excellent cafes, and its neighboring islands, Nusa Ceningan and Nusa Penida. These are the key highlights that draw travelers here.

### 3. **Which is better: Nusa Penida or Nusa Lembongan?**

Both islands are worth visiting! But if you can only choose one, Nusa Lembongan is more developed, with better facilities and more activities. If time allows, I recommend visiting both to experience their unique charms.

### 4. **How long is the ferry from Bali to Nusa Lembongan?**

The ferry ride is quite short, taking only about 30 to 45 minutes from Bali to Nusa Lembongan.

### 5. **How many days should you stay on Nusa Lembongan?**

You can explore the main attractions of Nusa Lembongan in a single day, but if you want to savor the island's beauty at a leisurely pace, I suggest staying for 3 days. If you also plan to explore Nusa Ceningan and Nusa Penida, allow about 4 days. Spending at least one night on the island is essential to truly experience its stunning sunsets and laid-back island vibe.

### 6. **Is Nusa Lembongan suitable for a family trip?**

Nusa Lembongan offers plenty of family-friendly activities, such as snorkeling, playing on the beach, and watching the sunset, making it a great choice for a trip with kids. The island's facilities are also relatively well-developed, ideal for family vacations.

### 7. **Is Nusa Lembongan suitable for a digital nomad lifestyle?**

While the internet connection on Nusa Lembongan is weaker than in Bali, digital infrastructure has been improving in recent years. Some areas now have stable internet, making it suitable for short-term work or a digital nomad lifestyle.

### 8. **What are the must-see attractions on Nusa Lembongan?**

Beyond the Yellow Bridge and beautiful beaches, Nusa Lembongan's "Devil's Tears" is a famous natural attraction where waves crash against the cliffs, creating spectacular mist—definitely worth a visit.

### 9. **How do you get from Bali to Nusa Lembongan?**

Take a ferry from Sanur harbor in Bali to reach Nusa Lembongan. Multiple ferries run daily, making transportation very convenient.

### 10. **What is the best time to visit Nusa Lembongan?**

The dry season (April to October) is the best time to visit Nusa Lembongan. During this period, the weather is clear, the water is crystal clear, and it's perfect for outdoor activities like snorkeling and island hopping.

## Tips

1. **Book transportation and accommodation in advance**: As the number of visitors to Nusa Lembongan increases each year, it's advisable to book transportation and accommodation ahead of time to avoid last-minute hassles.
2. **Bring cash**: Although there are a few ATMs on the island, they sometimes run out of cash. Carrying enough Indonesian rupiah will ensure a smooth trip.
3. **Protect yourself from the sun**: The sun on the island is intense, so be sure to apply sunscreen and reapply regularly to avoid sunburn.
4. **Follow eco-friendly rules**: Please protect the marine environment by not littering and adhering to local eco-friendly regulations to preserve this beautiful tropical paradise.
