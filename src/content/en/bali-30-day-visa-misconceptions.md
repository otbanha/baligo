## What Is Indonesia's 30-Day Visa on Arrival?

Indonesia's 30-day visa on arrival is a short-term visa for travelers from many countries, allowing a stay of up to 30 days after entry. It is typically processed at Indonesian airports or seaports upon arrival and is suitable for tourism, visiting family, or other short-term stays.

## Application Process for the Visa on Arrival

Applying for Indonesia's 30-day visa on arrival is relatively straightforward. Upon arriving at an Indonesian airport, head to the visa application counter. You'll need to prepare the following documents and fees:

- A valid passport (with at least 6 months of validity)
- A return ticket or onward travel ticket
- Visa application fee (usually paid in cash or by credit card, around USD$35)

## Speed Up Immigration with an e-VoA

An Electronic Visa on Arrival (e-VoA) is a visa applied for and obtained online, eliminating the need to queue for a visa on arrival at immigration. Applicants simply complete the online application before departure, provide necessary personal information and documents, and pay the visa fee. Once approved, applicants receive an email notification and can present the e-Visa directly for clearance upon arrival. This method not only saves time but also greatly enhances entry convenience, making the journey smoother.

## Validity of the Visa on Arrival

Indonesia's 30-day Visa on Arrival is valid for 30 days from the date of entry. It's important to note that this 30-day period **includes both the day of entry and the day of departure**, so you effectively have a full 30 days to stay.

## Can You Leave on the 30th Day?

According to Indonesian visa regulations, you must leave before the visa expires. Therefore, the 30th day is the last day you can depart. If you plan to leave on the 30th day, make sure your flight time is before the visa expires to avoid any unnecessary trouble.

## Consequences of Overstaying

If you overstay, you will face a fine of **1,000,000 Indonesian Rupiah per day** (approximately USD$32). Additionally, you may face more serious legal consequences, such as being banned from re-entering the country. It is therefore recommended to depart before the visa expires to avoid any legal issues.

## Options for Extending Your Stay

If you want to extend your stay in Indonesia, you can apply for a visa extension. It's usually possible to extend for another 30 days, but you need to visit the local immigration office or apply online before your current visa expires, and submit the required application forms and fees.
