## 🚨 3 Foreign Tourists Stranded at Sea After Jet Ski Breakdown En Route from Java to Bali

On the morning of November 21, three foreign tourists departed from Alas Purwo in Banyuwangi, East Java, on a jet ski bound for Jimbaran, Bali, but suffered a mechanical failure and became stranded off the coast of Uluwatu.

According to data released by Indonesia's National Search and Rescue Agency (Basarnas), the three individuals are:

- 2 Indian nationals
- 1 Dutch national

Around 7 a.m. that day, the three set off on two Jet Skis. About an hour later, one of the Jet Skis suffered an engine failure and could no longer continue.

At approximately 8:30 a.m., authorities contacted the Denpasar Search and Rescue Center for assistance.

Basarnas immediately dispatched the KN SAR Arjuna 229 and a rubber inflatable boat (RIB) for the search. By 9:45 a.m., they successfully located all three individuals, who were safe and unharmed.

The rescue team then transported the three to Benoa Harbor.

Basarnas chief I Nyoman Sidakarya stated that the three tourists had originally been staying in North Lombok. After being rescued, they planned to first repair the Jet Ski before continuing their journey to Lombok.

He also reminded the public to check their physical condition, vehicle status, safety gear, and communication equipment before engaging in long-distance maritime activities.

**Reminder for Travelers:**

Although this incident resulted in no casualties, it serves as a reminder that Jet Skis are not suitable for long-distance sea crossings without thorough preparation.

If you're joining a Jetski activity in Bali, choose an operator with formal business arrangements and confirm:

- Whether life jackets and necessary safety equipment are provided
- Whether staff accompany or provide safety support
- The activity area and permitted sailing distance
- Whether sea conditions and mechanical status are checked before departure

## 🚢 North Bali Welcomes 1.77 Million Tourists! 5 Cruise Ships Expected to Dock at Celukan Bawang in December

The tourism market in Buleleng Regency, North Bali, continues to grow, with local authorities aiming to attract more domestic and international visitors during the year-end Christmas and New Year holidays.

According to a Bali Post report on November 22, the Buleleng Regency government announced that as of late November, the cumulative tourist arrivals for 2026 have reached:

**1,775,068 visitors**

Compared to the 1,706,796 visitors recorded in 2025, this represents an increase of approximately 4.05%.

Buleleng's tourism authorities have announced plans to launch travel packages by year-end through collaborations with hotels and travel operators, while stepping up promotions of local attractions.

One notable development is the rise of international cruise tourism.

The region expects around five cruise ships to dock at Celukan Bawang Port in December 2026.

The currently announced estimated port times are:

- Arrival time: approximately 8:00 AM
- Departure time: approximately 4:00 PM
- Docking location: Pelabuhan Celukan Bawang
- Tour arrangements: Local packaged sightseeing itineraries provided by cruise travel agencies

During their stay, cruise passengers will visit attractions around Buleleng as arranged by the travel agencies.

**What are the must-visit places in North Bali?**

Celukan Bawang is located in Buleleng Regency, with nearby attractions to explore:

- Lovina: Famous for dolphin watching and beachfront accommodations
- Gitgit: Waterfall attractions in North Bali
- Pemuteran: Diving, snorkeling, and marine conservation tourism
- Bali Aga villages: Discover traditional Balinese village culture
- Bedugul: Lakes, mountain scenery, and temples

However, these 5 cruise ships are still part of the local government's tentative plans, and the exact docking dates for each ship have not yet been announced.

For independent travelers, the increase in cruise tourism means North Bali is gaining international visibility, giving visitors more reasons to explore beyond southern Bali.
