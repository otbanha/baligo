# Bali News｜September 12, 2026

## 🏝️ Nusa Penida's Massive Infrastructure Plan Revealed! 7,092 Streetlights + Ring Road Included in PPP

Nusa Penida has a new infrastructure plan on the horizon.

The Klungkung Regency government has proposed advancing Nusa Penida's infrastructure through **KPBU (Public-Private Partnership)**, with plans including approximately **7,092 public lighting points (APJ)** and the construction and maintenance of a ring road around Nusa Penida.

If this plan moves forward smoothly, it's definitely something worth watching for travelers.

One of Nusa Penida's biggest issues in recent years is that **tourism development has far outpaced infrastructure**.

During the day, attractions are heavily crowded, but some roads are narrow and poorly lit, and nighttime traffic isn't as well-established as on mainland Bali.

So this proposed ring road and large-scale road lighting project could improve not just residents' lives, but also traffic safety and mobility for visitors on the island.

However, please note that **this is still in the proposal and planning stage, meaning the 7,092 streetlights won't be installed immediately.**

## 🔥 Bali Has Experienced 44 Forest and Land Fires This Year! The Dry Season Is Longer and Drier Than Usual

This year's dry season in Bali is no joke.

According to the latest data from BPBD Bali, the island has seen **44 forest and land fires (Karhutla)** so far this year, affecting approximately **196.68 hectares**.

Authorities point out that this year's dry season is **longer and drier than usual**, significantly increasing the risk of forest and land fires.

For travelers, this isn't a warning to avoid Bali altogether, but if you have upcoming plans involving:

**Hiking, waterfalls, jungle attractions, or trips to East or North Bali**

It's still wise to stay updated on local weather and fire conditions.

Above all, do not enter areas that show signs of smoke, flames, or have closure warnings.

Another issue this dry season is water resources.

In Badung's Petang area, **6 Subak irrigation zones have been affected by drought**, and local authorities have even had to deploy water trucks for supply.

## 🌊 Today, waves off southern Bali could reach up to 4 meters! Check sea conditions before any ocean trips

This tip is especially useful for travelers still in Bali today.

BMKG has been consistently warning about large waves in the waters around Bali, with some areas in the southern sea potentially seeing **waves up to about 4 meters**.

Affected areas include parts of southern Bali, so if you have plans today for:

**Surfing, snorkeling, SUP, diving, fishing, or other boat activities**

Be sure to check the latest sea conditions with your operator before heading out.

This is especially important for travelers heading from Sanur to Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, and other islands—don't just rely on the weather on Bali's mainland.

**Sunny skies ≠ calm seas.**

This is an easy-to-overlook point when planning a Bali trip.

## 🛂 574 Foreign Nationals Denied Entry to Bali in First 8 Months of This Year

Bali's immigration office has continued to tighten entry and stay regulations for foreign nationals this year.

Latest data shows that **574 foreign nationals were denied entry to Bali from January to August 2026**.

These cases involve various reasons, including immigration documents, residency eligibility, and other entry requirements.

Additionally, Bali's immigration authorities have been actively monitoring and enforcing rules against foreign nationals already in the country.

The key takeaway for regular tourists is simple:

**Make sure your passport validity, visa type, and length of stay are all in order.**

Especially now that Bali's immigration monitoring of foreigners is more proactive than in the past, don't assume that 'everyone used to do it this way' means it's still fine today.

If you're staying long-term, working, doing business, or working remotely, you should make sure your visa matches your actual activities.
