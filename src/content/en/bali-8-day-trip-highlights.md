**Bottom line**: The biggest surprises from my first 8-day, 7-night trip to Bali were—the weather wasn't as hot as I expected, but heavy rain was frequent (bring a light jacket); traffic was chaotic with almost no sidewalks (use Grab/Gojek to minimize walking); and the local bamboo-woven decor was incredibly creative and worth drawing inspiration from. The overall experience had both highs and lows compared to my vision of a laid-back island getaway, so it's best to go with a flexible mindset.

I finally made it to Bali this summer—a place I've always dreamed of visiting. This trip had plenty of moments that matched my expectations, along with some unexpected takeaways and reflections I'd love to share:

## **Weather**

![天氣](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_3121af7a76e9ab9aeae87f42ed213a36.jpg)

The weather in Bali wasn't as hot as I imagined, especially since heavy rain and bright sunshine often appeared on the same day. The rain could be torrential, hitting every day or two, but the wind wasn't too strong. I recommend bringing a light jacket, as it can get a bit chilly when it rains. When you're outdoors, always carry rain gear to avoid sudden downpours disrupting your plans. Sunscreen is also a must—even with the changing weather, the UV rays are intense when the sun is out.

## **Aesthetics and Decor**

Bali's shops, aesthetics, and decor are impressive, especially in some hotels and yoga studios that use bamboo and woven bamboo for roofs and chandeliers. Vietnam's bamboo weaving is more intricate, but Indonesia's is more creative and grand. I really loved the bamboo-woven chandeliers here, and they've given me plenty of ideas for decorating my own home in the future. These unique design styles not only reflect local culture but also blend nature with modernity in a beautiful way. During your trip, be sure to snap plenty of photos of these stunning decor pieces for future home inspiration.

## **Transportation**

Traffic in Bali is truly chaotic—there are almost no traffic lights, cars and motorcycles share the same lanes without separation, and dedicated sidewalks are nearly nonexistent. Walking around feels nerve-wracking, as you're constantly worried about getting hit. Wear comfortable shoes, as there are also plenty of puddles. The traffic rules here are vastly different from other cities, so it takes some time to adjust. When choosing how to get around, make good use of ride-hailing services like Grab or Gojek—they're convenient and safer, reducing the risks of walking.

## **Plants**

The vegetation in Bali is even denser than I imagined, making it a paradise for plant lovers. Lush greenery and all kinds of exotic plants are everywhere, many of which I'd never seen before in my life. The botanical gardens and nature reserves here are must-visit spots—not only can you admire rare plants, but you can also learn a lot about tropical flora. Don't forget to bring your camera along to capture these vibrant green landscapes.

## **Insects**

Bali also has plenty of insects, moths, and ants. Locals take a harmonious approach to dealing with them—every shop places offerings at the entrance for the little critters. It's not unusual to encounter bugs in a spa; locals usually don't kill them but instead pick them up with their bare hands 🪰. This attitude toward nature is fascinating and reflects the locals' respect for all life. During your trip, it's a good idea to carry insect repellent, especially when spending time outdoors, to avoid bites.

## **Accessory Shops**

The accessory shops in Bali are stunning—places like Blink make you want to step into almost every store. There are also many local designer brands that incorporate natural elements into their designs, offering great value for money. These accessories are not only beautifully crafted but also carry a strong local cultural vibe, making them perfect souvenirs or gifts. When shopping, take the time to chat with the shop owners to learn the stories behind each piece—it adds a deeper appreciation for their uniqueness.

## **Transportation**

![交通工具](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_dafbbe49d363e8f35135436c504badf2.png)

Using Grab or Gojek for taxis is very convenient, and most drivers are polite. A 20-minute ride costs around USD$3, and for longer distances, you can hire a private driver. Private car services are very flexible—drivers usually arrange the route based on your needs, helping you make better use of your time and visit more attractions. You can discuss the itinerary with the driver before departure to ensure you reach every spot smoothly.

## **Yoga Wear**

I thought yoga wear here would be cheap, but it turned out to be quite expensive. The well-known brand Indigo Luna starts at around USD$65 to USD$100, and the yoga wear downstairs at Alchemy costs over USD$160. At that price, it's better to buy Lululemon. Although the prices are high, the quality and design of these brands are excellent, so if you find a style you like, it's still worth buying. When shopping, compare several stores to find the best fit and price.

## **Yoga Classes**

![瑜珈課程](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6968936fa4c0c6ca57750fc1614ccd39.jpg)

Yoga Barn offers plenty of classes, but the venue feels a bit dated—maybe my expectations were too high. There are many flashy classes, but not many traditional yoga sessions. The free yoga classes at hotels are actually quite good. If you're into yoga, try out a few different studios to find the classes and instructors that suit you best. The variety of yoga classes here is vast, with options for everyone from beginners to advanced practitioners, catering to different needs.

## **Food**

Bali's cuisine mainly features pork ribs, fried noodles, and fried rice. If you love vegetable juices, you'll adore the fresh fruit juices here—they're incredibly fresh, and you'll find yourself having two glasses a day. The ingredients are fresh, and the flavors are unique, making them worth trying. While enjoying the food, take some time to learn about the local culinary culture and experience different taste sensations. Try local specialty restaurants to not only enjoy authentic dishes but also soak in the rich cultural atmosphere.

## **People and Service**

![人們和服務](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_3215b9db05b9e4049dd8c6ef5859a350.jpg)

Most people are very polite and warm, and it's safe to walk the streets at night. The spa services here are excellent, affordable, and offer great value for money. I recommend trying different spas throughout your trip to relax and enjoy the leisurely vibe. The locals' hospitality is truly heartwarming—whether in restaurants, shops, or attractions, you'll feel their friendliness and attentiveness. This cultural atmosphere adds so many wonderful memories to the journey.

My Bali trip was full of surprises and emotions. I hope these tips help you prepare better and enjoy the beauty of Bali. Whether you're exploring natural wonders, experiencing local culture, or simply relaxing, Bali has something for everyone. I hope you, too, can have a wonderful time in this dreamy place.
