# Missed public transport due to a delayed Bali return flight? 4 major transport options + budget table at a glance

Flying back from Bali to Taiwan, the worst-case scenario is a flight delay, late-night arrival, or red-eye flight. You step off the plane only to realize the airport MRT has already shut down and bus services are sparse. So what do you do? Let me break down the "lifesaver" transport options for frequent Bali travelers—no matter your budget, there's a choice for you.

## Why is it easy to miss public transport when returning from Bali?

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1785754655353-782540653612639836.png)

### Uncertain flight arrival times

- **Red-eye flights**: Many budget airlines' direct Bali flights depart at night (10:00 PM–11:30 PM), arriving in Taiwan between 4:00 AM–6:00 AM
- **Flight delays**: Weather, maintenance, and other factors often cause delays of 1–2 hours
- **Transit routes**: Flights via Hong Kong or Singapore have even less predictable schedules

### Sparse airport transport services

| Transport Mode | Operating Hours | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Airport MRT** | 6:00 AM–11:30 PM | Every 10–15 minutes |
| **Bus (Kuo-Kuang/Capital)** | 5:30 AM–11:30 PM | Every 30–60 minutes |
| **Airport Shuttle** | Varies by route | Every 30–60 minutes |

**Conclusion**: Late-night or early-morning arrivals mean you simply can't use these services, so you need to know your backup plan.

***

## Option 1: Airport Transfer Service (Most Convenient but Most Expensive)

### Highlights

- ✅ Door-to-door service, get in the car right after landing
- ✅ No need to drag luggage for transfers
- ✅ Safer for late-night trips

### Pricing

| Route | Vehicle Type | Price Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taoyuan Airport ↔ Taipei City | Sedan | $32–48 USD |
| Taoyuan Airport ↔ Hsinchu | Sedan | $39–58 USD |
| Taoyuan Airport ↔ Taichung | Sedan | $58–81 USD |

### Booking Platforms

- **Call a Taxi** (Taiwan local)
- **Uber Platform** (Uber Black premium service)
- **Hotel Booking** (if you've already reserved accommodation)

### Suitable For

- Travelers with ample budget who dislike transfers
- Travelers with lots of luggage
- Solo travelers who prioritize safety

***

## Option 2: Taxi Rank + Uber (Budget-Friendly)

### Highlights

- ✅ Hop in immediately at the airport—no reservation needed
- ✅ Affordable pricing, saving $10–$16 compared to transfer services
- ✅ Taxi drivers at the airport rank know the routes well

### Fare Guide

| Route | Price Range | Fare Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taoyuan Airport ↔ Taipei City | $16–$23 | Metered fare |
| Airport taxi rank | $19–$26 | Fixed price |
| Uber late-night hours | $23–$32+ | Surge pricing |

### Important Notes

⚠️ **Uber late-night surcharge**: Expect 1.5–2x surge pricing during early morning hours, making fares higher than usual
⚠️ **Long queues**: During holidays or when international flights arrive in waves, waiting times can be 30–60 minutes
⚠️ **Bring cash**: Most airport rank taxis only accept cash

### Best For

- Travelers on a budget who want to avoid transfers
- Groups of 2–3 sharing a ride (splitting costs makes it even more affordable)
- Destinations within Taipei City

***

## Option 3: Bus + Shared Scooter (Budget Combo)

### Highlights

- ✅ **Super budget-friendly**: Total cost only USD$5–10
- ✅ 24-hour bus service with frequent departures
- ✅ Flexible for the last mile home

### Steps

#### Step 1: Take the 24-hour bus

- **Kuo-Kuang Bus Route 1819**: Taoyuan Airport → Taipei Bus Station (30–40 min, USD$1–2)
- **Capital Bus Route 1882**: Taoyuan Airport → Taipei Bus Station (40–50 min, USD$1–2)

#### Step 2: Transfer to a shared scooter to get home

After getting off, use the app to find nearby shared scooters:

| Brand | Night Rate | Parking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GoShare** | USD$0.16/10 min | All Taipei |
| **WeMo** | USD$0.13–0.16/10 min | Taipei, New Taipei |
| **Ubike** | USD$0.32–0.48/monthly pass | Dense coverage in Taipei & New Taipei |

### Suitable For

- ✅ Has a driver's license and can ride a scooter
- ✅ Budget-conscious backpackers
- ✅ Not afraid of hassle, wants to experience local transportation
- ✅ Home is within 10 km

### Important Notes

⚠️ Not applicable if you don't have a license or can't ride a scooter
⚠️ Too much luggage makes riding a scooter difficult
⚠️ Be cautious of road conditions and safety when riding home late at night

## Option 4: Shared Car Rental (Exclusive Lazy Tool)

### Highlights

- ✅ Land at 4:00 AM and rent a car to drive home immediately
- ✅ Weekday rates as low as $2.5–$4 per hour
- ✅ Free parking (24-hour parking at Dudu Parking Lot)

### URiDE Shared Car Rental Plan

#### Pricing

| Time Slot | Vehicle Type | Hourly Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Weekday** | Small Car (Kia Picanto) | $3/hour |
| **Weekday** | Mid-to-Large (Focus) | $4/hour |
| **Weekend** | Small Car | $5/hour |
| **Weekend** | Mid-to-Large | $6/hour |

#### The Most Budget-Friendly Strategy

**"Flexi Rent & Return" Package**:

- Book within the first 6 hours (landing at 4:00 AM, must book by 10:00 AM)
- Rent from the airport's Didi Car Rental
- Drive home and return the car at any drop-off point
- Weekday one-way for 4 hours = $3 × 4 = **$12 total** (a steal!)

#### How to Sign Up

```plain
1. 下載 URiDE app
2. 註冊會員（需駕照 + 信用卡）
3. 預約「恣意租還 - 桃園機場站」
4. 持台灣駕照 + 護照領車
5. 開回家
```

### Why All the Secrecy Among Taiwanese Netizens?

> "It's great, but on weekends there are no cars left to rent..." "It used to be cheap on weekends too, but this year the weekend prices have doubled!"

This means:

- ✅ Weekdays are really a great deal
- ⚠️ Prices tend to spike on holidays
- ⚠️ You might not be able to rent a car on holidays

### Who It's For

- ✅ Have a Taiwan driver's license
- ✅ Have plenty of time (can wait for booking confirmation)
- ✅ Returning to Taiwan on a weekday (holiday prices are risky)
- ✅ Want to enjoy Taiwan's scenery while driving

### Important Notes

⚠️ Need a driver's license + credit card
⚠️ Can only book 6 hours in advance (may not work for last-minute flights)
⚠️ No guarantee of car availability on holidays
⚠️ Rental prices may surge (holiday rates have doubled before)

## Prevention Beats Reaction! 3 Reminders Before Booking Your Flight

### 1. Choose a Reasonable Arrival Time in Taiwan

- Try to book flights arriving in Taiwan between **6:00-9:00 AM** (via Airport MRT or bus).
- Avoid budget airlines with red-eye flights—the ticket may be cheap, but the cost of getting home skyrockets.

### 2. Book Airport Transfer in Advance

- Arrange airport transfer as soon as your flight is confirmed.
- You can snag early-bird discounts (saving around USD$3–6).

### 3. Pre-Register for Shared Mobility Apps

- Download URiDE, GoShare, and WeMo in Taiwan before your trip.
- Link your driver’s license and credit card—don’t wait until you land to set them up.

## FAQ

### Q: What if my flight is delayed from 2:00 PM to 11:30 PM—can I still catch the bus?

**A**: The bus runs until 11:30 PM. If you can clear customs by 11:00 PM, you might just make the last one. It’s wise to also book a backup taxi.

### Q: I have tons of luggage—what’s the best way to get around?

**A**: Skip the scooter-sharing option. If you're on a budget, take the bus + transfer to a friend or family member's scooter; if you have the budget, go for airport transfer directly.

### Q: Is it safe to ride a scooter home late at night?

**A**: Taiwan's late-night public safety is decent, but fatigue driving is a risk. If your flight is long and you're exhausted, it's best to take a taxi.

### Q: Is URiDE really unavailable on weekends?

**A**: On weekends, there are more people and fewer scooters, so it's best to book 6 hours in advance (as soon as the date opens). Or choose another option just in case.

### Q: What if I don't have a driver's license when returning to Taiwan?

**A**: Skip options 3 and 4. Choosing option 1 (airport transfer) or option 2 (taxi) is the safest bet.
