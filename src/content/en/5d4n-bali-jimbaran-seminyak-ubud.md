## Day 3

We left Jimbaran and headed to Seminyak, a lively area brimming with trendy vibes. We didn't pack the day with too many sights, instead focusing on food, shopping, and leisurely strolls to slowly soak up Seminyak's unique charm, so different from other parts of the island.

For lunch, we stopped at **Kilo Kitchen**, a renowned fusion restaurant that artfully blends European, Latin American, and Asian culinary elements. Every dish has its own distinct character.

Our favorite was the **Crab Dumplings**—thin, delicate wrappers filled with a sweet, bouncy shrimp paste-like filling that's incredibly satisfying; it's practically a must-order. The **Beef Tongue Tacos** were also memorable, with the tongue braised until tender and paired with corn tortillas for a rich, layered bite. The **Squid Ink Risotto** was packed with intense seafood flavor, while the **Rendang Beef Pasta** cleverly combined Indonesia's classic rendang spices with Italian pasta, surprisingly working beautifully together. If you enjoy trying different flavor profiles, I think Kilo Kitchen is worth a meal.

After eating, we headed to **Bintang Supermarket** to pick up souvenirs. It's a shopping spot often recommended by independent travelers, with a very comprehensive selection—from snacks, coffee, and seasonings to everyday items, you can find almost everything.

This time, we grabbed **Woca chocolate**, Indonesian beer, **Marubali coconut chips**, civet coffee, and Indonesian instant noodles, among other popular items. After browsing, our main takeaway was that if you're short on time, you can buy most of your souvenirs in one go here without needing to visit multiple places—it's super convenient for trip planning.

Next, we continued to **Seminyak Village** for some shopping. While it's not a huge mall, the atmosphere is comfortable and the brands have a stylish feel, making it perfect for a relaxed afternoon.

At **Sensatia Botanicals**, we bought facial scrub and cleanser. Their natural plant-based skincare products are frequently recommended by travelers and are a popular Balinese brand. After that, we browsed a few clothing stores and finally settled into a café to rest, sipping coffee in the air conditioning while sorting through our haul.

If you're not looking for serious retail therapy, I think **1 to 1.5 hours** at Seminyak Village is just right—enough time to leisurely browse the shops, enjoy a coffee, and soak up Seminyak's relaxed yet chic resort atmosphere.

After exploring Seminyak, it was time for one of the most anticipated parts of the day—checking into our second hotel!

This time we stayed at **Berry Amour Romantic Villas**, with a nightly rate of around USD$230. This property is all about a romantic getaway vibe, perfect for couples, newlyweds, or honeymooners.

During check-in, the hotel arranged a small, ceremonial welcome activity. The staff had us put bracelets on each other and even provided crowns for photos. It felt a bit shy-making, but it added a special touch to the experience.

Once the formalities were done, a staff member drove us to our villa in a golf cart. From the moment we stepped inside, it felt like a secluded retreat away from the outside world.

The villa came with a private pool, a small kitchen, and a soaking tub nearby for relaxation. What we loved most was that the bathroom was indoors—unlike the previous villa's semi-outdoor setup—so we didn't have to worry about wind, rain, or bugs while showering. It was much more comfortable overall.

If we had to choose between the two villas we stayed at on this trip, we personally preferred **Berry Amour Romantic Villas**. The layout, comfort, and romantic atmosphere suited our tastes better.

## Day 4

On the fourth day, we arranged a full-day private car service to explore different spots in central Bali. But before setting off, we couldn't miss the floating breakfast offered by the villa!

Although the breakfast at this villa wasn't quite as much to our taste as the previous one, since we were staying there, we still highly recommend trying the floating breakfast at least once. Placing the tray on the pool, enjoying breakfast while snapping photos—it really adds a ceremonial touch to the vacation. However, purely in terms of the food, we actually liked the congee the most; its flavor was surprisingly good and really boosted the overall breakfast experience.

After breakfast, we officially kicked off today's private car itinerary.

Our first stop was **Sangeh Monkey Forest**.

When it comes to Bali's monkey forests, most travelers immediately think of the Sacred Monkey Forest in Ubud. But this time, we specifically chose Sangeh Monkey Forest. Its biggest advantage is that there are far fewer tourists, making the visit less crowded and allowing for a more relaxed interaction with the monkeys.

As soon as we entered the park, a local guide accompanied us, handing out a bag of peanuts and leading us deep into the forest. He didn't just show the way—he also taught us how to properly feed the monkeys, explained their habits, and shared many small details to keep in mind when interacting with them.

The most fun part was that the guide would occasionally make specific sounds to attract the monkeys. Before long, the monkeys would jump onto our shoulders, arms, or nearby trees, giving us plenty of opportunities for interaction and photos. The whole experience felt very natural, and you could tell the guide was quite familiar with this group of monkeys.

![第四天](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1782548550073-mixcollage-27-jun-2026-03-16-pm-9129.jpg)

We really loved this activity. The whole experience lasted about an hour—not too long, but not rushed either. If you want to avoid the crowds at the Ubud Monkey Forest, this is definitely worth considering.

The entrance fee was about **USD$10**, and we also bought a souvenir photo taken at the park, plus a tip for the guide, which came to around **USD$6** more. For the overall experience, we felt it was well worth it and one of the most memorable spots on this trip.

After leaving **Sangeh Monkey Forest**, we continued on to one of the most popular areas on this journey—Ubud.

For lunch, we stopped at **Sun Sun Warung**, a budget-friendly Indonesian restaurant recommended by many travelers. The setting is simple, but the food is excellent and very affordable.

We ordered their signature **Dirty Duck Rice** and **Mie Goreng (Indonesian fried noodles)**—both were satisfying. The dirty duck had a crispy skin while the meat stayed tender, and it paired beautifully with Indonesian chili sauce for extra depth. The Mie Goreng was packed with rich Southeast Asian aromas—savory and flavorful without being greasy. The total for two people came to about **USD$16**, which is great value for Ubud. If you love authentic Indonesian cuisine, we highly recommend giving it a try.

After eating, we strolled through Ubud's lively streets, wandering past the famous **Ubud Royal Palace**. The main road is lined with unique shops, artisan boutiques, and cafés, giving the area a wonderfully relaxing vacation vibe.

However, the weather that day was scorching hot, so we soon decided to find a place to cool down.

We stopped by **Tukies Coconut Shop**, a well-known coconut dessert spot. Though there's no air conditioning, they offer a variety of coconut-based drinks and sweets—perfect for a refreshing break on a hot afternoon.

This time we ordered **coconut ice, coconut lemonade, and an American coffee**. The icy coconut drinks were incredibly refreshing and instantly chased away the heat. If you're sweating your way through Ubud, we highly recommend popping in for a drink and a rest.

After resting, we discovered another shop selling **Kopi Luwak (civet coffee)**. What made it special was that we could actually interact with the real civet cats up close.

![第四天](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1782548311880-luwak.jpg)

The civet cats in the shop were much larger than I expected, weighing about **10 kilograms**. The staff even let us hold one for photos—it was quite a unique experience.

Of course, since we were there, we also ordered a cup of civet coffee to try. It was incredibly smooth, with a mild bitterness, and overall very pleasant to drink. It felt like checking off one of those must-do experiences in Bali.

After walking around for a while, our feet started protesting, so we headed to the nearby **Ubud Traditional Spa** to unwind.

This time, we chose the **Rice Farmer Massage** package, which includes a **60-minute Rice Farmer Massage** plus a **30-minute foot massage**, priced at about **USD$26** per person.

![第四天](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1782548370433-spa.jpg)

The massage therapist's pressure wasn't as precise as the SPA we tried a few days earlier, but the overall service, ambiance, and price were all quite good. For Ubud's city center, the value for money was impressive. If you're planning a full day of shopping and sightseeing in Ubud, ending with a relaxing massage before heading back to the hotel makes for a very comfortable itinerary.

After wrapping up our time in Ubud, we returned to Seminyak, ready to cap off the fulfilling day with a great meal.

For dinner, we chose **Pison**, a popular Western-style restaurant with a diverse menu and live band performances in the evening. The atmosphere was very relaxed, making it a perfect spot to enjoy a leisurely dinner with friends or a partner.

As soon as we sat down, we ordered the shop's signature drink, **Avocado Espresso**. I was a bit skeptical about the combination of avocado and espresso at first, but one sip was truly surprising—the creamy smoothness of the avocado paired unexpectedly well with the coffee's aroma. I highly recommend giving it a try if you get the chance.

For our main courses, we ordered the **Steak with Truffle Mushroom Sauce** and the **Truffle Carbonara Pasta**. Both were of excellent quality, with a rich truffle aroma that wasn't overpowering, and the flavors had great depth. Paired with the live band performance, it made for an especially relaxing dinner and became one of the most memorable meals of the trip.

## Day 5

Before we knew it, we had arrived at the last day of this Bali trip.

We didn't plan much for the day. After checking out, we asked our driver **Benny** to take us to a local eatery he recommended: **Warung Babi Guling Pak Malen**.

This is a famous roast suckling pig restaurant, and around noon it was nearly full, clearly popular with both locals and tourists.

We ordered a plate of their signature roast suckling pig rice. At first glance, it reminded me of Taiwan's Chiayi turkey rice—simple, with a plate of rice topped with sliced meat and sides. It cost less than **USD$3**, which was really affordable.

However, the meat we got was a bit cool, so the texture wasn't as good as expected, and overall it wasn't particularly impressive. Of course, everyone's experience is different, so if you're nearby, it's still worth checking out this famous local spot.

***

On the way to the airport, Benny specially brought out a traditional Indonesian liquor he highly recommended called **Arak** for us to try.

Seeing it in a plastic bottle, my first reaction was a bit skeptical about its origins (laughs), but Benny said it's a common traditional liquor brewed by locals.

Arak is traditionally made by fermenting and distilling coconut blossom sap and other ingredients. It has a subtle rice and alcohol aroma—personally, I find it a bit like Taiwan's millet wine. It goes down smoothly without being too harsh, surprisingly quite enjoyable, and it's another way to experience Bali's local culture.

***

This **five-day, four-night Bali free-and-easy trip** truly left us with many wonderful memories.

Although we visited during the **December rainy season**, we only encountered heavy rain for about one day, which didn't affect our itinerary much. We didn't cram our schedule full either, leaving plenty of time to relax, making the trip feel even more comfortable.

Looking back, we only explored a small part of Bali in those five days, yet it was enough to make us fall deeply in love with this island.

The food here is so delicious you'll crave it again, the SPA massages remain a great value, and the Monkey Forest and civet cat interactions were both fascinating. The two villas we stayed in each had their own charm and are highly recommended.
