If you've ever been to Indonesia, or wandered through convenience stores in Bali or cafes in Canggu where digital nomads and backpackers hang out, you'll notice a quirky sight: many Westerners always have a few little yellow soft-pack syrups tucked into their pockets or bags at checkout.

Newcomers to Indonesia might mistake them for drink syrup packets or concentrated juice, but for expats and savvy travelers, these are far more popular than Western medicine—a go-to natural herbal cold remedy!

### The 'Masuk Angin' Experience That Even Westerners Love

What is masuk angin? Literally translated, it means 'wind entering the body.' After getting caught in the rain, sitting too long in air conditioning, staying up late, or enduring a long drive, you might feel sluggish, bloated, dizzy, or chilled. Indonesians would say, 'I've got masuk angin.'

It's not a formal medical diagnosis, but a very colloquial way of saying you've caught a chill or feel under the weather. These herbal syrups are the go-to daily remedy for Indonesians.

Westerners typically reach for Panadol or Ibuprofen when they have a headache or cold, but in Indonesia, they're won over by the local concept of 'expelling dampness and cold from the body.'

Many foreign tourists are skeptical at first, but after trying it once, they're completely hooked—even stocking up on these packets as souvenirs before heading home!

## Two 'Little Yellow Packet' Herbal Remedies That Foreigners Rave About in Indonesia

Stepping into an Indonesian convenience store (Indomaret or Alfamart), the shelves are packed with options, but only two leading brands are truly raved about by the Western crowd:

### **1. Tolak Angin (Sido Muncul)**

- One of Indonesia's most iconic brands, with a recipe passed down since the 1930s.
- Key ingredients include ginger, peppermint, fennel, clove leaves, honey, and other herbs.
- Taste profile: A distinct cooling sensation from the peppermint—when you tear open the packet and drink it, a refreshing, aromatic coolness rises through your nose.
- Best for: Long journeys, lack of sleep, or when you feel bloated.

### **2. Antangin JRG (Deltomed)**

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcLnEfgodQ-/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==

- JRG stands for Jahe (ginger), Royal jelly, and Ginseng.
- The ginger flavor is quite prominent, with a slight sweetness, and it feels warm when you drink it.
- Best for: After a day in air conditioning, surfing, or outdoor activities when you feel a bit chilly and want something warm to sip on.

### Want to sleep better? There's a sleep-friendly version too

The Antangin line also includes a **Good Night** variant, which adds passion flower and valerian to the formula, aiming to "relieve masuk angin" while promoting sleep. It comes in both tablet and liquid sachet forms.

Since it contains ingredients that help you relax and feel drowsy, it's best taken before bed—avoid it during the day if you plan to ride a bike or drive.

## How to drink the little yellow pack?

The simplest way is to tear it open and drink it straight. Some people dilute it with warm water, making it taste like a light ginger tea—this is a personal preference, not an official method, so feel free to adjust to your taste.

## A few tips before buying

- **It's a traditional herbal product** designed to help ease discomfort from masuk angin and maintain energy levels—not a cold medicine, and it shouldn't replace seeing a doctor.
- **Pregnant and breastfeeding women** are generally advised not to consume it; check the packaging instructions before purchasing.
- **People taking medication** (e.g., anticoagulants, blood pressure medication, diabetes drugs, anti-inflammatory painkillers) should consult a doctor or pharmacist first, as ingredients like ginger and ginseng may interact.
- **Tolak Angin comes in both sugary and sugar-free versions**—those watching their sugar intake should check the packaging label.
- **Good Night** combined with alcohol or sedatives may cause dizziness and drowsiness, so avoid it during your trip.

- **Instant Relief:** Whether you've been caught in the rain, overexposed to air conditioning, or have an upset stomach, you'll feel your stomach warm up and your breathing ease within minutes.
