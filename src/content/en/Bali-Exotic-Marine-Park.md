What's the biggest fear when traveling with kids? It's that 'see but can't touch' distance. At many attractions, kids just sit obediently in the audience, watching animal shows from afar, clapping, and that's it. Honestly, **watching a dolphin show from the audience is nothing special—letting kids interact with dolphins is the real rarity**—and Bali Exotic Marine Park is exactly the kind of place where children truly 'step into the story.'

## Where Is the Dolphin Park?

The park is located between Kuta and Nusa Dua, about a 15–20 minute drive from the city center. If you're staying in southern Bali, it's perfect for a half-day family outing. It's about a 15-minute drive from Bali's airport, making transportation quite convenient.

Many marine parks boast dolphin shows, but kids sit in the audience, possibly more than twenty meters away from the dolphins. Here, **children are no longer spectators—they're participants.**

The park offers a variety of official interactive experiences, allowing parents to choose based on their child's age, swimming ability, and courage:

- **Explorer – Easy Shore Interaction**
  Ideal for toddlers or kids new to dolphins. On the shore, they can kiss, touch, and take photos with dolphins for about 2 minutes. Though brief, the thrill of that close encounter is enough to keep them talking for a whole year.
- **Encounter – Deep Interaction at the Shore**
  A roughly 15-minute shoreline interaction that includes commentary, touching, kissing, and simple fish feeding. Perfect for young children who aren't ready to get in the water (ages 1 and up), with parents able to stay close by for peace of mind.
- **Discovery – Swim Together in the Water**
  Suitable for children and adults with basic swimming skills. Step into the pool to hug and interact with dolphins, and feel them gently nudging you as you move through the water—**this isn't riding, but a natural interaction where the dolphin guides you as you swim**. Lasting about 40 minutes, it's the park's most popular family experience.
- **Discovery+ – Private Upgrade**
  A private version of the previous experience, for up to 6 people. Ideal for families who want to avoid crowds and enjoy quality time together.

> 💡 **Special Note**: Some online articles mention "dolphin back riding," but this is not a permitted activity at the park. For animal welfare and safety, riding is strictly prohibited. The actual experience involves being gently nudged forward by the dolphin under a trainer's guidance—this is actually better for children, as it's both safe and allows them to feel the dolphin's intelligence and gentleness.

Each session is guided by a trainer throughout, ensuring safety while explaining dolphin habits, communication methods, and why we need to protect the ocean in a way kids can understand. **Watching your child go from nervous to excited, and from excited to gently stroking a dolphin—that moment of eye contact is more precious than any souvenir.**

## For Parents, It's More Than Just "Fun"

![對家長來說，這不只是「好玩」](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-05/1779966789235-597572246_1320717816737228_5729102268253149587_n.jpg)

Many parents ask: "Is it worth the money?" Honestly, the ticket price isn't cheap, but what you're buying isn't just a few minutes of interaction—it's:

- **Courage to Overcome Fears**: Some children are initially afraid of water or large animals, but with the guidance of trainers, they often step out of their comfort zones.
- **Cultivating Empathy**: Through hands-on interaction, children begin to wonder, 'Are the dolphins happy?' and 'How can we protect them?'—lessons no textbook can teach.
- **Family Memories Together**: When parents and children dive in, gasp, and laugh together, the intimacy of shared experience is far more profound than 'kids performing on stage while parents scroll on their phones.'

## What Else Does the Park Offer? No Boredom Even Without Getting in the Water

![園區還有什麼？沒下水也不無聊](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-05/1779966825794-472863975_2560314820966675_263615945628872436_n.jpg)

If you have little ones who aren't ready to get in the water yet, or want to enrich your itinerary, the park also features:
  **Shark and Stingray Touch Pool**
Trained sharks and stingrays are quite gentle, allowing children to gently touch their smooth skin and observe how they feed.
  **Sea Turtle and Tropical Fish Displays**
Large aquariums filled with colorful fish and leisurely sea turtles are perfect for young children to quietly admire.
  **Marine Education Mini-Lectures**
Held regularly, these sessions use stories, pictures, and simple Q&A to introduce children to marine ecosystems. They're engaging rather than dull.

## Tips for Parents (Honest Advice from Those Who’ve Been There)

✅ **Book in Advance**
Especially during peak season and weekends, walk-in queues are not only long but may also sell out. Online bookings sometimes come with discounts and allow you to confirm age and height restrictions beforehand.

✅ **Bring a Change of Clothes**
Kids will likely get completely wet after getting in the water. Towels, spare clothes, and waterproof bags are essential. Don’t forget sunscreen—the sun in Bali is no joke.

✅ **Wait After Meals Before Going in the Water**
It’s best to rest for at least an hour after eating. The park has dining options, but choices are limited and prices are on the high side. You can bring simple snacks for the kids to tide them over.

✅ **Photo Strategy**
Trainers are happy to help take photos, and the park also has official photographers, but those pictures cost extra. I highly recommend bringing your own waterproof camera or phone pouch so you can freely capture every surprised expression on your child’s face.

✅ **Age and Ability Restrictions**
Children aged 1 and above can join shore-based interactions, but water-based programs require basic swimming skills. Be sure to ask clearly when booking to avoid disappointment on the day.
