# October in Bali: The Sweet Spot at the End of the Dry Season 💕 Great Weather, Half the Price

October is Bali’s most underrated month.

Most Taiwanese travelers flock to July and August, but September and October are truly hidden gems. The weather still carries the dry season’s crispness, crowds drop significantly, and hotel prices start to become a steal. Best of all, October offers unique experiences you can only enjoy this month—Ubud’s artistic vibe, reflections in the rice paddies, and the last waves of the season.

Let me share my perspective on whether October is worth visiting.

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1787720154304-mixcollage-26-aug-2026-12-54-pm-5475.jpg)

***

## Weather: The Dry Season Lingers

October temperatures hover between 24–31°C, so shorts and T-shirts are all you need. But the real story isn’t the temperature—it’s the transition.

In a 'normal' year, October looks like this: the first half is classic dry season, but by the latter part, brief afternoon showers start popping up in the Ubud highlands, and by month’s end, the green hues of the rainy season quietly sweep across the island. **However, 2026 is different**—due to the El Niño phenomenon, Indonesia’s Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) has officially stated that **this year’s rainy season will be delayed beyond its usual timing**. Simply put, significant rainfall may not even begin by late October, and the entire month could be drier than average.

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1780923472183-9.jpg)

This is actually great news for October travel.

Here’s my advice: if you care about the weather, October is almost risk-free—the first two weeks are definitely crisp in the morning and blazing hot in the afternoon. Even toward the end of the month, since the rainy season is delayed, the weather will be more stable than in previous years. If you’re after low prices, late October is the perfect time—cheap, good weather, and fewer crowds. My own routine is to schedule outdoor activities in the morning (temples, rice terraces, surfing), then head into a café or art gallery after 3 PM to dodge the hottest hours.

According to Indonesia’s meteorological agency, October will see fewer rainy days than the historical average. This is a rare and favorable condition in recent years.

## October-Only Experiences: Don’t Miss These

October is a relatively festival-packed month in Bali. In just 31 days, you can experience literary events, traditional rituals, local cultural festivals, and even a water sports carnival—each reflecting a different side of Bali.

### Ubud Writers & Readers Festival (UWRF) — October 21 to 25

The Ubud Writers & Readers Festival is Southeast Asia’s biggest literary event. This year’s theme is **Samarasā: Awareness, Empathy, Action**, drawn from the Sanskrit concept of harmony.

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DQiUb6fktLu/?utm_source=ig_web_button_share_sheet

Over five days, writers, poets, and journalists from Indonesia and around the world gather at various venues in Ubud for talks, readings, and book launches. If you love conversation, deep thinking, and being struck by new ideas, this atmosphere is unmatched. Ubud is quiet all month, but this week suddenly comes alive, with cafés full of people clutching notebooks.

The best time in Ubud isn't the 'photo spot' listed in guides—it's when intellectuals gather like this. I buy the four-day pass, filling up all the free sessions, and I also recommend checking out the book fair area and nighttime readings—those are the warmest corners of the literary festival.

Official website: [www.ubudwritersfestival.com](https://www.ubudwritersfestival.com/)

**Practical tip**: Hotels in Ubud fill up quickly this week, so book early. If you just want to soak up the vibe, the free public events and book launches are also worth attending.

### Saraswati Day — October 31, Bali's 'Artsy Youth Festival'

On the last day of October, Bali celebrates **Saraswati**—the Hindu goddess of knowledge, learning, art, and wisdom. Across the island, temples and households set out offerings to honor scriptures, books, culture, and knowledge.

https://youtu.be/hYkf6sjCtqg?si=YAIHC4gPI_LRhmti

The festival's atmosphere is especially sacred and fascinating. Here's what you'll see:

- **Temples hold early morning ceremonies**—if you're interested, dress modestly and you can observe (no need to be a devotee)
- **Bookstores, schools, and art studios set out offerings**—especially in Ubud, where galleries, museums, and book cafes host special celebrations
- **Young people bring books and artworks to temples for blessings**—Bali buzzes with energy that day, with young folks moving about everywhere

What I love most about Saraswati is that it perfectly reflects Bali's deep respect for knowledge and culture—it's not a superficial tourist festival but a genuine spiritual practice. Visitors arriving in late October can witness the most "authentic Balinese faith."

**Travel Tip**: On this day, rent a scooter or hire a car and visit several temples in the morning to observe the rituals. In Ubud, Goa Gajah (Elephant Cave) and Pura Taman Saraswati (Saraswati Temple) hold special ceremonies. Since many tourists are unaware of this festival, crowds are thin—making it the perfect time to soak in the true spirit of traditional Bali.

### Mola Mola (Ocean Sunfish) Diving – Last Chance in October

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdGYoQ2yf7o/?utm_source=ig_web_button_share_sheet

October is the final month to dive with Mola Mola off Nusa Penida. These oddly shaped giants follow cold-water upwellings to shallow reef habitats for cleaning, diving to depths of about 30 meters—requiring an advanced diving certification. The cold currents are most active in early October; after November, warmer waters drive the Mola Mola back to the deep sea.

I've dived for them a few times myself, and honestly, spotting a Mola Mola is largely a matter of luck—it's never guaranteed. But October mornings usually offer the calmest sea conditions, making it the most stable period of the season. The key is to choose a reputable dive shop that will honestly tell you about visibility and sighting rates.

**My observation**: Many divers come specifically for the Mola Mola, but Nusa Penida is a diving paradise all month long. If you don't see one, don't be disappointed—the coral landscapes, fish schools, and sea turtles are more than worth the trip.

### Nusa Dua Fiesta

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789105783068-nusa_dua_festival_2026.webp)

For one week in October, the Nusa Dua Fiesta—a free cultural festival—opens up this usually resort-exclusive beach community to the public with music, dance, craft displays, and water sports.

Nusa Dua has always been a playground for high-end travelers, and backpackers often give it a miss. But during this week, the beach—often mistaken as "only for the wealthy"—turns into the most welcoming and lively spot. I visited two years ago, watching traditional dance performances on the sand while a nearby vendor grilled corn; the vibe was unexpectedly and authentically Balinese.

## October's Rice Terraces and East Bali: The Real Stars

From mid-October, farmers begin flooding their rice paddies in preparation for the next planting cycle. The terraces fill with water, turning into mirrors—truly, so clear they reflect the sky and coconut trees.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789107334589-3cd14b141237785e48dac56288dbf7d4.jpg)

In the east, at Pura Lempuyang. This temple features the famous 'Gates of Heaven' photo spot, usually packed with long queues, but in October the crowds drop significantly. My tip: arrive at 6:30 AM—the queue is nearly zero at that time, giving you more room to take beautiful photos at a relaxed pace. After the gate, if you keep climbing, you'll reach the higher sacred temples—spots that most Instagram tourists rarely venture to, but the scenery and tranquility are absolutely worth it.

## Surfing: The Season of Transition

October marks a seasonal shift for surfing. On the west coast, Uluwatu and Padang Padang still offer reliable waves this month, though they begin to fade. Meanwhile, the east and southeast coasts start to awaken—Sanur's beach breaks become more consistent, and Keramas on the east coast begins to show its power.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789107617297-ziggy-mackenzie-9.jpg)

If you're a skilled surfer, October is actually a rare month where you can experience wave conditions on both the north and south coasts in a single trip.

## Accommodation: October Is the Time to Bargain

A guesthouse typically costs around IDR 200,000 to 350,000 per night. But here's a little secret: from late October onward, hotels start offering discounts. With fewer travelers arriving in mid-October, operators begin luring bookings with deals. So if you're flexible, booking a late-October room in the middle of the month could snag you a discount or even a free upgrade.

Here's how I'd split it:

- **For festivals and cultural vibes**: Stay in Ubud during the first three weeks of October. The afternoon rains that start in mid-month have the least impact on the mountainous area, as they come and go quickly.
- **For the last of the dry season weather and cheap rates**: Head to the coast after mid-October. The bays in Nusa Dua and Sanur are more open, so rain doesn't accumulate there as much.

If you're thinking of trying a villa, October is a rare sweet spot. Usually, villas only get attention during peak season (July and above), but in October, villa operators tend to be more attentive and sometimes even throw in little surprises.

## Quick Practical Info

### Booking Strategy for Festival Months

October has several festivals of varying sizes that affect room rates in different areas. **During UWRF week (Oct 21–25), hotels in Ubud will be fully booked**, so book early. Other weeks, especially toward the end of the month, are actually cheaper. If you're not set on attending a festival, booking in late October can save you 20–30%.

### Visa and Required Fees

You'll need to pay an IDR 150,000 tourism tax to enter the island, which can be paid online or at the airport. The electronic visa on arrival (eVOA) can also be arranged in advance.

### Getting Around

Traffic in October is smooth. Renting a scooter (remember to bring an international driver's license and helmet—police checks are strict in 2026) costs IDR 70,000–100,000 per day. For an easier option, use Grab or Gojek to hail a ride, or hire a car with a driver for IDR 600,000–800,000 for a full day.

### What to Pack

- Sunscreen (high SPF — the October sun is still intense)
- Light rain jacket or small umbrella (not all-day rain, but be prepared for afternoon showers)
- Tops (quick-dry clothing will come in handy)
- **Sarong and sash for temple visits** (available for rent at every temple, but bringing your own is more convenient — October is a festival month, so you'll likely be entering temples often)
- Refillable water bottle (Bali has banned small plastic bottles; hotels and cafés will refill yours)
- **Lightweight, modest attire** (if you plan to attend the Ubud Writers & Readers Festival or watch temple ceremonies, comfortable yet respectful clothing is ideal)

### Safety and Health

**2026 Weather Anomaly Alert**: Due to the El Niño phenomenon, Indonesia's dry season has been notably extended this year, with the rainy season also delayed. The upside for October travel is less rainfall than usual, but it also means higher temperatures and potentially more noticeable dust and humidity in the air. If you're sensitive to air quality, bringing a dust mask is a good idea.

Mosquitoes start to increase in October (as the rainy season approaches), so be sure to apply insect repellent in the evening. Don't drink tap water—stick to bottled water or filtered water from your hotel. Regular pharmacies stock common medications, but bring your own important prescription drugs.

## Who Is October Best For?

Honestly, October is best for three types of travelers:

1. **Budget-conscious backpackers**—cheap, decent weather, and not too crowded
2. **Art and culture lovers**—the atmosphere at the Ubud Writers & Readers Festival is one of a kind
3. **Diving and surfing enthusiasts**—the tail end of mola-mola season and a transitional period for waves, offering a nuanced experience only true enthusiasts appreciate
4. **Families wanting to escape the crowds**—October offers a rare balance of feeling safe without being overwhelmed by tourists

**What makes 2026 special**: This year, due to El Niño, October's weather will be more stable and drier than usual. Indonesia's meteorological agency has confirmed the rainy season will be delayed, making October a virtually risk-free month for weather-sensitive travelers—you can confidently plan outdoor activities without worrying about sudden rain disrupting your plans.

October isn't the 'best' month, because 'best' doesn't really exist. But in 2026, October might be the 'smartest choice in years'—you spend the least money while experiencing the most authentic side of Bali, plus unusually stable weather.

## Final Thoughts

October in Bali feels like the island taking a deep breath. The crowds of July and August have faded, and the rainy season of November hasn't fully arrived yet. For this one month, the island gains a sense of peace.

You can claim a whole table at a café without waiting. The narrow lanes of Canggu are no longer a sea of people, and street vendors have time to chat with you. Prices do drop, but the quality of service doesn't follow suit—that's the October I love most.

If you've been waiting for an answer to the question, 'When is the best time to visit Bali?'—October is it!
