# October in Bali: The Sweet Spot at the End of the Dry Season 💕 Great Weather, Half the Price

October is Bali's most underrated month.

Most Taiwanese tourists flock to July and August, but September and October are truly hidden gems. The weather still carries the dry season's crispness, crowds drop significantly, and hotel prices start to become a steal. Best of all, October offers unique experiences you can only enjoy this month—Ubud's artistic vibe, reflections in the rice paddies, and the last waves of the season.

Let me share my perspective on whether October is worth visiting.

## Weather: The Dry Season Lingers

October temperatures stay between 24–31°C, so shorts and a T-shirt are all you need. But the real story isn't the temperature—it's the transition.

In a 'normal' year, October looks like this: the first half is classic dry season, but after mid-month, brief afternoon showers start popping up in the Ubud highlands. By month's end, the green hints of the rainy season have quietly swept across the island. **However, 2026 is different**—due to the El Niño phenomenon, Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) has publicly stated that **this year's rainy season will be delayed beyond its usual start**. Simply put, significant rainfall may not even begin by the end of October, and the entire month could be drier than average.

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1780923472183-9.jpg)

For October travel, this is actually fantastic news.

Here's my advice: If you care a lot about the weather, October is almost risk-free—the first two weeks are absolutely crisp in the morning with blazing afternoon sun. Even toward the end of the month, thanks to the delayed rainy season, the weather will be more stable than in previous years. If you're after low prices, late October is practically a golden window—cheap + great weather + fewer crowds. My personal routine is to schedule outdoor activities (temples, rice terraces, surfing) in the morning, then duck into a café or art gallery after 3 PM to escape the hottest hours.

According to Indonesia's meteorological agency, the number of rainy days in October will be lower than the historical average. This is a rare favorable condition not seen in recent years.

## October-Only Experiences: Don't Miss These

October is a relatively festival-packed month in Bali. In just 31 days, you can experience literary events, traditional rituals, local cultural celebrations, and even a water sports carnival—each showcasing a different facet of Bali.

### Ubud Writers & Readers Festival (UWRF) — October 21 to 25

The Ubud Writers & Readers Festival is Southeast Asia's biggest literary event. This year's theme is **Samarasā: Awareness, Empathy, Action**, derived from the Sanskrit concept of 'harmony.'

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DQiUb6fktLu/?utm_source=ig_web_button_share_sheet

Over five days, writers, poets, and journalists from Indonesia and around the world gather at various venues across Ubud for talks, readings, and book launches. If you love conversation, reflection, and being struck by new ideas, this atmosphere is unmatched. Ubud is quiet all month, but this week suddenly comes alive, with cafés full of people clutching notebooks.

Ubud is at its best not in the 'Instagrammable spots' the guides tout, but in these moments when intellectuals converge. I'd buy a four-day pass, pack in the free sessions, and also recommend checking out the book fair area and nighttime readings—those are the festival's warmest corners.

Official website: [www.ubudwritersfestival.com](https://www.ubudwritersfestival.com/)

**Practical Tip**: Hotels in Ubud will be quite full this week, so book early. If you just want to soak up the atmosphere, free community events and book launches are also worth attending.

### Saraswati Day – October 31, Bali's "Arts & Culture Day"

On the last day of October, Bali celebrates **Saraswati**—the Hindu goddess of knowledge, learning, art, and wisdom. Across the island, temples and households set out offerings to honor scriptures, books, culture, and knowledge.

https://youtu.be/hYkf6sjCtqg?si=YAIHC4gPI_LRhmti

The festival's atmosphere is both sacred and fascinating. Here's what you'll see:

- **Temples hold early morning ceremonies**—if you're interested, dress modestly and you can observe (no need to be a devotee)
- **Bookstores, schools, and art studios display offerings**—especially in Ubud, where galleries, museums, and book cafés host special celebrations
- **Young people bring books and artworks to temples for blessings**—Bali comes alive on this day, with youth moving about everywhere

What I love about Saraswati is how it truly reflects Bali's reverence for knowledge and culture—it's not a touristy spectacle but a genuine spiritual practice. Visitors at the end of October get to see the most "authentic Balinese faith."

**Travel Advice**: Rent a scooter or hire a driver for the day and visit several temples in the morning to witness the rituals. In Ubud, Goa Gajah (Elephant Cave) and Pura Taman Saraswati (Saraswati Temple) hold special ceremonies. Most tourists don't know about this festival, so crowds are thin—making it the perfect time to experience traditional Balinese spirit.

### Mola Mola Diving – The Last Chance in October

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdGYoQ2yf7o/?utm_source=ig_web_button_share_sheet

October is the final month to spot Mola Mola (ocean sunfish) while diving around Nusa Penida. These oddly shaped giants follow cold-water upwellings to shallow reef habitats for cleaning, diving to depths of about 30 meters—requiring an advanced dive certification. The cold currents are most active in early October; after November, warmer waters drive the Mola Mola back into the deep sea.

I've dived a few times myself, and honestly, spotting a Mola Mola is largely a matter of luck—it's not guaranteed. But October mornings usually offer the calmest sea conditions, making it the most stable season overall. The key is to choose a reputable dive shop that will honestly tell you about visibility and sighting rates.

**My observation**: Many divers come specifically for the Mola Mola, but Nusa Penida is a diving paradise all month long. If you don't see one that day, don't be disappointed—the coral landscapes, schools of fish, and sea turtles are equally worth the trip.

### Nusa Dua Fiesta

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789105783068-nusa_dua_festival_2026.webp)

October brings a week-long Nusa Dua Fiesta—a free cultural festival that opens up this usually resort-exclusive beach community to the public with music, dance, craft displays, and water sports.

Nusa Dua has always been a territory for high-end travelers, and backpackers don't usually venture here. But during this week, the beach—often mistaken as 'only for the wealthy'—turns into the most welcoming and lively spot. I visited two years ago, watching traditional dance performances on the sand while a nearby vendor grilled corn; the vibe felt surprisingly 'authentically Balinese.'

## October's Rice Terraces and East Bali: The True Stars

From mid-October, farmers flood the rice paddies to prepare for the next planting season. The terraces begin to fill with water, turning into mirrors—truly clear enough to reflect the sky and coconut trees.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789107334589-3cd14b141237785e48dac56288dbf7d4.jpg)

In the east, at Pura Lempuyang. This temple features the famous 'Gates of Heaven' photo spot, usually packed with long queues, but in October, the crowds noticeably thin out. My advice is to arrive at 6:30 AM, when the queue is nearly zero, giving you more time to take stunning photos at a relaxed pace. After climbing the gates, if you continue upward, you'll reach higher sacred temples—spots that most tourist photo-takers rarely visit, but the scenery and tranquility are absolutely worth it.

## Surfing: The Seasonal Crossover

October is a seasonal crossover for surfing. On the west coast, Uluwatu and Padang Padang still offer reliable waves this month, but they're starting to fade. Meanwhile, waves on the east and southeast coasts begin to awaken—Sanur's beach breaks become consistent, and Keramas on the east coast starts to fire up.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1789107617297-ziggy-mackenzie-9.jpg)

If you're a skilled surfer, October is actually a rare month where you can experience waves from both the north and south coasts in a single trip.

## Accommodation: October Is the Time to Negotiate

Guesthouses cost around IDR 200,000–350,000 per night. But here's a little secret: from late October, hotels start offering discounts. Since fewer tourists arrive after mid-October, operators begin using promotions to attract bookings. So if you're flexible, booking a late-October room in mid-to-late October could land you a discount or even a free upgrade.

Here's how I'd split it:

- **For festivals and cultural vibes**: Stay in Ubud for the first three weeks of October. The afternoon rains that start mid-month have the least impact in the mountains, as they come and go quickly.
- **For the last of the dry season weather and lower prices**: Head to the coast after mid-October. Bays like Nusa Dua and Sanur are more open, so rain doesn't accumulate there.

If you're considering a villa, October is a rare sweet spot. Villas usually only get attention during peak season (July and beyond), but in October, villa operators go the extra mile with hospitality and sometimes throw in little surprises.

## Quick Practical Info

### Booking Strategy During Festival Season

October has several festivals of varying sizes that affect room rates in different areas. **During UWRF week (Oct 21–25), hotels in Ubud sell out**, so book early. Other weeks, especially toward month-end, are actually cheaper. If you're not set on attending festivals, booking in late October can save you 20–30%.

### Visa and Required Fees

You'll need to pay an IDR 150,000 tourism tax upon entering the island, either online or at the airport. You can also purchase an electronic visa on arrival (eVOA) in advance.

### Getting Around

Traffic in October is smooth. Renting a scooter (remember to bring an international driver's license and helmet—police are cracking down hard in 2026) costs IDR 70,000–100,000 per day. An easier option is to use Grab or Gojek for rides, or hire a car with a driver for IDR 600,000–800,000 per day.

### What to Bring

- Sunscreen (high SPF—the October sun is still intense)
- Lightweight rain jacket or small umbrella (not all-day rain, but be prepared for afternoon showers)
- Tops (quick-dry clothing will come in handy)
- **Sarong and sash for temple visits** (available for rent at every temple, but bringing your own is more convenient—October is a festival month, so you'll often need to enter temples)
- Reusable water bottle (Bali has banned small plastic bottles; hotels and cafes will refill yours)
- **Lightweight yet modest attire** (if you plan to attend the Ubud Literary Festival or watch temple ceremonies, comfortable but respectful clothing is more appropriate)

### Safety and Health

**2026 Weather Anomaly Alert**: Due to the El Niño effect, Indonesia's dry season has significantly extended this year, and the rainy season has been delayed. For October travel, this means less rainfall than usual, but also higher temperatures and potentially more noticeable dust and humidity in the air. If you're sensitive to air quality, bringing a dust mask is a good idea.

Mosquitoes start to increase in October (as the rainy season approaches), so apply insect repellent in the evenings. Don't drink tap water; stick to bottled water or filtered water from your hotel. Regular pharmacies stock common medications, but bring your own important prescription drugs.

## Who Is October Best For?

Honestly, October is best for three types of travelers:

1. **Budget-conscious backpackers**—cheap, decent weather, not too crowded
2. **Art and culture lovers**—the atmosphere of the Ubud Literary Festival is unique
3. **Diving and surfing enthusiasts**—the tail end of mola-mola season and a transitional period for waves, a subtle delight only experienced travelers appreciate
4. **Families looking to escape the crowds**—October offers a rare balance of feeling safe without being overcrowded

**What makes 2026 special**: This year, due to the El Niño effect, October will be more stable and drier than usual. Indonesia's meteorological agency has confirmed that the rainy season will be delayed, making October a virtually "risk-free" month for weather-sensitive travelers—you can confidently plan outdoor activities without worrying about sudden rain disrupting your plans.

October isn't the "best" month, because "best" doesn't really exist. But in 2026, October might be the "smartest choice in years"—you spend the least money while experiencing the most authentic side of Bali, plus unusually stable weather.

## Final Thoughts

October in Bali feels to me like the island taking a deep breath. The July and August crowds have faded, and November's rainy season hasn't fully arrived—just this one month where the island gains a sense of calm.

You can claim a table at a café without waiting. Canggu's narrow lanes are no longer a sea of people, and street vendors have time to chat with you. Prices do drop, but the quality of service doesn't take a hit—that's the October I love most.

If you've been waiting for an answer to "When is the best time to visit Bali?"—October is it!
