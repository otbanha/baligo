# Key Topics and News Roundup for Bali Travelers (July 29, 2026)

As Bali continues to welcome the peak summer travel season, several new developments in weather, safety management, and sustainability are worth noting. Here are the top 5 travel news stories to watch today.

***

## 🌧️ BMKG Advisory: Heavy Rain, Strong Winds, and Higher Waves Expected in Bali

Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) has reported that although the dry season has begun, atmospheric conditions may still bring brief heavy rain, strong gusts, and higher waves in parts of Bali. Coastal and marine activities require extra caution.

**Traveler Tips:** If you're planning trips to Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, or activities like snorkeling, diving, or dolphin watching, be sure to reconfirm ferry schedules and weather conditions before heading out. For mountain destinations, it's advisable to bring a lightweight raincoat.

***

## 🚨 Theft Incident Reported at Ubud Boutique; Police Remind Travelers to Follow Local Laws

A boutique yoga apparel store in Ubud recently reported a suspected theft involving a foreign tourist, who is now under police investigation. This incident serves as a reminder that Indonesia enforces strict laws against criminal offenses like theft, and all individuals, regardless of nationality, are subject to legal procedures.

**Traveler's Tip:** When shopping in Bali, always adhere to local laws and store policies. If a dispute arises, try to resolve it with the merchant or seek police assistance to avoid any oversight affecting your trip.

***

## ♻️ Bali Launches Waste-to-Energy Project to Enhance Tourism Environment

Bali has officially initiated a Waste-to-Energy (WtE) project, aiming to reduce landfill waste through modern processing technology while addressing long-standing garbage issues in tourist areas. This is a key step in Bali's push for sustainable tourism, expected to improve the overall quality of the travel environment.

**Traveler's Tip:** During your trip, help by reducing the use of single-use plastics and sorting your waste according to accommodation and attraction guidelines, contributing to Bali's environmental preservation.

***

## 📋 Immigration Reiterates: Foreigners Must Not Host Events Without Permission

Bali's immigration authorities have reminded foreign nationals that anyone planning to hold commercial, public, or community events in Indonesia must obtain the necessary permits. Tourist visas cannot be used for activities inconsistent with their purpose. This reminder follows recent controversies involving unauthorized events by foreigners.

**Traveler's Tip:** General tourism, attending legal events, or private gatherings are unaffected. If you plan to host public classes, workshops, races, or commercial events, it's advisable to confirm compliance with Indonesian visa regulations and relevant laws in advance.
