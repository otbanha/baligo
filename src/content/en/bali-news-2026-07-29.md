# Key Topics and News Roundup for Bali Travelers (2026/7/29)

As Bali continues to welcome the peak summer travel season, several updates on weather, safety management, and sustainable development are worth noting for visitors. Here are the top 5 travel news stories to watch today.

***

## 🌧️ BMKG Alert: Heavy Rain, Strong Winds, and Higher Waves Expected in Bali

Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) reports that although the dry season has begun, atmospheric conditions may still bring brief heavy rain, strong gusts, and higher waves in parts of Bali. Caution is advised, especially for coastal and marine activities.

**Traveler Tips:** If you're planning trips to Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, or activities like snorkeling, diving, or dolphin watching, double-check ferry schedules and weather updates before heading out. For mountain areas, it's a good idea to bring a light rain jacket.

***

## 🚨 Theft Incident at Ubud Boutique; Police Remind Travelers to Follow Local Laws

A boutique yoga apparel store in Ubud recently experienced a suspected theft involving a foreign tourist, who is now under police investigation. This incident serves as a reminder to international visitors that Indonesia enforces strict laws against criminal offenses like theft, and all individuals, regardless of nationality, must comply with legal procedures.

**Traveler Tips:** When shopping in Bali, adhere to local laws and store policies. If a dispute arises, try communicating with the shop or seek police assistance to avoid letting a minor oversight disrupt your trip.

***

## ♻️ Bali Launches Waste-to-Energy Project to Enhance Tourism Environment

Bali has officially initiated a Waste-to-Energy (WtE) project, aiming to reduce landfill volume through modern waste processing technology while tackling the long-standing garbage issues in tourist areas. This is a key step in Bali's push for sustainable tourism, expected to improve the overall quality of the travel environment.

**Traveler Tips:** During your trip, help by reducing the use of single-use plastics and follow waste sorting rules at your accommodation and attractions to protect Bali's environment.

***

## 📋 Immigration Reiterates: Foreigners Must Not Host Events Without Permission

Bali's immigration authorities have reminded foreign nationals that anyone planning to hold commercial, public, or community events in Indonesia must obtain the necessary permits. Tourist visas cannot be used for activities that fall outside their intended purpose. This reminder comes after several recent incidents where foreigners sparked controversy by organizing events.

**Traveler Tips:** General tourism, attending legal events, or private gatherings are unaffected. However, if you plan to host public classes, workshops, runs, or commercial events, it's advisable to confirm compliance with Indonesian visa and regulatory requirements in advance.
