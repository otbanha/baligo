# Bali Travel Hot Topics + News Roundup (July 29, 2026)

As Bali continues to welcome the peak summer travel season, there are new updates on weather, safety management, and sustainable development that travelers should note. Here are the top 5 travel news stories worth paying attention to today.

***

## 🌧️ BMKG Advisory: Heavy Rain, Strong Winds, and Higher Waves Expected in Bali

Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BMKG) reports that although the dry season has begun, atmospheric conditions may still bring brief heavy rain, strong gusts, and higher waves in parts of Bali, especially along the coast and for marine activities—caution is advised.

**Traveler Tips:** If you plan to visit Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, or join snorkeling, diving, or dolphin-watching tours, reconfirm ferry schedules and weather conditions before departure. For mountain attractions, it's recommended to bring a light raincoat.

***

## 🚨 Theft Incident at Ubud Boutique, Police Remind Travelers to Follow Local Laws

A recent incident at a yoga apparel boutique in Ubud involving an alleged theft by a foreign tourist has led to a police investigation. This case serves as a reminder that Indonesia enforces strict laws against criminal offenses like theft, and all individuals, regardless of nationality, are subject to legal proceedings.

**Traveler Advice:** When shopping in Bali, always comply with local laws and store policies. If a dispute arises, try communicating with the shop owner or seek police assistance to avoid any oversight that could disrupt your trip.

***

## ♻️ Bali Launches Waste-to-Energy Project to Enhance Tourism Environment

Bali has officially initiated a Waste-to-Energy (WtE) project aimed at reducing landfill waste through modern processing technology while addressing long-standing garbage issues in tourist areas. This is a key step in Bali's push for sustainable tourism, expected to improve the overall quality of the travel environment.

**Traveler Advice:** During your visit, help reduce single-use plastics and follow waste sorting rules at your accommodation and attractions to protect Bali's environment.

***

## 📋 Immigration Reiterates: Foreigners Must Not Host Events Without Permits

Bali's immigration authorities have reminded foreign nationals that organizing commercial, public, or community events in Indonesia requires proper permits. Tourist visas do not permit activities inconsistent with visa purposes. This reminder follows recent controversies involving unauthorized events by foreigners.

**Traveler Advice:** General tourism, attending legal events, or private gatherings are unaffected. If you plan to host public classes, workshops, runs, or commercial events, verify compliance with Indonesian visa and regulatory requirements in advance.
