# Bali Travel Hot Topics + News Roundup (July 29, 2026)

As Bali continues to welcome the peak summer travel season, there are new updates on weather, safety management, and sustainable development that travelers should note. Here are the 5 most noteworthy travel news stories today.

***

## 🌧️ BMKG Advisory: Heavy Rain, Strong Winds, and Higher Waves Expected in Bali

Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics Agency (BMKG) has stated that although the dry season has begun, atmospheric conditions may still bring brief heavy rain, strong gusts, and higher waves in parts of Bali, especially along the coast and for maritime activities, where caution is advised.

**Traveler Tips:** If you plan to visit Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, or join snorkeling, diving, or dolphin-watching tours, reconfirm ferry schedules and weather conditions before departure. For mountain attractions, it's recommended to bring a lightweight raincoat.

***

## 🚨 Theft Incident at Ubud Boutique, Police Remind Travelers to Follow Local Laws

A boutique yoga apparel store in Ubud recently experienced an alleged theft incident involving a foreign tourist, with the suspect now under police investigation. This case serves as a reminder that Indonesia enforces strict laws on criminal offenses like theft, and all individuals, regardless of nationality, must comply with legal procedures.

**Traveler Advice:** When shopping in Bali, always follow local laws and store policies. If a dispute arises, try communicating with the shop first or seek police assistance to avoid minor oversights affecting your trip.

***

## ♻️ Bali Launches Waste-to-Energy Project to Enhance Tourism Environment

Bali has officially initiated a Waste-to-Energy (WtE) project, aiming to reduce landfill waste through modern processing technology while addressing long-standing garbage issues in tourist areas. This is a key step in Bali's push for sustainable tourism, promising to improve the overall travel environment in the future.

**Traveler Advice:** During your trip, consider reducing the use of single-use plastics and follow waste sorting rules at your accommodation and attractions to help preserve Bali's environment.

***

## 📋 Immigration Reiterates: Foreigners Must Not Host Events Without Permission

Bali's immigration authorities have reminded that foreigners planning to hold commercial, public, or community events in Indonesia must obtain the necessary permits and cannot use tourist visas for activities inconsistent with their visa purpose. This reminder follows recent controversies involving unauthorized events by foreigners.

**Traveler Advice:** General tourism, attending legal events, or private gatherings are unaffected. If you plan to host public classes, workshops, runs, or commercial activities, it's advisable to confirm compliance with Indonesian visa and related regulations in advance.
