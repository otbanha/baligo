# Bali Travel Hot Topics & News Roundup (2026/7/29)

As Bali continues to welcome the peak summer travel season, there are several new updates on weather, safety management, and sustainable development that travelers should note. Here are the top 5 travel news stories worth paying attention to today.

***

## 🌧️ BMKG Advisory: Heavy Rain, Strong Winds, and Higher Waves Expected in Bali

Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG) has stated that although the dry season has begun, atmospheric conditions may still bring brief heavy rain, strong gusts, and higher waves in parts of Bali, particularly along coastal areas and for marine activities.

**Traveler Tips:** If you're planning trips to Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, or activities like snorkeling, diving, or dolphin watching, be sure to check ferry schedules and weather updates before heading out. For mountain attractions, it's advisable to bring a lightweight raincoat.

***

## 🚨 Theft Incident Reported at Ubud Boutique; Police Remind Travelers to Follow Local Laws

A boutique yoga apparel store in Ubud recently reported a suspected theft involving a foreign tourist, who is now under police investigation. This incident serves as a reminder that Indonesia enforces strict laws against criminal offenses like theft, and all individuals, regardless of nationality, must comply with legal procedures.

**Traveler Tips:** When shopping in Bali, follow local laws and store policies. If a dispute arises, try to resolve it with the shop or seek police assistance to avoid any oversight affecting your trip.

***

## ♻️ Bali Launches Waste-to-Energy Project to Enhance Tourism Environment

Bali has officially launched a Waste-to-Energy project aimed at reducing landfill waste through modern processing technology, while addressing long-standing garbage issues in tourist areas. This is a key step in Bali's push for sustainable tourism, with the potential to improve overall travel conditions.

**Traveler Tips:** During your trip, help reduce single-use plastics and sort your trash according to accommodation and attraction guidelines to protect Bali's environment.

***

## 📋 Immigration Reiterates: Foreigners Must Not Host Events Without Permission

Bali's immigration authorities have reminded foreign nationals that hosting commercial, public, or community events in Indonesia requires proper permits. Tourist visas cannot be used for activities that fall outside their intended purpose. This reminder follows recent controversies involving unauthorized events by foreigners.

**Traveler Tips:** General tourism, attending legal events, or private gatherings are unaffected. If you plan to host public classes, workshops, runs, or commercial activities, check whether they comply with Indonesian visa and regulations in advance.
