If you've already explored Bali's beaches, spas, beach clubs, and popular waterfalls, here's a unique experience to try: the **Melukat purification ceremony at Taman Beji Griya Waterfall**.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1790487911942-b6c4c9403c0f0281ca386e811b8d96e1e8768de60f8a56575aa99a86b5961e64.avif)

It's not just about 'seeing a waterfall.'

This experience combines Bali's traditional **Melukat purification ceremony** with the forest, caves, and waterfall, allowing visitors to participate in a ritual led by a local guide, involving prayers, offerings, and holy water.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1790487944183-photo0jpg.jpg)

## The Most Unique Part: The Purification Ceremony in the Waterfall

The entire experience isn't just about snapping a few photos at the waterfall and leaving.

Visitors are guided through the ceremony process, dressed in appropriate attire, prepare offerings, and then enter the waterfall and cave area to complete prayers and holy water purification.

Klook describes it as a way to 'wash away negative emotions, relieve stress, and restore balance' through Melukat.

Of course, 'Healing' here should be understood more as **spiritual relaxation and a ceremonial experience**, rather than medical treatment.

Some people do feel a sense of relief after participating, but everyone's experience is different.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1790487984159-caption.jpg)

## What Are People Saying?

Overall reviews are quite positive.

In recent Klook reviews, many travelers specifically mention that **the guide is very important**—not only for guiding the ceremony but also for explaining Balinese culture and helping with photos. Some solo female travelers even said the guide made the experience better than expected.

Tripadvisor reviews echo similar sentiments, with some travelers describing it as a "very peaceful" experience, while others found the waterfall and the entire ceremony unforgettable.

But not everyone is a fan.

Some reviews note that the site is **quite commercialized**, and a few felt it didn't match their idea of a sacred experience.

So if you're expecting a "completely untouched, hidden Balinese paradise with no tourists," you might be disappointed.

## Another Important Note: Don't Just Go for the Waterfall

This is something many first-time viewers of photos often misunderstand.

According to current traveler information on Tripadvisor, **Taman Beji Griya is not a typical pay-at-the-gate waterfall attraction**—it's primarily centered around a purification ritual.

So don't treat it as:

"Let's just drop by and see the waterfall today."

It's better to plan it as:

**"Set aside a few hours today for a dedicated Balinese cultural experience."**

## Who is it for?

If you're interested in Balinese culture and religious rituals, or if this trip is about seeking a slower, quieter experience, Taman Beji Griya will be fascinating.

Especially for those who have already visited Tirta Empul (the Holy Spring Temple), this place stands out with its **forest + cave + waterfall + Melukat** combination, offering a different vibe.

But if your travel style is more about "snap photos at spots, eat good food, and move on to the next," this itinerary might not be the best fit.
