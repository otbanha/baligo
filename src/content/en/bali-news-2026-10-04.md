# Topics of Interest to Bali Tourists + News Roundup | October 4, 2026

## 🌧️ Sanur Begins Tukad Loloan River Channel Cleanup to Prevent Rainy Season Flooding

As Bali gradually enters the transition period between seasons, the **Tukad Loloan river channel in Sanur** has recently undergone cleanup works.

The Denpasar city government has deployed **2 large excavators and dozens of workers** for river dredging, primarily to remove long-accumulated sediment, aiming to improve drainage capacity and reduce the risk of river overflow during the rainy season. The project has been ongoing for about a month and a half.

For tourists, this does not mean 'Sanur is currently flooding,' but rather **local authorities are preparing for the rainy season in advance**.

If you're staying in Sanur recently, you can still enjoy the beach, ICON Bali, and Sanur city center as usual.

## ✈️ Ngurah Rai Airport Serves 15.5 Million Passengers in First 8 Months, International Travelers Account for About 10 Million

Ngurah Rai International Airport has released its latest operational data:

From January to August 2026, the airport served approximately 15.5 million passengers.

Among them:

- International passengers: approximately 10 million
- Domestic passengers: approximately 5.5 million
- Flight movements: approximately 94,000
- International routes: 41
- Domestic routes: 24

The airport currently operates a total of 65 routes. Among international routes, Singapore recorded the highest passenger volume at around 1.7 million, followed by Kuala Lumpur, Perth, Melbourne, and Sydney.

Additionally, starting August 17 this year, Ngurah Rai Airport has introduced an international-to-domestic terminal transfer bridge, making it more convenient for passengers connecting to domestic flights to move between the two terminals.

For travelers from Taiwan, Hong Kong, and other parts of Asia, this facility is especially noteworthy:

If your itinerary is:

**International flight arrives in Bali → Connecting domestic flight to another island or Indonesian city**

The transfer process now has a new connecting facility available.

## 🚤 Badung Water Taxi Terminal Under Review to Avoid Impacting Fishermen and Religious Activities

The Badung government reiterated today that the **Pantai Perancak water taxi terminal project** needs to be reassessed.

Local residents are concerned that the terminal's location could affect:

- Fishermen's operations
- Boat traffic
- Local religious activities
- Religious sites such as Pura Segara

The government has stated that it will reassess the location of the pier and related traffic routes, and is also studying whether alternative sites need to be found.

If this plan is implemented in the future, the original idea is to make water taxis a transportation option in southern Badung, helping to reduce some of the pressure on road traffic.

However, it is still in the **research and coordination phase**, so travelers should not yet consider it a new mode of transport that is ready for use.

## 🧵 Bali's Traditional Endek Weaving Faces a 'No One Weaves' Problem: High Demand but Few Young Artisans

This is a less common but highly relevant cultural news story for travelers to Bali.

Bali's traditional **Endek weaving** remains popular in the market, but the number of artisans actually engaged in hand-weaving is dwindling.

The Bali Craft Association notes an interesting contradiction emerging:

**Market demand is high, but there are not enough young people willing to learn and carry on the traditional weaving techniques.**

Some weaving artisans have even suggested establishing a dedicated weaving school to train the next generation of craftsmen.

For travelers, Endek is more than just 'a type of fabric souvenir from Bali.'

It embodies traditional motifs, dyeing techniques, weaving methods, and local culture.

So when you purchase Endek, clothing, or related handicrafts in Bali, **supporting authentic traditional craftsmanship and local artisans** is also a form of cultural support through tourism consumption.

## 🛕 Gianyar's Grand Temple Festival Returns After 48 Years, Drawing Around 2,000 Participants

Gianyar's **Pura Agung Manukaya Let** recently held a grand religious festival.

This Karya Agung ceremony took place **48 years** after the last one, which was in 1978.

During the main ceremony on October 3, approximately **2,000 people** from six traditional villages participated in the procession and religious activities.

The entire festival includes:

- Ngenteg Linggih
- Labuh Gentuh
- Pecaruan Agung
- Mendem Pedagingan
- Peed
- Nyenuk

and a series of traditional ceremonies.

If you're planning to visit the **Tampaksiring** or **Gianyar** area soon and encounter a large religious event, it's advisable to be mindful of local traffic controls and pedestrian crowds.

Also, pay attention to dress etiquette—avoid entering ceremonial areas without permission or disturbing participants during the rituals.

These large-scale ceremonies are actually a very unique cultural experience in Bali — **but they are meant to be 'observed and respected,' not treated as ordinary tourist performances.**
