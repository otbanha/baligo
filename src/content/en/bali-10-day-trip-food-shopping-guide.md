# Bali 10-Day Trip: Returning for the Fourth Year, a Typhoon Turned Five Days into Ten

https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1Cnsz9KqJZ/


From October 19 to 28, we returned to Bali once again—this marks our fourth consecutive year. This trip had an unexpected twist: a typhoon extended our original five-day itinerary into ten days, giving us a few extra days to enjoy Bali.

This travelogue isn't just about where we went; it's about why we return to this island year after year. From the weather and the warmth of the people to every unforgettable bite of food, the answer is simple: each visit makes us love it a little more.

## Why We Love Bali So Much

### Pleasant, Dry Climate

Bali is hot and sunny, but the air is dry, making it feel more comfortable than expected. The biggest practical perk is laundry: hand-washed clothes dry by the next day, so you never have to worry about running out of clean clothes during the trip.


The air quality also left a strong impression on us. Back in Taiwan, when we ride a scooter and wipe our face with a wet wipe, the tissue often turns black. Doing the same in Bali, however, doesn't happen. This is just our personal experience, but we've felt the same way every time we visit.

### Kind and Shy People

The locals always wear a shy smile on their faces. There were a few times when our car couldn't make a turn, and we rolled down the window to politely ask others to make way. In return, we often got a big smile, and the car would smoothly pass through. Such small interactions often brightened our entire day.

### Delicious Food to Our Taste

Balinese cuisine is rich in spices and bold flavors, perfectly suiting our palate. On this trip, we followed the restaurant and attraction recommendations shared by a travel group, and every single place exceeded our expectations: great food and beautiful scenery. We are truly grateful to the group admins for their careful curation, which added so many surprises to our journey.

## Food Map from This Trip

### Pork Dishes: Roast Pork Rice and Pork Ribs

**Warung Babi Guling Pande Egi** served the most amazing roast pork rice we had on this trip, and it was packed with locals. The flavors were rich, aromatic, and dry. There was also fried banana by the parking lot—delicious and cheap, an unexpected bonus.

https://youtube.com/shorts/sVxycGvyT9w?si=F01pQSLZnir4fMjv


Additional note: Pande Egi is located in the Beng area of Gianyar, surrounded by vast rice paddies. Dining here means enjoying roast pork with a countryside view, which is why it's often called 'roast pork rice with a view.' According to a Balinese government publication, the pork skin here is crispy. For a more indulgent experience, you can order a special set that includes pork skin, satay, and lawar. The owner is a young entrepreneur. Another food guide mentions that the special set and pork satay are affordably priced, with bold spice flavors that will delight fans of Balinese cuisine.

**Wahaha Pork Ribs** is located in Seminyak, and both the pork ribs and pork knuckles are incredibly tasty. The pork knuckle is freshly sliced by the chef on the spot, offering a fresh texture. The avocado chocolate is absolutely amazing.

Extra note: Wahaha was founded by Balinese Bli Hendra, with its first branch opening in Seminyak in December 2009. The signature pork ribs are marinated in Balinese spices, grilled over charcoal, and then brushed with their house-made original BBQ sauce. The main Seminyak outlet is on Sunset Road, and they've since expanded to Jimbaran, Pecatu, and Canggu. The Seminyak location features a spacious, airy indoor area with a garden at the back, making it ideal for family and friends to dine together.

### Seafood & Scenery: Steamed Prawns with Garlic Butter & Taman Dedari

**Bale Udang Mang Engking** in Kuta—the steamed prawns with garlic butter are the highlight here. They have水上茅草屋 (waterfront thatched huts) that need to be reserved in advance; the restaurant is especially beautiful in the evening.

https://youtu.be/mxnU1BxJCcM?si=3ewclKJQrpk7Qmwd

Extra note: Bale Udang is located at Jalan Nakula No.88 in Kuta. The restaurant surrounds a large fish pond, with traditional Balinese bamboo and thatch pavilions (bale) set around it. Each pavilion can seat up to eight people, and you can feed the fish while waiting for your meal. At night, the lights come on, creating an even more romantic atmosphere. The name 'udang' means shrimp, so shrimp dishes naturally take center stage; honey-glazed prawns and salted egg prawns are among the signature items. There's also a branch in Ubud, and it's halal-certified—no pork is served, making it a great choice for dining with friends who don't eat pork.


**Taman Dedari** is a scenic restaurant with exquisite food and beautiful views. The staff are professional and friendly, personally introducing each dish as it's served and asking about diners' preferences. The fairy statues in the garden are truly spectacular and eye-opening.

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_af793a02c768b26700df81f73fe8356f.jpg)

Extra note: Taman Dedari is actually located in Kedewatan, near Ubud, about 5 km (a 15-minute drive) from Ubud's center. It opened in early 2021 and is part of The Royal Pita Maha, overlooking the Ayung River—the widest and longest river in Bali. 'Dedari' means angel or fairy, inspired by the legend of Rsi Markandeya witnessing angels descending on the Ayung River. The garden features around 50 fairy statues, which are the most iconic scenery here.

### Local Eats: Satay & Evil Grilled Toast

**Sate Babi Bli Ketut** is a local satay stall. We first tried it with our guide and were blown away. The grilled meat has an intense smoky flavor, and the prices are very reasonable. This time, we even ordered it via Grab three days in a row.

Bonus info: Sate Babi Bli Ketut in Kuta, Bali, is a highly-rated pork satay specialist. Its signature dish is affordable and delicious grilled pork skewers, often served with white rice or sticky rice and a flavorful special chili sauce. The shop offers outdoor seating and fast, friendly service in a casual setting, popular with both tourists and locals who love authentic cuisine. Satay (sate babi) is a traditional dish made from pork marinated in Balinese spices, grilled, and served with a tomato-chili sauce or just chili and salt—unlike chicken satay, which is commonly paired with peanut sauce. The satay itself is aromatic and savory; it’s also an essential food in Balinese religious ceremonies, often eaten with white rice as a daily meal.

**Bendol's Roti Bakar - Kuta**'s grilled toast is what we call 'evil grilled toast'—a blend of condensed milk, peanut butter, and chocolate that’s incredibly rich. We ordered it via Grab for three days straight.

Bonus info: Bendol's Roti Bakar - Kuta, located in Kuta, Bali (tap to open side panel for more info), is a local charcoal-grilled toast (Roti Bakar) stall that stays open late. It offers a variety of sweet and savory filled toasts, such as cheese, chocolate, and condensed milk, at affordable prices, making it a favorite among late-night snackers and locals. Here’s some background on roti bakar: it means 'grilled bread' and originated during the Dutch colonial period as a way to use up day-old bread. Common spreads include condensed milk, butter, chocolate spread, and peanut butter. It later became a popular breakfast and street food across Indonesia, with new flavors constantly emerging.

### Tradition and International Flavors at a Legendary Restaurant

**Made's Warung Seminyak** is a legendary restaurant serving both traditional Balinese dishes and international cuisine, with a wide menu selection and an unbeatable signature fried rice. The dining space is spacious and comfortable, with live performances on weekend evenings, and other dishes are well worth trying.

Bonus info: The story of Made's Warung begins in Kuta. According to Fodor's, the restaurant started as a roadside stall run by Made in Kuta around 1969, later becoming the beloved establishment it is today. The Seminyak branch carries on the tradition with an open-air space and Balinese traditional dance performances—it’s recommended to book a seat near the stage. The menu ranges from classic Indonesian dishes to chef-adapted Italian and Japanese flavors; try the nasi campur and rujak kuah pindang (a fruit salad with a sour broth). Co-founder Made runs the place with her Dutch husband Peter Steenbergen, who drove from Amsterdam to Bali in 1973 and met her at the shop in Kuta.

## Shopping and Markets: From Malls to Local Life

• Icon Bali (Sanur): A shopping mall that opened in 2024, featuring traditional dance performances and a direct connection to the beach, letting you shop and enjoy the sea all at once.

https://youtu.be/hdIKOX5wghU?si=AAMKvUO3vdACuGBS

• Mall Bali Galeria (Kuta): A shopping center in Kuta with delicious J.CO Donuts & Coffee.

• Living World Mall Denpasar (Denpasar): Spacious with a comfortable layout, making for an easy stroll; when you get tired, there are cozy rocking chairs to rest in, and it offers many local Indonesian brands.

• Pasar Seni Kumbasari (Denpasar Traditional Market): This is the most local market, offering a glimpse into daily life. Tourists may not always come here, but we love it; it's packed with everything from art accessories to spices.

https://youtu.be/RRurkFC7UG4?si=NxDtfxrgEQ3RkfpT

Extended notes:
• Icon Bali: Officially opened on June 7, 2024, located next to Sanur Beach with two entrances—one facing the main road and one directly to the beach. Spanning 38,570 square meters with about 200 stores, including Uniqlo, H&M, and Marks & Spencer, plus Bali's first IMAX theater. The '8 Wonders of Icon Bali' include a floating market, butterfly garden, vertical forest, and cultural theater, with daily Balinese traditional dance performances on the cultural stage; the mall also provides space for local micro, small, and medium enterprises to showcase their products.
• Mall Bali Galeria: Located near the Dewa Ruci roundabout in Kuta, right next to Jl. Bypass Ngurah Rai, making it convenient. The most prominent building is the DFS Galleria duty-free store, and the mall houses Cinema XXI. Opening hours are daily from 10 AM to 10 PM.
• Living World Denpasar: Opened on March 24, 2023, as Kawan Lama Group's first Living World in Bali, located on Jl. Gatot Subroto Timur. It has a floor area of about 120,000 square meters across five floors, with over 350 stores announced before opening; it features energy-saving designs like solar panels and LED lights, and according to official sources, about 70% of employees are locals.
• Pasar Seni Kumbasari: Located on Jl. Gajah Mada, directly across the Badung River from Badung Market; this market spans two buildings, blending local daily trade with tourist crafts. Indonesia's Ministry of Tourism mentions over 1,000 stalls selling batik, wood carvings, silver jewelry, and woven rattan bags. Most vendors allow haggling; parking is limited, so it's easier to visit by scooter or on foot. Opening hours vary slightly across sources, generally from around 8-9 AM to 5-6 PM, so it's best to check before heading out.

If you want to experience another side of Bali, markets like this—closer to everyday life—leave a stronger impression than tourist spots.

## Best Driver Guide: Jimmi Liu

The most grateful part of this trip was meeting Jimmi Liu through the community group. To us, he was more than just a driver—he was the most caring and reliable friend we had in Bali.

When the typhoon caused our flight to be canceled, Jimmi quickly took us to the China Airlines counter on the second floor of the airport to negotiate a ticket change. In moments like this, having a companion who can communicate in the local language is especially important. Although we could manage in English, the local China Airlines staff immediately relaxed when they heard Jimmi speak, swiftly handling our ticketing so we could rearrange our itinerary.

From big matters like changing tickets to small ones like exchanging money, confirming meals, and checking bills, Jimmi was always there to help—not to mention all the other assistance he provided. With him by our side, our trip to Bali was filled with much more peace of mind and warmth.
