# Bali 10-Day Trip: Fourth Year Back, a Typhoon Turned Five Days into Ten

From October 19 to 28, we returned to Bali once again—this being our fourth consecutive year. An unexpected twist: a typhoon extended our original five-day itinerary to ten days, giving us a few extra days to soak in Bali.

This travelogue isn't just about where we went—it's about why we return to this island year after year. From the weather and the warmth of the people to every unforgettable bite of food, the answer is simple: each visit makes us love it a little more.

## Why We Love Bali So Much

### Pleasant, Dry Climate

Bali is hot and the sun is intense, but the air is dry, making it feel more comfortable than expected. The most practical benefit is laundry: hand-washed clothes dry by the next day, so you never have to worry about running out of clean clothes during the trip.

The air quality also left a strong impression on us. In Taiwan, when we ride a scooter and wipe our face with a wet tissue, the tissue often turns black; doing the same in Bali, however, doesn't produce that result. This is just our personal experience, but we've noticed it every time we visit.

### Kind and Shy People

The locals always wear shy smiles on their faces. A few times when our car couldn't make a turn, we rolled down the window and politely asked someone to make way. In return, we were often greeted with a big smile, and the car would smoothly pass through. Such small interactions often brightened our entire day.

### Food That Suits Our Palate

Balinese cuisine is rich in spices and bold in flavor, which perfectly suits our taste. On this trip, we followed restaurant and attraction recommendations shared by the community, and every single place exceeded expectations: delicious food with great views. We sincerely thank the community admins for their thoughtful curation, which added so many surprises to our journey.

## Food Map from This Trip

### Pork Dishes: Roast Pork Rice and Pork Ribs

**Warung Babi Guling Pande Egi** – The super delicious roast pork rice we had on this trip, frequented mostly by locals. The flavor was rich, aromatic, and dry. There was also fried banana by the parking lot—tasty and cheap, an unexpected bonus.

https://youtube.com/shorts/sVxycGvyT9w?si=F01pQSLZnir4fMjv

Bonus: Pande Egi in the Beng area of Gianyar sits amid vast rice paddies, offering suckling pig with countryside views—often called "suckling pig with a view." According to Bali provincial publications, the skin is crispy; for a fuller experience, order the special set with crackling, satay, and lawar. The young owner runs the place. Food guides note that the special set and pork satay are affordably priced, rich in spices—perfect for those who love Bali's bold flavors.

**Wahaha Pork Ribs** has branches in Seminyak, Jimbaran, and Sanur, serving delicious pork ribs and pork knuckles. The knuckles are freshly carved on-site, offering a fresh taste; the avocado chocolate is a standout.

https://youtu.be/RodQvCZKwD0?si=hZAnnnZobwrFYcMD

Bonus: Wahaha was founded by Balinese Bli Hendra, opening its first shop in Seminyak in December 2009. The signature pork ribs are marinated in Balinese spices, grilled over charcoal, and brushed with a house-made original BBQ sauce. The main Seminyak branch is on Sunset Road, with later expansions to Jimbaran, Pecatu, and Canggu. The Seminyak location features a spacious, airy interior with a garden out back, ideal for family and friends.

### Seafood & Views: Bale Udang Steamed Garlic Butter Prawns, Taman Dedari

**Bale Udang Mang Engking** is a floating bale restaurant where steamed garlic butter prawns are the highlight. It features water-facing thatched huts that require advance booking; the setting is especially beautiful at dusk.

https://youtube.com/shorts/CJe5rBgmvsE?si=jd9qa1Ke8-vWgvuB

Bonus: Bale Udang is located at Jalan Nakula No.88 in Kuta. The restaurant surrounds a large fish pond, with traditional Balinese bales (pavilions) made of bamboo and thatch—each seating up to eight people. You can feed the fish while waiting, and the atmosphere becomes even more romantic with evening lights. The name "udang" means shrimp, so shrimp dishes are the stars; honey-glazed grilled shrimp and salted egg shrimp are popular signatures. There's also a branch in Ubud, which is halal-certified and pork-free, making it suitable for friends who don't eat pork.

**Taman Dedari** is a scenic restaurant with exquisite food and beautiful views. The staff are professional and friendly, personally introducing each dish and checking on diners' preferences. The fairy garden sculptures are stunning and truly eye-opening.

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_af793a02c768b26700df81f73fe8356f.jpg)

Additional info: Taman Dedari is located in Kedewatan, about 5 km (a 15-minute drive) from central Ubud. Opened in early 2021, this attraction is part of Bali's Royal Group company, The Royal Pita Maha, overlooking the Ayung River—the widest and longest river on the island. "Dedari" means angel or fairy, inspired by the legend of ancestors witnessing angels descending upon the Ayung River. The park features around fifty fairy statues, making them its most iconic scenery.

### Local Bites: Satay and Evil Grilled Toast

**Sate Babi Bli Ketut** is a local satay joint. We first tried it with our guide and were blown away. The grilled pork skewers are incredibly smoky and flavorful, and the prices are a steal. This time, we ordered from Grab three days in a row. (Featured in [Kuta Food Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/kuta-best-food-guide/))

Additional info: Located in Kuta, Bali, Sate Babi Bli Ketut is a highly-rated pork satay specialist. Its signature dish is affordable and delicious grilled pork skewers, often served with white rice or sticky rice and a punchy homemade chili sauce. The stall offers outdoor seating with quick, friendly service in a casual setting, beloved by both tourists and locals seeking authentic street food. Satay babi (pork satay) is a traditional Balinese dish where pork is marinated in local spices, grilled, and served with a tomato-chili sauce or just chili and salt—unlike chicken satay's common peanut sauce. It's inherently savory and spicy, and it's also an essential food in Balinese religious ceremonies, often eaten with rice as a daily meal.

**Bendol's Roti Bakar - Kuta** serves what we call "evil grilled toast." It combines condensed milk, peanut butter, and chocolate into one heavenly, aromatic bite. Again, we ordered it via Grab three days straight. (Featured in [Kuta Food Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/kuta-best-food-guide/))

Additional info: Located in Kuta, Bali, Bendol's Roti Bakar - Kuta is a local charcoal-grilled toast (Roti Bakar) stall that stays open late. It offers a variety of sweet and savory filled toasts, such as cheese, chocolate, and condensed milk, at affordable prices, making it a favorite for late-night snackers and locals. Here's some background on roti bakar: meaning "grilled bread," it originated during the Dutch colonial period as a way to use up day-old bread. Common spreads include condensed milk, butter, chocolate spread, and peanut butter. It later evolved into a popular Indonesian breakfast and street snack, with endless flavor variations.

### Tradition and International Flavors at a Classic Eatery

**Made's Warung Seminyak** is a long-standing restaurant serving both traditional Balinese dishes and international cuisine. The menu is extensive, and their signature fried rice is absolutely divine. The dining space is spacious and comfortable, with live performances on weekend evenings. Other dishes are also well worth trying. (Featured in [Kuta Food Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/kuta-best-food-guide/))

Additional info: Made's Warung story begins in Kuta. According to Fodor's, the original was a roadside stall opened by Made in Kuta around 1969, which later became the beloved classic. The Seminyak branch carries on the tradition with an open-air layout and Balinese dance performances—it's recommended to book a table near the stage. The menu ranges from Indonesian classics to chef's twists on Italian and Japanese flavors. Try the nasi campur and rujak kuah pindang (a fruit salad with a tangy broth). The restaurant is co-run by Made and her Dutch husband, Peter Steenbergen, who drove from Amsterdam to Bali in 1973 and met Made at the original shop.

## Souvenirs Worth Taking Home

Beyond the food, I also picked up a few practical and lovely items on this trip:

- Rejoice 3 in 1 Shampoo Rich Soft Smooth: A locally sold shampoo that leaves hair feeling silky and smooth.
- Mustika Ratu Royal Blue & Moringa Body Lotion: A fragrant, non-greasy body lotion (noted in the original as the butterfly pea flower variant; the official name is Royal Blue & Moringa, with Moringa being the moringa plant—actual ingredients may vary, so check the packaging).
- Secret Garden Perfume: Available in many shopping malls, this scent we love is also very affordable.
- SHAKEEL BEADS: A jewelry store in Kuta with a huge selection—truly worth a visit.

Additional notes:

- Mustika Ratu: A well-known Indonesian brand specializing in herbal (Jamu) and natural cosmetics. The Royal Blue & Moringa is the exclusive scented body lotion for the 2025 Puteri Indonesia event, and the same series also offers fragrances like Rose Radiance, Javanese Jasmine, and Lily & Honey Nectar.
- Secret Garden: Originating from Secret Garden Village in Bali in 2016, this brand's philosophy is 'Inspired by Earth, Made for You.' According to reports, it now has 75 stores across Indonesia, so you can find it in major shopping malls. In October 2025, the brand launched its first Extrait de Parfum collection, featuring Bali-inspired scents.

## Shopping and Markets: From Malls to Local Life

Icon Bali (Sanur): A shopping mall that opened in 2024, featuring traditional dance performances and direct beach access, allowing you to shop and enjoy the sea at the same time.

https://youtu.be/hdIKOX5wghU?si=AAMKvUO3vdACuGBS

• Mall Bali Galeria (Kuta): A shopping center in Kuta, home to the delicious doughnut shop J.CO Donuts & Coffee.

Living World Mall Denpasar (Denpasar): Spacious and well-laid-out, making for a relaxed shopping experience. When you get tired, there are comfortable rocking chairs to sit on, and it features many local Indonesian brands.

> [【Living World Denpasar】Bali's Newest Shopping Mall Guide｜Brands, Food (Bali Haidilao), Supermarket, and Transportation](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/Living-World-Denpasar/)

Pasar Seni Kumbasari (Denpasar Traditional Market): This is the most local market, offering a glimpse into the daily lives of residents. Tourists don't always come here, but we love it; it's packed with everything from artistic ornaments to spices.

https://youtu.be/RRurkFC7UG4?si=NxDtfxrgEQ3RkfpT

Additional notes:

- Icon Bali: Officially opened on June 7, 2024, located next to Sanur Beach with two entrances—one facing the main road and another directly leading to the beach. Spanning 38,570 square meters, it houses around 200 stores, including Uniqlo, H&M, and Marks & Spencer, as well as Bali's first IMAX theater. The '8 Wonders of Icon Bali' features a floating market, butterfly garden, vertical forest, and cultural theater, with daily traditional Balinese dance performances on the cultural stage. The mall also provides space for local micro, small, and medium enterprises to showcase their products.

https://youtu.be/hdIKOX5wghU?si=L8xN-SO6rIfFbC9n

- Mall Bali Galeria: Situated next to the Dewa Ruci roundabout in Kuta, adjacent to Jl. Bypass Ngurah Rai, this mall is conveniently located. The most prominent building is the DFS Galleria duty-free store, and the mall also houses Cinema XXI. Operating hours are daily from 10:00 AM to 10:00 PM.
- Living World Denpasar: Opened on March 24, 2023, this is Kawan Lama Group's first Living World in Bali, located on Jl. Gatot Subroto Timur. With a floor area of approximately 120,000 square meters across five floors, it announced over 350 stores before opening. It features energy-saving designs like solar panels and LED lights, and according to official sources, about 70% of its employees are locals.

> [【Living World Denpasar】Bali's Newest Shopping Mall Guide｜Brands, Food (Bali Haidilao), Supermarket, Transportation at a Glance](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/Living-World-Denpasar/)

- Pasar Seni Kumbasari: Located on Jl. Gajah Mada, directly across the Badung River from Badung Market; this market spans two buildings, blending local daily trade with tourist crafts. According to Indonesia's Ministry of Tourism, it has over 1,000 stalls selling batik, wood carvings, silver jewelry, and woven bags. Most vendors are open to bargaining; parking is limited, so it's more convenient to visit by scooter or on foot. Operating hours vary slightly across sources, generally from around 8–9 AM to 5–6 PM, so it's best to double-check before heading out.

https://youtu.be/RRurkFC7UG4?si=AWvRDQe9z6YsPdto

If you want to experience another side of Bali, these everyday markets leave a stronger impression on us than tourist attractions.

## The Best Driver and Guide: Jimmi Liu

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-03/1774787449808-jimmi_new.jpg)

The person we’re most grateful for on this trip is Jimmi Liu, whom we met through a travel group. To us, he was more than just a driver—he became the most thoughtful and reliable friend we had in Bali.

When the typhoon canceled our flight, Jimmi rushed us to the China Airlines counter on the second floor of the airport the next day to negotiate a rebooking. At times like this, having a companion who can communicate in the local language is invaluable. While English worked fine, the local China Airlines staff visibly relaxed once Jimmi spoke, quickly handling our tickets and allowing us to rearrange the rest of our itinerary.

From big things like rebooking flights to small things like exchanging money, confirming meals, and checking bills, Jimmi was always there to help—and his assistance went far beyond that. With him by our side, our time in Bali felt much safer and warmer.

## Final Thoughts

Returning to Bali for the fourth year in a row—and staying five extra days due to a typhoon—only deepened our appreciation for the island’s charm: the dry weather, the warm smiles, the authentic cuisine, and a trustworthy local friend.

Once again, thank you to the group admins for their thoughtful recommendations, which filled our trip with delicious food, great shopping, and unforgettable fun. Next time we come, we’ll surely carry this anticipation back to the island.
