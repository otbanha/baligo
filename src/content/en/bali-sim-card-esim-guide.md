## Comparison of Indonesia's Top 3 Telecoms—From a Traveler's Perspective

### Telkomsel (Best Coverage, Top Pick for Travelers)

![Telkomsel（覆蓋最廣，旅客首選）](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-05/1779239732579-af57c619fd217399aaa52c62626b953e.jpg)

Indonesia's largest telecom provider with about 55% market share. In various parts of Bali, including mountain villages like Amed, Munduk, and Sidemen, Telkomsel's 4G signal is the most stable among the three carriers.

- **4G/5G Status:** Full 4G coverage from Kuta to Nusa Dua's main tourist areas, with 5G coverage spots in central Ubud
- **Most Popular Plan for Travelers:** The Perdana Tourist SIM (prepaid SIM card) is essentially the **Telkomsel Tourist SIMPATI** (priced around IDR 250,000–350,000 at the airport, with 15GB–35GB of data). Local phone shops in town often sell it for half or even a third of that price.
- **Nusa Penida Signal:** Strongest among the three, with stable 4G from the harbor to major attractions
- **Gili Islands Signal:** Signal available on Gili Trawangan, weaker on Gili Air, and intermittent on Gili Meno
- **Drawbacks:** About 30–40% more expensive than Indosat, and airport kiosks often inflate prices

**simPATI is a brand under Telkomsel—you're buying access to the same network, so no need to differentiate.**

### Indosat Ooredoo / IM3 (Best Value Choice)

Over 30% cheaper than Telkomsel, with signal that's perfectly adequate in urban tourist areas like Kuta, Seminyak, and Sanur.

- **4G Status:** Works fine in major tourist zones, but drops to 3G or no signal in remote areas like Amed and Munduk
- **Most Popular Plan:** Freedom Internet, IDR 50,000–80,000 for 10–20GB
- **Best For:** Travelers sticking to Kuta and central Ubud, avoiding remote areas

### XL Axiata (Fast in Cities, Weak in Remote Areas)

In Seminyak and Kuta, 4G speeds sometimes surpass Telkomsel, but coverage drops sharply at the edges.

- **Biggest Issue:** Extremely poor signal on Nusa Penida, with no signal at some attractions
- **Why It's Not Recommended for Travelers:** Signal optimization is concentrated in cities; stability drops once you leave core tourist zones
- **Only Suitable For:** Those staying exclusively in Kuta and Seminyak, needing high-speed signal, and not visiting outlying islands

**Quick Verdict on the Three Major Carriers: If your itinerary isn't limited to city centers, go with Telkomsel—you won't regret it.**

## Where to Buy Physical SIM Cards—A Complete Guide

![實體 SIM 卡哪裡買——完整攻略](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-05/1779240020594-e5c025ea6a97e3839ab39d4ff832db68.jpg)

### 1. At Bali Airport (DPS)

**Most convenient, but also the most pitfalls.**

**Location:** After clearing customs in the arrival hall, you'll find official Telkomsel and Indosat booths near the baggage carousels, along with plenty of unofficial vendors.

***

⚠️ **Scam Alert—This Part Is Important**

Here's what Klook blogs and eSIM brand articles won't tell you:

1. **Fake 'Installation Fee' (biaya installasi)**: Vendors may claim you need to pay IDR 200,000–800,000 for setup—this is a scam. Official SIM cards auto-configure upon insertion; no manual installation fee is required.
2. **Upselling with 'best package for tourist' pitches**: Politely decline and ask them to give you the standard Kartu Perdana (prepaid card) plan.

**Safe Practices:**

- Choose official booths (with Telkomsel / Indosat signage, not roadside tables)
- After inserting the card, dial *888# yourself to check your balance and confirm

***

**Installation Steps (DIY):**

1. Make sure your phone is unlocked (SIM-locked phones can't swap cards)
2. Locate the SIM card slot and use the ejector tool to remove your old card
3. Insert the new SIM card and restart your phone
4. If the network doesn't auto-configure, go to APN settings and enter them manually (see tutorial below)

**By the way:** For transportation from the airport to your accommodation, check out [Arriving in Bali: Airport Transfer Options and Practical First-Day Tips](/en/blog/bali-airport-transfer-guide/), which compares private car hire, taxis, and ride-hailing services.

### 2. Buying at City Convenience Stores

**Indomaret and Alfamart** are on nearly every street, and most locations sell prepaid SIM cards.

- **Purchase Process:** Tell the clerk "mau beli kartu perdana Telkomsel," show your passport, and the clerk will register it in the system.
- **Price:** Usually 20–30% cheaper than at the airport, but with fewer data plan options.
- **Important Notes:** You need the original passport (a photocopy sometimes works), and minors cannot purchase.
- **Recommended Scenario:** When you're not in a hurry, want to compare prices, or the airport booth queue is too long.

Keep in mind that many shop assistants in tourist areas are now quite used to handling SIM cards for foreign travelers. However, there's a common pitfall for tourists: **Not every convenience store can complete the "passport real-name registration" for you!** After you buy one:

- The SIM card might not work
- You'll have to register it yourself
- Or go to an official store to sort it out

Especially for foreign passport registration, some clerks really aren't familiar with the process.

### 3. Purchase at an Official Grapari Store

Grapari is Telkomsel's official service center, ideal for long-term travelers or those needing to resolve account issues.

- **Location:** Branches in Kuta, Denpasar, and Ubud. Search "Grapari Telkomsel" on Google Maps.
- **Required Documents:** Original passport and personal contact information.
- **Pros:** Monthly plans available (more cost-effective for stays over 30 days), on-the-spot customer service.
- **Cons:** You'll need to take a number and wait, possibly 30–60 minutes.

## Complete Installation Guide

![安裝完整教學](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-05/1779240073782-069909000_1519793545-where-to-buy-sim-card-in-ho-chi-minh.webp)

### Installing iPhone eSIM

**Method 1: QR Code Scan (Most Common)**

1. After purchasing the eSIM, get the QR Code image from the Klook / Airalo App
2. Go to your phone's "Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM"
3. Select "Use QR Code" and scan the screen
4. Set up "Primary SIM" (Taiwan number) and "Travel SIM" (Bali eSIM), assigning voice/data as needed

**Method 2: Manual Activation Code Entry**

1. If scanning fails, select "Enter Details Manually"
2. Input the SM-DP+ address and activation code (found in your purchase confirmation email)

**Common Errors:**

- _"Unable to Add Cellular Plan"_: Ensure your phone is unlocked and iOS version is 12.1 or later
- _eSIM installed but no signal_: Check if the activation code has already been used (each QR Code can only be used once)
- _Screenshot of QR Code won't scan_: Display it directly from the Klook App instead of scanning a screenshot

### Installing Android eSIM

The setup path varies by brand. Using Samsung as an example:

1. Settings → Connections → SIM Card Manager → Add Mobile Plan
2. Scan the QR Code or enter details manually
3. Once done, you can switch between the primary SIM and eSIM as needed

**Note:** Some Android phones (mainly US carrier versions) have the eSIM feature locked. Be sure to check before purchasing.

[Check if your phone supports eSIM](https://saily.com/zh-tw/esim-supported-devices/)

### Physical SIM Card Installation + APN Settings

**If automatic setup fails after swapping the SIM, manually configure the APN as follows:**

| Carrier | APN Name | Username | Password |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Telkomsel | `internet` | (blank) | (blank) |
| Indosat IM3 | `indosatgprs` | (blank) | (blank) |
| XL Axiata | `internet` | (blank) | (blank) |

**iOS setup path:** Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data Network → Enter APN field
**Android setup path:** Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names (APN) → Add

**Common mistakes:**

- _Still showing E/2G after setup_: Restart the phone and wait 2–3 minutes for the network to reconnect
- _APN field appears gray_: The phone may be carrier-locked; unlock it before swapping SIM cards

### Recommended Settings for Dual SIM with Shared Taiwan Number

Use a Bali SIM for internet while keeping your Taiwan number for calls:

1. **Set the Taiwan SIM as the "Voice Call" card**, and turn off mobile data on the Taiwan card (to avoid roaming charges)
2. **Set the Bali SIM as the "Mobile Data" card**
3. If your Taiwan card supports Wi-Fi Calling, enable it so others can reach your Taiwan number via Wi-Fi (completely free)

## Common Disasters & Instant Fixes

**Signal Suddenly Lost**

- First check if APN settings have been cleared (toggling Airplane Mode on and off sometimes resets APN)
- Dial \*888# to check your data balance; if it's zero, you'll need to top up
- For Telkomsel top-ups, you can buy a voucher at Indomaret—just tell the cashier "isi ulang Telkomsel XXX ribu"

**eSIM Activation Failed**

- Make sure you're on Wi-Fi when installing (a Wi-Fi connection is required to download the eSIM profile)
- Each QR Code can only be installed once. If you've switched phones, contact the eSIM brand's customer service for a new code
- Klook eSIM users can reach Chinese-language support directly in the app; they usually reply within an hour

**How to Top Up When Data Runs Out**

- Physical SIM: Buy a voucher at Indomaret / Alfamart, or top up using GoPay / Dana
- eSIM: Return to the purchase platform and buy another one (usually doesn't support direct top-up on the original SIM; just buy a new eSIM)

**What to do if you switch phones**

- Physical SIM: Simply remove it and insert into the new phone (make sure the new phone is unlocked)
- eSIM: You need to delete it on the original phone, then re-scan the QR code to install it (some brands support this; Klook customer service can assist)

**Do you need to turn it off after returning to Taiwan?**

- Physical SIM: Just remove it; the Indonesian number will automatically deactivate after 90 days of no use
- eSIM: You can delete the eSIM profile on your phone, or keep it as a backup (it will take up one eSIM slot)
- **Important:** After returning to Taiwan, make sure your Taiwan SIM is switched back to the primary data source to avoid roaming charges from the Bali SIM

## FAQ

**Is Bali eSIM the same as Indonesia eSIM?**
Yes, Bali is within Indonesia, so purchasing an Indonesia eSIM works across the entire island with no regional restrictions. Some brands label their plans as "Indonesia," covering all areas including Bali, Java, and beyond.

**Can I share a hotspot from one eSIM with travel companions?**
Most eSIMs (including Klook eSIM and Airalo) support hotspot sharing, but Holafly's unlimited data plans **explicitly prohibit** tethering. Always check the plan terms before purchasing to avoid issues.

**I don't speak English—can I buy a SIM at the airport counter?**
Official Telkomsel and Indosat counters at the airport offer English service, and you can also use a translation app on your phone. Just state the number of days and approximate data needs—staff are experienced with travelers. However, stay alert during transactions; see the scam warning section in this article for details.

**Is it safe to buy eSIM online with a credit card?**
Klook, Airalo, and Holafly are globally recognized platforms that use HTTPS encryption for transactions, making them as secure as standard online shopping. It's recommended to use a New Taiwan Dollar credit card with no foreign transaction fees (like Richart) for purchases.

**Which is more cost-effective: a Wi-Fi hotspot or a SIM card?**
For trips of 5 days or less with 3 or more people, a Wi-Fi device split per person might be cheaper. But for trips over 5 days, or with just 1–2 people, a SIM card or eSIM is almost always more affordable, plus you avoid worrying about the hotspot's battery life. See our full comparison of [Bali Wi-Fi vs. SIM cards](/en/blog/bali-sim-card-esim-roaming-guide/) in a separate article.

**For group travel, is eSIM or a physical SIM more economical?**
For 2 people, buying individual eSIMs is the easiest. For 3 or more, one Telkomsel physical SIM with hotspot sharing is the cheapest option (one IDR 120,000 SIM split three ways costs around USD$4 each). However, hotspot sharing drains battery faster, so bring a power bank.

**Will I have signal on Gili Trawangan?**
Telkomsel offers 4G signal near the Gili Trawangan dock, but coverage is spotty inland on the island. Signal on Gili Air and Gili Meno is extremely weak and unreliable. Most accommodations have Wi-Fi, so use that for important communications.

**Can I install an eSIM before clearing customs at the airport?**
Yes. Installing an eSIM requires Wi-Fi, and airport lounges offer free Wi-Fi. It's best to **install the eSIM profile before leaving Taiwan**, setting the activation time to start upon arrival in Indonesia. That way, once you land, switch to airplane mode to disable your Taiwan SIM, and the eSIM will be ready to use immediately.

**Can I buy a physical SIM card with a photocopy of my passport?**
Official regulations require the original passport, but some stalls accept a photo of your passport displayed on your phone. It's advisable to carry your original passport or save a high-resolution scan in your phone's photo gallery as a backup.

**Is Telkomsel the same as simPATI?**
Yes, simPATI is a prepaid brand under Telkomsel, using the exact same Telkomsel network towers, so signal and coverage are identical. Tourist SIMs sold at the airport are mostly labeled as simPATI or Kartu As, so there's no need to distinguish them. 

**Can I still use my Indonesian SIM card after returning to Taiwan?**
Technically yes, but the rates are based on Indonesian roaming charges, which are very expensive. After returning to Taiwan, immediately switch back to your Taiwan SIM or disable mobile data on the Indonesian SIM to avoid unexpected bills.

**Can I still make calls if my data runs out?**
For Telkomsel and Indosat prepaid SIMs, voice call and SMS balances are separate from data allowances. If your data runs out but you still have voice credit on your SIM, you can still make voice calls (for local Indonesian numbers).
