### 🛂 The immigration process was smoother than expected

This time, I handled almost everything online in advance, so the entry process was surprisingly fast.

#### 1️⃣ Electronic Visa (B1)

I applied for the electronic visa before departure, costing around USD$32.
After landing, I went straight to the e-gate, and it only took about 1 minute to clear customs—no waiting in line at all.

If you're visiting Bali during peak season, I really recommend getting the e-visa in advance. Waiting in line for VOA on-site can sometimes make you question your life choices 😆

#### 2️⃣ Electronic Customs Declaration

I also recommend filling this out in advance.
You can submit it online up to three days before departure. Just save the QR code screenshot on your phone, and scan it on-site when you arrive.

#### 3️⃣ Tourism Tax

I also paid the tourism tax online in advance, around USD$10.
Nowadays, many processes in Bali are gradually going digital, and handling things ahead of time really saves you a lot of hassle.

## Day 2｜Morning Beach Walk, Beach Club, Charity Visit, and a Slow Afternoon in Sanur 🌊

The rhythm of the second day was exactly my ideal Bali mode. No crammed-in attractions, no rushing from morning till night—just a seamless flow between the beach, coffee bars, community spaces, and Sanur's laid-back pace.

***

### 🌞 Seminyak Beach at Dawn, Even More Enchanting Than by Day

Around six in the morning, I walked straight out the back door of the hotel onto the sand.

This is exactly why I love staying at beachfront hotels. No need to plan an itinerary—just waking up and stepping onto the sand for a morning stroll is already worth it.

Seminyak Beach in the early morning is a world apart from the evening. No thumping music, no crowds of tourists—only the sound of waves, morning joggers, and a handful of foreigners already paddling out to surf.

And I have to say, the 'surfer hottie density' on Seminyak Beach in the morning is seriously high 🤣. Many hit the water before dawn, and the beach vibe is super chill. Watching everyone carry their boards into the sea gives you that feeling: 'This is the real Bali daily life.'

***

### 🍸 Atlas Beach Club｜The World's Largest Beach Club

During the day, I visited the much-talked-about Atlas Beach Club.

![Atlas Beach Club｜傳說中的全球最大 Beach Club](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1781014745875-atlas2.jpg)

Photo courtesy of Atlas Beach Club

It's famously known as the 'world's largest beach club,' and seeing it in person, the scale is truly staggering. The entire space is enormous—from the pool and DJ area to the restaurant and lounge zones, it feels almost like a small resort.

This time, thanks to a collaboration with TUL BLUE BERAWA, guests staying in suites or above can enter for free, and they even get front-row sofa seating plus a welcome drink. The front-row area makes a huge difference—you get a direct ocean view, with the waves, music, crowd, and sunset all around you. The atmosphere is incredibly immersive.

That said, I personally feel Atlas is more of a 'lively party-style Beach Club.' If you love music, a vibrant vibe, and photogenic spots, it’s a perfect fit. But if you prefer a quiet, relaxing escape, you might enjoy other beach clubs more.

### 💚 Bali Life Foundation｜The most profound stop on this trip

The most memorable part of the afternoon wasn't really the sights, but the visit to the Bali Life Foundation.

https://youtube.com/shorts/ljVgfCUTOyg

Courtesy of Lai Jiaying, Bali Life Foundation

The foundation opens for预约 visits every Friday from 4:00 to 7:00 PM. The campus is actually quite small, currently housing about 50 children.

When we arrived, we were about to unload the rice and cooking oil we brought for donation. A few young boys immediately ran over to help.

What shocked me the most was this—those kids were only about five or six years old, yet each one carried a 10-kilogram bag of rice on their own.

That image was quite striking. It suddenly hits you that some children grow up in environments vastly different from the world we're used to.

But at the same time, they weren't somber at all. Instead, they kept laughing, chatting, and even proactively helped welcome everyone.

![Bali Life Foundation｜這趟旅程最深刻的一站](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1781014986137-bali_life_foundation2.jpg)

Courtesy of Lai Jiaying

The foundation has dinner together at 6:00 PM, and visitors are welcome to stay and join. Compared to a simple 'tour,' it felt more like briefly stepping into their lives.

They also run a kindergarten offering half-day to full-day childcare services. Many foreign parents who come to Bali for surfing or work send their kids here, and this income helps support the foundation's operations.

I really like this model. It doesn't rely entirely on donations but strives to build a sustainable cycle that can run long-term.

### 🌅 Strolling Along Sanur Beach｜The morning sea breeze is truly gentle

I went for a morning stroll directly at Sanur Beach.

The sea in Sanur feels completely different from Bali's west coast. The waves here are much calmer, and in the early morning, the entire ocean surface has a mirror-like quality.

There's no Beach Club music or crowds of people. Just walkers, cyclists, and those sitting by the shore, lost in thought.

I really love the morning air in Sanur. The sea breeze isn't strong—it's gentle and soothing, naturally making you slow down.

Sometimes the most healing moments in travel aren't about how many sights you've seen, but about doing nothing at all—just sitting by the sea, staring at the horizon.

I've always admired this type of nonprofit organization. Because what they do often isn't just short-term help—it truly shapes a child's future.

And when you visit, you'll find the overall atmosphere is actually very warm and pressure-free. It feels more like a simple, genuine form of support.

### 🧶 A Heartwarming Cross-Border Crochet Gathering

This time, I happened to join a very special cross-border crochet gathering.

People from different countries came together, stitch by stitch, crocheting 'breast prostheses.'

![一場很溫暖的跨國鉤針聚會](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1781015375794-lady_bumps.jpg)

Provided by Lai Jiaying

These handmade crochet prostheses will all be donated to relevant associations for women recovering from breast cancer surgery. Compared to standard prostheses, these knitted ones are lighter, softer, and more gentle on the skin.

The atmosphere on-site was actually quite quiet. Everyone focused on their crocheting, heads down—some chatted, others shared stories. There wasn't much sentimental talk, but a gentle warmth slowly built up.

Sometimes the most precious travel memories aren't about visiting spectacular sights, but about these small, real human connections.

## Day 4｜Riding a Scooter Through Mountain Roads, from Tirta Empul Temple All the Way to Ubud and Sanur 🛵

Day four kicked off with scooter travel mode. We set off from Sanur sharp at 7 AM, riding straight into central Bali.

Honestly, riding a scooter in Bali gives you a unique sense of freedom. Along the way, you pass through small villages, rice terraces, temples, and mountain roads. Sometimes, just stopping at a red light and seeing roadside offerings, breakfast stalls, or students riding to school makes you think, 'Ah, this is the real Bali everyday life.'

But a heads-up: if you're not used to riding a scooter or unfamiliar with Southeast Asian traffic rhythms, it's much easier to just hire a driver 🤣

### 🙏 Tirta Empul Temple｜Coming Early Really Makes a Difference

Our first stop was straight to Tirta Empul Temple.

![聖泉寺｜一大早來真的差很多](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1781015518073-tirta_empul_3.jpg)

Tirta Empul Temple – Photo by Lai Jiaying

We arrived very early, so the crowds were still manageable. I'd seen plenty of online photos showing long queues, but coming in the morning truly makes a world of difference—both for photos and for the purification ritual experience.

The temple is most famous for its holy water purification. Many locals and tourists line up in order at different spouts to perform the cleansing ceremony.

The atmosphere on-site is actually quite serene. Even with a fair number of visitors, you can still feel a very peaceful energy.

But I have to give you a serious heads-up:

⭐️ No hairdryers. ⭐️ No hairdryers. ⭐️ No hairdryers.

Your hair will just have to air-dry naturally after getting wet 😅 Luckily, I brought my own towel—otherwise, riding a scooter with wet hair would have been a nightmare.

### 🧱 Penglipuran Village | A Traditional Village Full of Life

Next, I continued riding north and arrived at Penglipuran Village.

![彭利普蘭村｜很有生活感的傳統村莊](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1781015614405-pengli1.jpg)

Photo of Penglipuran Village by Lai Jiaying

This village has long been known as one of the cleanest villages in the world. The entire environment is impeccably maintained, and it preserves many traditional Balinese architectural features.

Unlike typical tourist spots, this is still a living village where people actually reside. Many homes have small shops out front selling drinks, snacks, and little souvenirs, giving it a very down-to-earth feel.

I personally loved the pace here. You don’t need to do anything special—just take a slow stroll and soak it all in.

A few shops also sell local treats, making it a great place to sit down and rest if you’re tired from riding.

https://youtu.be/XRR7ddLAiV4

Then we hopped onto a traditional local spider boat (Jukung) to head out and spot dolphins.

### 🧘‍♀️ Taman Beji Griya Waterfall｜Five-Stage Holy Water Purification Ritual

Our first stop is Taman Beji Griya Waterfall, a purification sanctuary that has become increasingly popular in recent years.

![‍ Taman Beji Griya Waterfall｜五階段聖水淨化儀式](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-06/1781016977848-beji.jpg)

Photo courtesy of Lai Jiaying at Taman Beji Griya Waterfall

It's best known for the 'Five-Stage Holy Water Purification Ritual (Melukat).'

Unlike typical waterfalls you visit on your own, the entire process here is guided by a specialist—from prayers and cleansing to the holy water blessings at each stage, offering a complete sense of ceremony.

Even for non-believers, you can feel how deeply purification and blessings are valued in Balinese culture.

If you're a woman joining a ceremony, it's best to wear a swimsuit underneath for convenience.

Besides Melukat, the complex offers various other Balinese healing experiences, such as blessing ceremonies and energy treatments, though most come at an additional cost.

### 🏠 An Unexpected Twist｜Invited to the Driver's Home

What came next turned out to be one of the most unique experiences of the entire trip.

The driver suddenly warmly invited me to his house for a visit.

To be honest, if it were my first time in Bali, I probably wouldn't have dared to say yes.

But after spending a few days together and getting familiar, I decided to go along and see.

When we arrived, I realized this was an experience that ordinary tourists could never easily access.

The driver showed me around a traditional Balinese house compound, explaining the family shrine, courtyard, and the living spaces for each family member.

It was completely different from the Bali you see in hotels and tourist spots.

This was real life.

The driver's mother runs a small shop.

As soon as she saw visitors arriving, she immediately brought out drinks and snacks to welcome us.

Even though we couldn't really communicate in the same language, I could still feel their overwhelming warmth and hospitality.

What surprised me even more was that they kept insisting I stay for dinner.

That kind of wholehearted hospitality is truly touching.

Sometimes the most precious travel memories aren't about the sights, but the people.
