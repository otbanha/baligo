**Key Takeaways**: This is a real-tested July peak season travelogue covering **Seminyak → Uluwatu → Jimbaran → Ubud**. Two most useful conclusions: ① Drivers in Bali often refuse cross-region trips or hike up prices midway—**full-day private car hire** (with driver Komang WikaSutresna, contact info at the end) is the most hassle-free solution; ② Arrive at Oneeighty Dayclub **within the first hour of opening** to snag front-row ocean views, and ask your driver to pre-book Uluwatu Kecak fire dance tickets to skip the queue.

> Ticket prices for these attractions fluctuate with the peak season, so check the latest reference prices on the **[Bali Ticket Price List](/en/tickets/)** before you go.

> The following content was contributed by group member [Lian Yuzhun](https://www.facebook.com/groups/baligo/permalink/1574999756380597/) and rearranged by us:

Since we were attending a friend's wedding, we chose to visit Bali in July. Travel information about Bali is incredibly scattered, and it took us two to three months to piece together our itinerary.

![【水明漾-烏魯瓦圖-金巴蘭-烏布】峇里島自由行完整行程分享](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_a22d6c37c8a63306b663eaf1af023ba8.jpg)

During the pre-trip planning, we discovered that local traffic is chaotic—drivers often refuse cross-region passengers or hike up prices midway. To avoid these headaches, we decided to hire a private driver. This not only saved us the trouble of finding rides but also made our journey smoother and more reassuring. Our driver was Komang WikaSutresna (details at the bottom). In the end, we felt that choosing a private car was a very wise decision!

## Day 1:

![水明漾夕陽。連育諄提供](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_6dc896b39c10af9bac23d825129c22b9.jpg)

Seminyak sunset. Photo by Lian Yuzhun

Having heard that Seminyak traffic is extremely congested, we chose to explore on foot in the morning without a car. We arranged with our driver to pick us up from Seminyak in the evening and take us to our villa in Uluwatu. Komang arrived early at the meeting point. We originally planned to go out for dinner on our own after reaching the villa, but Komang checked Google Street View along the way and noticed the road from the villa to the main street was very dark and unsafe to walk at night. When we arrived, he waited at the entrance until we checked in, then drove us to dinner. This thoughtful service made us feel truly warm.

## Day 2:

In the morning, we went to Oneeighty Dayclub—highly recommend this place. We arrived within an hour of opening, so we snagged front-row seats with ocean views.

A quick heads-up: Uluwatu Temple is full of monkeys—more aggressive than those at Taiwan's Chaishan. They'll snatch anything from both men and women, and we even saw plenty of shattered glasses on the ground. So it's best not to bring any valuables when you go.

## Day Three:

Today we attended a wedding, so we didn't arrange Komang's car service.

## Day 4:

Today's itinerary took us from Jimbaran to Ubud. Along the way, we discovered that the restaurant we planned to visit was temporarily closed. Komang immediately called to confirm and also checked the waterfall's official website, finding it closed due to the heavy rainstorm the day before (despite it being the dry season). However, Komang quickly offered us new suggestions, allowing us to smoothly complete the day's plans. After visiting the waterfall, we returned to Ubud's town center for a stroll, and finally asked Komang to take us for a massage to end the day.

Additionally, the Gefecon Villa Ubud where we stayed that night had a Taiwanese supervisor who was incredibly warm and helpful—she was happy to answer any questions we had.

## Day 5:

![聖泉寺。連育諄提供](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_dbc6e9fddb7c4642b0a7e4bfee0a2182.jpg)

Tirta Empul Temple. Photo by Lien Yu-Chun

The day's itinerary formed an O-shape, starting at Tirta Empul Temple, then heading to a café with a view of the volcano. We originally looked up AKASA Specialty Coffee, but Komang recommended another spot, Montana Del Café, saying that Taiwanese people seem to like it. Although the weather was bad and we couldn't see the volcano, the Korean-style croissants here were absolutely delicious!

https://youtube.com/shorts/fiYs5GKdINU

![Montana Del Café可惜天氣不好。連育諄提供](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_8bba492232d63a05b16658986210416d.jpg)

Montana Del Café — too bad the weather wasn't good. Photo by Lien Yu-Chun

Finally, we went to Tegallalang Rice Terrace. The entrance fee doesn't include the swing ticket — if you want to ride, you need to buy a ticket at the gate. There are also special outfits available for rent inside, perfect for taking beautiful photos. Although it leans toward being an Instagram hotspot, it's still enjoyable to wander through the rice terraces and listen to the screams of people on the swings.

https://youtu.be/op78Dk8CjL4

> For reference: [The Complete Ubud Guide: 100 Ways to Deeply Explore Ubud | Discover Ubud's Charm](/en/blog/ubud-guide/)

After the tour, we asked Komang to take us to our favorite restaurant of the trip — Kebun Bistro. Although their famous lamb chops were sold out, the steak recommended by the staff was incredibly delicious! We ordered it medium-rare, and when cut, there was no blood; the meat was very tender, and even the side dishes were tasty. Since it gets crowded, it's best to make a reservation ahead of meal times. (Already added to the [Ubud Guide Map](https://www.google.com/maps/d/u/0/edit?mid=1jrOaKVOZ4jpcB_kprr67w3ltg3WPbf4&usp=sharing))

## Day 6:

In the morning, we set off from Ubud to Tanah Lot Temple. You may need to wade through water to reach the temple, so it's best to wear water-friendly shoes and shorts. I've heard the sunset view is stunning, but unfortunately, our schedule placed us there at noon, so we couldn't enjoy it.

[Bali Must-Visit Attractions: Detailed Guide to Tanah Lot Temple, Tides, and Nearby Attractions](/en/blog/bali-tanah-lot-guide/)

When we arrived at Tanah Lot, we realized we had accidentally taken the key from our previous hotel. Komang immediately said he could return it for us in a couple of days (after getting approval from the hotel manager). We were so grateful to him.

After visiting Tanah Lot, we headed to Canggu for some shopping and a beach stroll. We tried Blacklist Coffee Roasters, recommended by Feli, and it turned out to be our favorite coffee spot on this Bali trip.

## Recommended Private Driver Service

In summary, for first-time visitors to Bali who want to explore attractions but don't have a clear plan, hiring a private driver is an excellent choice. Not only do you get professional advice, but the route planning is also more convenient, and you don't have to worry about luggage.

Our driver, Komang Wika Sutresna, is a very friendly person. When he heard we were attending a wedding, he was thrilled and even shared his wedding video from six months ago, along with a video call with his adorable son. Komang speaks good English and is very attentive. You can ask him anything during the trip, and he will patiently answer.

Komang is very dedicated to every guest. Throughout our journey, he was always meticulous—getting out of the car at each destination to explain how to explore the area. When we went to the spa, he greeted the staff to ensure our needs were well taken care of. Although language barriers prevented deep conversations, communication regarding the itinerary was never an issue. We highly recommend Komang to everyone!
