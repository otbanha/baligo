# Bali Traveler Hot Topics + News Roundup｜August 28, 2026

## 🛂 Bali's First Mall 'Immigration Service Center' Opens, Offering Passport and Residence Services at Discovery Mall

For expats living in Bali, a highly practical new service has arrived today.

Indonesia's Directorate General of Immigration has officially launched the Immigration Lounge at **Discovery Mall Bali in Kuta**, marking Bali's first immigration service point located inside a shopping mall.

Currently, services include Indonesian passport and foreign residence-related applications, with the residence service handling over **150 applications** daily. Operating hours are Monday to Friday **10:00 AM–5:00 PM WITA**.

Officials report that the center currently sees an average of **100–120 foreigners** per day for residence-related matters.

For short-term travelers, this service won't directly impact their trip, but it's a major convenience for those planning to stay long-term, extend their stay, or handle visa matters in Bali.

Plus, its location at **Kartika Plaza, Discovery Mall** makes it especially convenient for those staying in the Kuta area.

## ✈️ Bali Airport Surpasses 13.2 Million Passengers in First 7 Months, Reaching 4 Million Foreign Travelers

Bali's tourism momentum continues.

Ngurah Rai International Airport has released its latest data: **from January to July 2026, it served a total of 13.2 million passengers**, including approximately **8.5 million international travelers** and 4.7 million domestic travelers. During the same period, around 80,000 flights took off and landed.

Among them, Australian travelers accounted for about 1 million, or 25%, followed by:

China: 358,000
India: 327,000
South Korea: 174,000
UK: 172,000

Currently, international routes are operated by 44 airlines, connecting 42 routes across 16 countries.

This also explains why, when traveling to Bali in July and August, **the density of tourists at the airport, major attractions, and popular accommodation areas noticeably increases**.

If you have an early morning or late-night flight coming up, it's still wise to allow extra time for airport transfers.

## 🌾 Jatiluwih Isn't Just About Rice Terraces! Now Offering 'Rice Field Experience' Where Visitors Can Join the Harvest

If you've already visited Jatiluwih, you might think it's just about 'seeing rice terraces, taking photos, and eating.'

But the local scene is changing now.

The Jatiluwih tourist area has recently introduced interactive activities, allowing visitors to go beyond just viewing rice terraces from observation decks and instead **participate in rice harvesting, learn about traditional farming tools, and experience local agricultural life**.

It's currently harvest season there, making it a perfect time to experience Bali's traditional farming culture.

Jatiluwih is now in its peak tourist season, drawing an average of **1,500 visitors per day**, mostly from Europe, with the high season expected to last until the end of September.

This approach is far more meaningful than simply adding more 'photo spots.'

If you've already been to Tegallalang and are looking for a place to truly soak in Bali's rural culture, **Jatiluwih is worth dedicating half a day to a full day—far more rewarding than just checking in for photos.**

## 🌾 Bali Tightens Restrictions on Farmland Conversion, New Government Rules Protect Subak

The Bali provincial government today reiterated its commitment to stricter control over the conversion of agricultural land, aiming to prevent rice paddies from being continuously turned into housing, commercial developments, or other uses.

Governor Wayan Koster stated that productive farmland in Bali is steadily shrinking, putting pressure on the traditional irrigation system known as **Subak**.

According to data from BPS Bali, the area of rice paddies on the island has decreased from **78,626 hectares in 2017 to approximately 68,078 hectares today**.

This year, the Balinese government has also strengthened farmland protection through **Provincial Regulation No. 4 of 2026**, banning the transfer of agricultural land ownership via nominee arrangements.

Additionally, the government aims to encourage the tourism industry—such as hotels and restaurants—to increase their use of local Balinese agricultural products, providing farmers with a more stable market.

In the long run, this policy will actually affect the Bali that travelers see.

If farmland protection becomes stricter in the future, Bali may slow down the pace of development where rice fields are turned into villas, which would help preserve the Subak irrigation system, terraced landscapes, and rural culture.

## 🇫🇷 French national deported from Bali for allegedly producing and selling inappropriate content

Bali's immigration office announced today the deportation of a **30-year-old French national, LR**.

According to the immigration investigation, LR held a visitor stay permit and entered Bali on July 20 with a VoA. He was later reported for allegedly producing, promoting, and selling videos containing inappropriate content through digital platforms.

After the investigation, immigration authorities determined he had violated relevant regulations, imposed administrative immigration measures, and deported him from Ngurah Rai International Airport back to France on August 27.

Bali's immigration unit also reiterated that monitoring foreign nationals goes beyond checking visas and stay permits, and includes **the actual activities they engage in within Indonesia**.

This is the latest case in Bali's ongoing efforts to tighten oversight of foreign nationals.

For the average traveler, the key point is simple:

**Holding a valid visa does not mean you can engage in any activity in Indonesia.**

Especially for activities involving business, work, content creation, or others that may require specific permits, it's best to check the relevant regulations first.
