## 3 AM: Chasing a Volcano Sunrise on No Sleep

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791370933544-825327516_29012696578416915_6483081757367183897_n.jpg)

(Photo: Courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

It's 3 AM, and I'm up ridiculously early—honestly, I haven't slept a wink. I hop into a jeep solo, ready to catch the volcano sunrise.

The road condition? Let's just say 'absolutely terrible'—bumpy like a roller coaster, totally insane.

The sky's still pitch black. I'm dropped off at a waiting lounge to watch the sunrise, but there's nothing to see yet. I order a hot chocolate—20,000 IDR a cup.

What surprised me most on this trip was the jeep driver’s photography skills. It was pitch black, and I thought there was no way to get a good shot. But he took out his phone, manually circled it around me to create light, and I still have no idea how he pulled it off. The results were surprisingly impressive—truly masterful technique.

Then, the sun came out—and it was absolutely beautiful!

## Black Lava, Volcano Café, and a Fairy-Tale Restaurant

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791383961624-mixcollage-07-oct-2026-09-26-pm-1834.jpg)

(Photo by Chen Chenxi)

After watching the sunrise, the jeep bounced its way to the next stop: the Black Lava. The road was just as bumpy as before.

The driver shared the history of the place with me, explaining in English how the lava flowed down, then hardened into rock-like formations. But honestly, there wasn’t much to see—just a lot of volcanic rocks.

Next, we arrived at a café called **Amora**, where you can directly admire the volcano. The view was absolutely stunning—truly breathtaking.

Next up is a restaurant with incredibly impressive art installations — **Taman Dedari (Fairy Restaurant)**. It features a stunning fairy-themed landscape surrounded by lush greenery, absolutely beautiful.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384009109-bidadari.jpg)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

The author says this is a place where **the scenery far outweighs the food**: the dishes are average, but the installations are truly stunning — the most beautiful of any restaurant she's visited so far. The seating is spacious, and it's not overly crowded.

## A Series of Minor Mishaps in One Morning

That's solo travel for you — you have to handle everything on your own.

That morning, a major crisis struck: my tripod suddenly went missing. I had to rush to a local shop and buy a temporary octopus tripod. But right after buying it, I realized I'd left my microphone at the hotel.

So I called a Grab motorcycle and asked the driver to take me back to the hotel to grab it, giving him a little extra tip.

It was just one of those mornings where nothing went right!

## Ordered Dirty Duck Rice Again, But Got Braised Chicken Instead

At Fairy Restaurant, I ordered the dirty duck rice again because the previous place was so delicious. This time, the cooking method seems a bit different—let me give it a try.

Wow, so tender! A completely different style—this one is softer and mushier. Later, I realized it might be **Balinese-style braised chicken**, not exactly dirty duck rice.

The side dish this time is long beans, which I'm not a fan of. The biggest downside of this place is probably that it's outdoors, with quite a few flies buzzing around. However, its landscaping is the most beautiful of all the restaurants so far. Comparatively, the first place was more impressive in flavor; this one is a bit more ordinary, but as a scenic restaurant, it's decent.

## Alas Harum: Rice Terraces, Luwak Coffee, and a Zipline That Ends in a Flash

Next up is the wildly popular **Alas Harum**. Before entering, you have to go through security, and **no water bottles are allowed**—you must leave them at the counter.

## Alas Harum Coffee Tour

I joined a coffee tour, just the two of us (Yeah, two). There's a civet cat, the star of luwak coffee.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384062561-coffee_making.jpeg)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

The staff explained the production process:

- The beans are roasted for about 45 minutes to an hour, stirred continuously until they change color and release their aroma.
- Grinding coffee beans the traditional way takes about 20 to 30 minutes until the powder becomes fine
- I also tried my hand at grinding coffee

This short coffee tour was pretty cool.

## Alas Harum: Commercialization and Ticket Redemption Tips

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384122738-alas1.jpg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

But it's a bit too commercialized here—every attraction requires a number ticket and waiting.

Let me share a solo travel pitfall: **If you booked tickets online, you can actually just walk straight down to redeem them**. I thought I had to go to the ticket office, but the redemption point was further down, so I wasted time queuing for nothing. After getting a number, the pace was slow, and it wasn't that thrilling.

> **Author's Note**: It's crowded here, and you can't bring water inside. The queue for the swings is insane, but other attractions (like the zipline) have no wait at all—arrive early if you can.

The zipline? It's over in a flash—gone in an instant.

## Cretya Ubud: A Three-Tiered Pool with Rice Terrace Backdrop

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384158740-mixcollage-07-oct-2026-09-24-pm-1702.jpg)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

The park features various landscapes, with most basic facilities concentrated in the center: restrooms, changing rooms, showers, lockers, and more. Lockers and towels require an additional rental fee.

After dropping off my things, I headed downstairs first. **Cretya Ubud** is the pool club inside Alas Harum, and the entrance ticket can be exchanged for one drink.

- There are **three levels of pools**
- The round one is a **hot spa**
- The backdrop features stunning rice terraces
- The atmosphere is more laid-back, perfect for a leisurely chat to spend the afternoon

> Quick reference: [Alas Harum Guide: Ubud Rice Terrace Paradise, Cretya Ubud, Cretya Sunset Complete Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/alas-harum/)

## Teba Sari: Balinese Freshwater Fish Wrapped in Leaves

Next to Alas Harum, there's a restaurant called **Teba Sari**. Today I ordered Balinese freshwater fish wrapped in leaves, and it started with crackers served with Sambal chili sauce.

This grilled fish has a strong spice aroma with a slight kick. Since the flavor is quite bold, it goes really well with rice. I think this place is better than the scenic restaurant we had at lunch—the food there was just average, and while the view was stunning, there were bugs. Here, the view is nothing special, but the food definitely stands out.

## Taking a Grab Alone: The Driver Was a Maniac

After dinner, I took a Grab from Ubud to the Bintang Supermarket in Seminyak. The driver I got was an absolute maniac—speeding the whole way, weaving through traffic like crazy, and somehow still finding time to chat with me nonstop. Incredible.

So here’s a tip for solo travelers: if you can, consider taking a **Grab bike**. It’s cheaper and super convenient if you don’t have much luggage. But some drivers really do race like mad, which can be terrifying—you have to be okay with that.

## Supermarket, Bintang Beer, and a 140,000 IDR Foot Massage

The supermarket was a lot of fun to browse—they had everything you could possibly need.

- **Chocolate**: I heard it’s delicious. What I saw was Belgian chocolate. Since Bali grows cacao beans, Balinese chocolate is also quite famous—definitely worth a try.
- **Bintang Beer**: I’ve heard it’s a must-drink in Bali, and everyone seems to recommend the lemon flavor.

> Quick reference: [【Recommended by Netizens】Six Categories, Over 30 Must-Buy Items, Bali Souvenir Guide: The Best Way to Keep Precious Memories](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-souvenirs-gift-guide/)

Next, I tried the affordable foot massage recommended by Threads users. A 60-minute foot massage costs only **140,000 IDR**—seriously cheap.

But the service and atmosphere were pretty average: it was my first time seeing foot washing done with just a quick rinse of plain water, and there were no extra tea or snacks—just a straightforward massage. Still, the place was packed with people, everyone wanting to come, probably because it was such a good deal.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384196406-massage.jpeg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

In the evening, I leisurely enjoyed a beer by myself. Bintang—it's really good!

## South to Nusa Dua: Pandawa Beach

Next, I headed to the southernmost **Nusa Dua**. In the morning, I visited **Pandawa Beach**.

- Entrance fee: **30,000 IDR**
- Shuttle to the cliff road: **30,000 IDR**

The cliffs along the way were stunning, and the shuttle took only about 3 minutes—super fast. The long wait earlier was just because there were too many people ahead of me. On the way back, you don't have to take the shuttle; you can walk down yourself in about 15 minutes.

> **Author's note**: The famous cliff road is right here, with lots of tourists, so getting a good photo depends on the angle.

This beach is incredibly beautiful and not crowded at all. The water is crystal clear, even more transparent than other beaches, perfect for a leisurely afternoon.

## Riug Paragliding: 10 Minutes Overlooking the Indian Ocean

https://youtube.com/shorts/eCTaZX10U7Y?feature=share

Next up was paragliding. The staff let me bring two cameras at once: his GoPro and my own 360 camera, shooting from both sides.

I chose to fly for just **10 minutes**, one speed and done, overlooking the Indian Ocean—absolutely stunning.

Once you're up in the air, it's actually very stable—just windy. Holding a selfie stick for a while can make your arm a bit sore. You might feel slightly dizzy during turns; the instructions say to **look in the direction of the turn** to avoid getting too dizzy.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791374059428-825327724_29012696465083593_8807600909330091373_n.jpg)

(Photo: Chen Chenxi)

> **Author's Note**: For just over USD$30, this 10-minute paragliding experience is well worth it. The included GoPro only captures yourself, so I brought my own 360-degree camera to also capture the stunning Indian Ocean views.

## FINNS: Laid-Back by Day, a Whole New World by Night

Finally, we arrived at **FINNS**. Technically, entry is free, but I didn't want to wait in line, so I bought the fast pass. I just checked the general entrance, and it's packed right now.

**Entry Guidelines:**

- You'll go through security first. Professional camera gear and water bottles are not allowed inside.
- The dining area with live performers has free seating, and there are plenty of entertainers around.
- Staff will guide you to the counter, and payments are made via a number card system.
- The fast pass includes a **240,000 IDR** credit, plus a locker and towel.

During the day, FINNS has a more laid-back vibe, but once night falls, it transforms into something completely different...

"Welcome to the world's best beach club!"

And here, I met two Japanese girls, then ran into them again and again—multiple times. (That's the story behind the video title 'Encountering Adorable Japanese Girls.')

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384246110-finns.png)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

**Author's Note**: FINNS is more relaxed before sunset but gets especially lively after dark. It's recommended to **arrive before 7 PM** to catch the fireworks.
