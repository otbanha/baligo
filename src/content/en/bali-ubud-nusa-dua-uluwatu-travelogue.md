## 3 AM: Chasing a Volcano Sunrise Without Sleep

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791370933544-825327516_29012696578416915_6483081757367183897_n.jpg)

(Photo: Courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

It's 3 AM, and I'm up ridiculously early—honestly, I haven't slept at all. I hop into a jeep solo, ready to catch the volcano sunrise.

The road is nothing short of 'absolutely terrible'—bumpy as a roller coaster, totally insane.

The sky is still pitch black. I'm dropped off at a waiting room to watch the sunrise, but for now, I can't see a thing. I order a hot chocolate—20,000 IDR a cup.

What surprised me most on this trip was the jeep driver's photography skills. It was pitch black, and I thought there was no way to capture anything, but he took out his phone and manually circled light around me. I still have no idea how he pulled it off, but the results were surprisingly impressive—truly masterful technique.

Then, the sun came out, and it was stunning!

## Black Lava, Volcano Café, and a Fairytale Restaurant

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791383961624-mixcollage-07-oct-2026-09-26-pm-1834.jpg)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

After watching the sunrise, the jeep bounced its way to the next stop, the 'Black Lava'—and the road was just as bumpy.

The driver shared the history of the place with me in English: the lava flowed down, then hardened, turning into something like stone. But honestly, there wasn't much to see—just a lot of volcanic rock.

Next, we stopped at a café called **Amora**, with a direct view of the volcano. The scenery was absolutely spectacular—truly breathtaking.

Next up is a restaurant with incredibly impressive art installations—**Taman Dedari (Fairy Restaurant)**. It features a stunning fairy-themed landscape surrounded by lush greenery, absolutely beautiful.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384009109-bidadari.jpg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

The author says this is a place where **the scenery far outweighs the food**: the dishes are average, but the installations are truly stunning—the most beautiful of any restaurant she's ever been to. The seats are spacious, and it's not overly crowded.

## A Series of Minor Mishaps in One Morning

That's solo travel for you—you have to handle everything on your own.

That morning, a major crisis struck: my tripod suddenly went missing. I had to rush to a local store and buy a makeshift octopus tripod on the spot. But right after buying it, I realized I had left my microphone at the hotel.

So I called a Grab bike and asked the driver to take me back to the hotel to grab it, even giving him a little extra tip.

It was just one of those mornings where nothing went right!

## Ordered Dirty Duck Rice Again, But Got Braised Chicken Instead

Back at the Fairy Restaurant, I ordered the dirty duck rice again because the first place was just so good. This time the cooking method seemed a bit different—let me give it a try.

Wow, so tender! Completely different style—this one is softer and mushier. Later I realized it might actually be **Balinese-style braised chicken**, not really dirty duck rice.

The side dish this time was long beans, not my favorite. The biggest downside of this place is probably that it's outdoors, with quite a few flies buzzing around. But its landscaping is the most beautiful of all the restaurants I've visited so far. Comparatively, the first place was more impressive in flavor; this one is a bit more ordinary, but as a scenic restaurant, it's still pretty good.

## Alas Harum: Rice Terraces, Luwak Coffee, and a Zipline That Ends in a Flash

Next up is the super popular **Alas Harum**. You have to go through security before entering, and **no outside water is allowed**—you need to leave it at the front counter.

## Alas Harum Coffee Tour

I joined a coffee tour—just two of us (Yeah, two). There's a civet cat, the star of luwak coffee.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384062561-coffee_making.jpeg)

(Photo provided by Chen Chenxi)

The staff explained the production process:

- The beans are roasted for about 45 minutes to an hour, stirred continuously until they change color and the aroma comes out.
- It takes about 20 to 30 minutes to grind coffee beans into a fine powder using traditional methods.
- I also tried my hand at grinding coffee.

This short coffee tour was pretty cool.

## Alas Harum: Commercialization and Ticket Redemption Tips

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384122738-alas1.jpg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

However, it feels a bit overly commercialized here—every attraction requires a queue number and waiting.

Let me share a solo travel pitfall: **If you booked tickets online, you can actually just walk straight down to redeem them**. I initially thought I had to go to the ticket office, but the redemption point was further down, so I wasted time waiting in line for nothing. After getting a queue number, the process was quite slow, and the rides weren't that thrilling.

> **Author's Note**: The place is very crowded, and you can't bring water inside. The queue for the swings is incredibly long, while other attractions (like the zip line) have no wait at all—I recommend arriving early.

The zip line? It's over in a flash—gone in an instant.

## Cretya Ubud: A Three-Tiered Pool with a Rice Terrace Backdrop

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384158740-mixcollage-07-oct-2026-09-24-pm-1702.jpg)

(Photo provided by Chen Chenxi)

The park features a variety of landscapes, with most basic facilities concentrated in the center: restrooms, changing rooms, showers, lockers, and more. Lockers and towels require an additional rental fee.

After putting my things away, I headed downstairs first. **Cretya Ubud** is a pool club within Alas Harum, and the entrance ticket can be exchanged for one drink.

- There are **three levels of pools**
- The circular one is a **hot spa**
- The backdrop features stunning rice terraces
- The atmosphere is more laid-back, perfect for a leisurely chat to spend the afternoon

> Quick reference: [Alas Harum Guide: Ubud Rice Terrace Paradise, Cretya Ubud, Cretya Sunset Complete Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/alas-harum/)

## Teba Sari: Balinese Freshwater Fish Wrapped in Leaves

Right next to Alas Harum is a restaurant called **Teba Sari**. Today I ordered Balinese freshwater fish wrapped in leaves, and it started with crackers served with Sambal chili sauce.

This grilled fish is quite heavy on the spices, with a slight kick of heat. Since the flavors are bold, it pairs really well with rice. I think this place is a bit better than the scenic restaurant we had at lunch—the lunch spot had pretty ordinary food, with the main draw being the beautiful views, though there were bugs. Here, the views are just average, but the food wins out.

## Taking a Grab Solo: The Driver Was a Madman

After eating, I took a Grab from Ubud to the Bintang Supermarket in Seminyak. The driver I got this time was like a madman—speeding the whole way, weaving through traffic like crazy, and somehow still finding time to chat with me nonstop. Incredible.

So, for anyone thinking of solo travel: if circumstances allow, you might want to take a **Grab bike**—it's cheaper and convenient if you don't have much luggage. But some drivers really do race like maniacs, which can be scary, so you need to be okay with that.

## Supermarket, Bintang Beer, and a 140,000 IDR Foot Massage

The supermarket was fun to browse—they had everything you could want.

- **Chocolate**: I heard it's really good. What I saw was Belgian chocolate. Bali produces cacao beans, so Balinese chocolate is also famous—worth a try.
- **Bintang Beer**: I've heard it's a must-try in Bali, and everyone seems to recommend the lemon flavor.

> Quick reference: [【Recommended by Netizens】Six Categories, Over 30 Must-Buy Items, Bali Souvenir Guide: The Perfect Choice to Cherish Precious Memories](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-souvenirs-gift-guide/)

Next, I tried the affordable foot massage recommended by Threads users. A 60-minute foot massage costs just **140,000 IDR**—seriously cheap.

But the service and atmosphere were just average: this was the first time I saw them rinse my feet with just plain water and nothing else—no tea or snacks, just a straightforward massage. Still, the place was packed with people, everyone wanting to come, probably because it's such a great deal.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384196406-massage.jpeg)

(Photo: Chen Chenxi)

In the evening, I leisurely enjoyed a beer by myself. Bintang—it's really good!

## Heading South to Nusa Dua: Pandawa Beach

Next, I headed to the southernmost **Nusa Dua**. In the morning, I visited **Pandawa Beach**.

- Entry ticket: **30,000 IDR**
- Shuttle to the cliff road: **30,000 IDR**

The cliffs along the way were stunning, and the shuttle took only about 3 minutes—super fast. The long wait earlier was just because of the crowd. On the way back, you don't have to take the shuttle; you can also walk down, which takes about 15 minutes.

> **Author's note**: The famous cliff road is right here, packed with tourists, and getting a good photo depends a lot on the angle.

This beach is gorgeous and not crowded at all. The water is incredibly clear—more transparent than any other beach—perfect for a lazy afternoon.

## Riug Paragliding: 10 Minutes Over the Indian Ocean

https://youtube.com/shorts/eCTaZX10U7Y?feature=share

Next up was paragliding. The staff let me bring two cameras at once: his GoPro and my own 360 camera, shooting from both sides.

I chose to fly for just **10 minutes**, ending after one speed run, soaring over the Indian Ocean—absolutely stunning.

Once you're up in the air, it's actually very stable—just the wind is strong, and holding a selfie stick for a while can make your arm a bit sore. Turning can make you feel slightly dizzy; the instructions say to **look in the direction of the turn** to avoid getting too dizzy.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791374059428-825327724_29012696465083593_8807600909330091373_n.jpg)

(Photo: Chen Chenxi)

> **Author's note**: For just over USD$30, this paragliding experience is well worth the 10-minute flight. The included GoPro only captures yourself, so I brought my own 360-degree camera to also capture the stunning Indian Ocean views.

## FINNS: Relaxed by Day, a Different World by Night

Finally, we arrived at **FINNS**. Technically, entry is free, but I didn't want to wait in line, so I bought a fast-pass. I checked the general entrance earlier, and it's packed with people right now.

**Entry Guidelines:**

- You'll go through security first; professional camera gear and water are not allowed inside.
- The dining area with live performers has free seating, and there are plenty of entertainers around.
- Staff will guide you to the counter, where you pay using a numbered card system.
- The fast-pass includes **240,000 IDR** in credit, plus a locker and towel.

During the day, FINNS feels more laid-back, but once night falls, it transforms into something completely different...

"Welcome to the world's best beach club!"

And here, I met two Japanese girls, then ran into them again—and again, for the Nth time. (That's the story behind the video title, 'Encountering Adorable Japanese Girls.')

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384246110-finns.png)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

> **Author's Note**: FINNS is more relaxed before sunset, but gets especially lively after dark. It's recommended to **arrive before 7 PM** to catch the fireworks.
