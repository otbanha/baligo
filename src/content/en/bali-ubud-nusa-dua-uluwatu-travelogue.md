## 3 a.m., Chasing a Volcanic Sunrise Without Sleep

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791370933544-825327516_29012696578416915_6483081757367183897_n.jpg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

It's 3 a.m., and I'm up ridiculously early—honestly, I haven't slept a wink. I hop into a jeep solo, ready to catch the volcanic sunrise.

The road is in what I can only describe as 'absolutely terrible' condition—bumpy as a roller coaster, totally insane.

The sky is still pitch black. I'm dropped off at a waiting room to watch the sunrise, but for now, there's nothing to see. I order a hot chocolate—20,000 IDR a cup.

What surprised me most on this trip was the jeep driver's photography skills. It was pitch black, and I thought there was no way to capture anything, but he took out his phone and manually circled it around me to create light. I still have no idea how he pulled it off, but the results were surprisingly impressive—his technique was truly masterful.

Then, the sun came out, and it was stunning!

## Black Lava, Volcano Café, and a Fairy-Tale Restaurant

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791383961624-mixcollage-07-oct-2026-09-26-pm-1834.jpg)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

After watching the sunrise, the jeep rattled on to the next stop—the Black Lava—and the road was just as bumpy.

The driver shared the history of the place with me in English: the lava flowed down, then hardened, turning into something like stone. But honestly, there wasn't much to see here—just a lot of volcanic rock.

Next, we stopped at a café called **Amora**, where you can directly view the volcano. The scenery was absolutely spectacular—truly breathtaking.

Next up is a restaurant with incredibly impressive art installations—**Taman Dedari (Fairy Restaurant)**. It features a stunning fairy-themed setup, surrounded by lush greenery that’s absolutely beautiful.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384009109-bidadari.jpg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

The author says this is a place where **the scenery far outshines the food**: the dishes are average, but the installations are truly stunning—the most beautiful of any restaurant they’ve visited. The seating is spacious, and it’s not overly crowded.

## A Morning of Minor Mishaps

That’s solo travel for you—you have to handle everything on your own.

A major crisis hit this morning: my tripod suddenly went missing. I had to rush to a local store and buy a makeshift octopus tripod on the spot. Just after buying it, I realized I’d left my microphone back at the hotel.

So I called a Grab bike and asked the driver to take me back to the hotel to grab it, throwing in a little extra tip.

It was just one of those mornings where nothing went right!

## Ordered Dirty Duck Rice Again, Got Braised Chicken Instead

At the Fairy Restaurant, I ordered dirty duck rice again because the last place made it so delicious. This time, the cooking method seems a bit different—let me give it a try.

Wow, so tender! A completely different style—this one is a bit softer and mushier. Later I checked, and it seems to be a **Balinese-style braised chicken**, not really a crispy duck dish.

The side dish this time was long beans, not my favorite. The biggest downside of this place is probably that it's outdoors, with quite a few flies buzzing around. However, its landscaping is the most beautiful of all the restaurants so far. Comparatively, the first place was more impressive in flavor; this one is a bit more ordinary, but as a scenic restaurant, it's still pretty good.

## Alas Harum: Rice Terraces, Luwak Coffee, and a Zipline That Ends in a Flash

Next up is the super popular **Alas Harum**. You have to go through security before entering, and **you can't bring water**—water must be left at the front counter.

## Alas Harum Coffee Tour

I joined a coffee tour, just the two of us (Yeah, two). There were civet cats, the stars of luwak coffee.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384062561-coffee_making.jpeg)

(Photo provided by Chen Chenxi)

The staff explained the production process:

- Roasting the beans for about 45 minutes to an hour, stirring continuously until they change color and the aroma emerges
- Grinding into coffee powder using traditional methods, taking about 20 to 30 minutes until the powder becomes fine
- I also tried grinding coffee

This short coffee tour was pretty cool.

## Alas Harum: Commercialization and Ticket Exchange Tips

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384122738-alas1.jpg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

But it's a bit too commercial here—every attraction requires a queue number and waiting.

Let me share a solo travel lesson: **If you booked online, you can actually just walk straight down to exchange your ticket.** I thought I had to go to the ticket office, but the exchange counter was further down, so I wasted time waiting in line for nothing. After getting the queue number, the pace was slow, and it wasn't that thrilling.

> **Author's note**: It's very crowded here, and you can't bring water inside. The queue for the swing is insane, while other attractions (like the zipline) have no wait at all. I recommend arriving early.

The zipline? It's over in an instant—gone in a flash.

## Cretya Ubud: A Three-Tiered Pool with Rice Terrace Backdrop

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384158740-mixcollage-07-oct-2026-09-24-pm-1702.jpg)

(Photo: Provided by Chen Chenxi)

The park features a variety of landscapes, with most basic facilities concentrated in the center: restrooms, changing rooms, showers, lockers, and more. Lockers and towels require an additional rental fee.

After dropping off my things, I headed downstairs. **Cretya Ubud** is a pool club within Alas Harum, and the entrance ticket can be exchanged for one drink.

- There are **three levels of pools**
- The circular one is a **hot spa**
- The backdrop features stunning rice terraces
- The vibe is more laid-back, perfect for a leisurely chat to spend the afternoon

> Quick reference: [Alas Harum Guide: Ubud Rice Terrace Paradise, Cretya Ubud, Cretya Sunset Complete Guide](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/alas-harum/)

## Teba Sari: Balinese Freshwater Fish Wrapped in Leaves

Right next to Alas Harum is a restaurant called **Teba Sari**. Today I ordered Balinese freshwater fish wrapped in leaves, starting with crackers served with Sambal chili sauce.

This grilled fish is quite aromatic with spices and a slight kick. Since the flavors are bold, it pairs perfectly with rice. I think this place is a bit better than the scenic restaurant I visited at noon: the midday spot had mediocre food, though the views were stunning—but there were bugs; here, the views are average, but the food wins out.

## Riding Grab Solo: The Driver Was a Mad Dog

After dinner, I took a Grab from Ubud to the Bintang Supermarket in Seminyak. The driver I got this time was like a mad dog—speeding the whole way, weaving through traffic like crazy, and somehow still had time to chat with me nonstop. Absolutely insane.

So I want to share this with solo travelers: if circumstances allow, you can actually take a **Grab motorcycle**—it's cheaper and convenient if you don't have much luggage. But some drivers really do race like maniacs, which can be terrifying, so you need to be okay with that.

## Supermarket, Bintang, and a 140,000 IDR Foot Massage

The supermarket was fun to browse—they had everything you could want.

- **Chocolate**: I heard it's delicious. What I saw was Belgian chocolate. Bali produces cacao beans, so Balinese chocolate is also famous—worth a try.
- **Bintang Beer**: I've heard it's a must-try in Bali, and everyone seems to recommend the lemon flavor.

> Quick reference: [【Recommended by Netizens】Over 30 Must-Buy Items Across 6 Categories: Bali Souvenir Gift Guide to Cherish Your Memories](https://gobaligo.id/en/blog/bali-souvenirs-gift-guide/)

Next, I tried the affordable foot massage recommended by Threads users. A 60-minute foot massage costs only **140,000 IDR**—really cheap.

But the service and environment were pretty basic: it was the first time I saw a foot wash that just rinsed with plain water and nothing else, no extra tea or snacks—just pure massage. Still, the place was packed with people, all wanting to come in, probably because it's such a good deal.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384196406-massage.jpeg)

(Photo provided by Chen Chenxi)

In the evening, I leisurely enjoyed a beer by myself. Bintang—truly delicious!

## South to Nusa Dua: Pandawa Beach

Next, I headed to the southernmost **Nusa Dua**. In the morning, I arrived at **Pandawa Beach**.

- Entrance ticket: **30,000 IDR**
- Shuttle to the cliff road: **30,000 IDR**

The cliffs along the way were stunning, and the shuttle took only about 3 minutes—super fast. The long wait was only because there were too many people ahead. On the way back, you don't have to take the shuttle; you can also walk down yourself in about 15 minutes.

> **Author's note**: The famous cliff road is right here, packed with tourists, and photo angles really matter.

This beach is incredibly beautiful and not crowded at all. The water is crystal clear—more transparent than any other beach—perfect for a lazy afternoon.

## Riug Paragliding: 10 Minutes Over the Indian Ocean

https://youtube.com/shorts/eCTaZX10U7Y?feature=share

Next up was paragliding. The staff let me bring two cameras at once: his GoPro and my own 360 camera, shooting from both sides simultaneously.

I chose to fly for just **10 minutes**, one speed and done, overlooking the Indian Ocean—absolutely stunning.

Once up in the air, it was actually quite stable, but the wind was strong, and holding the selfie stick for a while made my arm a bit sore. Turning made me slightly dizzy; the instructions said to **look in the direction of the turn** to avoid feeling too woozy.

## FINNS: Laid-Back by Day, a Different World by Night

Finally, I arrived at **FINNS**. You can actually enter for free, but I didn't want to queue, so I bought a fast-track entry. I just checked the general entrance, and it's packed right now.

**Entry Guidelines:**

- Security check required upon entry; professional camera equipment and water are not allowed inside
- Free seating is available in the dining area with live performers
- Staff will guide you to the counter; charges are deducted using a number card
- Fast-track entry includes a **240,000 IDR** credit, plus a locker and towel

By day, FINNS is more laid-back, but once night falls, it transforms into something extraordinary...

"Welcome to the world's best beach club!"

And there, I met two Japanese girls, then ran into them again—and again, until our paths crossed countless times. (That's the story behind the video title, 'Encountering Cute Japanese Girls.')

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1791384246110-finns.png)

(Photo courtesy of Chen Chenxi)

> **Author's Note**: FINNS is more relaxed before nightfall and gets especially lively after dark. It's recommended to **arrive before 7 PM** to catch the fireworks.
