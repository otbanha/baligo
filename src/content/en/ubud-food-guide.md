**Bottom line**: For must-eats in Ubud, focus on three experiences—if you want **Instagram-worthy vibes**, head to **Taman Dedari** (Google 4.6⭐, giant fairy statue + afternoon tea); for **waterfall views**, go to **D'Tukad River Club** (a treehouse day club atop Tegenungan Waterfall with 360-degree waterfall views); for **local classics**, stick with famous dirty duck rice and pork rib spots. Below is a full list organized by rating and setting.

When you come to Ubud, Bali, beyond admiring the rice terrace scenery and exploring ancient temples, you absolutely can't miss the food experiences here! From traditional Indonesian dishes to creative Western restaurants, from cafés hidden among the rice paddies to street food in bustling markets, Ubud gathers the most vibrant culinary culture in Bali. This guide handpicks must-eat restaurants and local foods in Ubud, letting your taste buds enjoy an unforgettable Bali journey!

## Boni Bali Restaurant (Google Rating 4.3⭐) 👉🏼 Map: [Immerse in Scenic Beauty](/map/ubud/)

![Boni Bali Restaurant (谷歌評價4.3) 地圖：沈浸山水美景](https://images.gobaligo.id/vocus/vocus_5046f26f284c95d89a998876cafa9852.jpg)

Enjoying Indonesian traditional cuisine while overlooking the breathtaking views of the Tegalalang Rice Terraces is one of the great pleasures of traveling to Bali.

Located in northeastern Bali, the Tegalalang Rice Terraces are a UNESCO World Heritage site. Spanning a total area of 2,500 hectares, they consist of thousands of stepped rice paddies that cascade layer upon layer into the horizon. Bathed in sunlight, the terraces display a vibrant palette of gold, green, and brown, creating a stunningly beautiful scene.

Boni Bali Restaurant sits on the edge of the Tegalalang Rice Terraces, serving Indonesian traditional cuisine. Here, visitors can savor classic dishes like Nasi Goreng, Sate Ayam, and Rendang.
