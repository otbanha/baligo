## 1. Avoid Peak Travel Seasons

First up, let's start with choosing your travel dates! Avoid visiting Bali during peak seasons when flight, accommodation, and activity prices tend to skyrocket. Opting for the off-season or less busy periods not only saves you money on flights and stays but also offers a more peaceful travel experience.

So when is Bali's peak season? Generally, the dry season with lower rainfall is the best time to visit Bali, roughly from April to October, which is also the peak travel season. Wondering about the downsides of avoiding peak season? [Check here](/en/blog/bali-best-time-to-visit/) to learn about Bali's rainy season.

Beyond seasonal distinctions, these five periods also see crowds flocking to Bali, ranked by the volume of people and traffic:

1. Indonesia's Eid al-Fitr holiday is a significant occasion. During this roughly ten-day break, a large number of domestic Indonesian tourists flock to this paradise island for a vacation. It's the absolute peak season in Bali, ranking number one for crowds!

2. The Christmas-New Year holiday period typically attracts global travelers, as it's a long break for most Western countries, and Indonesians also find ways to take time off. This makes the Christmas-New Year season the second busiest in terms of crowds and traffic. Here's a short video showing the situation on the highway to the airport on December 29, 2023: [link](https://www.facebook.com/groups/indonesia.travelguide/permalink/3579975912271532/).

3. The period around the Chinese Lunar New Year is especially unique. Besides China and Taiwan, countries like Hong Kong, Malaysia, Singapore, South Korea, and Vietnam also enjoy two to five days off; the Philippines, Thailand, and Indonesia have a single day off on the first day of the new year. The Lunar New Year holiday ranks third for attracting massive crowds to tourist attractions.

4. From July 1 to September 5, Europe's long summer break and the Northern Hemisphere's student summer vacation typically create a travel surge. Many families and students use this time to vacation in Bali. Airfare and hotel prices during summer break are never cheap!

5. The Australian school holidays and the Indonesian international school break (one week between September 20 and October 10) also bring in many Australian tourists and families with children attending international schools in Indonesia, who embark on trips during this period.

During these periods, tourist numbers surge dramatically, and prices for accommodations and attractions may rise due to high demand and limited supply. Therefore, if you plan to visit Bali during these peak seasons, it's best to book early and prepare thoroughly to ensure a pleasant, worry-free trip.

For travelers, Bali offers three convenient, **direct** transport options: renting a scooter, taking a taxi, or hiring a private driver. We won't discuss the bus system that stops at every station.

## 9. Pay with Cash

In Bali, cash is the most widely accepted payment method. Be sure to carry enough cash to avoid extra fees from card payments.

Using credit cards abroad has several drawbacks:

1. You may incur additional charges, such as foreign transaction fees, withdrawal fees, and exchange rate markups.
2. You might be charged a surcharge by retailers if you don't pay in the local currency.
3. You could overspend if you don't keep track of your daily expenses.
4. You may face fraud risks if your card lacks a security chip or gets skimmed.

So, be cautious when using credit cards overseas.

However, Indonesian rupiah notes often amount to millions (the exchange rate is roughly 1 TWD to 500 IDR). If you'd rather not carry that much cash, consider using the e-wallet in the Gojek app mentioned in the previous tip.

## 16. Tipping Appropriately

Tipping appropriately during your travels is a cultural courtesy and a way to express gratitude for service. When traveling in Bali, giving a proper tip can help you build friendly relationships with locals while respecting local customs. Here are Xiao Jie's tips on [occasions and considerations for tipping](https://youtu.be/B_oT905RkQw), so you can make the right choices on your journey.

Additionally, a common question often arises in our Bali community: "Can I tip in US dollars?" Let's share some thoughts on this. In Indonesia or Bali, the only circulating currency is the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). So, if you wish to give a tip, please use the local currency—Indonesian Rupiah. Avoid using other currencies or foreign money for tipping. This not only shows respect for the local culture but also conveys your sincere gratitude to the service provider.

Let's think from another perspective: imagine if a foreign friend handed you foreign currency in Taiwan—wouldn't that feel a bit inconvenient? Similarly, Bali's residents might face the same difficulty, potentially causing them unnecessary trouble.

Therefore, to ensure our tips accurately convey our gratitude, we should **always tip in Indonesian Rupiah**. This not only respects the locals but also better expresses our appreciation and thanks for the service.
