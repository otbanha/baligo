## 1. Avoid Peak Travel Seasons

First off, let's start with choosing your travel time! Avoid visiting Bali during peak seasons, when flight, accommodation, and activity prices tend to skyrocket. Opting for the off-season or less busy periods not only saves you money on flights and lodging but also lets you enjoy a more peaceful travel experience.

So when is Bali's peak travel season? Generally, the dry season with lower rainfall is the best time to visit Bali, roughly from April to October—that's the high season. Wondering about the downsides of avoiding peak season? Check out [this link](/en/blog/bali-best-time-to-visit/) to learn about Bali's rainy season.

Beyond seasonal distinctions, these five periods also see crowds flooding into Bali, ranked by the volume of people and traffic:

1. Indonesia's Eid al-Fitr holiday is a significant occasion. During this roughly ten-day break, a large number of domestic Indonesian tourists flock to this paradise island for a vacation. It's also the peak season with the highest crowds in Bali!

2. The Christmas-New Year holiday period typically attracts travelers from around the world, as it's a long break for most Western countries, and Indonesians also try to take time off. Therefore, the Christmas-New Year holiday ranks as the second busiest period for crowds and traffic. Here's a short video showing the situation on the highway to the airport on December 29, 2023: [link](https://www.facebook.com/groups/indonesia.travelguide/permalink/3579975912271532/).

3. The period around the Chinese Lunar New Year is especially notable. Besides China and Taiwan, countries like Hong Kong, Malaysia, Singapore, South Korea, and Vietnam also have two to five days off; the Philippines, Thailand, and Indonesia have a single day off on the first day of the new year. The Lunar New Year holiday ranks third in attracting large crowds to tourist spots.

4. From July 1 to September 5, Europe's long summer break and the Northern Hemisphere's student summer vacation typically create a travel surge, with many families and students heading to Bali for a holiday. Airfare and hotel prices during summer break are never cheap!

5. Australian school holidays and Indonesian international school holidays (one week between September 20 and October 10) also bring in many Australian tourists and families with children attending international schools in Indonesia, who embark on trips during this time.

## 6. Choosing the Right Transportation

For travelers, Bali offers three convenient, **direct** transportation options: renting a scooter, taking a taxi, or hiring a private driver. We won't discuss bus systems that stop at every station.

Renting a scooter in Bali is perfect for travelers who want to explore freely; taxis are ideal for short trips or those who prefer not to drive; while hiring a private driver suits visitors seeking deeper experiences and customized itineraries—if you're traveling with companions, splitting the cost isn't too steep. Based on your needs and budget, you can find the most suitable option among these choices. Whichever you pick, always prioritize **safety first** to make your Bali trip even more enjoyable!

## 9. Pay with Cash

In Bali, cash is the most widely accepted payment method. Be sure to carry enough cash to avoid extra fees from card payments.

Using credit cards abroad has several drawbacks:

1. You may incur additional charges such as foreign transaction fees, withdrawal fees, and exchange rate markups.
2. You might be charged a surcharge by retailers if you don't pay in the local currency.
3. You could overspend if you don't keep track of your daily expenses.
4. You may face fraud risks if your card lacks a security chip or gets skimmed.

So, be cautious when using credit cards overseas.

However, carrying millions of Indonesian Rupiah (with a TWD:IDR exchange rate of about 1:500) can be cumbersome. If you'd rather not carry that much cash, consider using the e-wallet in the Gojek app mentioned in the previous tip.

## 16. Tipping Appropriately

Tipping appropriately during your travels is a cultural courtesy and a way to express gratitude for services. When visiting Bali, giving proper tips can help you build friendly relationships with locals while respecting local customs. Here are some tips from Xiao Jie on [occasions and considerations for tipping](https://youtu.be/B_oT905RkQw) to help you make the right choices during your journey.

Additionally, a common question often arises in our Bali community: "Can I tip in US dollars?" Let's share some thoughts on this topic. In Indonesia or Bali, the only circulating currency is the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). So, if you wish to give a tip, please use the local currency—Indonesian Rupiah. Avoid using other currencies or foreign money for tipping. This not only shows respect for the local culture but also conveys your sincere gratitude to the service provider.

Let's think from another perspective: imagine if a foreign friend handed you foreign currency in Taiwan—wouldn't you find it a bit inconvenient? Similarly, Bali's residents might face the same difficulty, potentially causing unnecessary hassle.

Therefore, to ensure our tips accurately convey our gratitude, we should **always tip in Indonesian Rupiah**. This not only respects the locals but also better expresses our appreciation and thanks for the service.

Finally, consider slowing down your travel pace. Staying longer in one place not only lets you dive deeper into local life but also helps save on accommodation costs. Some guesthouses offer tempting discounts for weekly or monthly stays, providing a more immersive travel experience.
