# Solo Travel in Ubud, Bali! YouTuber Casper Stays at a Five-Star Hotel, Explores Secret Rice Field Trails & Tirta Empul Temple

On his second trip to Bali, beyond the beach resort vibes of Seminyak and Kuta, there's a completely different way to travel: slow down in Ubud and savor a few leisurely days.

This time, YouTuber Casper returns to Bali and chooses to focus his trip on the central town of Ubud for a three-day, two-night solo adventure. He skips the rush of sightseeing and avoids cramming his schedule, instead checking into a five-star resort in the heart of town, strolling through Ubud's streets, and visiting nearby cultural sites.

From the Sacred Monkey Forest and Ubud Palace to a serendipitous rice field trail and the culturally rich purification ritual at Tirta Empul Temple, this journey reveals a side of Ubud that's completely different from Bali's southern beaches. The video link is [here](https://youtu.be/YjAojbze5W8?si=l0FG3slDdxeCguZi).

## Alaya Resort Ubud: Stay in the Heart of Town with Rice Field Views

For this trip, Casper chose to stay at **Alaya Resort Ubud**, booking three consecutive nights.

Usually, when traveling solo, he prefers budget-friendly accommodations, but this time he decided to splurge a little and pick a five-star resort where he could truly relax and enjoy himself.

The hotel's biggest advantage is its location—right in the center of Ubud, within walking distance to the Sacred Monkey Forest and Ubud Palace, with plenty of restaurants and shops nearby.

What makes it even more special is that the hotel rooms offer views of the rice paddies, allowing guests to soak up Ubud's signature laid-back atmosphere even while staying in the lively town center.

Casper also shared the hotel's breakfast and complimentary activity experiences, including a hands-on workshop where guests can make their own herbal tea and body scrub. For travelers looking to pack more activities into their stay, this is a feature worth noting.

## Strolling Around Ubud Town: Sacred Monkey Forest, Ubud Palace, and Coconut Ice Cream

After checking into the hotel, Casper began exploring Ubud town.

His first stop was the famous **Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary**. This is not just a place to see monkeys, but also a nature reserve that combines tropical forest, ancient temples, and Balinese culture.

Compared to the monkeys at Uluwatu Temple, Casper found the monkeys here relatively gentler. Still, he reminded everyone to keep an eye on their belongings and avoid feeding or provoking the monkeys.

Next, he headed to Ubud Palace, making a special stop along the way to try the famous Tukies Coconut Shop's coconut ice cream.

Made with fresh coconut meat, coconut chips, and palm sugar syrup, this ice cream is a perfect refreshing treat for a hot day while exploring the streets of Ubud.

## A Hidden Gem: Sweet Orange Walk Trail Rice Paddy Path

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1790578807013-index.jpg)

One highlight of this Ubud trip that I found truly noteworthy was the **Sweet Orange Walk Trail**, discovered by Casper by chance.

The trail entrance is tucked away in a small alley near Ubud Palace, not particularly conspicuous, and easy to miss if you're not paying attention.

At the start, the path is quite narrow, even making Casper wonder if he had taken a wrong turn. But as you continue, the view suddenly opens up to reveal a vast expanse of lush green rice paddies.

Along the way, besides the rural scenery, you'll also see stone slabs engraved with messages left by travelers from around the world.

There's no fixed destination here—it's perfect for a leisurely stroll at your own pace, offering a different perspective of Ubud beyond the city center.

If you've already explored Ubud Palace and the traditional market and are looking for a quieter place to wander, this trail is worth considering.

## Tirta Empul: Experience a Traditional Balinese Purification Ritual

On the second day, Casper headed to the famous **Tirta Empul Temple** near Ubud to personally experience Bali's traditional purification ritual.

Tirta Empul is renowned for its holy spring water and is one of Bali's most important Hindu temples. Many local devotees come here to pray and perform cleansing ceremonies, and it also attracts numerous foreign travelers eager to learn about the local culture.

This time, Casper didn't just tour the temple—he actually stepped into the water to take part in the ritual, with a local guide explaining the process.

From preparing the traditional sarong and understanding the prayer etiquette to experiencing the different water spouts in sequence, the entire experience left a deep impression on him.

He also mentioned that the spring water was colder than expected, making him a bit nervous at first. But after completing the ritual, he felt a wonderful sense of physical and mental relaxation.

If you're planning to visit Tirta Empul, it's recommended to learn about the dress code and ritual etiquette in advance, and to respect the local religious culture—don't treat it merely as a photo spot.
