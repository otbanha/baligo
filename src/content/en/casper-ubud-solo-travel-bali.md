# Bali Ubud Solo Travel Recommendation! YouTuber Casper Stays at a Five-Star Hotel, Explores a Secret Rice Field Trail and Tirta Empul Temple

On his second visit to Bali, beyond the beach resort vibes of Seminyak and Kuta, there’s a completely different way to travel: slow down in Ubud and savor a few leisurely days.

This time, YouTuber Casper returns to Bali, choosing to focus his trip on central Ubud for a three-day, two-night solo adventure. He skips the rush of sightseeing and avoids cramming his schedule, instead checking into a five-star resort in the heart of town, strolling through Ubud’s streets, and visiting nearby cultural sites.

From the Sacred Monkey Forest and Ubud Palace to a serendipitously discovered rice field trail and the culturally rich purification ritual at Tirta Empul Temple, this journey reveals Ubud’s unique charm, so different from Bali’s southern beaches. ## Alaya Resort Ubud: Stay in the City Center with Rice Field Views

For this trip, Casper chose to stay at **Alaya Resort Ubud**, booking three consecutive nights.

When traveling solo, he usually prefers budget-friendly accommodations, but this time he decided to splurge a little and pick a five-star resort where he could truly relax and unwind.

The hotel’s biggest advantage is its location—right in the heart of Ubud, within walking distance of the Sacred Monkey Forest and Ubud Palace, with plenty of restaurants and shops nearby.

What makes it even more special is that the hotel rooms offer views of the rice paddies, allowing guests to soak in Ubud's signature laid-back atmosphere even while staying in the bustling town center.

Casper also shared his experience with the hotel's breakfast and complimentary activities, including a hands-on workshop where guests can make their own herbal tea and body scrub. For travelers looking to add more experiences to their stay, this is a noteworthy feature.

## Strolling Through Ubud Town: Sacred Monkey Forest, Ubud Palace, and Coconut Ice Cream

After checking into the hotel, Casper began exploring Ubud town.

His first stop was the famous **Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary**. This is not just a place to see monkeys, but also a nature reserve that combines tropical forest, ancient temples, and Balinese culture.

Compared to the monkeys at Uluwatu Temple, Casper found the monkeys here relatively gentler, but he still reminded everyone to keep an eye on their belongings and avoid feeding or provoking the monkeys.

Next, he headed to Ubud Palace (Ubud Palace), making a special stop along the way to try the famous Tukies Coconut Shop coconut ice cream.

Made with fresh coconut meat, coconut chips, and palm sugar syrup, this ice cream is a perfect way to cool down while exploring the streets of Ubud on a hot day.

## A Hidden Gem Discovered by Chance: Sweet Orange Walk Trail Rice Paddy Path

![](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-09/1790578807013-index.jpg)

One of the highlights of this Ubud trip that I found truly noteworthy was the **Sweet Orange Walk Trail**, stumbled upon by Casper by accident.

The entrance to this trail is tucked away in a small alley near Ubud Palace, not particularly conspicuous—easy to miss if you're not paying attention.

At first, the path is quite narrow, even making Casper wonder if he had taken a wrong turn. But as you continue, the view suddenly opens up to reveal a vast expanse of lush green rice paddies.

Along the way, besides the pastoral scenery, you'll spot stone slabs engraved with messages left by travelers from around the world.

There's no specific destination to reach here—it's perfect for a leisurely stroll at your own pace, soaking in a different side of Ubud beyond the town center.

If you've already explored Ubud Palace and the traditional market and are looking for a quieter place to wander, this trail is worth considering.

## Tirta Empul: Experience a Traditional Balinese Purification Ritual

On the second day, Casper headed to the famous **Tirta Empul Temple** near Ubud to personally experience Bali's traditional purification ritual.

Tirta Empul Temple is renowned for its sacred spring water and is one of Bali's most important Hindu temples. Many local devotees come here to pray and perform purification ceremonies, and it also attracts numerous foreign travelers eager to learn about the local culture.

This time, Casper didn't just tour the temple—he actually stepped into the water to take part in the ritual, with a local guide explaining the process step by step.

From preparing the traditional sarong and learning the prayer etiquette to experiencing the different water spouts in sequence, the entire process left a deep impression on him.

He also mentioned that the spring water was colder than expected, making him a bit nervous at first. But after completing the ritual, he felt a wonderful sense of physical and mental relaxation.

If you're planning to visit Tirta Empul Temple, it's recommended to learn about the dress code and ritual etiquette in advance, and to respect the local religious culture—don't treat it merely as a photo spot.

## Bali Supermarket Souvenir Picks: Casper's Three Favorite Snacks

At the end of the trip, Casper also made a special stop at a local supermarket to pick up souvenirs.

This time, he shared three snacks he personally loves:

- **Merubail Toasted Coconut Chips:** A local Balinese brand with a thin, crispy texture and distinct coconut aroma—a snack he always buys on every visit.
- **YAVA Spicy Nuts:** A savory-sweet-spicy flavor that's quite addictive, perfect for those who enjoy bold-tasting snacks.
- **SilverQueen Chocolate:** A well-known Indonesian chocolate brand with plenty of nuts, affordable price, and great for gifting to friends or colleagues.
