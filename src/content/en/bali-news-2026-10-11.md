# Trending Topics & News Roundup for Bali Travelers | October 11, 2026

## 

🌊 High Tide and Seawater Intrusion Warning Issued for Multiple Bali Coasts Through October 18

The Bali Region III Meteorological Station of the Indonesian Agency for Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysics (BMKG) has issued a warning for high tides and coastal flooding (banjir rob), effective from October 9 to 18.

Potentially affected areas include:

- Southern coast of Jembrana
- Southern coast of Tabanan
- Coastal areas of Badung
- Denpasar coastal area
- Gianyar coastal area
- Southern coast of Klungkung

Meteorological authorities note that the new moon period on October 10 may be linked to rising sea levels.

For travelers, this doesn't mean all the beaches mentioned above will flood, but if your accommodation is near the coast, or you plan to take beach walks, photos, or join water activities, it's advisable to pay attention to local warnings.

## 🚁 Bali Trials Drone Food Delivery: Three-Month Test from Canggu North to Tabanan

Bali is testing a new way to deliver food. SKYY Network, in collaboration with Nuanu Creative City, Café del Mar Bali, and Grab Indonesia, has launched a three-month drone food delivery trial.

Current test locations include Nuanu Creative City in Tabanan and Café del Mar Bali. The project aims to evaluate the safety, operational procedures, infrastructure needs, and customer experience of drone-based food delivery in real-world settings.

A major challenge of this test is that the flight routes are within the controlled airspace near Ngurah Rai International Airport, making aviation safety and flight management critical.

This is still a pilot program and does not mean that drone food delivery via Grab is available across all of Bali, nor is there evidence that regular delivery drivers will be replaced anytime soon.

For travelers, this is a noteworthy new development in travel tech. If the trial goes smoothly, it could offer a fresh dining delivery option in certain areas in the future.

## 🛵 Motorcycle Fire on Bali Mandara Toll Road, No Injuries Reported

On October 9, a Yamaha Mio motorcycle caught fire on the Bali Mandara Toll Road heading toward Nusa Dua, around the 2.8-kilometer mark.

Preliminary assessments by the highway operator suggest the fire may have been caused by an electrical short circuit. No injuries were reported.

Bali Mandara is a key toll road connecting Denpasar, Sanur, and Nusa Dua, and is sometimes used by travelers heading between the airport and southern resort areas.

If you plan to ride a motorcycle to Nusa Dua, make sure to check whether the road allows motorcycles, and confirm rental terms, your license, and insurance coverage. Don't assume that just because motorcycles are allowed on regular roads, they can use all highways.

Before setting out, also inspect your vehicle's condition, especially the electrical system, battery, and fuel system. If you notice any unusual smells, smoke, or issues, pull over in a safe spot and seek help immediately.

## 🕯️ 24th Anniversary Commemoration of the Bali Bombings on October 12 in Kuta

October 12, 2026 marks the 24th anniversary of the Bali bombings. The Badung Regency government, local communities, and survivor groups are preparing a memorial event at the Ground Zero monument in Legian, Kuta.

The event is expected to start at 5 PM, featuring peace songs, interfaith prayers, and a flower-laying ceremony, concluding around 8 PM. The organizers have coordinated safety arrangements with the police.

The core of this event is to honor the victims, survivors, and their families, conveying a message of peace and unity.

If you're heading to Legian, central Kuta, or the Ground Zero area on October 12, allow extra travel time and respect the memorial ceremony. In case of road closures, follow instructions from police and staff.

## 🐟 Protected Manta Rays Seized in Pengambengan, Reminding Travelers to Choose Seafood Responsibly

Authorities in Pengambengan, Jembrana, recently seized over a dozen protected manta ray species, with the case under investigation. The capture and trade of such marine life violate wildlife conservation regulations.

This news differs from general seafood supply or port development, focusing instead on the trade and conservation of protected marine species.

For travelers to Bali, while enjoying seafood, avoid purchasing products from protected marine species of unknown origin. If restaurants or markets offer unfamiliar marine species, ask about their name and source, and don't buy or eat them just because they're rare.

Bali's marine ecosystem is a vital part of its tourism appeal, and travelers' consumption choices can influence conservation efforts.
