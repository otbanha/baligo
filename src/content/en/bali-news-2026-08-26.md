# Bali Travel Hot Topics + News Roundup｜August 26, 2026

## ⚠️ Chinese Tourist Drowns While Snorkeling at Komodo's Long Pink Beach

A Chinese female tourist recently drowned while snorkeling at **Long Pink Beach in Komodo National Park**.

Local SAR teams and other tourists immediately assisted in the rescue, and the injured person was taken to a medical facility, but ultimately passed away.

Located on the north side of Padar Island in Komodo National Park, Long Pink Beach is a common stop on day trips or island-hopping tours from Labuan Bajo.

This incident serves as a reminder for travelers heading to **Labuan Bajo, Komodo, and Padar Island**:

**Even when the weather is good and the water looks calm, snorkeling still carries risks.**

When snorkeling, make sure life jackets are available, lifeguards and crew are present, and avoid drifting too far from the boat or safe zone just to take photos.

Also, Flores recently experienced a magnitude 7.7 earthquake, so travelers heading to NTT should still check the latest sea conditions and scenic area updates.

## 🔥 Bali's Dry Season Fire Risk Rises, Experts Remind Travelers to Watch for Forest Fires

Multiple regions in Indonesia have recently seen forest and land fires. Although **there are currently no large-scale forest fires occurring in Bali**, the island is in its dry season, with some areas already facing water shortages and fire risks.

Areas currently at higher risk include **Bangli, Buleleng**, and others, which are also popular destinations for travelers, such as Kintamani, Penglipuran, Lovina, and Munduk.

For most tourists, there is no need to cancel Bali trips because of this.

However, if you plan outdoor activities like hiking, camping, or forest trekking, do not light fires or burn trash; when heading to remote areas, it's also advisable to keep an eye on the latest announcements from local authorities and BMKG.

## ☀️ Bali Launches Major Solar Project, Gilimanuk to Become a Renewable Energy Hub

Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto recently attended the groundbreaking ceremony for a large-scale solar power project in **Gilimanuk**, Bali.

The Bali provincial government also announced it will prepare land for approximately **1,000 MWp of solar power capacity**, prioritizing non-productive state-owned land to avoid conflicts with agriculture and other essential uses.

This energy transition plan won't have an immediate significant impact on travelers, but in the long run, Bali aims to reduce its reliance on diesel and other fossil fuels while increasing the share of renewable energy in the island's power supply.

Gilimanuk, located at the western tip of Bali, serves as a key ferry gateway to Java. Beyond its transportation role, this area may gradually take on more energy and large-scale infrastructure functions in the future.

## 🏖️ Nusa Penida Kelingking Beach Road Renovations, Paired with a New Attraction Fee System

The roads around Nusa Penida's iconic **Kelingking Beach** have recently undergone renovations.

The project primarily aims to improve road conditions leading to popular attractions and aligns with the implementation of a new DTW (Tourist Attraction) fee system.

Currently, several major beaches and viewpoints in Nusa Penida have started charging additional entrance fees. **These fees are separate from the Nusa Penida island tourism fee that foreign visitors are already required to pay.**

In other words, when visiting popular spots like Kelingking and Diamond Beach, travelers may face:

**Island entry fee + individual attraction fees.**

If you're planning a day trip to Nusa Penida soon, it's advisable not to just look at tour or car rental quotes—make sure to confirm whether these attraction fees are already included.

## 📱 QRIS Cross-Border Payments Expand, Making Digital Payments More Convenient for Bali Travelers

Indonesia's central bank continues to promote **QRIS cross-border payments**, allowing travelers from select partner countries to use their familiar mobile payment methods by scanning QR codes at Indonesian merchants.

ANTARA Bali reports that the expansion of QRIS cross-border payments is boosting Bali's tourism industry, particularly by making it more convenient for foreign tourists to handle daily transactions.

For Taiwanese travelers, here's what to keep in mind:

**QRIS does not mean that all Taiwanese bank apps can scan it directly.**

Whether you can use it depends on whether your bank or payment platform has partnered with Indonesia's QRIS cross-border system.

So when visiting Bali, I still recommend bringing **a credit card + some Indonesian rupiah cash + a usable e-payment option**, and don't rely on just one payment method.
