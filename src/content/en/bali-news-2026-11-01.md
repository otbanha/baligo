## 🎫 Jatiluwih Rice Terrace Fee Sparks Controversy! Management Clarifies: No Ticket Needed for Passing Through

Bali's UNESCO World Heritage site, the Jatiluwih Rice Terraces, recently sparked debate among locals over entry fees.

The incident began when a Balinese resident driving through the Jatiluwih area was asked by entrance staff to confirm whether they had purchased a ticket. A video of the incident circulated on social media, stirring controversy.

In response, the Jatiluwih management clarified on October 31 that they do not charge entry fees to locals simply passing through. The staff checks vehicles mainly to determine whether drivers are just passing by or planning to enter the site as tourists.

The management also apologized to affected residents.

However, visitors who enter the site and stop to explore—even just to eat at a restaurant or grab a drink—must still pay the tourist fee as per regulations.

### What Does This Mean for Travelers?

This clarification is important for travelers planning to visit Jatiluwih.

Jatiluwih is Bali's famous terraced rice field area and part of the UNESCO World Heritage site. Passing through on the way to another destination is a different experience from actually entering the scenic area for sightseeing.

If you plan to stop and enjoy the rice terraces, take photos, or dine at the scenic area's restaurant, you should still budget for the entrance fee.

## 🗼 North Bali's Turyapada Tower Expected to Complete by End of December! Aerial Cable Car Installation Begins in November

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-10/1793462696886-15450.webp)

Northern Bali is about to welcome a major new tourist landmark!

Located in Buleleng Regency, the main tower of Turyapada Tower is already complete, and surrounding tourist facilities and landscaping are currently underway.

Bali Governor Wayan Koster updated the local parliament on the project's progress on October 30, stating that cable car equipment imported from China has been arriving gradually, with installation expected to begin in November and the overall project targeted for completion by the end of December.

In addition to the aerial cable car, the surrounding area will include:

- Landscaped gardens
- Restaurants
- Relaxation spaces
- Roads leading to the tower area
- Cable car station and related facilities

The overall project is expected to be completed by December 2026, with an official opening scheduled afterward.

The total investment for this tower is approximately 6,705 billion Indonesian Rupiah. In addition to its communication functions, it is also expected to become a new tourist landmark in North Bali.

### When can you visit?

It is not yet open to the public, as the surrounding roads and tourist facilities are still under construction.

If the project is completed on schedule, 2027 could offer travelers a new option for exploring North Bali.

I believe this project is worth paying attention to. Bali's tourism resources have long been concentrated in the south and Ubud. If the north can add attractive new attractions, it will help disperse some of the tourist crowds.

## 🚌 Bali Considers Improving Public Transportation! Airport to Kuta, Legian, and Nusa Dua Traffic in Focus

Bali has long faced traffic congestion issues, especially in popular tourist areas such as Denpasar, Canggu, Seminyak, and Kuta.

On October 30, the Indonesian Online Journalists Association Bali Chapter (IWO Bali) held a public transportation policy discussion, inviting Nyoman Suyasa, Chairman of the Third Committee of the Bali Regional House of Representatives, to discuss how to improve transportation issues on the island.

The meeting specifically highlighted the need for a more comprehensive public transportation system from Ngurah Rai International Airport to major tourist areas such as Nusa Dua, Kuta, and Legian.

Currently, the Metro Dewata bus service has resumed operations, but passenger numbers have not shown significant growth. Participants believe that simply increasing the number of buses is not enough; seamless transfers between different modes of transport must also be ensured.

The discussion also covered:

- Establishing an integrated public transportation system.
- Researching the development of electric vehicles and public transport.
- Evaluating the possibility of restricting private vehicle use.
- Improving connections between buses and tourist attractions.
- Studying long-term transportation solutions such as trams or subways.

### What should travelers pay attention to?

These discussions are still at the policy stage, with no new private vehicle bans announced, nor any official plans for a tram or metro system.

For now, travelers should plan their trips using existing transportation options.

However, improved public transit from the airport to major tourist areas in the future would be a huge convenience for visitors who don't want to rent a scooter or rely on ride-hailing every time.

## ⛴️ Indonesia to Inspect 2,140 Vessels Starting November, Including 821 Tourist Boats, Ahead of Year-End Travel Season

Indonesia's Ministry of Transportation has announced a nationwide maritime safety inspection from **November 1 to 30, 2026**, targeting 2,140 vessels in preparation for the Christmas and 2027 New Year travel peak.

The inspection covers various types of vessels:

- 511 passenger ships
- 63 long-distance roll-on/roll-off ships
- 460 ferries
- 162 traditional boats
- 821 tourist boats
- 123 high-speed vessels

Among them, the inspection of 821 tourist boats is particularly noteworthy for travelers.

Inspection items include vessel safety equipment, crew qualifications, passenger capacity, fire-fighting equipment, life-saving gear, emergency evacuation routes, and whether the vessel is operating legally.

The Ministry of Transportation also stated that tourist boats must undergo on-site inspections and meet requirements before obtaining navigation permits.

Additionally, the government is studying alternative routes between Java and Bali to ease traffic pressure on the Ketapang to Gilimanuk ferry route, including potential routes like Jangkar to Padang Bai, though this depends on port and vessel readiness.

### What does this mean for Bali travelers?

The year-end is a peak travel season for Bali and other Indonesian tourist islands, and travelers heading to Nusa Penida, Lembongan Island, and other offshore islands also use various types of tourist boats.

This inspection is primarily about ensuring vessel safety in advance, not declaring a halt to all boat operations.

If you plan to take a speedboat or join marine sightseeing activities around the year-end, check whether the boat company holds a legal operating license and confirm that there is sufficient life-saving equipment on board.
