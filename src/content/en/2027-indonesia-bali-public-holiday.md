# 2027 Bali Holiday Guide: Plan Ahead to Avoid Crowds and Snag Cheap Flights

Planning a trip to Bali? The Indonesian government's just-released 2027 holiday calendar is crucial for you!

Because these holidays don't just mean days off for locals—they also mean **Bali will be packed with people, hotel prices will skyrocket, and flights will be outrageously expensive**. If you know how to use this holiday schedule, you can smartly choose your travel timing—either join the long holiday crowds for festive vibes, or snag bargains during the off-peak periods before and after the holidays.

## Key Highlights

2027 has a total of **18 national holidays** and **8 collective leave days**. The March Eid al-Fitr period is the longest consecutive break of the year, while the May Golden Week features three major holidays in quick succession. This complete holiday schedule is your best tool for planning a 2027 Bali trip.

## 8-Day Collective Holidays: Peak Periods You Need to Know

Below are the collective holidays approved by the Indonesian government. Around these dates, Bali and major attractions will be packed with crowds, and flights and hotels will be sky-high:

https://youtu.be/15zXGMEE3Q8

1. **February 6 (Saturday)** — Lunar New Year
    - Massive influx of travelers from China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and Singapore
    - Temples and traditional villages will be very crowded
2. **March 8 – 15** — Nyepi Day + Eid al-Fitr Holiday
    - **Most important!** This is Indonesia's longest consecutive holiday week of the year
    - Flights are hardest to book, hotels are most expensive, and attractions are most crowded
    - However, there will be a festive religious atmosphere worth experiencing
3. **March 26 (Friday)** — Good Friday
    - Connects with the weekend, forming a long weekend
    - More Western tourists
4. **May 17 (Monday)** — Eid al-Adha
    - Connects with the weekend, Bali sees an influx of Indonesian family travelers
    - Scooter rentals and restaurants tend to fill up
5. **May 20 (Thursday)** — Vesak Day
    - A Buddhist holiday; temples in Bali hold ceremonies
    - If you want to take temple photos, get up early
6. **December 24 (Friday)** — Christmas holiday
- Connected to Christmas and New Year
- The last peak period of the year, with all resorts fully booked

## Practical Tips for International Travelers

💡 **Why is Laylat al-Qadr observed twice?** The Islamic calendar is lunar-based, with only 354-355 days per year. So the same Islamic festival may span two Gregorian years. In 2027, Laylat al-Qadr falls on both January 5 and December 26—this is normal.

🎫 **Flight Booking Tips**

- Flight prices surge 2-3 months before holidays
- Recommended timing: Buy tickets 1–2 weeks before peak season (fewer people think of this)
- If you don't mind crowds: Grab 'last-minute deals' on the holiday itself
- Best savings: Travel in the off-season to save 20–50% on airfare

🏨 **Hotel Booking Tips**

- During peak seasons (Eid, Christmas), book 2–3 months in advance
- Avoid peak seasons: The same hotel can be over a third cheaper

🕌 **Festival Etiquette**

- Some temples may be closed to the public during Eid
- Dress modestly and remove shoes when entering temples for ceremonies
- Always ask locals for permission before taking photos
- Avoid eating or drinking in front of Muslims during Ramadan

☀️ **Weather & Seasons**

- May to October is the **dry season**, with the best weather
- November to March is the **rainy season**; it often rains in the afternoon but mornings are beautiful
- Traveling in the off-season offers a different kind of scenery

📱 **SIM Card & Internet**

- During peak season, airport SIM cards sell out quickly, so it's best to buy in advance
- No need to rush during the off-season
