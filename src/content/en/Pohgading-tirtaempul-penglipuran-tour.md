# Poh Gading Waterfall + Tirta Empul Temple + Penglipuran Village | Bali Ubud Private Day Tour Guide

If you're looking to plan a Bali day trip that covers waterfalls, temples, traditional villages, and rice fields all in one go—without having to figure out the transportation routes yourself—this private day tour departing from Ubud or Kuta is worth considering.

The highlight of this itinerary is that it strings together several distinct attractions in a single day, including the lesser-visited **Poh Gading Waterfall**, the famous **Tirta Empul Temple**, the traditional Balinese village of **Penglipuran Village**, and the **Tegallalang Rice Fields**. The tour includes private vehicle transfers, making it a more relaxed option for first-time visitors to Bali, families, or anyone who doesn't want to keep switching between different modes of transport.

## Visit 4 Different Styles of Bali Attractions in One Day

This tour isn't just a random collection of Ubud sights—it combines natural landscapes, religious culture, and traditional villages, so you can experience completely different sides of Bali in a single day.

### Poh Gading Waterfall

The first stop is **Poh Gading Waterfall**, with about 2 hours allocated.

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcN9PiWT9Ln/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&igsi=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==

Compared to Bali's most popular waterfalls like Tegenungan and Sekumpul, Poh Gading Waterfall is a new hotspot that only went viral in 2026.

The tour includes waterfall entrance fees and a guided experience, with an official itinerary lasting about 2 hours. Since waterfall activities often involve water, it's recommended to bring swimwear, a towel, and a change of clothes.

### Tirta Empul Holy Water Temple

https://youtube.com/shorts/fiYs5GKdINU

Next, head to Bali's renowned **Tirta Empul Holy Water Temple**, with a stop of about 1 hour.

Tirta Empul is one of Bali's most important Hindu temples, famous for its sacred spring water pools. Many travelers come here specifically to experience the traditional Balinese purification ritual.

However, note that visiting the temple and actually participating in the Melukat purification ritual are two different things. If you just want to tour the temple, dress appropriately according to local guidelines; if you plan to take part in the purification ritual, you should learn about the procedures and dress code in advance.

This is what I find valuable about this tour—it's not just about 'taking photos,' but about engaging with Bali's significant religious culture.

### Penglipuran Village

The third stop is **Penglipuran Village**, with a stay of about 1 hour.

https://www.tiktok.com/@herjoliejourney/video/7596216999454330142?is_from_webapp=1&sender_device=pc

Penglipuran Village is renowned for its orderly traditional architecture, clean streets, and well-preserved traditional village landscape, making it a popular cultural attraction in Bali.

If it's your first time in Bali, you might focus on beaches, cafes, villas, and Instagram-worthy spots, but stepping into traditional villages like this reveals another side of the island.

This stop lasts about an hour, so it's best suited for a leisurely stroll, taking photos, and soaking in the village atmosphere rather than an in-depth exploration.

### Tegallalang Rice Fields

Finally, head to the famous **Tegallalang Rice Fields** north of Ubud.

https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dao1uTFviag/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==

This is arguably one of Ubud's most iconic landscapes, with layered terraces and tropical greenery—a quintessential 'Bali scenery' in many travelers' minds.

The itinerary allows about an hour here, perfect for a short walk, photos, and admiring the rice terrace views.

If you've visited Tegallalang many times before, this stop might feel less fresh compared to Poh Gading Waterfall; but if it's your first time in Bali, pairing it with Tirta Empul Temple and Penglipuran Village on the same day makes for a well-rounded route.

## What to Bring on This Tour?

If you decide to join, it's best not to just bring your phone and camera.

Since the tour includes a waterfall, the official recommendation is to bring:

**Change of clothes, sunscreen, sunglasses, hat, swimsuit, and towel.**

For footwear, I'd recommend shoes with good grip suitable for outdoor walking, rather than just wearing flip-flops for the photos.

Also, even if the sky in Bali doesn't look scorching hot, the UV rays can still be very strong. Don't wait until you're sunburned to remember to reapply sunscreen.

## Is This Tour Worth Joining?

If we think of this tour as a way to collect several classic Ubud highlights in one go, I'd say the combination makes sense.

**Waterfall + Tirta Empul Temple + Traditional Village + Rice Terraces**—these four spots are completely different in nature, so you won't be stuck looking at similar Instagram-worthy places all day.

However, if you've been to Bali many times and are already very familiar with Ubud's attractions, you don't necessarily need to cram all four spots into one day. In that case, it's better to pick two or three that interest you most and spend more time at cafes, spas, or restaurants.

If it's your first time in Bali, I think the biggest advantage of this private day tour isn't the number of sights, but the fact that **you don't have to handle transportation or tickets yourself—just let the driver take you around for the day**.

Especially for first-timers who aren't familiar with traffic in the Ubud area, this kind of tour is indeed hassle-free.
