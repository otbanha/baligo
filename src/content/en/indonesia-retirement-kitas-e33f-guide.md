## Indonesia Retirement Visa Cheat Sheet: If You're Over 55 and Want to Stay in Bali Long-Term, You Need to Understand This E33F

Honestly, more and more friends are asking me whether they can just stay in Indonesia—specifically Bali—after retirement and never leave. The answer is yes, and the bar isn't as high as you might think. Indonesia has a retirement residence permit (KITAS) specifically for foreigners aged 55 and over, called the E33F, which allows you to stay legally long-term. Whether you're planning your own retirement life or arranging a warm, convenient place for your parents to spend their golden years, this guide has you covered.

### Who Is This Retirement KITAS Really For

The E33F is a one-year renewable residence permit for foreigners aged 55 and above.

Here's a common misconception—even though it's called a 'retirement KITAS,' you **don't** need to prove you've actually retired, nor do you need to show a government-issued pension certificate. What really matters are two things: your age and your financial capacity.

The financial threshold is pretty straightforward: you need a monthly fixed income or source of funds of at least USD$3,000, along with bank statements from the last three months showing a minimum balance of USD$2,000.

Applications are submitted through a sponsor via the eVisa system. If your spouse is under 55, they can apply for a dependent KITAS to stay with you; children under 18 are also eligible under the same arrangement.

There's also an option called the E33E, which is valid for up to five years and doesn't require a sponsor. Sounds tempting, but the bar is significantly higher—you need to deposit at least USD$50,000 into a personal account at a state-owned Indonesian bank, while still meeting the monthly income requirement of USD$3,000. Honestly, unless you're particularly well-funded, the E33F is the more practical choice for most people.

### What This Permit Can and Cannot Do

With a retirement KITAS, you can live freely and travel anywhere in Indonesia. As long as you hold a valid re-entry permit, you can also enter and exit the country without restrictions, and engage in activities related to investment or business.

But a heads-up: this permit is **not** for working as an employee for an Indonesian company and drawing a salary, nor can it be used directly for buying, selling goods, or offering services. Simply put, it's a document for you to 'stay' here, not a work permit.

### How the Application Process Generally Works

The first step is to confirm that you meet the requirements for age, financial documents, and other conditions. Then, you'll need to prepare a full set of application documents, which typically takes one to three weeks.

After submitting the application, the official processing time is five working days, but based on what I've seen, it's safer to expect seven to ten days.

If you apply from abroad, you must enter Indonesia within 90 days after approval. Your KITAS officially takes effect the moment you pass through customs. In some cases, you may also need to visit the immigration office separately for biometrics (fingerprinting, photo-taking, etc.).

Once you have your KITAS, local registration documents like the SKTT (residence registration certificate) are handled separately—don't confuse the two.

### What Documents You Need to Prepare

The main documents include:

- Passport
- Bank statements from the last three months
- Proof of income of at least USD$3,000 per month
- Insurance valid for at least one year
- Rental agreement
- Photos and a short CV
- Marriage or divorce certificate (if applicable)
- Documents related to two local domestic helpers

If documents are issued abroad, they may need to be translated and legalized. It's best to prepare these early to avoid delays.

### How much does it cost?

- E33F One Year: IDR 16,000,000
- Annual Renewal: IDR 17,000,000
- Dependent KITAS for spouse or children: IDR 12,500,000 per year
- KITAS Closing: IDR 1,500,000

This price covers government fees, document review and preparation, submission, communication with the immigration office, and accompanying services if biometrics need to be redone. Insurance, rental housing, document translation and notarization, SKTT, and residence certificates are all additional costs.

The above amounts are quotes from one agency. Fees vary significantly across different agencies in the market, so it's best to confirm the actual costs directly with the agency you plan to work with.

### How Renewal Connects to KITAP

The retirement KITAS must be renewed annually. The renewal application must be submitted within the last 30 days of the current validity period.
