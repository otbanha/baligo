# What is Indonesian QRIS? A Complete Guide to Cashless Payments in Bali 2026

When traveling to Bali, you'll likely spot a small card with a colorful square pattern and the word "QRIS" at street stalls, convenience stores, massage parlors, and even beach club counters. This is Indonesia's unified mobile payment QR code standard, which has rapidly transformed local spending habits in recent years. This article explains what QRIS is, whether Taiwanese travelers can scan and pay directly, and the practical cashless payment options available in Bali.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361369033-kjc6my8wmxwkk4flysvvmmknrmekbidltfkklf1y.png)

## What Exactly is QRIS?

QRIS stands for "Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard," launched by Bank Indonesia in 2019 to unify the country's fragmented mobile payment QR codes. Before QRIS, e-wallets like GoPay, OVO, and DANA each had their own payment codes, forcing merchants to display a row of QR codes for different users—a chaotic system. With QRIS, businesses need only one QR code; regardless of which app consumers use, they can scan the same code to complete payment—conceptually similar to Taiwan's TWQR system.

The system's adoption has been remarkable. According to Bank Indonesia data, by the first quarter of 2026, QRIS transaction volume had grown over 133% year-on-year. In Bali alone, more than 1.1 million merchants now accept QRIS, with over 1.15 million users. From roadside Nasi Goreng stalls to chain convenience stores like Indomaret and Alfamart, nearly all support QRIS scanning.

## Can Taiwanese Travelers Scan QRIS Directly with Their Phones?

This is the most common question, and the answer is: **not yet.**

Bank Indonesia has been actively promoting the "QRIS Cross-border" initiative, allowing travelers from certain countries to use their home mobile payment apps to scan local QRIS codes in Indonesia, and vice versa. As of mid-2026, officially launched partner countries include Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, and China. Bank Indonesia is also piloting cross-border QRIS with Saudi Arabia and India.

However, Taiwan is not currently on this partnership list, so popular mobile payment apps like LINE Pay and JKO Pay cannot directly scan Bali merchants' QRIS codes for checkout. To go cashless with your phone in Bali, you'll still need to use a local e-wallet.

## Practical Solution for Bali: Get a Local E-Wallet

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361435327-2024_11_11_157146_1731331014._large.jpg)

▲QRIS is accepted at all supermarkets and convenience stores, and increasingly at more shops, even roadside vendors

The good news is that signing up for an Indonesian e-wallet is quite easy—you can register using a Taiwanese phone number, and almost all of them support QRIS payments. The three most popular options are GoPay, DANA, and OVO:

- **GoPay**: Part of the super app Gojek, it lets you pay, book rides, and order food delivery—making it the go-to choice for foreign travelers, with the lowest registration barrier. However, note that GoPay currently doesn't allow direct top-ups with foreign credit cards; the main way to add funds is by paying cash at convenience stores like Indomaret or Alfamart, or asking a Gojek driver to help. Some guides mention you can use a foreign debit card for fee-free top-ups, but success varies by issuing bank, so it's best to test with a small amount after arrival.
- **DANA**: More foreigner-friendly in that you can directly link a Visa or Mastercard for QRIS payments without needing an Indonesian ID (KTP), making the process relatively straightforward.
- **OVO**: Also supports QRIS payments, but some advanced features may require local ID verification, making it a good backup option.

In practice, it's recommended to primarily use GoPay (since it handles rides, delivery, and payments all in one), while keeping some cash on hand for small purchases, as some smaller vendors still only accept cash.

## Basic Steps for Paying with QRIS

1. Open your e-wallet app (using GoPay as an example)
2. Tap the 'Scan' or 'Scan QR' function
3. Point it at the QRIS code on the shop's table or at the checkout counter
4. Confirm the merchant name and amount are correct, then enter your password or use your fingerprint to complete payment
5. Once the screen shows a successful payment message, you're done—no signature or extra steps needed

QRIS payments have no minimum amount limit, so even a tiny purchase of just one US dollar is possible. That's why even street vendors are happy to apply for QRIS.

## Things to Keep in Mind When Using QRIS

- **Always double-check the merchant name before paying**: A QRIS code usually displays the receiving merchant's name. Before paying, make sure the name matches the shop in front of you to avoid scanning the wrong or a fake QR code.
- **Be sure to enter or verify the amount yourself**: Most QRIS codes are 'dynamic,' so the app will automatically show the amount after scanning. Still, it's wise to confirm the amount again before paying.
- **Save the successful payment screen**: Occasionally, unstable networks can leave the payment status unclear. It's a good idea to keep a screenshot of the transaction record for future reconciliation.
- **Manage your e-wallet balance**: Since the stored balance is usually tied to the app, it's best to top up small amounts as needed for each trip, rather than leaving too much unused balance in your account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I have to use an Indonesian e-wallet to pay? Can't I use a credit card?** Most hotels, restaurants, and shopping malls in Bali still accept credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) directly. QRIS is mainly for small shops, street vendors, and traditional markets that previously only accepted cash. The two methods can complement each other. Generally, any shop that accepts cards will also accept QRIS; but shops that accept QRIS may not necessarily accept cards.

**Q: Are there fees for topping up?** Cash top-ups at convenience stores usually come with a small fee. The actual rate varies by app and top-up method, so check the fee details in the app before adding funds.

**Q: Will QRIS allow direct cross-border payments from Taiwan in the future?** Bank Indonesia's list of QRIS cross-border partners continues to expand, with recent additions including Japan, South Korea, and China, along with pilot programs in Saudi Arabia and India. Whether Taiwan will be included remains to be seen in future official announcements.

## Final Thoughts

QRIS has become the mainstream payment method in Bali. Even though Taiwanese travelers can't yet use local apps for cross-border QR payments directly, by signing up for GoPay or DANA in advance and combining them with cash and credit cards, you can easily handle everything from street food stalls to upscale restaurants in Bali—no more worrying about running out of cash or dealing with change.
