# What is Indonesian QRIS? A Complete Guide to Cashless Payments in Bali 2026

When traveling to Bali, you'll inevitably spot a small card with a colorful square pattern at street stalls, convenience stores, massage parlors, and even beach club counters, with 'QRIS' written beside it. This is actually Indonesia's unified national mobile payment QR Code standard, and the key driver behind locals' rapidly changing spending habits. This article fully explains what QRIS is, whether Taiwanese travelers can scan and pay directly, and the practical cashless payment methods available in Bali.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361369033-kjc6my8wmxwkk4flysvvmmknrmekbidltfkklf1y.png)

## What Exactly is QRIS?

QRIS stands for 'Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard,' launched by Bank Indonesia in 2019 to unify the country's previously fragmented mobile payment QR codes. Before QRIS, e-wallets like GoPay, OVO, and DANA each had their own payment codes, forcing merchants to display a whole row of QR codes for different users to scan—a chaotic mess. After QRIS launched, businesses only need one QR code; no matter which app a consumer uses, they can scan the same code to complete payment—conceptually similar to Taiwan's TWQR system.

The system's adoption has been remarkably rapid. According to Bank Indonesia data, as of the first quarter of 2026, QRIS transaction volume grew over 133% year-on-year. In Bali alone, more than 1.1 million merchants now use QRIS, with over 1.15 million users. From roadside Nasi Goreng stalls to chain convenience stores like Indomaret and Alfamart, nearly all support QRIS scan-to-pay.

## Can Taiwanese Travelers Scan QRIS Payments Directly with Their Phones?

This is the question on everyone's mind, and the answer is: **not yet.**

Bank Indonesia has been actively promoting the 'QRIS Cross-border (QRIS Antarnegara)' initiative, allowing travelers from select countries to scan local QRIS codes using their own mobile payment apps, and vice versa. As of mid-2026, officially launched partner countries include Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, and China. Bank Indonesia is also conducting cross-border QRIS trials with Saudi Arabia and India.

However, Taiwan is not currently on this partnership list, so popular Taiwanese mobile payment apps like LINE Pay and JKO Pay cannot directly scan Bali merchants' QRIS codes for checkout. To go cashless with your phone in Bali, you'll still need to use a local e-wallet.

## Practical Solution for Bali: Get a Local E-Wallet

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361435327-2024_11_11_157146_1731331014._large.jpg)

▲QRIS can be used at all supermarkets and convenience stores, and an increasing number of shops—even street vendors—now accept it.

The good news is that signing up for an Indonesian e-wallet is quite easy. You can register using a Taiwanese mobile number, and almost all of them support QRIS payments. The three most popular options are GoPay, DANA, and OVO:

- **GoPay**: Part of the super app Gojek, it allows you to pay, book rides, and order food delivery—making it the go-to choice for foreign travelers, with the lowest registration threshold. However, note that GoPay currently doesn't allow direct top-ups with foreign credit cards. The main way to add funds is by paying cash at convenience stores like Indomaret or Alfamart, or asking a Gojek driver to help. Some guides mention you can top up fee-free with an overseas debit card, but success depends on your issuing bank, so it's best to test with a small amount after arrival.
- **DANA**: More foreigner-friendly, as you can directly link a Visa or Mastercard for QRIS payments without needing a local Indonesian ID (KTP). The process is relatively straightforward.
- **OVO**: Also supports QRIS payments, but some advanced features may require local identity verification, making it a good backup option.

In practice, it's recommended to use GoPay as your primary option (since it handles rides, food delivery, and payments all in one), while keeping some cash on hand for small purchases, as a few smaller vendors still only accept cash.

## Basic Steps for Paying with QRIS

1. Open your e-wallet app (using GoPay as an example)
2. Tap the 'Scan' or 'Scan QR' function
3. Point it at the QRIS code on the shop's table or at the checkout counter
4. Confirm the merchant name and amount are correct, then enter your password or use your fingerprint to complete payment
5. A payment success screen will appear, and you're done—no signature or extra steps needed

QRIS payments have no minimum amount, so even a tiny purchase like a one-dollar item works. That's why even street vendors are happy to apply for QRIS.

## Things to Keep in Mind When Using QRIS

- **Always double-check the merchant name before paying**: The QRIS code usually shows the receiving merchant's name. Confirm it matches the shop you're at to avoid scanning the wrong or fake QR code.
- **Enter or verify the amount yourself**: Most QRIS codes are 'dynamic,' so the app will auto-fill the amount after scanning. Still, it's wise to double-check the amount before confirming payment.
- **Save the payment success screen**: Occasionally, unstable networks can leave the payment status unclear. Keep a screenshot of the transaction record for future reference.
- **Manage your e-wallet balance**: Since topped-up funds are usually locked inside the app, it's best to top up small amounts as needed for each trip to avoid leaving too much leftover balance in your account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I have to use an Indonesian e-wallet to pay? Can't I use a credit card?** In Bali's city center, hotels, restaurants, and shopping malls mostly still accept credit cards (Visa/Mastercard). QRIS mainly solves payment for small shops, street vendors, and traditional markets that previously only took cash. The two methods can complement each other. Generally, any shop that accepts cards will also accept QRIS, but not all QRIS-accepting shops take cards.

**Q: Are there fees for topping up?** Cash top-ups at convenience stores usually come with a small fee. The exact rate varies by app and top-up method, so check the fee details in the app before topping up.

**Q: Will QRIS allow direct cross-border payments from Taiwan in the future?** Bank Indonesia's list of QRIS cross-border partners continues to expand, recently adding Japan, South Korea, and China, with pilot programs in Saudi Arabia and India. Whether Taiwan will be included remains to be seen in future official announcements.

## Final Thoughts

QRIS has become the mainstream payment method in Bali. Even though Taiwanese travelers can't yet scan and pay directly with local apps across borders, by simply signing up for GoPay or DANA in advance and using a combination of cash and credit cards, you can easily handle everything from street food stalls to upscale restaurants in Bali—no more worrying about running out of cash or dealing with change.
