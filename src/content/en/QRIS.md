# What is Indonesian QRIS? A Complete Guide to 'Cashless Payments' for Bali Travel in 2026

When traveling to Bali, you'll definitely spot a small card with a colorful square pattern and the word 'QRIS' at street stalls, convenience stores, massage parlors, and even beach club counters. This is Indonesia's unified mobile payment QR code standard, and it's been a key driver of the rapid shift in local spending habits in recent years. This article explains what QRIS is, whether travelers from Taiwan can scan and pay directly, and the practical cashless payment options available in Bali.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361369033-kjc6my8wmxwkk4flysvvmmknrmekbidltfkklf1y.png)

## What Exactly is QRIS?

QRIS stands for 'Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard,' launched by Bank Indonesia in 2019 to unify the country's previously fragmented mobile payment QR codes. Before QRIS, e-wallets like GoPay, OVO, and DANA each had their own payment codes, forcing merchants to display a row of QR codes for different users to scan—a messy situation. After QRIS launched, businesses only need one QR code; regardless of which app a consumer uses, they can scan the same code to complete payment—conceptually similar to Taiwan's TWQR system.

The system's adoption has been remarkably fast. According to Bank Indonesia data, as of the first quarter of 2026, QRIS transaction volume grew over 133% year-on-year. In Bali alone, more than 1.1 million merchants now accept QRIS, with over 1.15 million users. From roadside Nasi Goreng stalls to chain convenience stores like Indomaret and Alfamart, almost all support QRIS scan-to-pay.

## Can Travelers from Taiwan Scan and Pay with QRIS Directly?

This is the most common question, and the answer is: **not yet.**

Bank Indonesia has been actively promoting the 'QRIS Cross-border' initiative, allowing travelers from certain countries to use their own mobile payment apps to scan local QRIS codes in Indonesia, and vice versa. As of mid-2026, officially launched partner countries include Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, and China. Bank Indonesia is also running pilot programs for cross-border QRIS with Saudi Arabia and India.

However, Taiwan is not currently on this partnership list, so popular Taiwanese mobile payment apps like LINE Pay and Jkopay cannot directly scan Bali merchants' QRIS codes for checkout. To go cashless with your phone in Bali, you'll still need to use a local e-wallet.

## Can AlipayHK scan QRIS for payment?

AlipayHK currently cannot make direct payments at merchants in Bali (Indonesia). You can transfer money from AlipayHK to GoPay, then use GoPay's QRIS for purchases.

Only the mainland China version of Alipay (Alipay Mainland) supports Indonesia's QRIS payment system.

## Practical Solution in Bali: Apply for a Local E-Wallet

The good news is that applying for an Indonesian local e-wallet is quite easy—you can register with a Taiwanese phone number, and almost all of them support QRIS payments. The three most popular options are GoPay, DANA, and OVO:

- **GoPay**: Part of the super app Gojek, it allows you to pay, book rides, and order food delivery, making it the most common entry point for foreign travelers, with the lowest registration threshold. However, note that GoPay currently does not allow direct top-ups with foreign credit cards. The main ways to top up are by paying cash at convenience stores like Indomaret or Alfamart, or asking a Gojek driver to help. Some guides also mention using overseas debit cards for fee-free top-ups, but success depends on your card issuer, so it's best to test with a small amount upon arrival.
- **DANA**: One advantage for foreign travelers is that you can directly link a Visa or Mastercard for QRIS payments without needing an Indonesian ID (KTP), making the process relatively straightforward.
- **OVO**: Also supports QRIS payments, but some advanced features occasionally require local identity verification, making it a good backup option.

In practice, it's recommended to primarily use GoPay (since it handles ride-hailing, food delivery, and payments all in one), while also keeping some small cash on hand, as a few small vendors still only accept cash.

## Basic Steps for Using QRIS Payments

1. Open your e-wallet app (using GoPay as an example)
2. Tap the "Scan" or "Scan QR" function
3. Point it at the QRIS code on the shop's table or counter
4. Confirm the merchant name and amount are correct, then enter your password or use your fingerprint to complete the payment
5. Once the screen shows a successful payment message, you're done—no signature or extra steps needed

QRIS payments have no minimum amount, so even a small purchase of just a few cents can be made, which is why even street vendors are willing to apply for QRIS.

## Things to Keep in Mind When Using QRIS

- **Always verify the merchant name before paying**: A QRIS code typically displays the receiving merchant's name, so confirm it matches the shop in front of you before paying to avoid scanning a wrong or fake QR code.
- **Always enter or double-check the amount**: Most QRIS codes are "dynamic," meaning the app will automatically show the amount after scanning, but it's still wise to confirm the amount is correct before proceeding.
- **Keep a screenshot of the successful payment screen**: Occasionally, an unstable network can leave the payment status unclear, so it's a good idea to save a screenshot of the transaction record for future reconciliation.
- **App Balance Management**: Since stored balances are mostly tied to the app, it's recommended to top up in small amounts multiple times based on each trip's needs to avoid leaving too much unused balance in your account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I have to use an Indonesian e-wallet for payments? Can't I just use a credit card?** In Bali's city areas, most hotels, restaurants, and shopping malls still accept credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) directly. QRIS mainly solves the problem for small shops, street stalls, and traditional markets that previously only accepted cash. The two methods can complement each other. Generally, any store that accepts credit cards will also accept QRIS; but stores that accept QRIS may not necessarily accept credit cards.

**Q: Are there fees for topping up?** Cash top-ups at convenience stores usually come with a small fee. The actual rate varies by app and top-up method, so check the fee details within the app before adding funds.

**Q: Will QRIS allow direct cross-border payments from Taiwan in the future?** Bank Indonesia's list of QRIS cross-border partners is still expanding, with recent additions including Japan, South Korea, and China, plus pilot programs in Saudi Arabia and India. Whether Taiwan will be included depends on future official announcements.

## Conclusion

QRIS has become the mainstream payment method in Bali. Even though travelers from Taiwan can't yet use local apps for cross-border QR payments directly, by setting up GoPay or DANA in advance and combining them with cash and credit cards, you can easily handle everything from street food to fine dining in Bali—no more worrying about running out of cash or dealing with change.
