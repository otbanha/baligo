# What is Indonesian QRIS? A Complete Guide to Cashless Payments for 2026 Bali Travel

When traveling to Bali, you'll definitely see a small card with a colorful square pattern and the word 'QRIS' at street stalls, convenience stores, massage parlors, and even beach club counters. This is Indonesia's unified mobile payment QR code standard, which has rapidly transformed local spending habits in recent years. This article fully explains what QRIS is, whether Taiwanese travelers can scan and pay directly, and the practical cashless payment methods available in Bali.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361369033-kjc6my8wmxwkk4flysvvmmknrmekbidltfkklf1y.png)

## What Exactly is QRIS?

QRIS stands for 'Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard,' launched by Bank Indonesia in 2019 to unify the country's fragmented mobile payment QR codes. Before QRIS, e-wallets like GoPay, OVO, and DANA each had their own payment codes, forcing merchants to display a row of QR codes for different users to scan—a chaotic system. After QRIS launched, businesses only need one QR code; regardless of which app consumers use, they can scan the same code to complete payment—conceptually similar to Taiwan's TWQR system.

The system's adoption has been astonishing. According to Bank Indonesia data, as of the first quarter of 2026, QRIS transaction volume grew over 133% year-on-year. In Bali alone, more than 1.1 million merchants now accept QRIS, with over 1.15 million users. From roadside Nasi Goreng stalls to chain convenience stores like Indomaret and Alfamart, almost all support QRIS scanning payments.

## Can Taiwanese Travelers Scan QRIS Directly with Their Phones?

This is the most common question, and the answer is: **not yet.**

Bank Indonesia has been actively promoting the 'QRIS Cross-border' initiative, allowing travelers from certain countries to use their own mobile payment apps to scan local QRIS codes in Indonesia, and vice versa. As of mid-2026, officially launched partner countries include Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, and China. Bank Indonesia is also piloting cross-border QRIS with Saudi Arabia and India.

However, Taiwan is not currently on this partnership list, so popular mobile payment apps like LINE Pay and JKO Pay cannot directly scan Bali merchants' QRIS codes for payment. To go cashless with your phone in Bali, you'll still need to use a local e-wallet.

The good news is that signing up for an Indonesian e-wallet is quite easy—you can register with a Taiwanese phone number, and almost all of them support QRIS payments. The three most popular options are GoPay, DANA, and OVO:

- **GoPay**: Part of the super app Gojek, it lets you pay, book rides, and order food delivery—making it the go-to choice for foreign travelers, with the lowest registration barrier. However, GoPay currently doesn't allow direct top-ups with foreign credit cards; the main way to add funds is by paying cash at convenience stores like Indomaret or Alfamart, or asking a Gojek driver to help. Some guides mention using overseas debit cards for fee-free top-ups, but success varies by issuing bank, so it's best to test with a small amount after arrival.
- **DANA**: More foreigner-friendly, as it allows direct linking of Visa or Mastercard for QRIS payments without needing a local ID (KTP), making the process simpler.
- **OVO**: Also supports QRIS payments, but some advanced features may require local identity verification, making it a good backup option.

In practice, it's recommended to use GoPay as your primary option (since it covers rides, food delivery, and payments all in one), while keeping some cash on hand for small purchases, as some smaller vendors occasionally only accept cash.

## The Basic Process of Using QRIS

1. Open your e-wallet app (using GoPay as an example)
2. Tap the "Scan" or "Scan QR" function
3. Point it at the QRIS code on the shop's table or counter
4. Confirm the merchant name and amount are correct, then enter your password or use your fingerprint to complete payment
5. A payment success screen appears, and you're done—no signature or extra steps needed

QRIS payments have no minimum amount limit, so even a tiny purchase worth just one US dollar works. That's why even street vendors are willing to apply for QRIS.

## Things to Keep in Mind When Using QRIS

- **Always verify the merchant name before paying**: The QRIS code usually displays the receiving merchant's name. Before paying, make sure the name matches the shop in front of you to avoid scanning a wrong or fake QR code.
- **Always enter or double-check the amount**: Most QRIS codes are "dynamic codes," and the app will automatically bring up the amount after scanning. Still, it's a good idea to confirm the amount is correct before paying.
- **Save the payment success screen**: Occasionally, an unstable network can leave the payment status unclear. It's wise to keep a screenshot of the transaction record for future reconciliation.
- **Manage your e-wallet balance**: Since the balance after topping up is usually tied to the app, it's best to top up in small amounts based on your current trip needs, to avoid leaving too much unused balance in the account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I have to use an Indonesian e-wallet to pay? Can't I use a credit card?** In Bali's city areas, hotels, restaurants, and shopping malls mostly still accept direct credit card payments (Visa/Mastercard). QRIS mainly solves payment scenarios for small shops, street vendors, and traditional markets that previously only accepted cash. The two methods can complement each other. Generally, shops that accept credit cards will also accept QRIS; shops that accept QRIS may not necessarily accept credit cards.

**Q: Will there be a service fee for top-ups?** Convenience store cash top-ups generally incur a small handling fee, with the actual rate varying by app and top-up channel. You can check the current fee details within the app before topping up.

**Q: Will QRIS allow direct cross-border payments from Taiwan in the future?** Bank Indonesia's list of QRIS cross-border partners is still expanding, with recent additions including Japan, South Korea, and China, as well as pilot programs in Saudi Arabia and India. Whether Taiwan will be included remains to be seen in future official announcements.

## Final Thoughts

QRIS has become the mainstream payment method in Bali. Even though travelers from Taiwan can't yet use local apps for cross-border QR payments directly, by signing up for GoPay or DANA in advance and combining them with cash and credit cards, you can easily handle everything from street food stalls to upscale restaurants in Bali—no more worries about running out of cash or dealing with change.
