# What is Indonesian QRIS? A Complete Guide to Cashless Payments for 2026 Bali Travel

When traveling to Bali, you'll inevitably spot a small card with a colorful square pattern at street stalls, convenience stores, massage parlors, and even beach club counters, with "QRIS" written beside it. This is actually Indonesia's unified national mobile payment QR Code standard, and the key driver behind the rapid shift in local spending habits. This article fully explains what QRIS is, whether Taiwanese travelers can scan and pay directly, and the practical cashless payment methods available in Bali.

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361369033-kjc6my8wmxwkk4flysvvmmknrmekbidltfkklf1y.png)

## What Exactly is QRIS?

QRIS stands for "Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard," launched by Bank Indonesia in 2019 to unify the fragmented mobile payment QR codes that previously existed. Before QRIS, e-wallets like GoPay, OVO, and DANA each had their own payment codes, forcing merchants to display a whole row of QR codes for different users to scan—a chaotic mess. After QRIS launched, businesses only need one QR code; no matter which app a consumer uses, they can scan the same code to complete payment—conceptually similar to Taiwan's TWQR system.

The system's adoption has been remarkably fast. According to Bank Indonesia data, as of the first quarter of 2026, QRIS transaction volume grew over 133% year-on-year. In Bali alone, more than 1.1 million merchants now accept QRIS, with over 1.15 million users. From roadside Nasi Goreng stalls to chain convenience stores like Indomaret and Alfamart, nearly all support QRIS scan-to-pay.

## Can Taiwanese Travelers Scan QRIS Payments Directly with Their Phones?

This is the question on everyone's mind, and the answer is: **not yet.**

Bank Indonesia has been actively promoting the "QRIS Cross-border (QRIS Antarnegara)" initiative, allowing travelers from select countries to use their own mobile payment apps to scan Indonesian QRIS codes and vice versa. As of mid-2026, officially launched partner countries include Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, and China. Bank Indonesia is also running cross-border QRIS pilot programs with Saudi Arabia and India.

However, Taiwan is currently not included in this partnership list, so commonly used mobile payment apps in Taiwan like LINE Pay and JKO Pay cannot directly scan QRIS codes at Bali merchants for payment. To go cashless with your phone in Bali, you'll still need to use a local e-wallet.

## Practical Solution for Bali: Get a Local E-Wallet

![raw-image](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-08/1786361435327-2024_11_11_157146_1731331014._large.jpg)

▲QRIS can be used not only at all supermarkets and convenience stores but also at an increasing number of shops, even roadside vendors

The good news is that signing up for an Indonesian e-wallet is quite easy—you can register using a Taiwanese phone number, and almost all of them support QRIS payments. The three most popular options are GoPay, DANA, and OVO:

- **GoPay**: Part of the super app Gojek, it allows you to pay, book rides, and order food delivery, making it the most common choice for foreign travelers with the lowest registration threshold. However, note that GoPay currently does not allow direct top-ups with foreign credit cards. The main way to top up is by paying cash at convenience stores like Indomaret or Alfamart, or asking a Gojek driver to help. Some guides also mention using overseas debit cards for fee-free top-ups, but success varies by issuing bank, so it's best to test with a small amount after arriving.
- **DANA**: A more foreigner-friendly option, as it allows you to directly link a Visa or Mastercard for QRIS payments without needing a local Indonesian ID (KTP), making the process relatively straightforward.
- **OVO**: Also supports QRIS payments, but some advanced features may require local identity verification, making it a good backup option.

In practice, it's recommended to primarily use GoPay (since it handles rides, food delivery, and payments all in one), and have DANA as a backup for card payments. Also keep some cash on hand for small purchases, as some smaller vendors occasionally only accept cash.

## Basic Steps for Paying with QRIS

1. Open your e-wallet app (using GoPay as an example)
2. Tap the "Scan" or "Scan QR" function
3. Point it at the QRIS code on the shop's table or at the checkout counter
4. Confirm the merchant name and amount are correct, then enter your password or use fingerprint to complete payment
5. The screen will show a successful payment message—no signature or extra steps needed

QRIS payments have no minimum amount limit, so even a tiny purchase like a one-dollar item can be paid this way. That's why even street vendors are willing to apply for QRIS.

## Things to Keep in Mind When Using QRIS

- **Always verify the merchant name before paying**: The QRIS code usually displays the receiving merchant's name. Before paying, make sure the name matches the shop in front of you to avoid scanning the wrong or fake QR code.
- **Be sure to enter or double-check the amount**: Most QRIS codes are "dynamic," meaning the app will automatically fill in the amount after scanning. Still, it's a good idea to confirm the amount again before paying.
- **Keep the successful payment screen**: Occasionally, unstable internet can leave the payment status unclear. It's wise to save a screenshot of the transaction record for future reconciliation.
- **Manage your e-wallet balance**: Since the stored balance is usually tied to the app, it's best to top up small amounts as needed for each trip, rather than leaving too much leftover in your account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I have to use an Indonesian e-wallet to pay? Can't I use a credit card?** Most hotels, restaurants, and shopping malls in Bali still accept credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) directly. QRIS is mainly for small shops, street vendors, and traditional markets that previously only accepted cash. The two methods can complement each other. Generally, any store that accepts cards will also accept QRIS; but not every store that accepts QRIS will take cards.

**Q: Are there fees for topping up?** Cash top-ups at convenience stores usually come with a small fee. The exact rate varies by app and top-up method, so check the fee details in the app before adding funds.

**Q: Will QRIS allow direct cross-border payments from Taiwan in the future?** Bank Indonesia's QRIS cross-border partnership list continues to expand, with recent additions including Japan, South Korea, and China, along with pilot programs in Saudi Arabia and India. Whether Taiwan will be included remains to be seen in official announcements.

QRIS has become the mainstream payment method in Bali. No more worrying about running out of cash or dealing with change hassles.
