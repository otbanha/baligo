**Bottom line**: Bali is one of the best destinations in Southeast Asia for solo travel—safe, tourist-friendly, and affordable. For getting around, make good use of **Gojek ride-hailing** or private drivers. For accommodation, Ubud (quiet, cultural vibe) or Canggu (hostel social scene, lively atmosphere) are recommended. This article includes a complete 6-day, 5-night itinerary you can follow directly.

Are you considering a solo trip to Bali? Let me tell you first: **This is absolutely a great idea.**

Bali is one of my top picks for solo travel in Southeast Asia. It's safe, tourist-friendly, reasonably priced, and being 'alone' here never feels awkward. Whether you want to unwind in peace or meet new friends, Bali has you covered.

## 1. Where to Stay? Choosing the Right Area Matters

![一、住哪裡？選對區域很重要](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-04/1775351493354-mixcollage-05-apr-2026-08-11-am-1312.jpg)

Bali is large, and different areas have completely different vibes:

- **Ubud**: Top choice for solo travelers. Yoga, meditation, healthy food, rice terrace views—you'll feel at ease walking around alone, surrounded by fellow solo travelers. Plenty of cafes and a thriving hostel culture.
- **Canggu**: A digital nomad hub with beaches, cafés, surfing, and nightlife. Perfect for those who love energy and don’t mind noise. The downside is heavy traffic.
- **Seminyak**: A bit more upscale, with excellent restaurants, spas, and beach clubs. Quieter than Canggu.
- **Nusa Dua**: A high-end resort area. If you have a generous budget and just want to unwind, consider it—but it’s better suited for couples or families; solo travel here can feel a bit dull.
- **Jimbaran**: Famous for sunset seafood barbecues.

**My advice**: If you have a short trip (5–7 days), split your stay between **Ubud** and **Canggu** for a few days each to experience two very different sides of Bali.

## 2. How to Get Around?

This is the biggest headache for solo travelers in Bali. Honestly: **Traffic in Bali is terrible, and it’s not very walkable.**

https://youtu.be/ePutHL_Ob1Q

A few solutions:

1. **Gojek / Grab**: Southeast Asia's version of Uber, cheap and convenient. Opting for a motorbike ride alone is the best deal (from central Ubud to nearby attractions costs around USD$1–2). However, during peak hours (especially in Canggu), you might have to wait longer for a ride.
2. **Rent a scooter**: If you have an international driver's license (and enough riding experience), this is the most flexible option. It costs about USD$3–5 per day. **But be warned**: Bali's traffic is chaotic and road conditions vary—beginners should not take the risk.
3. **Hire a private driver**: About USD$39–58 per day (8–10 hours), ideal for longer trips (like the Ubud highlands or Uluwatu). While hiring a driver alone isn't cheap, you can split the cost with hostel buddies.

https://youtu.be/lIV-T2jYhVk

**My advice**: Use Gojek motorbikes for short trips, and hire a driver or join a local tour for longer journeys or day trips.

## 3. Dining Alone Without Awkwardness

Bali's restaurants are very solo-diner friendly.

https://youtu.be/VuG8s-u_I0Q

- **Warung (local eateries)**: Sit at the counter alone and enjoy Nasi Goreng (fried rice) or Mie Goreng (fried noodles), costing around USD$2–3 per serving—cheap and delicious.
- **Healthy vegetarian restaurants**: Ubiquitous in Ubud, many offer counter seating or communal long tables.
- **Night markets / street stalls**: Perfect for eating while walking alone. Recommended: satay and Babi Guling (suckling pig).

The only thing to note: some upscale restaurants or beach clubs aren't keen on solo diners taking up a large table—ask first or opt for a seat at the bar.

## 4. Is It Safe? Honest Thoughts for Solo Travelers

![四、安全嗎？給獨旅者的真心話](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-04/1775351807418-3e530ee91368827f35ce3d2046220e06.jpg)

Bali is generally safe, but there are a few things to keep in mind:

- **Always wear a helmet when riding a scooter**: Police will fine you, and it's genuinely dangerous.
- **Watch out for monkeys**: In places like Uluwatu and the Sacred Monkey Forest, monkeys will snatch glasses, phones, and food. Never turn your back on them.
- **Keep valuables on you at all times**: Especially your passport, wallet, and phone. Use the locker at your hostel if available.
- **Don't accept drinks from strangers**: This applies everywhere.
- **Avoid walking alone down dark alleys late at night**: Especially in Canggu or some of the more secluded parts of Kuta.

**A quick heads-up**: Balinese people are generally warm and friendly, but if someone is overly eager to chat and insists on taking you to their 'friend's shop,' they usually have an agenda. A polite decline is all it takes.

## 5. The Best Part of Solo Travel: Making New Friends

Coming alone doesn't mean you have to go it alone the whole time.

![五、獨旅最棒的事：認識新朋友](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-04/1775352447434-54bd8eecd33bfc05d58f6767e3816f0a.jpg)

- **Stay in hostels**: Ubud and Canggu have some fantastic hostels with common areas, free breakfast, yoga classes, and coworking spaces—making it easy to strike up conversations. Top picks: Ubud Raya, Puri Garden, Tribal Bali.
- **Join day tours**: Think sunrise hikes up Mount Batur, snorkeling trips, or Thai cooking classes. Many participants are also solo travelers.
- **Take yoga or meditation classes**: Ubud is a yoga hub in Asia, and people naturally chat before and after class.
- **Hit up digital nomad cafes in Canggu**: Bring your laptop, sit at the bar or a shared table, and conversations flow easily.

## 7. Final Reminder: Slow Down

Many first-time solo travelers try to pack their itineraries full. But in Bali, **the most beautiful moments are often the blank ones**—sitting in a café by the rice fields daydreaming, watching the waves at the beach doing nothing, or chatting late into the night with new friends from the hostel.

The essence of solo travel isn't about how many sights you see, but how at ease you are with yourself.

Bali will gently remind you: you can feel whole even on your own.
