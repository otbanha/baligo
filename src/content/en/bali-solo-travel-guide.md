**Bottom line first**: Bali is one of the best destinations in Southeast Asia for solo travel—safe, tourist-friendly, and reasonably priced. For getting around alone, make good use of **Gojek ride-hailing** or private drivers. For accommodation, Ubud (quiet, cultural vibe) or Canggu (hostel social scene, lively atmosphere) are recommended. Are you considering traveling to Bali alone? Let me tell you first: **This is absolutely a great idea.**

Bali is one of my favorite Southeast Asian destinations for solo travel. It's safe, tourist-friendly, reasonably priced, and being 'alone' here never feels awkward. Whether you want to quietly unwind or meet new friends, Bali has something for you.

## 1. Where to Stay? Choosing the Right Area Matters

![一、住哪裡？選對區域很重要](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-04/1775351493354-mixcollage-05-apr-2026-08-11-am-1312.jpg)

Bali is large, and different areas have completely different vibes:

- **Ubud**: Top choice for solo travelers. Yoga, meditation, healthy food, rice field views—you'll feel at ease walking alone, and you'll find plenty of fellow solo travelers everywhere. Lots of cafes and a thriving hostel culture.
- **Canggu**: A digital nomad hub with beaches, cafés, surfing, and nightlife. Ideal for those who love energy and don't mind noise. The downside is heavy traffic.
- **Seminyak**: A bit more upscale, with excellent restaurants, spas, and beach clubs, and quieter than Canggu.
- **Nusa Dua**: A high-end resort area. If you have a generous budget and just want to unwind, consider it—but it's better suited for couples or families; solo travelers might find it a bit dull.
- **Jimbaran**: Famous for sunset seafood barbecues.

**My advice**: If you have limited time (5–7 days), split your stay between **Ubud** and **Canggu** for a few days each to experience two very different sides of Bali.

## 2. How to Get Around?

This is the biggest headache for solo travelers in Bali. Honestly: **Traffic in Bali is terrible, and it's not very walkable.**

https://youtu.be/ePutHL_Ob1Q

A few solutions:

1. **Gojek / Grab**: The Southeast Asian version of Uber—cheap and convenient. Ordering a motorcycle taxi alone is the most cost-effective option (from central Ubud to nearby attractions, roughly USD$1–2). However, during peak hours (especially in Canggu), you might have to wait longer for a ride.
2. **Rent a scooter**: If you have an international driver's license (and enough riding experience), this is the most flexible way. It costs about USD$3–5 per day. **But please note**: Traffic in Bali is chaotic, and road conditions vary—beginners are not advised to take the risk.
3. **Private car hire**: About USD$39–58 per day (8–10 hours), suitable for longer trips (like the Ubud highlands or Uluwatu). While hiring a car alone isn't cheap, you can split the cost with hostel mates.

https://youtu.be/lIV-T2jYhVk

**My advice**: Use Gojek motorcycle taxis for short trips, and hire a car or join a local tour for longer journeys or day trips.

## 3. Dining Alone Without Awkwardness

Bali's restaurants are very solo-diner friendly.

https://youtu.be/VuG8s-u_I0Q

- **Warung (local eateries)**: Sit at the counter and enjoy Nasi Goreng (fried rice) or Mie Goreng (fried noodles) for around USD$2–3 per serving—cheap and delicious.
- **Healthy vegetarian restaurants**: Ubiquitous in Ubud, many offer counter seating or communal long tables.
- **Night Markets / Street Stalls**: It's perfectly fine to eat while walking around solo. I recommend satay and Babi Guling (roast suckling pig).

One thing to note: some upscale restaurants or beach clubs aren't keen on solo diners taking up large tables—just ask first or grab a seat at the bar.

## 4. Is It Safe? Honest Thoughts for Solo Travelers

![四、安全嗎？給獨旅者的真心話](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-04/1775351807418-3e530ee91368827f35ce3d2046220e06.jpg)

Bali is generally safe, but there are a few things to keep in mind:

- **Always wear a helmet when riding a scooter**: Police will fine you, and it's genuinely dangerous.
- **Watch out for monkeys**: At places like Uluwatu and the Sacred Monkey Forest, monkeys will snatch glasses, phones, and food. Don't turn your back on them.
- **Keep valuables on you at all times**: Especially your passport, wallet, and phone. Use the hostel safe if available.
- **Don't accept drinks from strangers**: This applies everywhere.
- **Avoid walking alone down dark alleys late at night**: Especially in some of the more remote areas of Canggu or Kuta.

**A quick note**: Balinese people are generally friendly and warm, but if someone is overly eager to chat and keeps insisting on taking you to their "friend's shop," there's usually an agenda. A polite decline is all it takes.

## 5. The Best Part of Solo Travel: Making New Friends

Traveling alone doesn't mean you have to go it alone the whole time.

![五、獨旅最棒的事：認識新朋友](https://images.gobaligo.id/images/2026-04/1775352447434-54bd8eecd33bfc05d58f6767e3816f0a.jpg)

- **Stay at hostels**: Ubud and Canggu have some fantastic hostels with common areas, free breakfast, yoga classes, and coworking spaces—making it easy to strike up conversations. Top picks: Ubud Raya, Puri Garden, Tribal Bali.
- **Join day tours**: Think sunrise hikes up Mount Batur, snorkeling trips, or Thai cooking classes. Many participants are also solo travelers.
- **Take yoga or meditation classes**: Ubud is a yoga hub in Asia, and people naturally chat before and after sessions.
- **Canggu's digital nomad cafés**: Bring your laptop, sit at the bar or a shared table, and conversations flow easily.

## 7. Final Reminder: Slow Down

Many first-time solo travelers try to pack their itinerary full. But in Bali, **the most beautiful moments are often the blank spaces**—sitting in a café by the rice fields daydreaming, doing nothing by the sea watching the waves, or chatting late into the night with new friends from the hostel.

The essence of solo travel isn't about how many sights you visit, but how at ease you are with yourself.

Bali will gently remind you: being alone can make you feel whole.
